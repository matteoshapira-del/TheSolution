# Ethics and Governance of a Short-Video Persuasion Experiment on the Israeli–Palestinian Conflict

Scope: approvals, safeguards and procedures for an academic study (likely Israel-based, with possible EU/US collaborators) that shows participants a sequenced ("orchestrated") set of real-world short-form clips in a mock app and measures political-attitude change before and after. Current as of November 2026. Research notes compiled 2026-11-07.

---

## 1. Ethics review: what boards require (US Common Rule, Israeli committees, EU committees, APA 8.07/8.08, AoIR 2019)

### Takeaway
Every relevant regime will require full prior committee review, not exemption. Deliberately shifting attitudes about a live war, using graphic real footage and concealing the persuasive aim, fails the US "benign behavioral intervention" exemption and meets several EU/Finnish-style triggers for mandatory review (deviation from informed consent, strong stimuli, risk of mental harm, safety threat). In Israel the review body is the university ethics committee (IRB), not a Ministry of Health Helsinki Committee, unless the study touches a medical setting. Hebrew University has run all its committees to IRB standards since June 2025. Minors in schools also need Ministry of Education Chief Scientist approval.

### Cited Findings

**US Common Rule (45 CFR 46), which applies if US federal funds or US-IRB institutions are involved**
- The exemption for "benign behavioral interventions" (46.104(d)(3)) covers adults only. It requires prospective agreement to the intervention, and interventions must be "brief in duration, harmless, painless, not physically invasive", unlikely to have "a significant adverse lasting impact", and the investigator must have "no reason to think the subjects will find the interventions offensive or embarrassing". — [45 CFR 46.104, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.104)
- Under (d)(3)(iii), deception is allowed within that exemption only if "the subject authorizes the deception through a prospective agreement", meaning participants are told they "will be unaware of or misled regarding the nature or purposes of the research". — [45 CFR 46.104, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.104)
- Exemption (d)(3) is not among the exemptions that may apply to research with children (Subpart D). Only (d)(1), (4), (5), (6), (7), (8) are listed. — [45 CFR 46.104(b)(3), LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.104)
- Some institutions are stricter than the federal floor. For example, Kenyon College treats research involving deception as not eligible for exempt status. — [Kenyon College IRB](https://www.kenyon.edu/sponsored-faculty-projects/research-compliance/institutional-review-board-irb/deception-in-research/). Oregon State requires the protocol to "clearly address why deception or incomplete disclosure are necessary". — [Oregon State IRB](https://research.oregonstate.edu/irb/policies-and-guidance/guidance/research-involving-deception)
- An IRB may waive or alter consent elements (the legal route for incomplete disclosure) only if all of the following hold: (i) no more than minimal risk; (ii) the research "could not practicably be carried out without the requested waiver or alteration"; (iii) identifiable data are necessary if used; (iv) the waiver "will not adversely affect the rights and welfare of the subjects"; (v) where appropriate, subjects get pertinent information afterwards. — [45 CFR 46.116(f)(3), LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.116)
- Consent must begin with "a concise and focused presentation of the key information" (46.116(a)(5)(i)). It must state how confidentiality of identifying records is maintained (b)(5) and whether de-identified data may be reused (b)(9). Additional elements include unforeseeable risks (c)(1) and sharing significant new findings (c)(5). — [45 CFR 46.116, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.116)

**APA Ethics Code (2002, effective 1 June 2003, amended 2010 and 2016, current version effective 1 January 2017)** — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- 8.07(a): psychologists do not use deception "unless they have determined that the use of deceptive techniques is justified by the study's significant prospective scientific, educational, or applied value and that effective nondeceptive alternative procedures are not feasible." — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- 8.07(b): "Psychologists do not deceive prospective participants about research that is reasonably expected to cause physical pain or severe emotional distress." — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- 8.07(c): deception must be explained "as early as is feasible, preferably at the conclusion of their participation, but no later than at the conclusion of the data collection", and participants must be permitted "to withdraw their data." — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- 8.08(a): psychologists must provide "a prompt opportunity for participants to obtain appropriate information about the nature, results, and conclusions of the research" and take "reasonable steps to correct any misconceptions." 8.08(b): if debriefing is delayed or withheld, they must take "reasonable measures to reduce the risk of harm." 8.08(c): if procedures harmed a participant, they must "take reasonable steps to minimize the harm." — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- 3.04(a): psychologists "take reasonable steps to avoid harming … research participants … and to minimize harm where it is foreseeable and unavoidable." — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- 8.03 (recording voices and images): if the design includes deception, "consent for the use of the recording is obtained during debriefing." This matters if the mock app records webcam or screen data. — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)

**AoIR Internet Research: Ethical Guidelines 3.0 (franzke, Bechmann, Zimmer, Ess)**
- The guidelines were approved unanimously by AoIR membership on 6 October 2019 and supplement the 2002 and 2012 guidelines. They are often cited with a 2020 date as a technical report. — [AoIR IRE 3.0 page](https://aoir.org/ire30/); [bibbase citation](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/franzke-anjabechmann-zimmer-ess-internetresearchethicalguidelines30-2020)
- IRE 3.0 keeps a deliberative "process approach" that is "reflective and dialogical", and it structures ethics by the stages of research (sec. 3.1.1). — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- Vulnerability principle (sec. 3.2.5): "the greater the vulnerability of our subjects, the greater our responsibility and obligation to protect them from likely harms". The listed vulnerable categories include minors, "politically sensitive research", downstream harms, and "special emotional states such as grieving and/or trauma". — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- Visual images of "political protest, police violence, terrorist attacks, events in war" evoke stronger emotional impact, and "greater impact entails greater care and reflection." — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- A companion "levels of impact" framework lists, among classic harms, "manipulating news feeds", "psychological, physiological interventions without debriefing, intentional triggering, deception without cause." — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)

**Israel: university ethics committees vs. Helsinki Committees**
- Tel Aviv University's IRB states it "is not the 'Helsinki Committee' which approves human trials under the law and operates solely in medical institutions". The IRB "may not … approve clinical trials". — [TAU Institutional Review Board](https://acad-sec.tau.ac.il/senate/etics-en)
- TAU has a Departmental Review Board for Humanities, Social Sciences, Law, Arts and Management. All human research needs prior written approval, and approval is valid for one year unless stated otherwise. Review covers risk–benefit, treatment of subjects, and "respecting privacy and informed consent". — [TAU Institutional Review Board](https://acad-sec.tau.ac.il/senate/etics-en)
- Hebrew University: since June 2025, "all university ethics committees (the Executive committee and the five Faculty committees) operate under IRB standards". Researchers must check in advance "whether the funder requires a review of the application according to the IRB standard". — [HUJI Research Involving Human Subjects](https://en-research.huji.ac.il/experiments-involving-human-subjects)
- HUJI rules include the following. Researchers must complete CITI ethics training. Where "a vulnerable population is involved – approval must be obtained in advance". Consent forms must say the research "will collect data about you that can identify you". Participants receive the university Privacy Notice, and the university has a Data Protection Officer. Any protocol deviation needs prior written approval. — [HUJI Research Involving Human Subjects](https://en-research.huji.ac.il/experiments-involving-human-subjects)
- Medical experiments need a Helsinki Committee at a medical centre. HUJI notes research may also need Helsinki approval when material or information comes from patients at a medical institution. — [HUJI Research Involving Human Subjects](https://en-research.huji.ac.il/experiments-involving-human-subjects); [HUJI ethics submission](https://en-research.huji.ac.il/submitting-request-ethics-approval)
- A 2024 Research Ethics article reports that 2006 Ministry of Health Director-General guidelines made Helsinki approval "a pre-requisite for performing not only clinical research, and also social and behavioral research in medical settings". So MoH Helsinki rules reach behavioural research only when it is run in medical settings, for example recruiting via hospitals or HMOs or from trauma clinics. — [Research Ethics 2024, "The development and evolution of ethics review boards"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17470161241240594) (via search excerpt; the full text returned 403)
- Research in Israeli schools needs approval from the Ministry of Education Chief Scientist under a Director-General circular dated 13 January 2022. If the online system grants an exemption, the researcher still needs parental approval, school approval and possibly institutional approval. Topics that need a full permit include "illegal behaviors, including harassment, bullying or violence". — [Herzog College ethics page](https://www.herzog.ac.il/en/research-page/6275-2/); [Herzog: Guidelines of the Chief Scientist](https://www.herzog.ac.il/en/research-page/guidelines-of-the-chief-scientist-of-the-ministry-of-education/)
- HUJI humanities guidance (as surfaced in search) says university ethics approvals "do not replace approval from the Chief Scientist of the Ministry of Education". — [HUJI Humanities ethics form guidance](https://en-hum.huji.ac.il/node/3202776)

**EU university committees and EU funders**
- University of Helsinki, applying the Finnish National Board on Research Integrity's 2019 guidelines, requires an ethics committee statement whenever any of these apply: participation "deviates from the principle of informed consent"; research on children under 15 without separate parental consent; "exposes participants to exceptionally strong stimuli"; "involves a risk of causing mental harm that exceeds the limits of normal daily life"; or "could involve a threat to the safety of participants or researchers". — [University of Helsinki ethical review, human sciences](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/research/services-researchers/ethical-review-research/humanities-social-sciences-and-behavioural-sciences)
- Horizon Europe applications include an ethics self-assessment. If funded, it "will become part of the Grant Agreement and may give rise to binding contractual obligations". The self-assessment has a dedicated "Misuse of results" section, and examples of misuse risk include "discrimination and stigmatization". — [EUREKA: How to complete your ethics self-assessment (June 2026, v3.1)](https://www.eurekanetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/how-to-complete-ethics-self-assessment.pdf); [EC Horizon Europe "Identifying ethics issues"](https://www.statewatch.org/media/3429/eu-com-horizon-europe-identifying-ethics-issues-7-22.pdf)
- EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(a) prohibits AI systems that use subliminal or "purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques" that materially distort behaviour with "reasonably likely" significant harm, and intent is not required. Commentary on the Commission's guidelines says Art. 2(6) excludes systems built solely for scientific R&D. The Art. 2(8) research/testing exclusion does not cover real-world testing, so testing on real people falls back under Art. 5. — [Covington, Commission guidelines on prohibited AI practices](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/european-commission-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-under-the-eu-artificial-intelligence-act/); [Modulos summary](https://docs.modulos.ai/frameworks/eu-ai-act/prohibited-practices-and-transparency)

### Inferences
- A study that deliberately shifts attitudes about the war using graphic real footage cannot plausibly be "harmless" or free of "offensive" content under 46.104(d)(3)(ii). Under the Common Rule it should go to full or expedited IRB review, not exemption, and any minors would need full review under Subpart D.
- Concealing the persuasive aim is "incomplete disclosure". In the US that needs a 46.116(f) alteration of consent, and it is hard to justify if the board judges the risk above minimal, as it may for participants exposed to war trauma. "Authorized deception" in the consent form ("some aspects of the study will not be described until the end") is the standard way to stay within APA 8.07 and Common Rule norms.
- APA 8.07(b) creates tension: concealing the study's aim while showing footage reasonably expected to cause "severe emotional distress" is barred. The team should therefore either disclose the graphic nature of the content in full (keeping only the persuasive direction concealed) or remove clips that are likely to cause severe distress.
- Israeli path: faculty or university ethics committee (IRB-standard at HUJI). Add a Helsinki Committee only if recruiting via medical or mental-health settings, and Ministry of Education approval for any school-based minors. If any US-federal-funded collaborator is engaged, check whether the Israeli institution holds a Federalwide Assurance and whether the US partner's IRB must also review or rely on the Israeli one.
- EU collaborators will likely trigger their own committee review: in Finnish-model terms, for deviation from consent, strong stimuli and mental harm. If EU-funded, they also need a misuse-of-results assessment that becomes contractually binding.
- If the "orchestrated" feed uses an AI recommender that adapts to users, EU partners should get a legal opinion on AI Act Art. 5 and Art. 2(8). A fixed, researcher-curated sequence is less likely to be an "AI system" at all. This is an inference from commentary, not settled law.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the official Reichman University ethics committee page, so its specific procedures are unverified.
- The legal status of Israeli university ethics committees (statutory vs. institutional) is unconfirmed. Only the MoH Helsinki framework for medical research is clearly regulatory. The 2024 Research Ethics article was not accessible in full (403).
- I did not verify whether specific Israeli universities hold US Federalwide Assurances.
- I did not retrieve a specific German, Dutch or other EU university committee policy beyond the Finnish model and Horizon Europe.

## 2. Deception and debriefing: when concealment is allowed; dehoaxing/desensitizing; does debriefing reverse induced change?

### Takeaway
Concealing the persuasive aim is allowed only if it is necessary (no non-deceptive alternative), justified by significant value, not expected to cause severe distress, and followed by prompt debriefing with the right to withdraw data. Evidence going back to Ross, Lepper and Hubbard (1975) shows that a standard "outcome" debriefing leaves substantial residual belief. Only "process" debriefing that explains the perseverance mechanism, or "enhanced", claim-specific fact-check debriefs, reliably reverse induced beliefs. Recent work (2024–2026) shows standard debriefs often fail for politically charged falsehoods. There is little direct evidence on reversing induced attitude change, as opposed to factual belief, after video persuasion.

### Cited Findings
- Holmes (1976, American Psychologist vol. 31) set out two debriefing functions in companion papers: dehoaxing (revealing the deception) and desensitizing (pp. 868–875). — [Cambridge Handbook chapter, "Debriefing and post-experimental procedures"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-research-methods-and-statistics-for-the-social-and-behavioral-sciences/debriefing-and-postexperimental-procedures/DBE6131AEF39E8D8D848A48B02EA040B); [citation listing](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/10508422.2012.732505?scroll=top)
- Dehoaxing means telling participants about the deception and why it was used. Desensitizing means reducing negative feelings (stress, embarrassment) the study produced. — [SAGE Encyclopedia of Research Design, "Debriefing"](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/researchdesign/chpt/debriefing). Desensitizing does not always succeed, for example when participants become aware of unflattering behaviour they showed. — [Lancaster University psychology glossary](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/desensitizing/)
- Ross, Lepper & Hubbard (1975, JPSP 32(5), 880–892) ran two experiments (60 high-school students; 144 undergraduates). After a standard "outcome" debriefing, "both actors and observers showed substantial perseverance of initial impressions". "Process" debriefing, with "explicit discussion of the perseverance process", "generally proved sufficient to eliminate erroneous self-perceptions." — [bibbase abstract](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/ross-lepper-hubbard-perseveranceinselfperceptionandsocialperceptionbiasedattributionalprocessesinthedebriefingparadigm-1975) (DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.32.5.880)
- McFarland, Cheam & Buehler (2007, JESP 43(2), 233–240) replicated and extended the perseverance effect in the debriefing paradigm. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222426414_The_perseverance_effect_in_the_debriefing_paradigm_Replication_and_extension). Wegner, Coulton & Wenzlaff (1985) challenged the attributional account, arguing attributional processing is not a necessary component of perseverance. — [Wegner et al. 1985 PDF](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/DANWEGNER/pub/Wegner,Coulton,&Wenzlaff%201985.pdf)
- Greenspan & Loftus (2021/2022, Memory & Cognition) note that freshly deceived participants may suspect the debriefing is itself another deception. An enhanced debriefing that explains how misinformation and memory errors arise outperformed typical debriefing. — [Greenspan & Loftus PDF](https://faculty.sites.uci.edu/eloftus/files/2022/08/Greenspan-Loftus_Debriefing_2022print.pdf); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-021-01223-9)
- Greene & Murphy (2023, PLoS ONE 18(1): e0280295) ran a preregistered one-week follow-up of a COVID-19 fake-news study (N = 1,547 analysed of 4,228). The debrief re-presented each fake story labelled as fabricated with true information, explained how false memories form, re-consented participants, re-debunked at follow-up and supplied reliable sources. False memory fell from 12% to 7.25%, but 11.52% still held both memory and belief for the stickiest story. The authors concluded "researchers can safely and ethically conduct misinformation research on sensitive topics" when debriefing is thorough. — [PLoS ONE / PMC9858761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9858761/)
- Clayton et al. (2024, PNAS Nexus, pgae502): the "enhanced" debrief (an extensive fact-check plus active acknowledgment of exposure to falsehoods) raised belief accuracy by more than 2 points on a 7-point scale, while the original debrief produced no meaningful change. After a standard debrief, false beliefs about COVID-19 vaccines and election security persisted, while debriefs worked better for non-political falsehoods. — [Phys.org summary](https://phys.org/news/2024-12-expose-misinformation-proper-debriefs-inculcating.html); [PNAS Nexus article](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/3/12/pgae502/7913313)
- Hoes & Altay (2026, Political Science Research and Methods, online 10 Aug 2026) ran a preregistered study with N = 896 (Prolific). The specific debrief (claim-level fact-check with source links) cut belief in previously seen false claims by 0.78 points on a 7-point scale. The general warning debrief had no significant effect, and it also slightly reduced belief in true claims (−0.20), "suggesting warnings may heighten skepticism". Of 346 misinformation studies reviewed, fewer than 30% reported any debriefing. — [PSRM, Hoes & Altay 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/article/debriefings-in-misinformation-research-should-include-factchecks-to-correct-misperceptions-and-increase-perceived-learning/F1A46ABFE0132333B187FC4F70E1AA54)
- Work on mitigating belief perseverance after retraction tests "awareness training and counter-speech" as add-ons to correction. — [PMC9994702](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9994702/); [PMC10871482](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10871482/)
- APA 8.07(c) requires explaining deception no later than the end of data collection and allowing data withdrawal. 8.08(b) requires harm-reduction measures if debriefing is delayed. — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a study designed to move attitudes, a debrief should include: (1) dehoaxing that names the intended direction of persuasion and the curation and sequencing technique ("the order and selection of clips were chosen to push views toward X"); (2) a process debrief explaining perseverance, i.e. that impressions often persist after their basis is discredited (per RLH 1975); (3) claim-level corrections for any factually false or misleading clip, with links to fact-checks (per Hoes & Altay; Clayton et al.); (4) an optional counter-balancing exposure or balanced summary; (5) desensitizing and wellbeing check-in; (6) re-consent and data withdrawal; (7) follow-up re-debrief, as in Greene & Murphy.
- Because standard debriefs fail most for politically charged material, an IRB is likely to require the team to measure post-debrief attitudes, at least a short post-debrief re-measure and ideally a delayed follow-up. The team should then report residual effects as a safety outcome.
- If the design varies persuasion direction (for example pro-Israeli vs. pro-Palestinian sequences), symmetric debriefing is needed for each arm. A pure-control or balanced arm offers a reference for whether debriefed participants return to baseline.
- A general "some content may be false" warning alone is inadequate and may induce general cynicism toward true content (Hoes & Altay).

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly tests whether debriefing reverses attitude change (policy preferences, outgroup affect) induced by curated real-world short-form video. Existing evidence concerns false feedback (RLH 1975) and factual misinformation.
- I did not retrieve Holmes's (1976) own effectiveness data.
- McFarland et al. (2007) details beyond the title (e.g., whether process debriefing fully eliminated perseverance) were not verified.

## 3. Participant welfare: graphic footage, hateful content, Israeli/Palestinian participants since October 2023, minors, vulnerable groups

### Takeaway
There is strong observational evidence that exposure to graphic October 7 and war footage is associated with PTSD symptoms in Israelis. Population trauma burdens are very high on both sides. Content warnings do not reduce distress, though they raise anticipatory anxiety slightly, so they are a consent tool, not a harm-reduction tool. The safer path is to screen out or limit the most graphic clips, offer real-time opt-out and skip, screen for acute distress, set up a referral pathway in Hebrew and Arabic, and protect research staff who curate the footage. Minors require Ministry of Education and parental approval in Israel. The US benign-intervention exemption cannot be used for minors, and Finnish-model EU rules require committee review for under-15s without parental consent.

### Cited Findings
- A Tel Aviv University study found that 23% of adult Jewish Israelis suffered from PTSD after October 7. — [AFTAU](https://www.aftau.org/news_item/tau-research-23-of-adult-jewish-israelis-now-suffer-from-post-traumatic-stress-disorder/)
- A TAU smartwatch study (PLOS Mental Health, 2025) of more than 5,000 Israelis found heavy viewers of news and graphic attack footage on platforms such as Telegram and TikTok were at higher risk of PTSD. PTSD rates were about 22.9–36.0% around two months after October 7 and 15.9–24.7% after seven months (as reported). — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250910/Smartwatches-may-help-detect-PTSD-linked-to-media-exposure.aspx)
- An ongoing TAU study (Prof. Gilead, about 1,000 people) found watching October 7 videos was associated with lasting increases in post-traumatic symptoms beyond direct exposure, baseline PTSD and compulsive consumption. The researcher publicly advised against rewatching the footage. — [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/sy7kxmmkjl). Related Israeli expert advice to stop watching the videos: [Israel21c](https://archive.israel21c.org/to-keep-calm-stop-watching-october-7-videos-say-experts/) (search-level attribution; full text not fetched)
- A national Israeli study reported that about one in five Israelis were still struggling to function. It could not establish causality but supported recommendations to avoid unnecessary exposure to disturbing images. — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/one-in-five-israelis-still-strugglng-to-function-since-oct-7-new-study/amp/)
- Bridgland, Jones & Bellet (2023, Clinical Psychological Science) ran a meta-analysis of content and trigger warnings. Warnings did not reduce distress, fear or anxiety after viewing. They produced small increases in anticipatory anxiety and did not change how often people avoided the material. — [APS news release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/2023-october-content-warnings-distress.html); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/trigger-warnings-do-not-work-according-to-recent-meta-analysis/)
- AoIR IRE 3.0 (sec. 3.1.3): "simply reviewing and curating, e.g., videos of beheadings and other forms of extreme violence can have serious consequences for researchers' psychological health". This "require[s] … one or more therapeutic counter-measures as part of the research project". Institutions should have support procedures for researchers facing online threats or harassment. — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- AoIR lists trauma and grief, minors and politically sensitive research as heightened-vulnerability contexts (sec. 3.2.5). — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- APA 8.07(b) bars deceiving participants about research reasonably expected to cause "severe emotional distress". 8.08(c) requires steps to minimize harm when it occurs. — [APA Ethics Code PDF](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf)
- Dehumanization context: during the 2014 Gaza war, community samples of Israelis and Palestinians expressed "extreme (and comparable) levels of blatant dehumanization", which predicted hostility. Exposure to dehumanizing content therefore touches a live driver of support for violence. — [Kellogg/Bruneau & Kteily 2017, PLoS ONE](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2017/the-enemy-as-animal-symmetric-dehumanization-during-asymmetric-warfare)
- Minors, US: exemption (d)(3) (benign behavioral interventions) does not apply to children. — [45 CFR 46.104, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.104)
- Minors, Israel: school-based research needs Ministry of Education Chief Scientist approval or an exemption plus parental and school approval. The guidelines also refer to students signing informed consent, and violence-related topics require a full permit. — [Herzog: Chief Scientist guidelines](https://www.herzog.ac.il/en/research-page/guidelines-of-the-chief-scientist-of-the-ministry-of-education/); [Herzog ethics committee](https://www.herzog.ac.il/en/research-page/6275-2/)
- Minors, EU (Finnish model): committee review is required for research on children under 15 without separate parental consent. — [University of Helsinki](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/research/services-researchers/ethical-review-research/humanities-social-sciences-and-behavioural-sciences)
- Vulnerable populations at HUJI require advance approval. Approval from a responsible authority such as the Ministry of Education can stand in for the university's own approval. — [HUJI](https://en-research.huji.ac.il/experiments-involving-human-subjects)

### Inferences
- Recommended safeguards follow from the findings above. None are mandated verbatim by a single source.
  - (a) Exclude people under 18, or run a separate, non-graphic protocol for minors.
  - (b) Pre-screen and exclude or route out participants with recent direct exposure: bereaved families, evacuees, released hostages and their families, reservists back from Gaza, Gaza residents, and people with current PTSD symptoms above a threshold. Alternatively, allow them only into non-graphic arms.
  - (c) Specific consent disclosure of graphic and violent content, even where the persuasive direction is concealed (APA 8.07(b)).
  - (d) Visible skip, "stop" and withdraw controls on every clip in the mock app, with no penalty to compensation.
  - (e) Blur or thumbnail the most graphic frames, or cap graphicness by an a priori rating.
  - (f) Distress check-ins during and after viewing, with a pre-specified threshold that triggers a protocol (stop the session, offer a call-back).
  - (g) A resource sheet in Hebrew, Arabic and English listing support services, supplied in the debrief.
  - (h) Researcher wellbeing measures for the clip-curation team (AoIR 3.1.3).
- Because content warnings do not reduce distress (Bridgland et al.), the ethics case should rely on screening, limiting graphicness, opt-out and aftercare. The warnings themselves are there to respect autonomy and support informed consent.
- Palestinian participants (citizens of Israel, East Jerusalem, West Bank, Gaza diaspora) face very high current trauma burdens and also distinctive legal and social risks from expressing views (see Section 4). That supports a separate risk assessment for each subgroup and community consultation.

### Gaps
- Several search-surfaced studies report severe PTSD and distress in Palestinian populations since 2023, for example a reported 51.9% probable PTSD among 819 West Bank undergraduates (Feb–Apr 2024) and a reported 83.5% probable PTSD among 405 Gaza adults (Nov 2024–Jan 2025). I could not verify these against the primary papers, so they are indicative only and should not be cited as confirmed figures. Candidate sources: [Springer Discover Psychology article](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s44202-025-00457-x); [MSF Epicentre (older baseline)](https://epicentre.msf.org/publications/trauma-related-psychological-disorders-among-palestinian-children-and-adults-gaza-and)
- A dose-response figure (around 7% PTSD among non-viewers vs. over 32% among heavy viewers) appeared in search summaries of TAU work, but I could not tie it to a specific primary source.
- I did not find an Israeli or Palestinian institutional standard for distress protocols specific to war-footage research. I also did not compile specific hotline names or numbers (e.g., Hebrew- and Arabic-language mental-health lines), and these need verifying before use.

## 4. Data protection and security: GDPR Art. 9, Israel PPL Amendment 13, minimisation, pseudonymisation, storage, identification and targeting risk

### Takeaway
Political opinions are special-category data under GDPR Art. 9, lawful for research only under explicit consent (9(2)(a)) or the research derogation (9(2)(j)) with Art. 89 safeguards. Israel's Amendment 13 (in force 14 August 2025) puts political opinions in its new "highly sensitive data" category, adds DPO and reporting duties, and gives the Privacy Protection Authority real fining powers. EU→Israel transfers rely on an adequacy decision reaffirmed in January 2024 but under civil-society pressure. In the post-October 2023 climate, documented arrests and disciplinary actions over social media posts make re-identification of participants' political views a concrete targeting risk, especially for Palestinian citizens of Israel.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR Art. 9(1) prohibits processing personal data "revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs" (among other categories) unless an Art. 9(2) exception applies. — [GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- The Art. 9(2) exceptions include (a) "explicit consent … for one or more specified purposes" and (j) processing "necessary for … scientific or historical research purposes" in accordance with Art. 89(1). Art. 9(4) lets Member States add conditions for genetic, biometric and health data. — [GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- Israel Amendment 13 was approved by the Knesset on 5 August 2024 and took effect on 14 August 2025. — [DLA Piper Data Protection Laws of the World: Israel](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=IL); [Herzog Fox & Neeman](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/amendment-no-13-to-the-israeli-privacy-protection-law/)
- "Highly sensitive data" under Amendment 13 includes "political opinions, religious beliefs, criminal records, location data, biometric identifiers", plus medical, genetic and sexual orientation data. Translations of the term vary: "Especially Sensitive Data" (DLA Piper), "Highly Sensitive Data" (Herzog), "Data of Special Sensitivity" (Chambers). — [Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/amendment-no-13-to-the-israeli-privacy-protection-law/); [Chambers 2026 Israel guide](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/israel)
- A DPO is mandatory for controllers or processors "primarily involved in processing highly sensitive data on a significant scale", for those whose core activities involve systematic monitoring at significant scale, and for registrable databases. Controllers holding highly sensitive data on more than 100,000 people must report to the PPA within 30 days. — [Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/amendment-no-13-to-the-israeli-privacy-protection-law/)
- Under Amendment 13 the PPA can issue cease orders, administrative warnings and financial penalties ("hundreds of thousands of NIS (or even millions of NIS in certain circumstances)"). Courts may award statutory damages up to NIS 10,000 without proof of harm. The DPO must ensure a data-security procedure and a database-definition document exist under the 2017 Data Security Regulations. — [Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/amendment-no-13-to-the-israeli-privacy-protection-law/)
- One source reports registration and notification breaches attract NIS 150,000, doubled for databases with more than 1 million data subjects. This is secondary commentary. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/israels-privacy-reforms-bite-2026-expanded/)
- On 15 January 2024 the European Commission's first review of 11 adequacy decisions concluded that Israel's should be maintained, citing developments since 2011 including regulations for EEA-originating data. — [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/2024/01/19/european-commission-reviews-and-reaffirms-adequacy-decisions-for-11-jurisdictions/)
- In December 2024 the EDPB said the Commission's report did not fully describe each country's laws and practices. Civil-society letters in April 2024 and June 2025 urged reassessment of Israel's adequacy, citing surveillance and the Gaza war. — [EDPB letter Dec 2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_letter_20241205_european-commission-review-of-11-existing-adequacy-decisions_en.pdf); [Statewatch open letter June 2025](https://www.statewatch.org/media/5001/israel-adequacy-status-open-letter-june-2025.pdf); [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366628239/European-Commission-ignores-calls-to-reassess-Israel-data-adequacy)
- Targeting context: by 22 October 2023 Adalah was monitoring 89 cases of students facing discipline over social media expression at 25 Israeli institutions, and had documented at least 80 arrests tied to social media posts. AP later reported more than 270 Palestinian citizens arrested in the broader speech crackdown and over 100 suspended or expelled, per Adalah. These are advocacy-group counts. — [Adalah briefing, 23 Oct 2023](https://www.adalah.org/uploads/uploads/Crackdown_Freedom_Speech_23_October_2023.pdf); [AP via KVIA](https://kvia.com/news/ap-national/2023/11/30/wartime-israel-shows-little-tolerance-for-palestinian-dissent/); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/israel-hamas-war-free-speech-crackdown-palestinians-students-rcna120985)
- AoIR IRE 3.0: researchers should be mindful that data "could be used directly or indirectly against individuals in other countries or political systems". It asks whether inferred data could help authorities "target or justify crackdowns on minorities". — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- AoIR also says researchers whose identity triggers strong ideological reaction face "death threats" and "doxing", and points to an operational-security appendix (7.1). — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- HUJI consent forms must disclose collection of identifiable data and give participants the university Privacy Notice and DPO contact. — [HUJI](https://en-research.huji.ac.il/experiments-involving-human-subjects)

### Inferences
- Lawful basis: for EU-side processing, use explicit consent (9(2)(a)) for the political-opinion items, backed by 9(2)(j) and Art. 89 safeguards where national law allows. Concealing the persuasive aim must not conceal the processing purpose. The consent must still state that political opinions will be collected and analysed.
- Minimisation: collect no names; avoid free-text that may reveal identity; record no webcam or face video unless it is essential; collect only coarse demographics (age band, broad region rather than town; avoid precise location, which Amendment 13 also treats as highly sensitive); and drop platform handles. Recruitment-panel IDs should be stored separately, with a keyed link held by one custodian, and destroyed after the data-withdrawal window.
- Security: host the mock app and data on university-controlled infrastructure in Israel or the EU with encryption at rest, and avoid third-party analytics SDKs in the app. Use role-based access and a written database-definition document plus a security procedure under the 2017 Israeli Data Security Regulations.
- Disclosure control for open data: publish only aggregated or synthetic data, or use controlled-access repositories. Small cells (e.g., Arab citizens of a given town × religious affiliation) could re-identify people. Treat any subgroup with n<10 as suppressible.
- A study of a few thousand participants is unlikely to reach Amendment 13's 100,000-person reporting threshold. Universities may still require DPO sign-off because the data are "highly sensitive". This is an inference; the thresholds come from secondary sources.
- Researcher safety: avoid publicising the persuasion direction or the identities of junior staff before publication. Plan for harassment, per AoIR 3.1.3.

### Gaps
- I did not find Israeli PPA guidance specific to academic research under Amendment 13, nor any research exemption in the amended law. Herzog's summary mentions none.
- Specific Israeli rules on cross-border transfer of research data (Privacy Protection (Transfer of Data to Databases Abroad) Regulations) were not retrieved.
- Whether the Commission has re-reviewed Israel's adequacy since June 2025 was not confirmed.
- GDPR Art. 8 (child consent age for information society services) and Israeli age-of-majority rules for research consent were not verified with sources.

## 5. Lessons from controversies: Facebook emotional contagion (2014), Meta 2020 election studies, Cambridge Analytica, Zurich/Reddit r/changemyview (2025)

### Takeaway
Each case points to a norm the team should adopt. Facebook 2014: platform terms of service are not informed consent, and outsourcing data collection does not avoid ethical review. Meta 2020: industry access can compromise independence ("independence by permission"), and platform-side changes during a study can confound inference. Cambridge Analytica: research-collected personality and political data can be repurposed for political micro-targeting. Zurich 2025: an ethics committee's non-binding advice was ignored, unconsenting people were persuaded by fabricated identities, the PI was formally warned, and the findings went unpublished. Together they set the norm that persuasion research needs real consent, binding review, rules against impersonation, and no deployment to unconsenting publics.

### Cited Findings

**Facebook emotional contagion (Kramer, Guillory & Hancock, PNAS 2014)**
- The experiment manipulated the News Feeds of N = 689,003 users. The authors claimed it "was consistent with Facebook's Data Use Policy, to which all users agree prior to creating an account on Facebook, constituting informed consent for this research." — [Kramer et al. 2014 PDF](https://longevity.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/25/2018/06/Kramer-Guillory-Hancock-2014.pdf)
- Cornell's IRB determined that because Facebook ran the experiment for internal purposes, "the project did not fall under Cornell's Human Research Protection Program". — [PNAS Editorial Expression of Concern, PMC4115552](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4115552)
- The PNAS Editorial Expression of Concern (Inder Verma, PNAS 111(29):10779, July 2014) said "Questions have been raised about the principles of informed consent and opportunity to opt out". It noted that Facebook as a private company was not bound by the Common Rule, and expressed concern that the collection "may have involved practices that were not fully consistent with" consent and opt-out principles. — [PMC4115552](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4115552); [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/07/03/rapid-mood-swing-pnas-issues-expression-of-concern-for-controversial-facebook-study/)
- The APA Monitor reported critics' focus on deception, lack of IRB review and informed consent. — [APA Monitor, Oct 2014](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2014/10/research-ethics)

**Meta 2020 US Election Research Project**
- The project was a collaboration between Meta and 17 US academics led by Talia Stroud and Joshua Tucker. It produced three Science papers and one Nature paper in July 2023 using data from September to December 2020. — [Tech Policy Press primer](https://www.techpolicy.press/a-primer-on-the-meta-2020-us-election-research-studies/); [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/examining-the-meta-2020-us-election-research-partnership/)
- Experimental participation "was compensated and required informed consent". The deactivation study randomized 19,857 Facebook and 15,585 Instagram users, and the reshares study enrolled 193,880 adults. — [Tech Policy Press primer](https://www.techpolicy.press/a-primer-on-the-meta-2020-us-election-research-studies/)
- Independent rapporteur Michael W. Wagner ("Independence by permission", Science 381:388–391, 27 July 2023) observed for more than 500 hours across more than 350 meetings. He concluded "independence by permission is not independent at all" and "Meta set the agenda in ways that affected the overall independence of the researchers". He also judged the studies "rigorous, carefully checked, transparent, ethical, and path-breaking". — [Science, Wagner 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi2430); [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/a-primer-on-the-meta-2020-us-election-research-studies/)
- In September 2024 a Science eLetter (Grabowicz et al.) argued that Meta had rolled out 63 "break glass" News Feed changes around November 2020, overlapping the study window (24 Sep–23 Dec 2020), which may have shaped conclusions about algorithms and untrustworthy news. Science published the editorial "Context matters in social media" and did not seek a correction, judging the paper's caveats sufficient. — [UCD news](https://www.ucd.ie/newsandopinion/news/2024/september/27/neweletterinsciencedebunksmeta-fundedstudysuggestingitsnews-feedalgorithmsarenotmajordriversofmisinformation); [Holden Thorp](https://holdenthorp.substack.com/p/more-doubts-for-facebook-on-polarization); [IU OSoMe](https://osome.iu.edu/research/blog/science-eletter-social-media-algorithms-can-curb-misinformation-but-do-they)

**Cambridge Analytica (misuse case)**
- Aleksandr Kogan's "This is your digital life" app harvested data on up to 87 million Facebook users, including friends who had never used the app. — [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/policy-legislation/31483/facebook-fined-500000-by-the-ico-following-cambridge-analytica-data-scandal); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/10/uks-information-commissioner-will-fine-facebook-the-maximum-500k-over-cambridge-analytica-breach)
- The UK ICO fined Facebook the statutory maximum of £500,000 under the Data Protection Act 1998 (penalty notice 24 Oct 2018), noting it would have been "significantly higher" under GDPR. Facebook settled on 30 Oct 2019 without admitting liability. Facebook also paid a $5 billion FTC penalty with new privacy-governance obligations. — [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252451285/ICO-issues-maximum-500000-fine-to-Facebook); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/facebook-pays-644000-uk-fine-over-cambridge-analytica-case-doesnt-admit-fault/); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/uk-ico-imposes-maximum-fine-on-facebook-for-compromising-user-data)

**University of Zurich / r/changemyview (2024–2025)**
- The UZH Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences ethics committee reviewed the study in April 2024 and called it "exceptionally challenging". It advised better justification, informing participants "as much as possible", and full compliance with platform rules. UZH said "Recommendations from the ethics committees are not legally binding." — [Retraction Watch, 29 Apr 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/04/29/ethics-committee-ai-llm-reddit-changemyview-university-zurich/)
- Neither moderators nor commenters were told until after data collection. r/CMV bans undisclosed AI, and Reddit rules bar impersonation "in a misleading or deceptive manner". — [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/04/29/ethics-committee-ai-llm-reddit-changemyview-university-zurich/)
- The researchers posted 1,783 AI-generated comments (Dec 2024–Mar 2025). Personas included a rape victim, a trauma counsellor specialising in abuse, and a Black man opposed to BLM. A personalization condition used gender, age, ethnicity, location and political orientation inferred by another model from users' Reddit histories. The leaked draft reported 13–25% persuasion vs. a 3% human baseline. — [Internet Governance Project, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.internetgovernance.org/2026/09/08/the-ai-that-claimed-to-be-a-rape-survivor/); [UZH draft extended abstract](https://retractionwatch.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ExtendedAbstract-Zurich-AI-Reddit.pdf)
- Outcomes: the PI received a formal warning; the committee "intends to adopt a stricter review process"; the researchers decided not to publish. Reddit's chief legal officer called the study "deeply wrong on both a moral and legal level", banned all associated accounts and issued "formal legal demands". — [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/04/29/ethics-committee-ai-llm-reddit-changemyview-university-zurich/)
- Conflicting accounts: the IGP essay reports UZH initially concluded that suppressing publication "would not be proportionate" before the researchers withdrew the paper themselves. — [IGP](https://www.internetgovernance.org/2026/09/08/the-ai-that-claimed-to-be-a-rape-survivor/)
- A 2026 analysis of the released comments (Jaidka & Ahmed) found identity tactics in more than two-thirds of comments. — [arXiv 2606.05256](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05256); [IGP](https://www.internetgovernance.org/2026/09/08/the-ai-that-claimed-to-be-a-rape-survivor/)
- Commentators argue a controlled, consented design could have answered the same question, and that fabricating identities confounded the results (argument quality vs. persona). — [IGP](https://www.internetgovernance.org/2026/09/08/the-ai-that-claimed-to-be-a-rape-survivor/)

### Inferences
- Norms to adopt:
  - Platform ToS or panel terms are not consent; obtain study-specific consent (Facebook 2014).
  - Do not run on a live platform or with unconsenting audiences; the mock-app design already follows this (Zurich, Facebook).
  - Treat ethics committee conditions as binding, document compliance with them, and do not deviate without re-approval (Zurich; HUJI rules).
  - Do not fabricate personal identities or testimonies in stimuli, and label any re-edited or synthetic clips (Zurich).
  - If any partner provides data or the platform environment, set out independence terms in writing in advance and consider an independent observer (Meta 2020).
  - Log any change to the stimulus set or app during fielding, so that changes during fielding do not confound the results (Meta 2020 eLetter).
  - Never collect personality or political profiles beyond the study's purpose or share them with third parties (Cambridge Analytica).
- The Zurich case is the closest analogue: political persuasion, personalization from inferred political orientation, and trauma-related identities. Boards are likely to ask explicitly how the study differs from it.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve UZH's formal post-2025 policy changes beyond the stated intent to tighten review.
- The full text of Wagner's Science piece and the Science "Context matters" editorial were not retrieved directly; quotes come via secondary reporting.

## 6. Dual-use and misuse; funding transparency and conflicts of interest; pre-registration of directional hypotheses

### Takeaway
Guidance (AoIR IRE 3.0, Horizon Europe) expects researchers to anticipate misuse of persuasion methods and models: micro-targeting, use by state actors against minorities, and function creep. They should keep tools closed by design and limit what is released. Defence or security funding of social science has a history of controversy over independence (the Pentagon's Minerva programme), so full funding disclosure is essential for research on a live conflict. Pre-registration is standard in the most-cited exemplars (inoculation trials, misinformation debrief studies). I found no specific published norm on pre-registering the direction of attitude change, though general pre-registration practice implies it.

### Cited Findings
- AoIR IRE 3.0 (AI/ML section) warns that AI-driven insights into social dynamics "may be used for … tailoring microtargeted communication and thus potentially undermining democracy". It says "Once an AI system has left the hands of the original researchers, they may not have any control over how their models are used by others". Researchers "should thus engage actively with the reality that their methods and models may be misused by others, and find ways to mitigate risks and harms". — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- AoIR's checklist of questions includes: "Which actors will likely be interested to use the methodology for malevolent purposes, and how?"; "Is it possible to contain the potential malevolent future uses by design?"; "Up to which hypothetical data reuse moment is it appropriate to hold the researcher responsible?" — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- AoIR notes ethics review frameworks typically limit themselves to immediate impacts on stakeholders and may not assess long-term impacts of research outputs. — [AoIR ethics3.pdf](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)
- Horizon Europe treats misuse of results as a cross-cutting ethics issue with a dedicated guidance note and self-assessment section. Security-related misuse and dual-use are handled under security screening. — [EUREKA ethics self-assessment guide](https://www.eurekanetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/how-to-complete-ethics-self-assessment.pdf); [ESE summary](https://www.ese-hormones.org/what-we-do/research/european-research-funding/funding-programmes/horizon-europe/novelties-in-horizon-europe-lessons-learned/ethics-self-assessment)
- The US DoD's Minerva Research Initiative drew concern from anthropologists that military funding was "tainting the independence" of social science. Critics also raised the degree to which Pentagon priorities shaped what research gets done. — [Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/node/70745); [SSRC Items: The Minerva Controversy](https://items.ssrc.org/category/the-minerva-controversy/page/2/); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2008/04/16/pentagon-olive-branch-academe)
- NPR reported the Pentagon was ending the controversial programme. — [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/812869515)
- The Meta 2020 case shows how a funder or partner shaping the agenda compromises independence even with formal no-veto promises: "independence by permission is not independent at all". — [Science, Wagner 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi2430)
- Pre-registration examples: Roozenbeek et al. (2022) reported seven preregistered studies. Greene & Murphy (2023) and Hoes & Altay (2026) were preregistered. — [Bristol news release on Roozenbeek et al.](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2022/august/misinformation-media-release.html); [PMC9858761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9858761/); [PSRM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/article/debriefings-in-misinformation-research-should-include-factchecks-to-correct-misperceptions-and-increase-perceived-learning/F1A46ABFE0132333B187FC4F70E1AA54)
- The UZH project framed its aim as "investigating the potential of artificial intelligence to reduce polarization", showing that prosocial framing does not answer misuse or consent concerns. — [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/04/29/ethics-committee-ai-llm-reddit-changemyview-university-zurich/)

### Inferences
- Keeping the tool closed:
  - The mock app runs only behind consented, authenticated study sessions, with no public URL and no app-store release.
  - Stimulus sequences are stored in controlled-access repositories with data-use agreements, not open GitHub.
  - Publish sequencing logic at the level of principle, not as a turnkey "orchestration" engine.
  - Do not share personalization models trained on participants' political data.
  - Sign data-use and non-deployment agreements with collaborators.
  - Run a pre-publication misuse review: who could weaponise this (state information operations, partisan campaigns, armed groups) and what to withhold.
- Funding and conflicts of interest: declare all funders and in-kind support, including any Israeli Ministry of Defense, Directorate of Defense R&D, government "public diplomacy" (hasbara) units, Palestinian Authority, Gulf, EU, US-government or advocacy-group money, in consent materials where material, and in publications. Get written guarantees that funders have no role in design, analysis or the decision to publish. Ethics committees will likely examine whether a government or defence funder has an interest in a particular direction of opinion change. A study funded by a party to the conflict and designed to move opinion toward that party's position would face severe credibility and ethics objections. This is inference, consistent with the Minerva and Meta independence debates.
- Directional pre-registration: register the expected direction for each arm (or a two-sided, symmetric design), primary outcomes, debrief-reversal outcomes, harm and stop rules, and the stimulus list before fielding, ideally as a Registered Report. That shows the team is not tuning sequences to achieve an advocacy goal.

### Gaps
- I found no published standard specifically on pre-registering the direction of persuasion or attitude change. The recommendation above is inference from general practice.
- I did not find Israeli university policies on defence or government-funded social science research or on declaring such funding to ethics committees. ICMJE and journal-level conflict-of-interest rules were not retrieved.
- I did not find a formal "dual-use research of concern" framework that applies to social or behavioural persuasion research. Existing DURC policies are life-science-focused, though I did not retrieve sources on this.

## 7. Ethically safer designs common in the field

### Takeaway
The field's most defensible designs avoid pushing a partisan position. They instead build resistance to manipulation (inoculation and prebunking), reduce dehumanization, animosity or support for violence, correct misperceptions, or use balanced exposure. Several have been tested in Israeli–Palestinian or polarized settings, sometimes with lasting effects. Adversarial collaboration, where researchers with opposing priors co-design and pre-register, is an established method that maps well onto a two-community team.

### Cited Findings
- Inoculation and prebunking (Roozenbeek, van der Linden, Goldberg, Rathje & Lewandowsky, Science Advances 2022): five short videos on manipulation techniques (emotional language, incoherence, false dichotomies, scapegoating, ad hominem) were designed to be "nonpolitical, fictitious, and humorous", with no real figures. They were tested in seven preregistered studies including a YouTube field study. Recognition of manipulation techniques rose about 5% in the field, at roughly $0.05 per view, with effects consistent across liberals and conservatives. — [Bristol news](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2022/august/misinformation-media-release.html); [Poynter](https://poynter.org/?p=1024305)
- Paradoxical thinking (Hameiri, Porat, Bar-Tal, Bieler & Halperin, PNAS 2014, 111(30):10996–11001): a long-term field campaign of extreme messages that matched the "ethos of conflict" made Israeli participants, especially centre and right, more conciliatory. The effects were still present one year later, and participants reported voting more for dovish parties in 2013. — [CRIS IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/paradoxical-thinking-as-a-new-avenue-of-intervention-to-promote-p/)
- Follow-ups found paradoxical messages "unfroze" rightists' conflict-supporting beliefs, while inconsistency-based messages worked better for centrists. A message has to be extreme "but not too extreme" or it is rejected. — [HUJI CRIS: paradoxical thinking vs inconsistency](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/paradoxical-thinking-as-a-conflict-resolution-intervention-compar/); [HUJI CRIS: paradigm review](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/paradoxical-thinking-interventions-a-paradigm-for-societal-change/)
- Dehumanization (Bruneau & Kteily 2017): Israelis and Palestinians showed symmetric, extreme blatant dehumanization during the 2014 Gaza war, and it predicted hostility. This makes dehumanization a non-partisan, welfare-relevant target. — [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2017/the-enemy-as-animal-symmetric-dehumanization-during-asymmetric-warfare)
- Perspective-giving (Bruneau & Saxe 2012, JESP): perspective-giving by Palestinians to an Israeli improved attitudes toward Israelis, while a control essay had no effect. Members of a lower-power group may react negatively to being asked to take the dominant group's perspective, which is a design caveat. — [Saxe Lab PDF](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2012JESP.pdf)
- Intergroup contact reduced dehumanization and meta-dehumanization in Bruneau, Hameiri, Moore-Berg & Kteily (2021), a US–Muslim virtual contact study. — [CRIS IUCC](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/intergroup-contact-reduces-dehumanization-and-meta-dehumanization/)
- Megastudy (Voelkel, Stagnaro, Chu, Willer et al., Science 2024): 25 interventions selected from 252 submissions were tested on more than 32,000 US participants. 23 of 25 reduced partisan animosity, and several reduced support for undemocratic practices and partisan violence, with some effects lasting over two weeks. Correcting exaggerated misperceptions of the other side was among the most effective approaches. — [Stanford Sociology](https://sociology.stanford.edu/scalable-interventions-reduce-polarization-support-partisan-violence-and-anti-democratic-attitudes); [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/challenge-yields-experimental-interventions-to-strengthen-u-s-democracy)
- Adversarial collaboration: an approach traced to Daniel Kahneman, promoted by Cory Clark and Philip Tetlock's Penn Adversarial Collaboration Project, in which scholars with opposing views co-design studies. Clark and Tetlock (2022) argued "adversarial collaborations will improve behavioral science". The approach is hard and time-consuming, and some collaborations fall apart. — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/adversarial-collaboration-makes-feuding-scholars-work-together); [APA Monitor 2025](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2025/04-05/adversarial-research-collaboration)
- An AI-assisted adversarial collaboration on a "minority salience" debate (with Clark and Tetlock among the authors) is listed in Hebrew University's research repository. — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/ai-assists-adversarial-collaboration-in-debate-on-minority-salien/)

### Inferences
- Redesign options that keep the "orchestrated short-video" paradigm but lower ethical risk:
  - (a) Re-target the outcome from political position (e.g., support for a specific policy or side) to dehumanization, meta-perceptions, support for violence against civilians, or ability to spot manipulative editing.
  - (b) Use a symmetric design with pro-Israeli-framed, pro-Palestinian-framed, balanced and neutral-control sequences, so that the research question becomes "how does sequencing persuade?" rather than "push opinion toward X".
  - (c) Pair the persuasion arm with a prebunking arm that teaches recognition of sequencing and curation techniques, then use this as part of the debrief for everyone.
  - (d) Use synthetic or fictional-conflict stimuli, as Roozenbeek did, for the mechanism test, with real-conflict clips only in a balanced condition.
  - (e) Co-lead with Palestinian and Jewish-Israeli researchers in an adversarial-collaboration structure that jointly approves stimulus lists, framing, hypotheses and debrief text, and pre-registers competing predictions.
- Hameiri et al. (2014) is a precedent that Israeli ethics committees have approved field campaigns intended to shift conflict attitudes toward conciliation. The direction (de-escalatory, toward peace) and long-term outcome measurement are part of what makes such studies defensible. The team should be ready to defend any direction that is not de-escalatory.
