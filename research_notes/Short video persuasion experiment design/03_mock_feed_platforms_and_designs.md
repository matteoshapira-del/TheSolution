# Mock feed platforms, real-feed experiments and design guidance for a sequenced short-video exposure experiment (Israeli–Palestinian conflict)

Scope note: current as of 7 November 2026. Every factual claim carries a URL. Where a claim comes from a secondary summary or a vendor, that is flagged. Software licences were checked against GitHub repository metadata and raw LICENSE files where possible.

## 1. Existing research platforms and tools (licence, stack, logging, video support, hosting, studies)

### Takeaway
Three tools are already close to a "random but orchestrated" short-video app. **DICE-TikTok** (oTree; vertical swipe feed; logs watch time per participant × video and sequence position; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), **WatchLens** (React/TypeScript + FastAPI + PostgreSQL; TikTok preset; pluggable recommenders; 33-event exposure-aware log; MIT; published at RecSys 2026) and **VidShare**, the Truman-based video clone (Node.js; MIT; used in JCMC 2024 and PNAS Nexus 2025). The older general-purpose simulators are the Truman Platform, the Mock Social Media Website Tool and the (Mis)Information Game. They are mature and Qualtrics/Prolific-friendly, but they are text- and image-first. The (Mis)Information Game does not support longer video or sound. Truman's standard export has no per-post dwell time.

### Cited Findings

**Truman Platform (Cornell Social Media Lab)**
- The lab describes it as an "open-source, complete social media simulation" for running experiments with random assignment of participants to conditions; researchers create and control every simulated "actor" and its posts, likes, comments and notifications — [Truman GitBook](https://truman.gitbook.io/the-truman-platform); [Cornell SML project page](https://socialmedialab.cornell.edu/projects/the-truman-platform)
- Licence: MIT (copyright Dominic DiFranzo 2017; portions from Sahat Yalkabov's Hackathon Starter, also MIT) — [cornellsml/truman LICENSE](https://github.com/cornellsml/truman/blob/master/LICENSE)
- Stack: Node.js (current docs require Node v18.17.0; originally built on Node v10 in 2019), npm. The repo contains a Dockerfile, docker-compose.yml, `.env.example` and a PM2 `ecosystem.config.js`. The project structure comes from Hackathon Starter, and the demo runs on Heroku — [Truman prerequisites page](https://truman.gitbook.io/the-truman-platform/setting-up-truman/installing-truman/installing-the-prerequisites); [cornellsml/truman](https://github.com/cornellsml/truman); [truman_2023 README](https://github.com/cornellsml/truman_2023)
- Logging/export: `node data-export.js` writes a CSV with one row per participant. It holds ID (an MTurk ID), Qualtrics ResponseID, condition, counts and lists of liked or flagged actor posts and comments, blocked, reported and followed actors, participant-created posts and comments by study day, `TimeOnSite` in seconds and a `PageLog` of URLs. There is **no per-post dwell-time column** — [Truman: Exporting Study Data](https://truman.gitbook.io/the-truman-platform/getting-started/exporting-study-data)
- Recommended citation: DiFranzo & Bazarova (2018), ICWSM '18 workshop paper. Published studies listed include Taylor et al. 2019 (PACM HCI, cyberbullying bystanders), Masur, DiFranzo & Bazarova 2021 (PLoS ONE, self-disclosure norms), Bhandari et al. 2021 (JCMC, flagger transparency), Aghajari et al. 2023 and 2024 (CHI, misinformation norms) and Agha et al. 2024 (CHI, teen safety nudges) — [Truman: Citation and Papers](https://truman.gitbook.io/the-truman-platform/getting-started/citation-and-papers)

**VidShare (Truman-based short-video clone)**
- A "video-based social media simulation platform built with The Truman Platform", where "users perceive they are participating in a real social media platform similar to YouTube Shorts and TikTok". The condition is set by a `c_id` URL query parameter, and the demo is hosted on Heroku — [cornellsml/vidshare_2025 README](https://github.com/cornellsml/vidshare_2025)
- Studies: Zhao, Bazarova, DiFranzo, Hui, Kizilcec & Margolin (2024), JCMC 29(1), on objection strategies against problematic content; Zhao et al. (2025), PNAS Nexus, on restorative vs retributive objection appeals; Bae et al. (2025), NCA presentation — [Truman: Citation and Papers](https://truman.gitbook.io/the-truman-platform/getting-started/citation-and-papers); [vidshare_2025 README](https://github.com/cornellsml/vidshare_2025)
- Licence: the GitHub metadata shows "other". The parent Truman licence is MIT, but VidShare's own LICENSE text was not verified — [GitHub search metadata via cornellsml/vidshare_2025](https://github.com/cornellsml/vidshare_2025)

**Social Media TestDrive (Cornell, with Common Sense Education)**
- A free digital-literacy curriculum delivered through a life-like simulated social network with scripted bots, launched nationally in August 2019. It is an educational tool rather than an attitude-experiment platform — [Cornell SML: Social Media TestDrive](https://socialmedialab.cornell.edu/2019/08/08/current-project-social-media-for-education); [Cornell CALS news](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/kids-can-test-drive-social-media-safely-new-simulations)
- The code is built on Truman (`social-media-testdrive/truman_testdrive`). NSF-funded follow-on work led from Lehigh adds AI conversational agents ("Social Media Co-Pilot") — [GitHub truman_testdrive](https://github.com/social-media-testdrive/truman_testdrive); [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1032719); [Lehigh Engineering](https://engineering.lehigh.edu/node/169047)

**Mock Social Media Website Tool (Jagayat, Boparai, Pun & Choma, Toronto Metropolitan University)**
- Licence: Apache-2.0, per the GitHub metadata for `arvinsroom/mocksocialmediawebsite` and the fork `gs3bopar/mocksocialmediawebsite` — [GitHub repo](https://github.com/arvinsroom/mocksocialmediawebsite)
- It simulates **Facebook and Twitter** timelines. Post types are TEXT, PHOTO, **VIDEO** (mp4 recommended), LINK and the Twitter-only REPLYTO, RETWEET and QUOTETWEET. Media is uploaded in batches of 20 MB or less, and the developers recommend "highly compressed and sub-HD videos" because large media can crash the study. A language spreadsheet supports multilingual conditions — [Docs: Social Media Pages](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/configuring-your-study/social-media-pages/)
- Instagram, TikTok, Reddit and YouTube interfaces, and per-post viewing or hover time, are listed as **future** features — [Docs: Future Development](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/future-development/)
- Hosting: local deployment or AWS. The free tier covers "hundreds of participants for the first year", after which the developers estimate about CAD 35/month. It can be adapted to Azure, Google Cloud or in-house servers — [Docs: Overview](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/overview/); [Docs: Setting up AWS](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/deploying-online/setting-up-aws/)
- Data: a CSV with uniqueResponseID, a six-digit completion code (for MTurk or Prolific), condition, access code, participant ID, consent, startedAt and finishedAt in UTC, and columns listing the post IDs that received LIKE, LOVE, HAHA, WOW, SAD, ANGRY and LINKCLICK. A fuller JSON export and a media zip are also available — [Docs: Data](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/data/)
- Randomisation: pages given the same order number are shown in random order, and Access Codes or condition URLs handle assignment — [Docs: Sequencing Pages](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/configuring-your-study/sequencing-pages/)
- Qualtrics: the whole tool can be embedded in a Qualtrics Text/Graphic question via an iframe. Small displays such as the iPhone mini have reply-dropdown problems — [Docs: Embedding into Qualtrics](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/deploying-online/embedding-into-qualtrics/)
- Studies: the docs list only forthcoming work, including Jagayat, Pun & Choma on user reputation indicators and Schäfer et al. on hate speech and counter-speech effects on stereotypes and polarised attitudes — [Docs: Research](https://docs.studysocial.media/research)
- A 2024 Current Opinion in Psychology primer by the tool's developers compares the (Mis)Information Game, the Mock Social Media Website Tool and Truman — [Curr Opin Psychol 55:101726](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.copsyc.2023.101726); OSF project — [OSF m2xd8](https://osf.io/m2xd8)

**The (Mis)Information Game (Butler, Lamont, Wan, Prike, Nasim, Walker, Fay & Ecker)**
- Published in Behavior Research Methods 56:2376–2397 (2024). The code licence is CC BY 4.0 — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/); [GitHub](https://github.com/TheMisinformationGame/MisinformationGame)
- Stack and hosting: Node.js/npm plus Google Firebase for hosting and storage, with study design in a Google Sheets/Excel template. On the free Spark plan, studies "will stop working appropriately if the daily usage limit is exceeded" — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)
- Logging: time to first and last interaction (ms), dwell time per post (ms visible), reactions (like, dislike, share, flag) and response times, comments, follower and credibility changes, presentation order and total duration — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)
- Video: text, images and GIFs only. It "does not currently support the inclusion of longer videos or sound" — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)
- Integration: four Qualtrics linking modes, and a Prolific ID passed via `?id={{%PROLIFIC_PID%}}`. Qualtrics and the simulator cannot communicate directly, and one integration error forced the exclusion of 8 participants — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)
- Validation: Study 1 (N = 201 Prolific US adults) found that a follower-maximising prompt increased sharing of true and false posts without changing belief. Study 2 (N = 48) rated it easy to use (82.2%), engaging (80.2%) and realistic (69.8%) — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)

**DICE / DICE-TikTok (oTree-based; Roggenkamp, Boegershausen & Hildebrand)**
- DICE (Digital In-Context Experiments, formerly oFeeds) is a suite of oTree apps simulating news, shopping and social feeds. Content is driven by a CSV, a no-code GUI is available at dice-app.org, and the method paper is in the Journal of Marketing (DOI 10.1177/00222429251371702). It argues for post-level dwell times as behavioural proxies — [DICE-TikTok README](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok); [EUR Pure record](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/dice-advancing-social-media-research-through-digital-in-context-e/); [oFeeds docs](https://howquez.github.io/oFeeds/)
- **DICE-TikTok** offers "full-screen vertical videos, swipe-to-navigate, like and comment interactions, and a bottom navigation bar". For each participant × video it exports `watch_time_seconds` (paused time excluded), `liked`, `has_comment`, `comment` and `sequence_position`, plus device type, screen resolution and touch capability per session. CSV columns `condition` and `sequence` support between-subjects conditions (assigned "in rotation") and fixed or randomised positions. Survey redirects pass participant IDs as URL parameters. It deploys to oTreeHub, and the README says it is designed for iOS Safari and Android Chrome — [DICE-TikTok README](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok)
- Licence: the LICENSE files in `Howquez/DICE` and `Howquez/DICE-tiktok` are **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** — [DICE LICENSE](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE/blob/main/LICENSE); [DICE-tiktok LICENSE](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok/blob/main/LICENSE). This conflicts with a secondary summary describing oFeeds as MIT-licensed — [oFeeds GitHub](https://github.com/Howquez/oFeeds)

**WatchLens (Kim & Lee, Yonsei; RecSys '26)**
- An open-source, video-native platform for recommendation experiments. Frontend React/TypeScript; backend FastAPI + PostgreSQL; learned policies via RecBole; single-server Docker Compose deployment. Licence MIT — [arXiv 2608.04807 (alphaXiv)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04807); [GitHub WatchLens](https://github.com/WatchLens/WatchLens)
- It ships TikTok (9:16 swipe) and YouTube presets. Content can be self-hosted or embedded from YouTube. Built-in policies are random, popularity, recency, similarity and RecBole, and custom policies run as Python plug-ins or external HTTP services. It includes pre-, post- and inter-session surveys — [GitHub WatchLens README](https://github.com/WatchLens/WatchLens)
- User study: 30 students, at least 20 min/day for 4 days, within-subject and counterbalanced. Compared with diversity-based recommendations, similarity-based ones raised cumulative watch time (1,078.5 s vs 700.2 s median) and session length. The authors note the single-server design has not been tested under high concurrency — [arXiv 2608.04807](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04807)

**Other custom short-video clones used in published or preregistered studies**
- Incerti, Elkobi & Mattingly built "a clone of the TikTok app" that allows "seamless scrolling through hundreds of videos". 8,600 US respondents were recruited via Cint/Lucid. The paper does not describe the tech stack or the code release — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14118)
- An MIT preregistration (PI Shakked Noy) uses a randomised feed of TikTok, Reels and Shorts videos for up to 30 minutes. Videos and order are randomised at participant × video level and stratified 33% political / 33% "enviable" / 33% other. Recruitment is through Prolific (US, ages 18–30, N = 200) — [AEA RCT Registry 18673](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/18673)
- Liu et al. (PNAS 2025) built "a custom-built video platform with a naturalistic, YouTube-like interface presenting real YouTube videos and recommendations" — [PMC11874454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874454/)
- MIT's Yourfeed (2022) is an open research tool for social-media studies. Participants then move on to a survey for post-task attitudes — [arXiv 2207.07478](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07478)

**Browser-extension approaches (reorder real feeds)**
- **FeedMonitor** (Stanford HCI) is a Manifest V3 extension for Chrome, Edge and Firefox. It overrides XHR/fetch in page context, sends feed JSON to a researcher server (a Flask example is included) and returns a modified feed (rerank, remove, edit or add). It is desktop-only, because mobile apps use certificate pinning. The method paper is in ACM Transactions on Social Computing (DOI 10.1145/3800557) — [GitHub StanfordHCI/FeedMonitor](https://github.com/StanfordHCI/FeedMonitor); [arXiv 2406.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19571v2). GitHub search metadata shows **no licence** for this repo.
- The Prosocial Ranking Challenge used a browser extension across Facebook, Reddit and X with five LLM-classifier-based rankers (N = 9,386, six months) — [arXiv 2603.19626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19626). One ranker's code is MIT-licensed — [HumanCompatibleAI/ranking-challenge-perspective](https://github.com/HumanCompatibleAI/ranking-challenge-perspective)
- Alexandria re-ranks the X feed in real time based on a user's chosen values (ICWSM 2026) — [ICWSM/AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42697); [project page](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~farnaz/pubs/alexandria/)

### Inferences
- For a mobile-first short-video experiment, the shortest path is to fork **DICE-TikTok** (if the CC BY-NC-SA licence is acceptable for academic non-commercial use and oTree is acceptable) or **WatchLens** (MIT; better if a simulated recommender arm is needed). Both log per-video watch time and position. Neither was confirmed to support Hebrew/Arabic right-to-left layouts, which must be tested.
- Truman/VidShare gives the most realistic social layer: scripted actors, comments and notifications. Its standard export lacks per-clip dwell time and percentage watched, so the team would need to add instrumentation.
- The Mock Social Media Website Tool supports VIDEO posts and multilingual conditions, but only in Facebook and Twitter layouts, not a vertical-swipe interface. The (Mis)Information Game is unsuitable for video with sound.
- Browser-extension approaches (FeedMonitor) suit field studies on real X, Facebook or Reddit desktop feeds. They cannot cover TikTok, Reels or Shorts apps on mobile, where most short-video consumption happens.

### Gaps
- Licence text for VidShare, Social Media TestDrive and the Truman derivatives ("other" in GitHub metadata) was not individually read. FeedMonitor has no licence file listed, so reuse terms are unclear.
- No published studies using the Mock Social Media Website Tool were found; the docs list only forthcoming work.
- The tech stack and code availability of the Incerti et al. TikTok clone are not described in the paper text retrieved.
- Right-to-left (Hebrew/Arabic) rendering support was not documented for any tool.

## 2. Real-feed field experiments and their designs: effect sizes, durability, critiques

### Takeaway
Large platform-partnered and independent feed manipulations during elections usually changed **what people saw and how much they used the platform**, but produced **null or very small attitude effects** over weeks to months. Examples are the chronological feed, removing reshares, reducing like-minded sources and YouTube filter bubbles. The exceptions are targeted content-level reranking: removing or adding partisan-animosity posts shifted feeling thermometers by about 2–3 points out of 100 (Piccardi et al. 2025), and multi-ranker bridging achieved about 0.03–0.04 SD (Prosocial Ranking Challenge 2026). Short-video-specific experiments show emotional effects more reliably than attitude effects. The exception is attitudes toward a foreign country (China) moved by influencer content. Durability is rarely measured.

### Cited Findings

**Meta 2020 US Election studies (2023)**
- *Chronological feed* (Guess et al., Science 381:398–404): moving consenting Facebook and Instagram users to reverse-chronological feeds for 3 months "substantially decreased the time they spent on the platforms and their activity". It increased political and untrustworthy content and, on Facebook, decreased uncivil and slur content. It did **not** significantly alter issue polarisation, affective polarisation, political knowledge or other key attitudes — [Science, doi:10.1126/science.abp9364](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abp9364); [NYU CSMaP summary](https://csmapnyu.org/research/academic-research/how-do-social-media-feed-algorithms-affect-attitudes-and-behavior-in-an-election-campaign)
- *Reshares removed* (Guess et al., Science 2023): this "substantially decreases the amount of political news, including content from untrustworthy sources", decreases clicks and reactions, "produces clear decreases in news knowledge", and "does not significantly affect political polarization or any measure of individual-level political attitudes" — [Science, doi:10.1126/science.add8424](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.add8424)
- *Like-minded sources reduced* (Nyhan et al., Nature 2023): N = 23,377 Facebook users, with exposure to like-minded sources cut by about one-third, September–December 2020. For the median user, slightly over half of content came from like-minded sources. Cross-cutting exposure rose and uncivil language fell, with no measurable change on eight preregistered attitudinal outcomes (affective polarisation, ideological extremity, candidate evaluations, belief in false claims) — [Nature, s41586-023-06297-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w); [Syracuse Maxwell summary](https://www.maxwell.syr.edu/research/article/like-minded-sources-on-facebook-are-prevalent-but-not-polarizing)
- *Critique* (Bagchi, Grabowicz et al., Science eLetter/paper 2024): around early November 2020 Meta deployed 63 "break-glass" news-feed changes that cut views of misinformation by at least 24%. These overlapped the 24 Sept–23 Dec 2020 study window, so the control (algorithmic) feed was unusually benign. The original authors replied that their conclusions stand and that the paper had noted possible algorithm changes. Critics also raised Meta's funding and its 12 co-authors — [UMass Amherst news](https://www.umass.edu/news/article/team-led-umass-amherst-debunks-research-showing-facebooks-news-feed-algorithm-curbs); [UCD news](https://www.ucd.ie/newsandopinion/news/2024/september/27/neweletterinsciencedebunksmeta-fundedstudysuggestingitsnews-feedalgorithmsarenotmajordriversofmisinformation)
- Co-author Jennifer Pan: the chronological feed "is no silver bullet" for polarisation; coverage notes that a 3-month election window cannot assess long-term effects — [Niskanen Center](https://www.niskanencenter.org/are-claims-that-social-media-polarizes-us-overblown/)

**YouTube experiments and audits**
- *Liu et al. (PNAS 2025)*: four experiments, N = 8,883 (MTurk/CloudResearch ×3, YouGov ×1), on gun control and minimum wage. Participants watched a seed video, then chose among 4 recommendations after each video for up to 5 videos, with at least 30 s of watching required. Arms were slanted (3 like-minded/1 opposing) vs balanced (2/2), and Study 4 used fixed "rabbit-hole" sequences of increasing extremity. Sessions lasted about 15–30 min (mean about 23). Minimum detectable effects were about 0.02–0.04 on a 0–1 policy index. The only significant attitude effect was about 0.03 (conservatives, Study 3). Slant did shift choices (liberals chose liberal videos about 6 pp more often) and, among moderates, watch time. The authors "cannot rule out effects from long-term exposure or on small vulnerable subsets", and the recommendations were not personalised — [PMC11874454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874454/)
- *Hosseinmardi et al. (PNAS 2024)*, "counterfactual bots": relying only on YouTube's recommender produced **less** partisan consumption than real users' trajectories, most of all for heavy partisan consumers. The sidebar recommender "forgets" partisan preference within about 30 videos. The authors conclude that consumption "mostly reflect[s] individual preferences" since YouTube's 2019 changes — [PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.2313377121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2313377121). The lead author notes the study was limited to desktop — [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2024-02-youtube-algorithm-isnt-radicalizing-people.pdf)
- *Ribeiro et al. (FAT* 2020)* audited 330,925 videos on 349 channels (Media, Alt-lite, Intellectual Dark Web, Alt-right) and reported evidence of user migration from milder to more extreme channels. Critics include Ledwich & Zaitsev (logged-out recommendation simulations) and Munger & Phillips ("supply and demand" framing). Later work found mixed evidence — [arXiv 1908.08313](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.08313v4.pdf); [Ribeiro blog response](https://manoelhortaribeiro.github.io/posts/2019/08/radicalization-youtube); [Hosseinmardi et al. 2021 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.12843)

**TikTok audits and short-video experiments**
- *Ibrahim, Jang, Aldahoul, Kaufman, Rahwan & Zaki*: 323 sock-puppet audit experiments in Texas, New York and Georgia during the 2024 race. Republican-seeded accounts got about 11.8% more co-partisan recommendations, and Democratic-seeded accounts about 7.5% more opposite-party content. The skew exceeded what 48 engagement-based counterfactual models predicted — [arXiv 2501.17831](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17831v2). PsyPost reports PNAS Nexus publication — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/tiktoks-algorithm-exhibited-pro-republican-bias-during-2024-presidential-race-study-finds/). The authors report that TikTok tightened bot detection after 11 Nov 2024, which ended post-election auditing — [Springer Nature Communities post](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/auditing-tiktok-s-algorithm-during-the-most-consequential-election-of-our-lives)
- *Incerti, Elkobi & Mattingly (arXiv Jan 2026)*, TikTok clone, N ≈ 8,600: 20% Forced (no skipping) vs 80% Free Choice. Arms were placebo only, placebo + state media, or placebo + pro-China influencer. Sessions lasted 4 minutes, drawing on pools of 67 influencer and 89 state-media videos. Influencers raised China favourability by about +0.21 (Free) and +0.17 (Forced) on a 4-point scale, while state media lowered it by about −0.09 (Free). Each extra minute of influencer content added about +0.1. There was no effect on a petition behaviour and no durability follow-up — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14118)
- *Landgrave, Anstett & Alsharawy (Political Studies Review, 2026)*: one week in 2023 with University of Missouri students in Democratic-leaning, Republican-leaning and non-political (animal videos) TikTok conditions. There was no attitude change, but sadness, anxiety and anger rose in both political conditions — [CU Boulder A&S Magazine](https://www.colorado.edu/asmagazine/2026/03/23/tiktok-doesnt-change-minds-it-changes-moods)
- Israel–Gaza on TikTok: a WSJ bot audit reported that some new accounts were served highly polarised war content within hours, which TikTok disputed. This is a descriptive audit, not an attitude experiment, and is reported here via a secondary source — [Outside the Beltway summary](https://outsidethebeltway.com/tiktok-and-teh-israel-hamas-war/). No randomised TikTok-exposure experiment on Israel–Gaza attitudes was found in this search, and a widely circulated "30 minutes a day" claim could not be verified as causal — [NY Sun](https://www.nysun.com/article/spending-30-minutes-on-tiktok-a-day-significantly-increases-the-chances-of-holding-antisemitic-and-anti-israeli-beliefs-study-finds)

**Browser-extension and LLM-reranker field experiments (2024–2026)**
- *Piccardi, Saveski, Jia, Hancock, Tsai & Bernstein (Science 390(6776), Nov 2025)*: a preregistered 10-day field experiment on X's web "For you" feed (3 baseline + 7 intervention days, July–Aug 2024) with N = 1,256 completers. A RoBERTa political classifier plus GPT-3.5-Turbo scored 8 factors of antidemocratic attitudes and partisan animosity (AAPA). Feeds were reranked in two parallel experiments (Reduced, n = 727; Increased, n = 529). Post-survey out-party thermometer: +2.11 (Reduced; 95% CI 0.15–4.06) and −2.48 (Increased). In-feed: +3.24 and −2.56. In-feed anger and sadness moved by about ±4–5 points, while post-survey emotion effects were not significant. Reduced exposure lowered minutes/day (−4.71) and likes. The pipeline added about 3 s to the first feed load in all arms. There was no long-term follow-up, and the sample was web-only US partisans — [Science abstract via Europe PMC/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adu5584); [arXiv 2411.14652](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14652v2)
- Commentators noted that the sample was limited to web-browser X users willing to install an extension — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/11/28/social-media-algorithms-can-alter-political-views-browser-extension-study-shows); [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/algorithms-shift-polarization-why-does-policy-still-miss-the-real-problem)
- *Prosocial Ranking Challenge (Stray et al., arXiv March 2026, 44 authors)*: 9,386 desktop users on Facebook, Reddit and X over six months of the 2024 election, randomised to control or 5 rankers. The pooled affective-polarisation reduction was 0.03 SD (p < 0.05), with a 1.5-degree decrease in the thermometer gap. Engagement barely moved (Facebook −0.37 min/day, Reddit −0.2, X +0.32). In-feed surveys showed more reported negative experiences and no effects on well-being, news knowledge, empathy or support for partisan violence — [arXiv 2603.19626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19626). The team's blog gives Add News −0.044 SD and Uprank Bridging/Downrank Toxic −0.042 SD, and says power is insufficient to distinguish rankers. Only users who kept the extension installed for at least a week were analysed — [Ranking Challenge Substack](https://rankingchallenge.substack.com/p/its-possible-to-reduce-polarization)

**Older and adjacent exposure experiments relevant to condition design**
- *Bail et al. (PNAS 2018)*: 909 Democrats and 751 Republicans were paid to follow a counter-ideological Twitter bot for 1 month. Republicans became more conservative (cautious estimate +0.12 on a 7-point scale), and Democrats moved slightly and non-significantly more liberal — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2018/08/republicans-who-follow-liberal-twitter-bots-actually-become-more-conservative/); [DukeSpace record](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/afeb5af1-1876-40da-abf5-51e47750c5f5)
- *Guess & Coppock (BJPolS 2020)*: three large survey experiments (2 MTurk, 1 national) found no evidence of backlash to counter-attitudinal information, even under favourable conditions — [ISPS Yale](https://isps.yale.edu/research/publications/isps18-025); [Coppock site](https://alexandercoppock.com/guess_coppock_2020.html)
- *Durability*: Coppock, Ekins & Kirby (QJPS 2018) found op-ed effects of about 0.5 points on a 7-point scale that persisted at least one month — [ISPS Yale](https://isps.yale.edu/research/publications/isps18-09); [Now Publishers](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/QJPS-16112)
- *Megastudy* (Voelkel, Willer et al., Science Oct 2024): 25 short online interventions (videos, text, chatbots, quizzes), N = 32,059. 23 of 25 reduced partisan animosity, only 6 reduced anti-democratic attitudes, and some effects persisted at two-week follow-ups. One "democratic collapse" message increased support for violence among the most conservative Republicans — [Stanford Sociology](https://sociology.stanford.edu/scalable-interventions-reduce-polarization-support-partisan-violence-and-anti-democratic-attitudes); [Northwestern Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2024/megastudy-testing-25-treatments-to-reduce-anti-democratic-attitudes/?p=1)

### Inferences
- Expect small effects. The YouTube study could detect only effects of about 0.02–0.04 on a 0–1 scale with N ≈ 1,650–2,700 per study, and reranking studies found about 0.03–0.04 SD or 2–3 thermometer points. A single-session short-video exposure on a high-salience, entrenched issue like Israel–Palestine will likely need large N per arm, or within-person or panel designs, to detect attitude change.
- Behavioural and emotional outcomes (watch time, skipping, in-feed emotion) are more sensitive than attitudes, and should be preregistered as primary or secondary outcomes alongside attitude change.
- Feeds that change both "what" and "how much" people watch confound dose with content. Liu et al.'s fixed-choice structure and Incerti et al.'s Forced vs Free Choice split are useful templates for separating the two.

### Gaps
- Exact Meta-study percentages for time spent and untrustworthy exposure were not retrieved from the full texts; only abstract-level statements are cited.
- No study measured durability of short-video feed effects beyond the session. Incerti et al. explicitly call for repeated-exposure studies.
- No randomised experiment of TikTok-style exposure on Israeli–Palestinian attitudes was found.

## 3. Experimental design for a sequenced feed

### Takeaway
Existing studies offer tested templates for each design element. Hameiri et al. (PNAS 2014) is the closest Israeli precedent. It embedded three 30-second paradoxical-thinking clips among three generic commercials in counterbalanced 5-minute sessions across six waves, with a 6-generic-commercials control, and found effects lasting a year. Liu et al. supply balanced-vs-slanted choice architectures. Incerti et al. supply placebo filler plus Forced vs Free choice. WatchLens supplies within-subject counterbalanced recommender policies. DICE supplies rotation assignment with fixed or random positions. Demand effects appear limited in online survey experiments, but the purpose should still be masked with filler clips.

### Cited Findings
- **Paradoxical-thinking precedent (Israel)**. Hameiri, Porat, Bar-Tal, Bieler & Halperin (PNAS 2014): 249 Jewish Israelis at baseline and 161 completers, recruited through an online survey company and paid about 26 ILS. Participants were randomised to paradoxical (n = 81) or control (n = 80) over a 9-wave panel. Waves 2–7 ran every 3–4 days for about one month before the January 2013 election. Each session was a 5-min video with 3 generic commercials plus 3 "The Conflict" clips (30 s each; 15 different clips) in counterbalanced order. The control was 6 generic commercials, 3 of them shared with the treatment. Effects included unfreezing (η² = .03), lower perceived Palestinian responsibility (η² = .07; strongest among centrists), willingness to compromise (η² = .04) and self-reported dovish vote (OR = 2.92). At one year, willingness to compromise still differed (η² = .04, n = 102). Caveats: underpowered, 41% one-year retention, five of six pilot clips failed, and self-reported voting — [PMC4121792](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- Paradoxical-thinking interventions moderated attitudes mainly among those holding the most adamant views, per a 2019 review — [Hameiri, Bar-Tal & Halperin, SIPR](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/paradoxical-thinking-interventions-a-paradigm-for-societal-change/)
- **Wartime Israeli tournament**. Ushomirsky, Aldar, Roth & Halperin (EJSP 56(5), Aug 2026) tested seven social-media-based brief message interventions with N = 1,237 Jewish Israelis during the January 2025 ceasefire. "Achievements-driven loss aversion" increased support for ending the war across participants, and "attitude-based recategorisation" worked mainly on the left — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/changing-minds-in-times-of-war-an-intervention-tournament-to-incr/)
- **Balanced vs one-sided (slanted) recommendation architecture**. The 3/1 vs 2/2 recommendation sets, liberal or conservative seed (ideologues saw only a like-minded seed), and fixed escalating "rabbit hole" sequences in Study 4 come from Liu et al. — [PMC11874454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874454/)
- **Counter-attitudinal exposure**. Bail et al. found backfire among Republicans — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2018/08/republicans-who-follow-liberal-twitter-bots-actually-become-more-conservative/). Guess & Coppock found backlash rare across three experiments — [ISPS Yale](https://isps.yale.edu/research/publications/isps18-025)
- **Filler/placebo and choice architecture**. Incerti et al. used placebo videos (entertainment, nature, non-China influencers) mixed with treatment videos, 4-minute sessions, and Forced (no skip) vs Free Choice (skip allowed) arms. They note that feeds differed in non-target video share — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14118). Landgrave et al. used an animal-video non-political control — [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/asmagazine/2026/03/23/tiktok-doesnt-change-minds-it-changes-moods)
- **Dose**. Incerti et al. estimate about +0.1 favourability per extra minute of influencer content — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14118). Liu et al. used about 23-minute sessions of up to 5 videos — [PMC11874454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874454/). The MIT preregistration used up to 30 minutes, assuming about 30 s per clip — [AEA RCT 18673](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/18673). Piccardi et al. used a 7-day intervention — [arXiv 2411.14652](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14652v2)
- **Stratified content composition and randomisation**. The MIT preregistration randomises at participant × video level for which videos appear and in what order, and stratifies the feed into thirds by LLM-classified type — [AEA RCT 18673](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/18673)
- **Within-subject counterbalancing**. WatchLens used two groups receiving two recommender policies in reverse order over 4 days — [arXiv 2608.04807](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04807). Hameiri et al. counterbalanced clip and commercial order within sessions — [PMC4121792](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- **Assignment mechanics in tools**. DICE-TikTok assigns conditions "in rotation", with `sequence` fixed or blank (random) — [DICE-TikTok README](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok). In the Mock Social Media tool, equal order numbers give random order — [Docs](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/configuring-your-study/sequencing-pages/). The (Mis)Information Game uses the Qualtrics Randomizer to assign conditions, with Pre-Defined post order optionally randomised — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/). VidShare uses a `c_id` URL parameter — [vidshare_2025](https://github.com/cornellsml/vidshare_2025)
- **Simulated personalisation**. WatchLens offers random, popularity, recency, similarity and RecBole policies. The learned policy is trained on logged interactions, and feed and watch-page policies can be set separately — [GitHub WatchLens](https://github.com/WatchLens/WatchLens). Hosseinmardi et al.'s counterfactual-bot logic, comparing user-driven vs rule-based trajectories, separates algorithm from user choice — [PNAS 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2313377121)
- **In-feed measurement**. Piccardi et al. used brief in-feed surveys (ecological momentary assessment) of emotion and animosity alongside pre/post surveys and attention checks — [arXiv 2406.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19571v2)
- **Demand effects**. Mummolo & Peterson (APSR 2019) randomly told respondents the experimenter's intent across 5 designs (N > 12,000) and found no evidence of demand effects in online survey experiments. They argue that heavy obfuscation and deception "may be unnecessary in a variety of settings" — [Princeton](https://jmummolo.scholar.princeton.edu/publications/demand-effects-survey-experiments-empirical-assessment)
- **Attention and comprehension checks (Prolific rules)**. Only instructional manipulation checks and nonsensical-item checks are allowed. In studies of 5 minutes or longer, participants must fail at least 2 checks to be rejected. Time limits and "internal measures" are not valid rejection grounds — [Prolific attention-check policy](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445153-prolific-s-attention-and-comprehension-check-policy); [Prolific blog](https://blog.prolific.co/weve-updated-our-attention-and-comprehension-check-guidance/)
- **Risk of backfire in subgroups**. A megastudy treatment on "democratic collapse" increased support for violence among strong conservatives — [Northwestern Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2024/megastudy-testing-25-treatments-to-reduce-anti-democratic-attitudes/?p=1)

### Inferences
- A plausible between-subjects skeleton, assembled from the precedents above:
  - (a) placebo/filler only;
  - (b) balanced (equal pro-Israeli and pro-Palestinian clips, interleaved with filler);
  - (c) one-sided pro-attitudinal;
  - (d) one-sided counter-attitudinal;
  - (e) paradoxical-thinking clips à la Hameiri;
  - (f) a simulated engagement-adaptive recommender that upranks the clip types a participant watches longest, as a WatchLens-style similarity policy.
- Arms (c) and (d) require a pre-survey measure of prior stance, so assignment can be conditioned on it (stratified blocks by stance × nationality/religion × age).
- Use a fixed treatment-to-filler ratio, for example 3 treatment : 3 filler as in Hameiri, so arms do not differ in topical density, which was the confound Incerti et al. flagged. Counterbalance positions with a Latin square over position slots, so each treatment clip appears in each slot equally often across participants.
- Separate exposure dose from choice. Use a Forced arm (no skip, or a minimum watch time like Liu's 30 s) alongside a Free-scroll arm, and analyse dose-response on logged watch time, recognising that it is post-treatment and endogenous in the Free arm.
- Because effects are small and possibly transient, consider a multi-session panel (Hameiri: 6 exposures over a month), with an immediate post-test and a delayed follow-up of 2–4 weeks, informed by the megastudy's two-week follow-ups and Coppock's one-month persistence.
- Include manipulation checks (perceived slant of the feed) and Prolific-compliant attention checks. Given Mummolo & Peterson, filler masking is mainly for realism rather than demand control.

### Gaps
- No formal methods source on Latin squares or stratified block randomisation for feed studies was retrieved. The recommendations above are standard practice, not sourced findings.
- Full design details and effect sizes for the Ushomirsky et al. 2026 interventions (format, panel, follow-up) were not available on the abstract page.
- No study was found that tested an engagement-adaptive simulated recommender on political attitudes; WatchLens tested only engagement outcomes.

## 4. Telemetry and data model

### Takeaway
Existing tools converge on an event-level log keyed by participant × item × position, with playback events (play, pause, seek, end, progress), visibility thresholds, interactions (like, comment, share, skip), session and tab state, and the condition or policy that produced each impression. Watch time should exclude paused time and be stored with a watch ratio. Timing should use the monotonic `performance.now()` clock (coarsened to 100 µs, or 5 µs if cross-origin isolated) plus server UTC timestamps. Device type, screen and touch capability should be logged per session.

### Cited Findings
- WatchLens logs 33 event types in 6 categories (session lifecycle, navigation, playback, impression, interaction, browser state). Playback covers play, pause, seek, end and progress, with watch duration and watch ratio. Every row carries the exposure context: the recommender that produced the impression and its feed position. Built-in metrics are CTR, watch time, watch ratio (median + IQR), session length and duration — [arXiv 2608.04807](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04807); [GitHub WatchLens](https://github.com/WatchLens/WatchLens)
- DICE-TikTok logs, per participant × video, `watch_time_seconds` (paused time excluded), `liked`, `has_comment`, `comment` and `sequence_position`. Per session it logs device type, screen resolution and touch capability — [DICE-TikTok README](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok)
- FeedMonitor events (each with user_id, tab_id, URL and extension version): RenderedTweets, TweetVisible, TweetVisible1Sec, TweetVisible3Sec, LinkClick, FavoriteTweet, CreateRetweet, CreateTweet, Alive heartbeat (default 10 s), UserLeaveTab, UserReturnOnTab, PageUnload (with total active time via TimeMe.js) and UrlChange — [GitHub FeedMonitor](https://github.com/StanfordHCI/FeedMonitor)
- The (Mis)Information Game logs dwell time (ms visible), time to first and last interaction (ms), reactions with response times, and presentation order — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)
- Incerti et al. logged watch time, percent of each video completed, scroll-away, and petition link clicks — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14118). Liu et al. logged choices among recommendations, skips, likes, dislikes, save-to-watchlist and watch time — [PMC11874454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874454/)
- The Piccardi et al. guide recommends logging which items each participant was actually exposed to (to interpret what replaced removed content), behavioural signals, in-feed EMAs, attention checks, and unsupported-device use (comparing logged activity with overall activity) — [arXiv 2406.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19571v2)
- The DICE paper proposes post-level dwell times as behavioural proxies for inattention, combined with survey responses — [EUR Pure](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/dice-advancing-social-media-research-through-digital-in-context-e/)
- Timing: `performance.now()` returns a high-resolution timestamp relative to `performance.timeOrigin` on a monotonic clock that "never decreases". It is coarsened to 100 µs in non-isolated contexts and 5 µs in cross-origin-isolated contexts. `Date.now()` follows the system clock and can be affected by clock adjustments — [MDN performance.now()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance/now)
- YouTube IFrame API telemetry: `onStateChange` gives −1 unstarted, 0 ended, 1 playing, 2 paused, 3 buffering and 5 cued. `getCurrentTime()`, `getDuration()` (0 until metadata loads) and `getVideoLoadedFraction()` are available, and `onAutoplayBlocked` fires when autoplay is blocked — [YouTube IFrame API reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference)
- Mock Social Media Website Tool timestamps are startedAt and finishedAt in UTC, and interactions are stored as `|$|`-delimited lists of post IDs — [Docs: Data](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/data/)
- Pseudonymous IDs: Prolific IDs are typically passed as URL parameters (for example `?id={{%PROLIFIC_PID%}}`), and completion codes verify completion — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/). FeedMonitor assigns a random UUID on install and recommends server-side or hash-based assignment for RCTs — [GitHub FeedMonitor](https://github.com/StanfordHCI/FeedMonitor)
- Privacy safeguards recommended for feed experiments: IRB approval and consent before any collection; state what is and isn't collected; secure storage under GDPR or local rules; client-side scoring where possible; review third-party (LLM API) data sharing — [arXiv 2406.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19571v2)
- Israel's Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 was approved 5 Aug 2024 and took effect in mid-August 2025 (sources say 14 or 15 Aug). It broadens "personal information", lists highly sensitive categories, requires privacy officers for some organisations and gives the Privacy Protection Authority financial-sanction powers — [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/major-amendment-to-israeli-privacy-law-set-to-take-effect/); [Israel Desks](https://israeldesks.com/israel-enacts-strict-privacy-reform/)

### Inferences
- Recommended minimal schema:
  - **`impression`**: participant_pid_hash, session_id, condition, block/stratum, feed_position, clip_id, clip_attributes (side, valence, type: treatment or filler), policy_id, server_ts_utc, client_perf_ms_start and client_perf_ms_end.
  - **`playback_event`**: type (play, pause, seek, ended, progress at about 250 ms or each quartile), media_time and client_perf_ms. From these, derive watch_time_excl_pauses, max_position_reached, pct_watched, completions, replays (loop count) and time_to_skip.
  - **`interaction`**: like, unlike, comment text, share, mute/unmute, swipe direction.
  - **`visibility`**: tab hidden or visible, app backgrounded, idle, heartbeat.
  - **`device`**: UA, viewport, touch, orientation, network type if available.
- For political-attitude data in the Israeli context (a likely "sensitive" category), store a salted hash of the Prolific or panel ID in the telemetry database. Keep the linking key separately, and send only the hash to the survey via URL parameters.

### Gaps
- No source specified best-practice sampling frequency for progress events. Browser `timeupdate` event frequency was not retrieved.
- Whether political opinion data is classed as "highly sensitive" under Amendment 13, and any research exemptions, were not confirmed.

## 5. Implementation: stack, video delivery, integration, hosting and security

### Takeaway
Self-host the clips: MP4, or HLS for longer clips, from a CDN or university object storage with CORS, played in an HTML5 `<video muted playsinline>` element. Embedded YouTube, TikTok or Instagram players are a poor fit. YouTube's policies forbid modifying or obscuring the player and caching content. Instagram's oEmbed has no documented playback API and needs app review. Autoplay with sound requires a user gesture on all major browsers. Proven stacks are oTree (DICE-TikTok, oTreeHub), React/TS + FastAPI + PostgreSQL in Docker (WatchLens), Node.js + Docker (Truman/VidShare) and Firebase (the (Mis)Information Game). Prolific and Qualtrics integration is via URL-parameter handoff and redirects, with iframes as a fallback.

### Cited Findings
- **Video delivery options in DICE-TikTok**: local `.mp4` in `static/` is fine for a few clips under 30 s and 10 MB. For larger studies the README recommends a CDN or S3 (Cloudinary, Bunny.net, AWS S3) with CORS. It also says "Do not embed YouTube or TikTok links directly — those platforms do not allow it" (the README's claim). It mentions `yt-dlp` for obtaining stimuli, subject to "your institution's policies" — [DICE-TikTok README](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok)
- **YouTube embed constraints**: the viewport must be at least 200×200 px, with 16:9 at 480×270 or larger recommended. Only playback started via the native play button counts toward official views. Unmuted playback without user interaction is likely to be blocked — [YouTube IFrame API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference)
- **YouTube developer policies**: clients must not "modify, build upon, or block any portion or functionality of a YouTube player" (III.I.6), obscure links (III.I.4), block ads (III.I.5), separate audio and video (III.I.7), nest the player to circumvent policies (III.I.21) or "download, import, backup, cache, or store copies" without written approval (III.E.1). Users must agree to a privacy policy that discloses use of YouTube API Services (III.A.2). With autoplay on, playback data is shared with YouTube on page load (III.E.4.i) — [YouTube API Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)
- **TikTok embed player**: URL parameters include `autoplay` (default off) and `controls`. A postMessage interface lets the host send commands such as seek and receive player events and error codes, including an autoplay error. The LIVE embed starts muted when autoplay = 1. The full message list was not retrieved — [TikTok Embed Player docs](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/embed-player/); [TikTok Embed LIVE docs](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/embed-live)
- **Instagram embeds**: oEmbed covers photo, video, Reel and Feed posts, but needs Meta App Review and the oEmbed Read permission, works only for public content and returns HTML that loads `embed.js`. No playback-event API was documented — [Meta: Embed an Instagram Post](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/oembed). Meta reportedly removed thumbnail and author_name fields from Instagram oEmbed responses as of 3 Nov 2025 (community report) — [Discourse Meta thread](https://meta.discourse.org/t/configure-an-app-token-for-instagram-oneboxes/223548)
- **Browser autoplay**:
  - Chrome: muted autoplay is always allowed. Autoplay with sound is allowed after user interaction with the domain, a high Media Engagement Index (desktop), or an installed PWA or home-screen site. Cross-origin iframes need `allow="autoplay"`. Developers should check the `play()` promise and show a play button if it is rejected — [Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)
  - iOS WebKit: muted or audio-less video can autoplay and call `play()` without a gesture. Unmuting without a gesture pauses playback. `playsinline` prevents automatic fullscreen on iPhone — [WebKit: New video policies for iOS](https://webkit.org/blog/6784/)
- **Latency tolerance**: about 2 s is well tolerated, attitudes decline beyond about 4 s, and about 10 s causes disengagement. Keep added delay equal across arms by running the same pipeline in control. Piccardi et al.'s one-time delay of about 3 s was noticed by 1.9% of participants — [arXiv 2406.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19571v2)
- **Proven stacks and hosting**:
  - WatchLens: Docker Compose (db, backend, frontend, data-nginx). Production needs a public hostname, HTTPS and `COOKIE_SECURE` — [GitHub WatchLens](https://github.com/WatchLens/WatchLens)
  - DICE-TikTok: oTreeHub one-click deployment, CSV exports — [DICE-TikTok README](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok)
  - Truman: Docker, PM2 and Heroku demos — [cornellsml/truman](https://github.com/cornellsml/truman)
  - Mock Social Media tool: AWS, with free tier then about CAD 35/month — [Docs: AWS](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/deploying-online/setting-up-aws/)
  - (Mis)Information Game: Firebase, with free-tier daily limits — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)
- **Qualtrics/Prolific integration patterns**:
  - Redirect with ID in the URL, new tab, iframe, or return-to-survey. Return-to-survey caused data loss without cookies — [PMC10991066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10991066/)
  - Iframe embed in a Qualtrics Text/Graphic question — [Mock Social Media docs](https://docs.studysocial.media/docs/deploying-online/embedding-into-qualtrics/)
  - Redirect to the survey with a URL parameter at the end of the feed — [DICE-TikTok](https://github.com/Howquez/DICE-tiktok)
  - Truman's export holds a Qualtrics ResponseID column — [Truman export](https://truman.gitbook.io/the-truman-platform/getting-started/exporting-study-data)
- **Prolific costs**: minimum £6 / $8 per hour, with £9 / $12 recommended. The hourly rate is judged on the median actual completion time — [Prolific researcher help](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/article/9cd998); [Prolific participant help](https://participant-help.prolific.com/en/article/f66985). Third-party guides say the platform fee is 33.3% for academic and non-profit users and 42.8% for corporate users. Prolific's own pricing page only says "a platform fee", so these figures are unverified — [CleverX](https://cleverx.com/blog/prolific-pricing-2026/); [Prolific pricing](https://www.prolific.com/pricing-demo)
- **Mobile limits of extension approaches**: modifying mobile apps may violate terms of service, and certificate pinning blocks interception. Custom apps with web views are possible but hard to distribute, especially on iOS — [arXiv 2406.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19571v2)

### Inferences
- Recommended build for the team:
  - **Front end**: a PWA in React or Svelte with a full-screen vertical `<video muted playsinline preload="metadata">` carousel, swipe gestures and an explicit "tap to unmute" start screen, whose first tap unlocks sound for the session on Chrome and Safari.
  - **Video**: pre-transcoded H.264 MP4 or HLS at 540p–720p, served from university object storage or a CDN with signed URLs. Pre-fetch the next two clips.
  - **Back end**: FastAPI or Node with PostgreSQL. The server assigns conditions, generates each participant's sequence (seeded, logged) and holds an append-only event table.
  - **Survey handoff**: Prolific to pre-survey to app to post-survey, using redirect URLs with a hashed PID and a signed session token.
  - **Hosting**: a university VM or Kubernetes cluster behind HTTPS, with IRB-approved data residency.
- Forking DICE-TikTok or WatchLens can save months. WatchLens already covers the recommender arm, surveys and exposure-aware logging.
- Clip rights: re-hosting downloaded TikTok or YouTube clips raises copyright and platform-terms questions (YouTube III.E.1). Commissioning or licensing clips, or getting creators' permission, avoids this and also allows Hebrew, Arabic and English versions to be matched on length and production quality.

### Gaps
- No university-specific security guidance (e.g., Israeli university IT or IRB rules for cloud hosting) was retrieved.
- The full TikTok embed postMessage event list, and whether TikTok's terms permit research telemetry on embeds, were not confirmed.
- No source quantified real-world mobile autoplay failure rates in research apps.

## 6. Recruitment platforms reaching Israeli, Palestinian and international samples; costs and data quality (bots and LLMs, 2024–2026)

### Takeaway
- **Jewish and Arab citizens of Israel**: reachable through domestic online panels (iPanel with 100k+ members and a dedicated Arab-sector panel, Midgam, Panel4All) and the TAU Cohen Institute probability web panel. Prolific lists Israel as a supported country, but its pool size there is unpublished.
- **Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza**: PCPSR's polling is face-to-face on tablets (n ≈ 1,200–1,650), not online panels. A mock app there would probably have to be administered on field-team devices.
- **Pricing**: Israeli panel costs are not public.
- **Data quality**: on international panels, the 2025 threat model includes autonomous LLM agents (Westwood: 99.8% evasion of standard checks) and human LLM use in open text (33–46% on MTurk in 2023). Prolific claims high detection accuracy and less than 1% flagged, but these figures are self-reported.

### Cited Findings
- **iPanel**: "largest panel in Israel; with over 100,000 panel members", ages 12+. Founded 2006 by Shiluv and Midgam Research, ESOMAR member since 2008. It has "a dedicated panel for the Israeli-Arab sector", says it does not supplement samples from external sources within a survey, and offers an omnibus with a representative 500-person sample — [iPanel site](https://www.ipanel.co.il/en/); [iPanel ESOMAR 28 answers](https://ipanel.co.il/files/iPanels-Answers-to-28-ESOMAR-Questions.pdf)

- **Panel4All**: used by Lazar Research for Maariv polls — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-885823)
- **TAU Cohen Institute**: describes "the first web-based probabilistic panel in Israel", with about 10,000 Jewish and 1,000 Arab participants at the time of the report — [TAU report PDF](https://social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/bicohen/Israel%20Probabilistic%20Web%20Panel%20FINAL%20130922.pdf)
- **INSS (Nov 2025)** example: 801 online interviews in Hebrew and 150 in Arabic — [INSS survey Nov 2025](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-november-2025/)
- **Panel experiment precedent**: Hameiri et al. used an Israeli internet survey company and paid about 26 ILS (about US$7.50) for a 9-wave panel. Retention was 64.7% across waves and 41% at one year, and gender predicted dropout — [PMC4121792](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- **PCPSR (Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research)**:
  - Poll 95 (1–4 May 2025): N = 1,270 face-to-face (830 West Bank in 83 locations, 440 Gaza in 44 locations), ±3.5%. Completed interviews upload automatically to a PCPSR-only server — [PCPSR Poll 95](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Poll%2095%20press%20release%206May2025%20ENGLISH.pdf)
  - Poll 96 (22–25 Oct 2025): ±3.5%. Gaza interviews took place partly in built-up and tent shelters for residents of inaccessible areas — [PCPSR Poll 96](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Poll%2096%20press%20release%20FINAL%20ENGLISH%2028%20Oct%202025.pdf)
  - Arab Barometer Wave IX (8–26 Oct 2025): N = 1,655 (855 Gaza, 800 West Bank), ±3% — [PCPSR AB9 Report #1](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/AB9%20Palestine%20Report%20%231-Gov-Domestic%20Balance%20of%20Power%20%2026Jan2026%20ENGLISH.pdf)
- **Prolific coverage**: Israel appears among supported countries, but country lists across help pages conflict. Per-country "active" counts (participated in the last 3 months) are visible only in the audience checker — [Prolific: Who are the participants](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445224-who-are-the-participants-on-prolific)
- **Other international panels**: Cint/Lucid was used for the 8,600-person US TikTok-clone study, balanced on age, gender, ethnicity and region — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14118)
- **Autonomous AI respondents**:
  - Westwood (PNAS 2025): an "autonomous synthetic respondent" built from a roughly 500-word prompt evaded detection 99.8% of the time, including "reverse shibboleth" items. It costs about $0.05 per survey vs about $1.50 for human respondents. Westwood calls for new validation standards and less reliance on low-barrier non-probability samples — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/a-researcher-made-an-ai-that-completely-breaks-the-online-surveys-scientists-rely-on/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1106172)
  - Critic Andrew Gordon, writing in Research Live, argues the threat is overstated: the agent was purpose-built, the full code was not shared, and no peer-reviewed evidence shows large-scale infiltration — [Research Live](https://www.research-live.com/article/opinion/the-right-safeguards-can-protect-public-opinion-polls-from-ai-pollution/id/5148723)
- **Human LLM use**:
  - Veselovsky, Ribeiro & West estimated that 33–46% of MTurk workers used LLMs on a summarisation task (2023) — [arXiv 2306.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07899v1). A 2025 expanded version tests mitigation strategies — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/prevalence-and-prevention-of-large-language-model-use-in-crowd-wo/)
  - In a 2024 Prolific sample, 34.3% reported using AI to answer open-ended questions (reported by 404 Media) — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/a-researcher-made-an-ai-that-completely-breaks-the-online-surveys-scientists-rely-on/)
- **Prolific countermeasures (vendor claims)**:
  - "LLM checks" (May 2025) flag copy-paste and tab-switching on free text, at 98.7% precision. "Bot checks" (early 2026) detected five agents with 100% accuracy in internal tests. An internal audit flagged 0.99% of participants for AI-generated responses. A "100% Human Guarantee" refunds twice the cost of agents detected — [Prolific: authenticity checks](https://www.prolific.com/resources/authenticity-checks-how-we-tested-the-most-accurate-method-for-identifying-agentic-ai); [Prolific help: preventing AI responses](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445207-how-do-i-prevent-ai-generated-responses-in-my-study); [Prolific: how it detects bots](https://www.prolific.com/resources/how-prolific-detects-bots-and-ai-in-online-research)
  - Prolific advises against researcher-built typing-speed or focus checks as noisy, and notes that CAPTCHAs do not stop AI-assisted humans — [Prolific help](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445207-how-do-i-prevent-ai-generated-responses-in-my-study)

### Inferences
- A video-watching app with per-clip playback telemetry is itself a strong bot and inattention screen. Implausible watch patterns, such as zero pauses, identical dwell distributions, no touch events on mobile or background-tab playback, can be preregistered as exclusion criteria, in addition to Prolific-compliant attention checks.
- Sample strategy by population:
  - Jewish and Arab citizens of Israel: iPanel or Midgam, with Hebrew and Arabic instruments.
  - International samples: Prolific, with quotas.
  - Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza: a field partner such as PCPSR running the app on tablets offline or pre-cached, given face-to-face norms and Gaza conditions. Ethical review for exposing war-affected populations to conflict video needs particular care.
- Budget for attrition in multi-wave designs. Hameiri lost about 35% across the exposure period and about 59% by one year.

### Gaps
- No public per-complete prices were found for iPanel, Midgam, Panel4All or PCPSR field work. Quotes must be requested.
- No published data-quality audits (bots, LLM use, speeding) specific to Israeli online panels were found.
- Prolific's active-participant count in Israel, and its Arabic-speaking subpool, were not retrievable from static pages.
- No online panel covering Palestinians in the West Bank or Gaza was identified. AWRAD and other Palestinian pollsters were not researched.
- Independent (non-vendor) validation of Prolific's bot and LLM detection accuracy was not found.
