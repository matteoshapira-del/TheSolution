# Participant-level outcome measures ("KPIs") for media-exposure experiments on the Israeli–Palestinian conflict

Scope note: these notes cover validated self-report scales, intervention effect sizes and durability, behavioural and engagement measures, design and analysis choices, and implicit measures. They are written for a pre/post/delayed-follow-up experiment with curated short-form videos and the serious game "The Solution". Items marked **[POST-OCT-2023]** come from data collected after 7 October 2023. Effect sizes marked "computed here" are my own arithmetic from means and SDs that the papers report; they are not stated in the papers.

---

## Q1. Which validated scales exist for each construct (item wording, structure, reliability, and use in Israeli or Palestinian samples)?

### Takeaway
Few of these constructs have one standard battery. The common practice in Israeli and Palestinian samples (Halperin, Bruneau, Kteily, Hameiri) is a set of short 2–8 item composites on 1–6/1–7 Likert scales or 0–100 sliders. Alphas usually fall between about .70 and .85, and dyads are reported as inter-item r. These composites sit alongside single-item 0–100 measures: the feeling thermometer, the Ascent of Man rating and the Pulse "humanity" score. For specific endgame solutions, the PSR/Tel Aviv University Palestinian-Israeli Pulse asks about each solution separately with a support/oppose item. Copying its wording gives the only national benchmark that has been tracked over time on both sides.

### Cited Findings

**Feeling thermometers / warmth**
- The ANES thermometer is a 0–100 "cold to warm" rating. Respondents are told that 50–100 means favourable/warm, 0–50 means "you don't feel favorably… and you don't care too much for that group", and 50 means neither warm nor cold. The ANES time-series battery dates to 1970 — [Wikipedia summary of ANES thermometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeling_thermometer); [Pasek & Krosnick 2010 ANES guide](https://calgara.github.io/Pol157_Spring2019/Pasek%20&%20Krosnick%202010.pdf). (Verbatim wording differs a little between ANES years; check the codebook at electionstudies.org before fielding.)
- Bruneau & Saxe (2012) used a 9-item outgroup-attitude battery with Israelis and Palestinians. It has 2 attitude items, 2 trust items (from Nadler & Liviatan 2006), 2 perceived-outgroup-bias items, 2 empathy items and 1 warmth item. Example items: "If I had a chance to introduce Palestinian/Israeli visitors to my friends and neighbors, I would be pleased to do so" and "Palestinians/Israelis are more violent by nature than Israelis/Palestinians" (reverse-scored) — [Bruneau & Saxe 2012, JESP (PDF)](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2012JESP.pdf)

**Affective polarization (relevant if the team also measures Israeli intra-Jewish left/right polarization)**
- Druckman & Levendusky (2019, *POQ*) compared thermometers, trait ratings, trust and social-distance items (comfort with friends, neighbours, or a child marrying an outgroup member). Nearly all are strongly interrelated, but social-distance items tap a distinct dimension. Respondents rating "the other party" mostly picture elites rather than ordinary voters — [Druckman & Levendusky (PDF)](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/jnd260/pub/Druckman%20and%20Levendusky%20Affective%20Polarization%20Measurement.pdf); [IPR working paper page](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/our-work/working-papers/2018/wp-18-12.html)
- A 2026 *Political Psychology* review by Druckman says that "significant uncertainty remains about how best to measure affective polarization" — [Druckman 2026, Political Psychology (PDF)](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/web.sas.upenn.edu/dist/9/244/files/2026/07/Political-Psychology-2026-Druckman-Measuring-affective-polarization-Matching-concepts-to-measures.pdf)
- Target specificity matters in this conflict. Bruneau & Kteily had Israelis rate separately "Palestinians, Arabs, Muslims, Residents of Gaza, Members of Hamas, Arab-Israelis", and Palestinians rate "Israelis, Israeli settlers, Israeli peace activists" — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)

**Blatant dehumanization: the Ascent of Man (Kteily, Bruneau, Waytz & Cotterill 2015, *JPSP* 109(5), 901–931)**
- Structure: a single item per target group. Participants see the "Ascent of Man" evolution image and move a slider to rate how "evolved" the average group member is. The paper is described as introducing and validating "a blatant measure of dehumanization" across a series of studies — [Penn repository record](https://repository.upenn.edu/asc_papers/569); [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2015/the-ascent-of-man-a-theoretical-and-empirical-case)
- Wording used in Israel/Palestine: "how evolved and civilized" the average group member is, on a 0–100 slider with only the endpoints labelled ("left side"/"right side" of the image). Scoring is ingroup minus outgroup (relative dehumanization) — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- A later JPSP supplement reworded the item to be "even more face valid": "How human are [target]?", anchored "Not at all human" to "Completely human" — [APA supplemental file pspi0000492](https://supp.apa.org/psycarticles/supplemental/pspi0000492/pspi0000492.docx)
- Levels during the 2014 Gaza war: Israelis (N = 521, Midgam panel, early August 2014) rated Palestinians 39.81 points below Israelis. Palestinians (N = 354, West Bank via Souktel, late August 2014) rated Israelis 37.03 points below Palestinians. Absolute outgroup ratings were M = 41.21 (SD = 32.46) and M = 45.03 (SD = 39.25). Israelis' dehumanization was slightly higher, F(1,827) = 5.89, η² = .007 — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Dehumanization was "uniquely associated" with all seven hostility outcomes among Israelis after controlling for SDO, conservatism, power and demographics. Among Palestinians it was associated with all outcomes except willingness to sacrifice Israeli lives — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Later work by the same group describes it as a single-item "gestalt" measure that does not separate different aspects of dehumanization — [Bruneau et al. 2018 (PDF)](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Bruneau_etal_2018.pdf) (attribution comes from a search summary; I did not check it in the PDF). Secondary summaries report that Ascent ratings of Arabs fell about 6 points (from ~10 to ~16 below Americans) after the Boston Marathon bombing, which suggests the measure is sensitive to events — [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/psychologists-reveal-our-blatant-dehumanisation-minority-groups) (secondary source; figures not checked against the original paper)
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** The July 2024 Palestinian-Israeli Pulse used a 0–100 "level of humanity" item for one's own side and the other side. Palestinians gave themselves 88 and Israeli Jews 6, and 71% chose 0. Israeli Jews gave themselves 87 and Palestinians 14, and 42% chose 0 (56% of settlers and 70% of ultra-Orthodox respondents). Israeli Arabs rated Palestinians 64 and Israeli Jews 54 — [Pulse Summary Report, 12 Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)

**Empathy and perspective-taking**
- "Parochial empathy" (Bruneau, Cikara & Saxe 2017, *SPPS* 8(8), 934–942) is ingroup empathy minus outgroup empathy. In all samples, ingroup and outgroup empathy had "independent, significant, and opposite" effects on intergroup outcomes after controlling for trait empathic concern — [Harvard DASH record](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-379a-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)
- In the 2014 Palestinian sample, parochial empathy was scored as compassion for Palestinian suffering minus compassion for Israeli suffering on 0–100 sliders — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Kampf & Nicolaidou (2024) measured "multiperspectivity" on six issues (water, refugees, borders, settlements, Jerusalem, security). Each is rated 1 = "Palestinians are absolutely right", 3 = "Both sides are equally right", 5 = "Israelis are absolutely right" — [Kampf & Nicolaidou 2024, ICS (PDF)](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/bitstream/20.500.14279/32689/2/Using%20social%20impact%20games.pdf)

**Intergroup trust**
- Two Nadler & Liviatan-derived items were used with Israelis and Palestinians: "I believe that the Palestinians/Israelis are adhering, and will adhere, to written agreements towards a lasting solution in the Middle East" and "I do not believe in the peaceful intentions of the Palestinians/…" — [Bruneau & Saxe 2012 (PDF)](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2012JESP.pdf)
- In the 2014 Palestinian sample the reverse-scored trust item did not correlate with the two positive items and was dropped. The composite used the two positive 0–100 items — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** Pulse item: agreement with "Palestinians can be trusted" (and the mirror item). 86% of Israeli Jews disagreed, and 94% of Palestinians said Israeli Jews are untrustworthy (86% in 2022). 64% of Israeli Arabs said Israeli Jews can be trusted — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- Goldenberg et al. (2018) used an incentivised trust game in which money was sent to a Palestinian confederate — [Goldenberg et al. 2018, PNAS (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904)

**Threat perception and fear**
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** Pulse fear item for Israeli Jews: "fear being harmed by a Palestinian terror attack, rocket fire or war". 52% answered "greatly or very greatly" in 2024, compared with 61% in 2022 and 42% in 2020. The Palestinian version asks about worry over being hurt by Israelis, land confiscation, home demolition, expulsion and similar (78% worried in 2024, 60% in 2022) — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** Pulse attribution-of-aims item: 66% of Israeli Jews chose "to commit genocide against us" as Palestinians' aim, and 61% of Palestinians chose the same for Israel's aim. Another 27% on each side chose "conquer land and expel" — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- Integrated threat theory (Stephan et al.) separates realistic from symbolic threat. A 2025 *Frontiers* study of 324 Israeli Jews and 325 Israeli Arabs found that perceived realistic threat mediated the link between ethnicity and justification of intergroup violence among Jews only — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1508324/full)

**Hope (and despair)**
- A 4-item hope scale adapted from Cohen-Chen et al. had α = .84 among Israelis (1–7). Sample item: "I have hope regarding the peaceful resolution of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict". In the Palestinian sample a 3-item 0–100 version lost its reverse-coded item, leaving two items with r = .50 — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Cohen-Chen, Halperin, Crisp & Gross (2014, *SPPS*): beliefs that conflicts are malleable were associated with hope and with support for concessions among Israeli Jews (correlational Study 1). An experimental malleability manipulation followed in Study 2 — [HUJI CRIS record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/hope-in-the-middle-east-malleability-beliefs-hope-and-the-willing/); [Aston repository PDF](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/28969/1/HopeintheMiddleEast_2014.pdf)
- Goldenberg et al. (2018) used a 3-item hope scale covering hope, despair and optimism about the future relationship with Palestinians — [Goldenberg et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904)

**Emotions and other short composites used in the 2014 Gaza-war samples** — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Emotional hostility (hatred, hostility, anger): α = .70 for Israelis (1–7) and α = .77 for Palestinians (0–100).
- Group-based guilt/shame: r = .77 for Israelis and r = .45 for Palestinians.
- Covariates: SDO (7 items, α = .73 for Israelis; 8 items, α = .62 for Palestinians); conservatism; perceived group power.

**Support for compromise and specific peace plans**
- Hameiri et al. (2014) measured willingness to compromise with one item at wave 7 (evacuating some West Bank settlements, 1–6). At the 1-year follow-up they used a 4-item scale (the settlement item plus three on Jerusalem and recognition), α = .84 — [Hameiri et al. 2014, PNAS (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- Bruneau & Kteily (Israelis) used a 4-item concessions scale (joint sovereignty over Jerusalem's holy sites; giving up control of Gaza, the West Bank and East Jerusalem Arab neighbourhoods), α = .76. Willingness to negotiate (with an Abbas-led side; with Hamas) was a 2-item measure, r = .47 — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Goldenberg et al. used a 6-item concessions scale (e.g., "Israel should release Palestinians' tax funds in order to promote the negotiations") and a 5-item two-state-support scale — [Goldenberg et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904)
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** The Pulse (fieldwork July 2024) asked about each framework separately. Support for the two-state "concept" was 21% among Israeli Jews (34% in Dec 2022, 42% in Sept 2020), 40% among Palestinians and 72% among Israeli Arabs. One state with equal rights: 14% of Israeli Jews, 25% of Palestinians, 49% of Israeli Arabs. "Annexation of the West Bank without equal rights for Palestinians": 42% of Israeli Jews, the highest the Pulse has recorded. One state without equal rights for Jews: 33% of Palestinians, 17% of Israeli Arabs. Two-state confederation: 35% of Palestinians (22% in 2022), 12% of Israeli Jews, 52% of Israeli Arabs — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- The Pulse also has a detailed "peace package" item. Its components are a demilitarised Palestinian state; withdrawal to the Green Line with equal land swaps; family unification in Israel for 100,000 refugees; West and East Jerusalem as the two capitals, with the Jewish Quarter and Western Wall under Israel and the Muslim/Christian quarters and Haram al-Sharif/Temple Mount under Palestine; both states democratic; a wider Arab peace; US/Arab guarantees; and end of conflict and claims. Support in 2024: 34% of Palestinians, 25% of Israeli Jews, 69% of Israeli Arabs. A split-sample incentives experiment raised support to as much as 55% (Palestinians) and 60% (Israeli Jews) — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- Pulse "elimination method": anyone who supports two states is counted only there. The remaining "other/undecided" group (rejects all options or answers don't know) was the largest Palestinian constituency (44%) and the second largest among Israeli Jews (34%) — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- Question format changes the headline numbers. PSR's January 2024 single-choice format (two states on 1967 borders / one state / confederation / other) produced a slim two-state plurality. PSR notes that this is much higher than support measured with a support/oppose item — [PSR, 15 Jan 2024](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/966); [PSR Arab Barometer report (PDF)](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/AB8%20Palestine%20Report%201%20English%20w%20table%20of%20findings.pdf) (seen through a search summary; I did not open these pages)
- Pulse samples (2024): 1,270 Palestinians face-to-face in 127 locations, including Gaza shelters and tent camps, 17–20 July 2024, MoE ±3.5%. 900 Israelis: 500 Jews via the Midgam online panel plus a settler oversample of 200, and 200 Arabs via iPanel, 18–29 July 2024, MoE ±3.34% — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)

**Support for violence and aggressive policies**
- Collective aggression (Israelis): 8 items, 1–7, α = .78. One item endorses bombing Palestinian schools and hospitals in response to Hamas rocket fire — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Acceptance of civilian casualties: a scenario about a wounded Israeli soldier in a dense Gaza neighbourhood asks for the maximum acceptable number of Palestinian civilian deaths (0–1,000). Mean 575, median 990, mode 1,000 (49.9% of the sample), which is a ceiling problem — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- Palestinian sample: a trolley-style item on willingness to sacrifice Israeli lives (save 4 Israeli children vs. 1 Palestinian man), reverse-scored 0–100 — [Bruneau & Kteily 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0181422)
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** Pulse justification items: 84% of Israeli Jews say 7 October "justifies current Israeli actions in the Gaza Strip", and 81% of Palestinians say the siege and blockade justify what Hamas did on 7 October — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)

**Conflict-supporting beliefs: the Ethos of Conflict (EOC; Bar-Tal, Sharvit, Halperin & Zafran 2012, *Peace and Conflict* 18(1), 40–61)**
- The 8 EOC themes load on a single factor. EOC partly mediates the link between general conservatism and judgments of specific solutions proposed to end the conflict — [HUJI CRIS record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/ethos-of-conflict-the-concept-and-its-measurement/)
- The eight themes are justness of own goals; security; delegitimization of the adversary; positive collective self-image; own victimization; patriotism; unity; and peace as the ultimate goal — [Bar-Tal, Beyond Intractability essay](https://www.beyondintractability.org/essay/ethos-of-conflict)
- A short version ("Adherence to the Ethos of Conflict Scale—Short Version", PsycTESTS 2014) is listed — [Academia.edu listing](https://independent.academia.edu/DanielBarTal) (weak source; check PsycTESTS)
- "Unfreezing" of EOC beliefs as an outcome: three 0–100 items on whether stimuli made participants doubt EOC sayings (e.g., "no partner on the Palestinian side"), α = .80. "Perceived Palestinian responsibility" was three 1–6 items, α = .84 — [Hameiri et al. 2014 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** Pulse items that map onto EOC themes: 84% of Israeli Jews and 83% of Palestinians agree their own side's victimisation is the worst compared with other persecuted peoples. 87% of Israeli Jews and 89% of Palestinians see their own group as rightful owners of the land from the river to the sea (1–6 ownership scale) — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)

**Knowledge**
- Cuhadar & Kampf (2014, *International Studies Perspectives* 15(4), 509–524) gave pre/post knowledge questionnaires. PeaceMaker raised knowledge of the conflict among Israeli-Jewish, Israeli-Palestinian, American and Turkish students — [HUJI CRIS record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/learning-about-conflict-and-negotiations-through-computer-simulat/); [Bilkent repository](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/e2f4c932-5fbf-4062-be91-10ff8f97ff91)

### Inferences
- A defensible core battery, built from the sources above, would be:
  1. 0–100 thermometers for several specific targets (e.g., "Palestinians in Gaza", "Palestinians in the West Bank", "Hamas", "Israeli Jews", "settlers").
  2. Ascent of Man or the 0–100 "how human" item for the same targets.
  3. 2-item trust (Nadler & Liviatan wording).
  4. Outgroup and ingroup empathy, scored as parochial empathy.
  5. 3–4-item hope.
  6. A fear/threat item (Pulse wording).
  7. Separate support/oppose items for two states, confederation, one equal state, annexation without rights (and the Palestinian mirror item), plus the detailed package. These map directly onto the endgame options in "The Solution".
  8. A short EOC or unfreezing measure.
  9. Collective aggression or a casualty-tolerance item.
  10. A knowledge quiz.
- **[POST-OCT-2023]** floor effects are severe. The Pulse humanity means are 6 and 14 out of 100, with 42–71% at zero, and the casualty item has a modal ceiling of 1,000. Single-item 0–100 measures will therefore have compressed variance in post-2023 Israeli and Palestinian samples. Multi-item Likert composites and target-specific items (civilians vs. Hamas) are likely to be more sensitive to change.
- Using Pulse wording for the solution items lets the team benchmark its sample against national data.

### Gaps
- I could not get the verbatim EOC items or their alpha from the primary 2012 article.
- I did not find test-retest reliability for the Ascent measure. This matters for reliable-change calculations (see Q4).
- I found no validated, published Israeli–Palestinian-specific realistic/symbolic threat scale with alphas.
- I found no psychometric validation of thermometers or Ascent in Hebrew and Arabic translations, beyond their use in these samples.
- Item-level wording for Halperin-lab "support for aggressive policies" scales was not retrieved.

## Q2. Which studies shifted these attitudes with media or interventions, by how much, and for how long?

### Takeaway
Interventions targeting the Israeli–Palestinian conflict have produced small-to-moderate effects, roughly d ≈ 0.2–0.45 or η² ≈ .03–.07, on compromise, hope and attitudes. Some of these effects lasted 6–12 months: paradoxical thinking and group malleability. Brief dialogue effects disappeared within a week (perspective-giving). Large social-media feed experiments and YouTube-like recommendation experiments found attitude effects precisely estimated near zero, even when exposure and engagement moved substantially. Short-form (TikTok-style) exposure studies mostly change emotions rather than attitudes, with some exceptions. I found no peer-reviewed randomized experiment of TikTok or Reels exposure on Israel–Gaza attitudes since October 2023.

### Cited Findings

**Paradoxical thinking (Hameiri, Porat, Bar-Tal, Bieler & Halperin)**
- PNAS 2014 design: 249 Jewish Israelis recruited online, 161 of whom completed all 8 waves. Waves ran every 3–4 days for about a month before the 22 Jan 2013 election. In waves 2–7 participants watched about 5 minutes of video: 3 paradoxical clips plus 3 generic ads, versus 6 ads in the control. Fifteen distinct clips were used in total — [Hameiri et al. 2014 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- Effects at wave 7:
  - Unfreezing: 44.72 (SD 26.63) vs 35.56 (SD 25.76), F(1,152) = 4.71, η² = .03.
  - Perceived Palestinian responsibility: F = 11.96, η² = .07. Concentrated among centrists (η² = .12); marginal for rightists; no effect for leftists.
  - Willingness to compromise: 4.04 (SD 1.71) vs 3.39 (SD 1.52), F(1,152) = 6.50, η² = .04.
  - Self-reported dovish vote: OR = 2.92, p = .05.
  — [Hameiri et al. 2014 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- 1-year follow-up: 102 of 249 retained (41%). Compromise was 3.61 (SD 1.21) vs 3.17 (SD 1.18), F(1,91) = 4.13, η² = .04. The authors note high attrition and that the study was underpowered — [Hameiri et al. 2014 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- Computed here from the reported means and SDs: d ≈ 0.35 (unfreezing), ≈ 0.40 (compromise at wave 7), ≈ 0.37 (compromise at 1 year).
- PNAS 2016 ("Moderating attitudes in times of violence"): a six-week field campaign in autumn 2015, during a wave of stabbing attacks. It ran online, on billboards and on flyers in a largely right-wing, religious city in central Israel. It had "a significant effect on the beliefs and attitudes of right-wing, hawkish participants", including lower support for aggressive policies — [Mindblog summary](https://mindblog.dericbownds.net/2016/11/paradoxical-thinking-intervention-can.html); [CAS catalog record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsjsr,edsjsr.26472209) (effect sizes not retrieved)
- PSPB 2018: paradoxical messages unfroze conflict-supporting beliefs more among rightists, while inconsistency-based messages worked better with centrists. The proposed mechanisms were identity threat, surprise and lower disagreement — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/paradoxical-thinking-as-a-conflict-resolution-intervention-compar/)
- Review (SIPR 2019): paradoxical thinking moderated attitudes most among those holding the most adamant views — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/paradoxical-thinking-interventions-a-paradigm-for-societal-change/)
- JSPP 2020 "sweet spot" study: the effect is moderated by the recipient's latitude of rejection (refugee attitudes in Israel) — [JSPP](https://jspp.psychopen.eu/index.php/jspp/article/view/1158)

**Perspective-giving (Bruneau & Saxe 2012, JESP 48(4), 855–866; pre-2014 but foundational)**
- Israeli–Palestinian dyads exchanged views in a brief structured video and text exchange of about 15 minutes. Palestinians changed more after perspective-giving than after perspective-taking (t(40) = 2.0) or control (t(44) = 2.6). The group × condition interaction was F(2,119) = 3.2, p < .05. Israelis showed warmth gains after perspective-taking — [Bruneau & Saxe 2012 (PDF)](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2012JESP.pdf)
- Durability: "changes in attitudes towards the out-group were transient: 1 week after the intervention, no significant effect remained" — [Bruneau & Saxe 2012 (PDF)](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2012JESP.pdf); [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2012/conflict-resolution-0315)

**Group malleability (Goldenberg, Cohen-Chen, Goyer, Dweck, Gross & Halperin 2018, PNAS 115(4))**
- 508 Jewish Israelis attended a 5-hour in-person "leadership" workshop with three arms (malleability, perspective-taking, coping control). Follow-ups were at 2 weeks, 2 months and 6 months. Retention was 97%, 80% and 59% — [Goldenberg et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904)
- Malleability vs coping, Cohen's d (sign follows the paper's coding):
  - Negative attitudes toward Palestinians: 0.18 (post), 0.20 (2 weeks), 0.24 (2 months), 0.34 (6 months).
  - Hope: −0.34, −0.28, −0.23, −0.34.
  - At 6 months only: concessions d = −0.45 and trust game d = −0.64. The dictator game was significant (p = .03).
  - Two-state support: no difference at any point.
  — [Goldenberg et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904) (one CI in the paper's Table 1 does not contain its estimate; check the original)

**PeaceMaker studies**
- Alhabash & Wise (2012, *IJOC*; pre-2014), with about 69 US students. Playing as the Palestinian president was followed by more positive explicit attitudes toward Palestinians and more negative ones toward Israelis. Playing as the Israeli prime minister produced no meaningful change. IAT scores did not change — [IJOC article page](https://ojs3.ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/1056)
- Cuhadar & Kampf (2014): knowledge rose in all four groups. Perspective-taking improved only among Turkish and American students, and only on a contemporary issue — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/learning-about-conflict-and-negotiations-through-computer-simulat/)
- Kampf & Nicolaidou (2024) summarise the literature: PeaceMaker improved multiperspectivity only among third parties (Americans and Turks), not among direct parties — [Kampf & Nicolaidou 2024 (PDF)](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/bitstream/20.500.14279/32689/2/Using%20social%20impact%20games.pdf)
- Kampf & Nicolaidou (2024) study itself: 2022–2023, with 61 Israeli and Palestinian students (31/30) per order condition plus 81 Israelis in single-game conditions. On the 1–5 multiperspectivity scale (3 = both equally right):
  - Israelis who played PeaceMaker first: 3.92 → 3.75 (t30 = 4.25).
  - Palestinians who played PeaceMaker first: 1.72 → 1.84 (t29 = −3.80).
  - Israelis who played Fact Finders (Cyprus) first: 3.81 → 3.38.
  - Palestinians who played Fact Finders first: 1.74 → 2.23.
  So a game about another conflict produced larger "spillover" shifts. No delayed follow-up was reported — [Kampf & Nicolaidou 2024 (PDF)](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/bitstream/20.500.14279/32689/2/Using%20social%20impact%20games.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2024.2331770)
- Gonzalez, Saner & Eisenberg (2013, *SSCR* 31(2), 236–243): repeated play combined with the game's model of the conflict "may make it possible to reduce personal bias and learn to stand in another's shoes" — [CMU KiltHub](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Learning_to_Stand_in_the_Other_s_Shoes_A_Computer_Video_Game_Experience_of_the_Israeli_-Palestinian_Conflict/6571241)

**[POST-OCT-2023] Israel–Gaza-war intervention studies**
- Ushomirsky, Aldar, Roth & Halperin (2026, *EJSP* 56(5), 885–902): a tournament of seven theory-based social-media interventions with 1,237 Jewish Israelis during the January 2025 ceasefire. A loss-framing message ("Israel's potential losses if the war continued") increased support for ending the war across the political spectrum. Recategorising by war attitudes worked mainly among leftists. Effect sizes and durability were not available on the record page — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/changing-minds-in-times-of-war-an-intervention-tournament-to-incr/)
- "Helping hurts" (*Peace and Conflict*, 2025): more than 350 Jewish Israelis saw a real campaign to improve Palestinians' daily lives. Right-wing viewers then became less supportive of concessions, felt less moral responsibility and were less hopeful, consistent with moral licensing — [BFHU summary](https://www.bfhu.org/2025/11/19/when-helping-hurts-why-acts-of-goodwill-can-stall-peace/); [AlphaGalileo](https://www.alphagalileo.org/en-gb/Item-Display/ItemId/265460)
- Ushomirsky, Hasson et al.: two intervention tournaments (N = 1,564) on the perceived heterogeneity of Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza after exposure to extreme violence. Results not retrieved — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/increasing-perceived-outgroup-heterogeneity-following-exposure-to/)
- Pulse 2024 split-sample experiment: one-sided incentives raised support for the peace package from 25% to as much as 60% among Israeli Jews and from 34% to as much as 55% among Palestinians. These are immediate survey-experiment effects — [Pulse Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)

**[POST-OCT-2023] TikTok/Reels and the Israel–Gaza war**
- I found no peer-reviewed randomized exposure experiment. A survey of 193 college-aged US TikTok users (Michigan, fall 2024) found that those who see TikTok as a credible news source reported stronger emotional reactions to war content, "without corresponding shifts in their declared political allegiance" — [War on the Rocks, Jan 2026](https://warontherocks.com/2026/01/tiktok-algorithmic-emotion-and-the-new-propaganda-battlefield/)
- Content supply: about 17 pro-Palestinian videos for every pro-Israel video on TikTok, according to a non-peer-reviewed research-centre report — [Cybersecurity for Democracy](https://cybersecurityfordemocracy.org/updating-our-israelgaza-analysis-2-years-on)
- Peer-reviewed content analysis of hate speech in TikTok conversations during the October 2023 escalation — [González-Esteban et al. 2024, Social Sciences (PDF)](https://www.uma.es/media/files/2024-Social_Sciences.pdf)

**Short-form video experiments on political attitudes (other topics)**
- Landgrave, Anstett & Alsharawy (*Political Studies Review*, DOI 10.1177/14789299251323741): students were randomized to a week of daily Democratic-leaning, Republican-leaning, or non-political (animal) TikToks. There was "no evidence that political attitudes changed", but political videos increased sadness, anxiety and anger. Attention was not measured — [CU Boulder, March 2026](https://www.colorado.edu/asmagazine/2026/03/23/tiktok-doesnt-change-minds-it-changes-moods)
- Incerti, Elkobi & Mattingly (arXiv, Jan 2026): more than 8,500 Americans used "a realistic clone of the TikTok app". Pro-China influencer content was "strikingly effective at increasing favorability toward China", while state-media content caused backlash. This is a preprint — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14118)
- Munger & Li ran a TikTok "edits" experiment with 2,303 attentive participants; edits of politicians shifted perceptions. A *PRQ* study found that policy-focused Harris ads moved favourability more than trendy short clips — [PsyPost (edits)](https://www.psypost.org/tiktok-edits-can-reshape-how-voters-see-politicians-study-suggests/); [PsyPost (Harris)](https://www.psypost.org/the-limits-of-viral-politics-how-tiktok-shaped-voter-views-of-kamala-harris/) (secondary coverage)

**Feed and recommendation experiments**
- Guess et al. (2023, *Science*), reverse-chronological feeds on Facebook and Instagram over 3 months in 2020: no significant change in issue or affective polarization, knowledge, or other key attitudes. Users saw more political and untrustworthy content, and time on the platform fell to about half — [CSMaP](https://csmapnyu.org/research/academic-research/how-do-social-media-feed-algorithms-affect-attitudes-and-behavior-in-an-election-campaign); [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-07-facebook-algorithm-doesnt-people-beliefs.pdf)
- Nyhan et al. (2023, *Nature*): 23,377 users had exposure to like-minded sources cut by about one-third from 24 Sept to 23 Dec 2020, with 5 survey waves. Effects on eight preregistered attitudes, including affective polarization, could "confidently rule out effects of ±0.12 s.d. or more". Several point estimates were ±0.03 SD or less. Exposure to uncivil content fell by 0.15 SD — [Nyhan et al. 2023 (PDF)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w.pdf)
- Liu et al. (2025, *PNAS* 122(8)): four experiments (N ≈ 9,000) on a YouTube-like platform. Slanted vs balanced recommendations had no detectable short-term effect on policy attitudes, media trust or affective polarization. Studies 1–3 had MDEs of 0.02–0.04 on a 0–1 attitude scale, and the authors argue that "the burden of proof… has shifted" — [Liu et al. 2025 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/9/583/files/2026/01/shorttermexposure_2025.pdf); [HKS page](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/short-term-exposure-filter-bubble-recommendation-systems-has-limited-polarization)
- In the same paper, the initial seed video did move attitudes: point estimates were 2–3× the algorithmic MDE, e.g., 0.04 on the 0–1 index in Study 1. The median prior persuasion experiment exposed people for 101 seconds; Tappin et al.'s microtargeted videos averaged 52 seconds and still moved attitudes — [Liu et al. 2025 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/9/583/files/2026/01/shorttermexposure_2025.pdf)

**Megastudy benchmark (US partisan animosity)**
- Voelkel et al. (2024, *Science*): 32,059 participants and 25 treatments of under 8 minutes each. 23 treatments reduced partisan animosity; only 6 reduced support for undemocratic practices. Some effects persisted at a two-week follow-up — [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2024/megastudy-testing-25-treatments-to-reduce-anti-democratic-attitudes/?p=1); [Stanford Sociology](https://sociology.stanford.edu/scalable-interventions-reduce-polarization-support-partisan-violence-and-anti-democratic-attitudes)
- Bruneau, Kteily & Falk (2018, *PSPB*): a "collective blame hypocrisy" exercise reduced collective blame of Muslims and anti-Muslim attitudes and behaviour. In press coverage the effects held at one month, though they were weaker at one year — [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/hhypocrisy-intervention-hostility-muslims); [paper PDF](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2021-03/Interventions%20highlighting%20hypocrisy%20reduce%20collective%20blame%20of%20Muslims%20for%20individual%20acts%20of%20violence%20and%20assuage%20anti-Muslim%20hostility.pdf)

**General decay benchmarks**
- Across 18 panel survey experiments, effects remained after 10 days at about half their original size. Informational treatments lasted longer than framing treatments — [Coppock 2017 manuscript (PDF)](https://alexandercoppock.com/coppock_2017b.pdf)
- Op-ed effects decayed by about half over 10 days and stayed at that level for up to a month — [Coppock, Ekins & Kirby 2018, QJPS](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/QJPS-16112); [Cato summary](https://www.cato.org/publications/outside-articles/long-lasting-effects-newspaper-op-eds-public-opinion)
- "Dynamic persuasion" (Baum, Berinsky et al., 2026, *PSRM*): a short article or video clip can persuade for up to a week. An adequately powered experiment isolating cumulative effects "would be virtually infeasible" — [Harvard (de Benedictis-Kessner) resource](https://jdbk.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/multiwave); [Cambridge record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S2049847026100958) (seen through a search summary)

### Inferences
- Realistic planning targets for a short-video session are about d = 0.1–0.3 immediately after exposure, with perhaps about half of that remaining after 1–2 weeks. The basis is the Coppock decay benchmarks, the Liu seed effects and Voelkel's single-session treatments. Durable effects of d ≈ 0.3–0.4 have appeared only with repeated exposure (Hameiri: 6 sessions) or intensive interventions (Goldenberg: 5 hours).
- Effects are heterogeneous by ideology (paradoxical thinking works on rightists and centrists) and by group position (perspective-giving works for Palestinians, perspective-taking for Israelis). Pre-specified moderators should therefore include political orientation and group membership. "Helping hurts" shows that well-intended positive content can backfire among right-wing Israelis.
- The PeaceMaker evidence suggests knowledge gains are reliable but attitude gains are weak among direct parties. "The Solution" should treat knowledge and multiperspectivity as primary game outcomes, and treat outgroup attitudes as secondary.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve effect sizes for Hameiri 2016 (PNAS), Ushomirsky et al. 2026, the heterogeneity tournaments, or Guess et al. 2023 (in SD units).
- I did not open the Alhabash & Wise full text because the PDF download returned an error page, so its exact statistics are missing.
- No peer-reviewed TikTok or Reels randomized experiment on Israel–Gaza attitudes was found. A widely cited Wall Street Journal bot audit appeared only in a search summary without a primary link.
- No study of "The Solution"–style multi-ending games was found.

## Q3. Behavioural and engagement measures (dwell, completion, skips, rewatches, likes/shares, donations or petitions, game choices) and how they combine with self-report

### Takeaway
State-of-the-art feed experiments log choices, skips, watch time and like/dislike/save events on custom platforms. They treat these as separate preregistered outcomes, distinct from survey attitude outcomes. Engagement often moves a lot (e.g., ±5–7 minutes of watch time, a 6 pp shift in like-minded choices) while attitudes do not. Incentivised economic games (dictator and trust games with a Palestinian recipient) and real-world proxies (self-reported vote) are the main behavioural anchors in Israeli samples.

### Cited Findings
- Liu et al. (2025) platform design. Participants start with a seed video, then choose among four recommendations, up to five videos in total. They must watch at least 30 seconds before skipping ahead. The platform records choices, skips versus full viewing, likes, dislikes and "save to watchlist", summarised as "Likes & saves minus dislikes". Surveys ran about one week before and immediately after, with a third wave about one week later in Studies 1–2 — [Liu et al. 2025 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/9/583/files/2026/01/shorttermexposure_2025.pdf)
- Liu et al. results. Slanted recommendations raised the share of like-minded videos chosen by about 6 pp. In some arms, time on the platform rose by 7.3 minutes or fell by 4.9 minutes, against an average of 23 minutes. Attitudes were essentially unchanged. Because wave 2 showed no attitude effects, wave 3 was not analysed — [Liu et al. 2025 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/9/583/files/2026/01/shorttermexposure_2025.pdf)
- Nyhan et al. (2023) reported behavioural engagement with on-platform content in SD units alongside the survey outcomes. Exposure and engagement shifted by about 0.04–0.15 SD while attitudes stayed at zero — [Nyhan et al. 2023 (PDF)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w.pdf)
- Guess et al. (2023): time spent on the platform roughly halved under chronological feeds, with no attitude change — [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-07-facebook-algorithm-doesnt-people-beliefs.pdf)
- Incerti et al. (2026) used a TikTok clone and reported that pro-China influencers drew "more engagement than state media" as well as larger attitude effects — [arXiv 2601.14118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14118)
- Landgrave et al. did not measure how viewers attended to the videos and plan to use eye-tracking — [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/asmagazine/2026/03/23/tiktok-doesnt-change-minds-it-changes-moods)
- Open research tools for simulated feeds:
  - The Truman Platform (Cornell) lets researchers curate every simulated actor, post, like, comment and notification, and randomizes conditions — [Cornell Social Media Lab](https://socialmedialab.cornell.edu/projects/the-truman-platform); [Truman docs](https://truman.gitbook.io)
  - "Fakebook" is a Facebook-like research environment (Docker) — [GitHub jpfefferlab/fakebook](https://github.com/jpfefferlab/fakebook)
  - A 2023 *Current Opinion in Psychology* review compares the Mock Social Media Website Tool, the (Mis)information Game and Truman — [record](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/cmedm__38048652) (seen through a search summary)
- Economic games: Goldenberg et al. used a dictator game (allocation to Palestinians) at the follow-ups and a trust game (money sent to a Palestinian confederate) at 6 months. The trust game showed the largest effect (d = −0.64 vs control) — [Goldenberg et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904)
- Real-world behaviour: Hameiri et al. asked about the vote two days after the 2013 election (dovish vs other parties, OR = 2.92). This is self-reported — [Hameiri et al. 2014 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- Bruneau, Kteily & Falk's interactive exercise reduced "anti-Muslim attitudes and behavior" (behavioural measure reported) — [Kellogg record](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2018/interventions-highlighting-hypocrisy-reduce-collective-blame-of-muslims-for)
- Game-choice logging precedent: PeaceMaker scores Israeli and Palestinian approval separately from the player's security, political and construction actions. Both must reach 100 to win, and the player loses if either drops below 50. These logs could serve as behavioural outcomes — [Kampf & Nicolaidou 2024 (PDF)](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/bitstream/20.500.14279/32689/2/Using%20social%20impact%20games.pdf)
- Gonzalez et al. studied learning across repeated PeaceMaker rounds, i.e., performance over play — [CMU KiltHub](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Learning_to_Stand_in_the_Other_s_Shoes_A_Computer_Video_Game_Experience_of_the_Israeli_-Palestinian_Conflict/6571241)

### Inferences
- In the cited studies, engagement metrics are outcomes or manipulation checks (did people watch?), not proxies for attitude change, because they can move strongly while attitudes do not. Using dwell time or likes to subset participants after treatment risks post-treatment bias (see Q4). Pre-registering them as secondary outcomes, or as moderators measured in a pre-treatment practice feed, avoids this.
- For "The Solution", a natural behavioural KPI is which endgame the player pursues or chooses. It can be compared with the self-reported solution-support items (Q1) to test whether game choices and stated preferences agree.
- Low-cost behavioural add-ons suited to online samples are a donation split between in-group and outgroup humanitarian charities, and the option to sign or skip a petition. Both are consistent with the economic-game logic above. I found no Israel-specific validation of them in this search.

### Gaps
- I found no published study of completion rate or rewatches specifically for short political videos about this conflict.
- I found no validated donation or petition paradigm in Israeli or Palestinian samples beyond the economic games.
- Details of how Incerti et al. measured dwell time and swipes in their TikTok clone were not on the abstract page.

## Q4. Design and analysis: pre/post vs post-only, follow-ups, attention and manipulation checks, MDE and sample size, reliable change, preregistration, and noise in individual-level KPIs

### Takeaway
Measuring the outcome before treatment greatly improves precision and generally does not bias treatment effects (Clifford et al. 2021). A 2025 replication found only a small attenuation and still recommends the design. Attention should be screened before treatment, not by dropping people who fail post-treatment checks. Follow-ups lose 20–60% of participants within months. Preregistration on OSF or AsPredicted, with MDEs stated, is standard in this literature. Individual-level change scores on single 0–100 items are dominated by measurement error, so treatment effects should be inferred at the condition level. Reliable change indices give a conservative per-person flag.

### Cited Findings

**Pretest sensitisation and repeated measures**
- Clifford, Sheagley & Piston (2021, *APSR* 115(3), 1048–1065): pre-treatment outcome measures "do not bias treatment effect estimates and greatly improve precision" across six experiments — [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/increasing-precision-without-altering-treatment-effects-repeated-measures-designs-in-survey-experiments/2E1637FFE36DFD0BA4AD0822634D8337); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:apsrev:v:115:y:2021:i:3:p:1048-1065_22)
- Jordan, Trexler & Ollerenshaw (2025, OSF preprint) re-ran six classic experiments in three samples (N = 13,163). They found "a small attenuation of treatment effects" in repeated-measures designs but still recommend them because the precision gains are large — [Prolific citations page](https://citations.prolific.com/paper/repeated-measure-designs-are-superior-for-most-experimental-survey-research-applications)
- 83 peer-reviewed studies cited Clifford et al. to justify repeated measures within four years, and about 40% used a within-subject variant — [PKU working paper (PDF)](https://www.nsd.pku.edu.cn/docs/2026-08/c51b2385e1064c139288b7866497dfee.pdf) (not independently verified)
- Bruneau & Saxe hid their 9 key items among about 50 randomized filler items at pre, post and 1-week waves to reduce demand and sensitisation — [Bruneau & Saxe 2012 (PDF)](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2012JESP.pdf)
- Liu et al. placed the pre-wave about one week before treatment, so that the pretest was separated in time from the post measure — [Liu et al. 2025 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/9/583/files/2026/01/shorttermexposure_2025.pdf)

**Attention and manipulation checks**
- Instructional manipulation checks ("screeners") identify inattentive respondents, and low attention may affect "as many as half" of respondents — [Berinsky, Margolis & Sances 2014, AJPS (via JEPS article)](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-experimental-political-science/article/how-dropping-subjects-who-failed-manipulation-checks-can-bias-your-results-an-illustrative-case/2DA8E679FE660543C82C82E9AEED4803)
- Dropping participants who fail post-treatment manipulation checks, controlling for post-treatment mediators, or subsetting on post-treatment variables can bias estimates — [Montgomery, Nyhan & Torres 2018, AJPS (PDF)](https://sites.dartmouth.edu/nyhan/files/2021/03/post-treatment-bias.pdf)
- Aronow, Baron & Pinson (2019) recommend measuring attentiveness before treatment. Kane & Barabas (2019) propose factual manipulation checks. Open-ended, text-as-data manipulation checks are a further option — [JEPS illustrative case](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-experimental-political-science/article/how-dropping-subjects-who-failed-manipulation-checks-can-bias-your-results-an-illustrative-case/2DA8E679FE660543C82C82E9AEED4803); [Political Analysis text-as-data checks (PDF)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C0A94C4CC5D0ECC72E8A670050D76ED4/S1047198721000024a.pdf/a-text-as-data-approach-for-using-open-ended-responses-as-manipulation-checks.pdf)
- Munger & Li analysed only participants who passed attention checks (N = 2,303) — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/tiktok-edits-can-reshape-how-voters-see-politicians-study-suggests/)

**Follow-up attrition**
- Hameiri et al.: 65% completed all 8 waves over a month, and 41% remained at 1 year — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)
- Goldenberg et al.: 97% at 2 weeks, 80% at 2 months and 59% at 6 months, with no differential attrition by condition — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904)
- Coppock: about 50% completion at a one-month follow-up cost the power to detect effects — [Coppock 2017 (PDF)](https://alexandercoppock.com/coppock_2017b.pdf)

**Power, MDE and covariates**
- Liu et al. calculated MDEs of 0.02–0.04 on a 0–1 scale with about 2,500 participants per study, at α = .05 and 80% power after multiple-testing correction. They state that the results reveal "practical limits to effect sizes that are feasibly detectable in academic experiments" — [Liu et al. 2025 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/9/583/files/2026/01/shorttermexposure_2025.pdf)
- Nyhan et al. framed precision as the effects they could rule out (±0.12 SD) and used exploratory equivalence bounds of ±0.1 SD — [Nyhan et al. 2023 (PDF)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w.pdf)
- EGAP: 0.80 power is the conventional target, assumptions should be explicit, and pre-treatment prognostic covariates raise power. Non-prognostic covariates add little — [EGAP: 10 Things About Power](https://methods.egap.org/guides/assessing-designs/power_en.html); [EGAP: Covariate Adjustment](https://methods.egap.org/guides/analysis-procedures/covariates_en.html); [EGAP power slides (PDF)](https://egap.github.io/learningdays-resources/Slides/power-slides.pdf)
- Hameiri et al. acknowledged their study was underpowered (161 completers) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4121792/)

**Reliable change (individual-level)**
- Jacobson & Truax (1991) RCI: RCI = (x₂ − x₁)/S_diff, where S_diff = √2·SEM and SEM = SD₁·√(1 − r_xx), with r_xx usually test-retest reliability. |RCI| > 1.96 indicates change beyond measurement error. Christensen & Mendoza (1986) corrected the earlier standard error. Maassen (2004) and Temkin et al. offer variants, and Chelune et al. adjust for practice effects — [Blampied 2022 review (PDF)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/75AC9F09E738AC53924DA77AE4C66D7D/S1754470X22000484a.pdf/reliable-change-and-the-reliable-change-index-still-useful-after-all-these-years.pdf); [Maassen 2004, JINS](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S1355617704106097); [PSYCTC RCI explainer](https://psyctc.org/psyctc/?p=3574) (formula as given in these reviews)

**Preregistration**
- Nosek et al. (2018, *PNAS*) argue that preregistration separates prediction from postdiction. Ledgerwood's commentary distinguishes preregistering predictions from preregistering analysis plans — [PMC: Nosek et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500); [FORRT summary](https://forrt.org/curated_resources/the-preregistration-revolution-needs-to/)
- AsPredicted is a short, domain-general template of about nine questions. Its questions were updated, so the OSF copy differs slightly — [Data Colada 64](https://datacolada.org/64); [COS template guide](https://www.cos.io/blog/choosing-preregistration-template-guide-for-researchers)
- Liu et al. preregistered on OSF, and Nyhan et al. preregistered eight attitudinal outcomes. Goldenberg et al. (2018) do not mention preregistration in the text — [Liu et al. (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.mit.edu/dist/9/583/files/2026/01/shorttermexposure_2025.pdf); [Nyhan et al. (PDF)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w.pdf); [Goldenberg (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5789904)

### Inferences (my calculations; standard formulas, not taken from a single source)
- **Sample size**, two-arm, α = .05 two-sided, 80% power, n per arm ≈ 2(1.96 + 0.84)²/d² ≈ 15.7/d²:
  - d = 0.40 → about 100 per arm.
  - d = 0.20 → about 390 per arm.
  - d = 0.10 → about 1,570 per arm.
  - Adjusting for a pretest that correlates ρ with the outcome multiplies the required n by about (1 − ρ²). With ρ = .7 that is ×0.51: about 200 per arm for d = 0.2 and about 800 for d = 0.1.
  - Adding about 40% attrition at a 1–2-month follow-up and corrections for multiple outcomes and arms raises these figures substantially.
- **Individual-level noise, illustrated.** Take a 0–100 thermometer with SD ≈ 25. The SDs of the unfreezing and Ascent measures are about 26–39 in the cited studies. Assume test-retest r = .80. Then SEM ≈ 11.2, S_diff ≈ 15.8, and a reliable individual change must exceed about 31 points. Most plausible treatment effects are a few points on average. So nearly every individual "KPI" change would fall within the noise band, while the condition-level mean difference can still be detected. Multi-item composites with higher reliability shrink the threshold.
- **Recommended layout:**
  - Wave 0: baseline, about 1 week before, with the key items embedded among fillers.
  - Wave 1: exposure plus immediate post.
  - Wave 2: delayed follow-up at about 1–2 weeks.
  - Wave 3: optional, at about 1–3 months.
  - Analysis: ANCOVA on the post score controlling for baseline.
  - Attention: pre-treatment attention screen; any post-treatment checks reported but not used for exclusion.
  - Engagement logs preregistered as secondary outcomes.
  - RCI reported only descriptively.

## Q5. Implicit measures (IAT and alternatives): current standing

### Takeaway
Implicit measures can be shifted briefly, but the shifts fade within hours to days and do not mediate changes in explicit attitudes or behaviour. The one IAT application to PeaceMaker found no change. For a media-exposure study focused on solution support, implicit measures are low-yield as primary KPIs.

### Cited Findings
- Forscher et al. (2019, *JPSP* 117(3), 522–559): a network meta-analysis of 492 studies (87,418 participants). Procedures can change implicit measures, but those changes "did not account for" changes in explicit measures or behaviour. Changes in explicit measures and behaviour were smaller and inconsistent — [Forscher et al. 2019 (PDF)](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2019-forscher.pdf); [Arkansas ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.uark.edu/psycpub/1); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/bias-behavior-14638/)
- Lai et al. (2016, *JEP: General* 145(8), 1001–1016): 9 interventions (N = 6,321) all reduced implicit racial preferences immediately. None was effective after a delay of several hours to several days, and explicit preferences were unchanged — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27454041/)
- PeaceMaker: implicit attitudes favoured Palestinians at baseline and "did not change significantly as a function of the treatment for both national groups", while some explicit attitudes shifted — [Alhabash & Wise 2012, IJOC](https://ojs3.ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/1056)
- The Ascent of Man is explicit, not implicit. It is only weakly correlated with subtle, infrahumanization-style measures and is the stronger predictor of intergroup behaviour — [Kteily et al. 2015 summary (Kellogg)](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2015/the-ascent-of-man-a-theoretical-and-empirical-case); [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/entities/publication/7fa37c44-1a11-4600-b53c-ab4f3859534e/full)

### Inferences
- If the team wants an implicit measure for exploratory reasons, it should be administered immediately post-exposure and treated as a short-lived process indicator, not a durable KPI. A brief IAT or an Affect Misattribution Procedure would be options. Given Forscher et al., changes in it should not be read as evidence of attitude or behaviour change.

### Gaps
- My searches did not cover the current standing of IAT alternatives (AMP, GNAT, evaluative priming) or their use in Israeli or Palestinian samples. I also found no Hebrew/Arabic implicit-measure validation for this conflict.
