## 1. Status quo / conflict management ("mowing the grass", the pre-Oct 7 "Concept", Netanyahu-era policy toward Hamas and the PA)

### Takeaway
From about 2009 to 2023, Israeli policy treated the conflict as something to contain rather than solve. Periodic short military campaigns in Gaza ("mowing the grass") were paired with economic relief for a Hamas-run Gaza, including Qatari cash that Israel facilitated, while the PA-run West Bank was kept separate. Critics, and some Netanyahu statements, link this to a goal of keeping the Palestinians politically divided. The October 7, 2023 attack is widely described, including by Israel's Shin Bet, as the collapse of this "conception." Netanyahu disputes that reading.

### Cited Findings
**Origin and doctrine**
- Efraim Inbar and Eitan Shamir of Bar-Ilan's Begin-Sadat (BESA) Center coined "mowing the grass" in "'Mowing the Grass': Israel's Strategy for Protracted Intractable Conflict," *Journal of Strategic Studies* (published online 2013, in print 2014). They treated it as a realistic strategy for a protracted, intractable conflict with non-state actors, not a path to a political solution. — [Wikipedia: Mowing the grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowing_the_grass); [Journal of Strategic Studies DOI page](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01402390.2013.830972)
- The approach uses short, sharp operations to degrade Hamas capabilities and buy "a period of quiet," with no long-term political settlement. The authors cautioned it could become outdated if non-state actors acquired statist characteristics or stronger capabilities. — [Wikipedia: Mowing the grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowing_the_grass)
- Inbar and Shamir applied the frame to the 2014 war in a July 2014 BESA paper, "Mowing the Grass in Gaza," republished by the Jerusalem Post. — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/Opinion/Columnists/Mowing-the-grass-in-Gaza-368516)
- In a 2018 speech, Naftali Bennett said that whoever does not mow the grass will have the grass mow them. — [Wikipedia: Mowing the grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowing_the_grass)

**Netanyahu-era policy toward Hamas and the PA, as analysed in the press**
- The NYT investigation "Buying Quiet" (Mark Mazzetti and Ronen Bergman, 10 Dec 2023) reported:
  - Qatari envoy Mohammed al-Emadi brought about $15 million a month into Gaza, with $100 paid to each approved family. By June 2021 Qatar was spending about $30 million a month, and billions flowed over roughly a decade.
  - The cabinet adopted a monthly cash plan in 2018. Defence Minister Avigdor Lieberman had called for a pre-emptive strike in a secret memo of 21 Dec 2016, which Netanyahu rejected. Lieberman resigned in Nov 2018, calling the policy "buying short-term peace at the price of serious damage to long-term national security."
  - Bennett continued the payments in 2021 even though he had earlier called them "protection money."
  — [Irish Times republication of NYT, 12 Dec 2023](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/middle-east/2023/12/12/buying-quiet-inside-the-israeli-plan-that-propped-up-hamas)
- The same NYT piece gave the working logic in the words of former intelligence official Yossi Kuperwasser: Hamas should be "strong enough to rule Gaza" but "weak enough to be deterred by Israel."
  - Israeli assessments repeatedly held that Hamas was "neither interested in nor capable of a large-scale attack."
  - Bezalel Smotrich said in 2015 that "Hamas is an asset."
  - Former NSC head Eyal Hulata described the approach as "if we buy quiet and pretend the problem isn't there, we can wait it out and it will fade away."
  - Netanyahu's office says the funds were humanitarian and that he weakened Hamas in three military operations.
  — [Irish Times/NYT](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/middle-east/2023/12/12/buying-quiet-inside-the-israeli-plan-that-propped-up-hamas)
- Tal Schneider argued in the Times of Israel (8 Oct 2023) that Netanyahu's policy treated Hamas as a partner at the expense of Abbas and Palestinian statehood. She noted that suitcases of Qatari cash had entered Gaza since 2018. A 2019 Netanyahu quote has circulated widely: "Anyone who wants to thwart the establishment of a Palestinian state has to support bolstering Hamas and transferring money to Hamas." It is attributed to a Likud meeting, but its provenance rests on press reports (originally Haaretz), not on a recording. — [Times of Israel, Schneider](https://www.timesofisrael.com/for-years-netanyahu-propped-up-hamas-now-its-blown-up-in-our-faces/)
- Netanyahu called the claim that he built up Hamas "ridiculous" (Politico interview). He has said Qatari money did not fund October 7, which "used cheap means." — [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/h1cqppnba); [Times of Israel liveblog](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/netanyahu-qatargate-probe-will-be-shown-to-be-a-total-libel/)
- **2025 Shin Bet probe.** The Shin Bet's internal October 7 probe listed the policy of "calm" in Gaza and Qatari funds reaching Hamas's military wing among the factors that let Hamas build up. Channel 12 reported an unpublished annex tying the failure directly to the Prime Minister's policy. Qatar denied that any aid reached Hamas's political or military wings. — [Times of Israel liveblog](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/report-shin-bet-probe-tied-oct-7-directly-to-pms-policy-of-sending-qatari-money-to-gaza-may-be-another-reason-hes-firing-bar/)
- **2026 TIME investigation.** Ynet's summary says documents show Israel helped move nearly $2 billion from Qatar to Gaza over more than a decade. The report says Netanyahu-led governments "actively encouraged continued payments" and that the funds allegedly covered about half the military wing's budget. A related report says the documents challenge Netanyahu's election-season defence. These are second-hand summaries of TIME. — [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/l9qc4pft1); [Ahram reprint of TIME, Oct 2026](https://english.ahram.org.eg/UI/Front/Inner.aspx?NewsContentID=580314)
- Ynet also reported that Israel sought an increase in Qatari cash for Gaza weeks before October 7 (headline only; not read in full). — [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/byabrjsnzg)

**Critiques**
- Palestinian scholars such as Nadim Rouhana (2025) argue the strategy targets civilians and infrastructure, not only "rival organizations." Avi Shlaim (Guardian, 2019) and Raphael Cohen (LA Times, 2023) called it "endless brute force" and "futile." — [Wikipedia: Mowing the grass (bibliography)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowing_the_grass)
- Opposition leader Avigdor Lieberman: "for years, Netanyahu nurtured Hamas." — [Yahoo/ToI](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/netanyahu-nurtured-hamas-years-avigdor-103400407.html)

**Status after 2023**
- The Gaza war (Oct 2023 onward) and the open-ended IDF presence along the "Yellow Line" (section 6) replaced "mowing the grass" with long-term territorial control. Israel held about 58–70% of Gaza in 2026, depending on the source. — [Carnegie, Zaha Hassan, 30 Jul 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/07/gaza-refugees-displacement-board-of-peace-trump-netanyahu-palestine-israel); [J Street, Jul 2026](https://jstreet.org/resources/nine-months-assessing-status-gaza-ceasefire)

### Inferences
- For modelling, the pre-2023 status quo works as a default interim state with low upfront cost but rising "tail risk." Its stability depended on deterrence assumptions that were invalidated catastrophically on October 7. A game could represent it as a state whose collapse probability rises with adversary capability build-up and with funding flows that are hard to audit.
- The PA–Hamas split was a structural feature of this approach, whether by design (critics) or by default (Netanyahu's position). That matters for any endgame that needs a single Palestinian interlocutor.

### Gaps
- The IDF's own October 7 probes (released 2025) and their use of "the concept" (*ha-konseptzia*) could not be fetched. The Hulata quote is the attributed source for "conception" language here.
- The original Haaretz report of the 2019 quote was not retrieved, so it is cited second-hand.
- The scholarly "conflict management" literature (e.g., Yaakov Amidror, Kobi Michael at INSS) was not retrieved.

## 2. "Shrinking the conflict" (Micah Goodman, *Catch-67*, 2017; Bennett–Lapid government, 2021–22)

### Takeaway
Goodman argues that neither a final-status deal nor the status quo is viable, so Israel should reduce the "quantity" of conflict through practical steps: movement, permits, economic access and more Palestinian self-rule, without withdrawal or a Palestinian state. The 2021 Bennett–Lapid coalition adopted the phrase as its consensus approach because its members could not agree on an end state. Critics on the left and among Palestinians call it a rebranding of occupation management. The right calls it naïve.

### Cited Findings
- **Origin.** Goodman's *Catch-67* (Hebrew 2017) argued that left and right offer "ideologically pure" solutions in a "false dichotomy" that keeps the status quo in place. His prescription: "We need to quantify the conflict and decrease the quantity of the conflict." It focused on the West Bank and excluded Gaza. — [CNN via KTVZ, 15 Sep 2021](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/)
- **Proposed measures.**
  - Less controversial: more Palestinian work permits in Israel, better trade access, and roads and bridges linking Palestinian cities.
  - More controversial: freezing settlement building outside the main blocs and giving Palestinians more land for construction.
  — [CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/)
- Goodman set out his steps in a 2019 *Atlantic* essay. +972 summarizes them as: streamlining permits and crossings with new technologies, Palestinian energy independence, smoother imports and exports, and better quality of life. — [+972 Magazine, Meron Rapoport, 12 Aug 2021](https://www.972mag.com/bennett-saar-goodman-shrinking-conflict/)
- **Adoption by the 2021 government.**
  - NPR described Goodman as an "unofficial counsel" to PM Bennett. Bennett pledged in his inaugural Knesset speech a "reduction of frictions and the shrinking of the conflict."
  - The coalition platform spoke of no permanent settlement in the foreseeable future, but of policies to shrink the conflict without compromising security.
  — [NPR via WUNC, 25 Aug 2021](https://www.wunc.org/2021-08-25/philosopher-micah-goodman-is-an-unofficial-counsel-to-israels-prime-minister); [+972](https://www.972mag.com/bennett-saar-goodman-shrinking-conflict/)
- Goodman on the coalition: "There is no consensus in the government about the end game, but they can agree about the game." — [CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/)
- Bennett told Channel 12 that the conflict "is not over territory." He said he would shrink it through "more crossings, quality of life, business, and industry." — [+972](https://www.972mag.com/bennett-saar-goodman-shrinking-conflict/)
- **Concrete steps, 2021.** CNN listed:
  - 15,000 new Palestinian work permits
  - a loan to the PA, reported by CNN as "$500 million" (see Gaps)
  - re-established Joint Economic Committees
  - legal status for Palestinian spouses in the West Bank
  - tentative approval of about 2,000 settlement housing units in Area C
  
  +972 reports permits for 800 Palestinian housing units in Area C alongside 2,000 settlement units. — [CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/); [+972](https://www.972mag.com/bennett-saar-goodman-shrinking-conflict/)
- **Core terms (as an end state):**
  - Territory: no withdrawal; the approach "doesn't preclude" Bennett's long-held preference for annexing Area C.
  - Security: full IDF control retained.
  - Jerusalem and refugees: not addressed.
  - Settlements: freeze outside blocs only in Goodman's more controversial version.
  - Political rights: expanded Palestinian autonomy short of statehood; no citizenship change.
  — [CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/)
- **Critiques.**
  - Right (Ran Baratz, former Netanyahu aide): the conflict is not caused by "friction," and the period is "just a lull between wars."
  - Left (Dahlia Scheindlin): the approach may "perpetuate the paradigm of Israel giveth and Israel taketh away."
  - Palestinian (Fatah dissident Samer Sinijwali): can it be resolved "if only one side agrees? I doubt it."
  — [CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/)
- +972 argues that "shrinking" treats an unequal occupation as a natural friction problem and is in effect "managing the occupation." It says centrists such as Lapid, who back two states but see no near-term path, could adopt it. — [+972](https://www.972mag.com/bennett-saar-goodman-shrinking-conflict/)
- Mustafa Barghouti said shrinking the conflict is not a popular concept among Palestinians. — [Reset DOC](https://www.resetdoc.org/story/shrinking-conflict-emerging-temptation-israels-mega-coalition/)
- **Outcome.** The Bennett–Lapid government fell in mid-2022 (Knesset dissolved June 2022; background fact, not re-verified this session). — [Wikipedia: Thirty-sixth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel)
- **2026 relevance.** Bennett's party "Together" (B'Yachad) won 12 seats in the 27 Oct 2026 election and recommended Gadi Eisenkot for PM. That keeps the "shrink, don't solve" constituency in the anti-Netanyahu bloc. — [Wikipedia: 2026 Israeli legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Israeli_legislative_election)

### Inferences
- "Shrinking" lies between pure management (section 1) and conditional statehood plans (sections 4 and 6). It changes Palestinian quality-of-life variables without touching sovereignty variables. A game can model it as a reversible "interim state" in which Israel holds every lever ("giveth and taketh"), with low Palestinian buy-in.
- Its main political function was coalition glue among parties with incompatible end states. The same function may recur if an Eisenkot-led coalition including Bennett forms after the 2026 election.

### Gaps
- Goodman's 2019 Atlantic essay could not be fetched (site blocked). His exact eight steps are given only via +972's summary.
- The PA loan figure is reported by CNN as "$500 million." Other reporting at the time described it as NIS 500 million (about $155 million); this could not be verified and needs checking.
- Lapid's Sept 2021 "economy for security" plan for Gaza and the Aug 2021 Gantz–Abbas meeting were not verified with sources this session.
- No public-opinion data was found specifically on "shrinking the conflict."

## 3. Economic peace (Netanyahu, 2008 onward) and the "Peace to Prosperity" workshop (Manama, Bahrain, 25–26 June 2019)

### Takeaway
"Economic peace" holds that Palestinian economic development should come first, or alongside, and make political compromise easier. Netanyahu framed it as a complement to, not a substitute for, political talks. The Trump administration's 2019 Bahrain workshop turned it into a $50 billion, ten-year investment prospectus that deliberately left out borders, Jerusalem and refugees. The PA boycotted the workshop, and it produced no funded program.

### Cited Findings
- **Netanyahu's concept.**
  - As opposition leader in 2008, Netanyahu told lawmakers that "rapid development is key, and not only for the sake of negotiations with the Palestinians."
  - He also said that "The economic peace is not an alternative for peace talks but will help guide a winning process," and argued that "quickened economic development has helped form the conditions for peace."
  — [Talking Points Memo, Benjy Sarlin, 30 Jul 2012](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/?p=96691)
- In his June 2009 Bar-Ilan speech (full text published by Al Jazeera), Netanyahu described economic peace as not a substitute for political peace but an important element in achieving it. — [Al Jazeera full text](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2009/7/11/full-text-of-netanyahus-speech)
- A Jerusalem Post preview reported that Netanyahu would use a Herzliya Conference speech to call for economic projects to help Palestinians (undated in results; year unconfirmed). — [Jerusalem Post](https://m.jpost.com/israel/netanyahu-to-call-for-moving-fence)
- **Bahrain workshop (25–26 June 2019).**
  - The workshop was the economic component of the Trump plan, led by Jared Kushner.
  - It proposed $50 billion of investment in infrastructure, tourism and education to create one million Palestinian jobs.
  - More than half would be spent in the Palestinian territories over ten years, with the rest split among Egypt, Jordan and Lebanon.
  — [Al Jazeera live, 26 Jun 2019](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/6/26/us-led-bahrain-meeting-on-palestine-all-the-latest-updates)
- The economic document was released 22 June 2019 and the political document on 28 Jan 2020. The published plan runs to 180 pages. — [Wikipedia: 2020 Trump Israel–Palestine plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)
- The 2020 consolidated text says Peace to Prosperity has "the potential to facilitate more than $50 billion in new investment over ten years" and aims to "create over one million Palestinian jobs." — [White House, *Peace to Prosperity* (Jan 2020) PDF](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Peace-to-Prosperity-0120.pdf)
- **Reactions.**
  - The PA refused to attend, accusing Washington of dangling cash to impose a political outcome while ignoring the occupation. Critics noted the plan had no proposals on borders, Jerusalem or refugees.
  - Kushner called the boycott "a mistake" and the plan the "opportunity of the century."
  — [Al Jazeera live](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/6/26/us-led-bahrain-meeting-on-palestine-all-the-latest-updates); [Yahoo/News](https://news.yahoo.com/jared-kushner-calls-trumps-peace-192212634.html)
- PA Prime Minister Mohammad Shtayyeh called the Bahrain summit "nonsense." — [The New Arab](https://newarab.com/news/palestinian-pm-calls-us-led-bahrain-economic-summit-nonsense)
- Egypt attended to "evaluate" the $50 billion plan. — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/1515446)
- **Implementation.** No dedicated fund was set up. The parallel US "Abraham Fund," launched with the 2020 accords and aiming for $3 billion, never received funds and no projects began. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- **Legacy in 2025–26.** Economic-peace logic reappears in the Trump 20-point Gaza plan (2025): an "economic development plan" designed by experts and a "special economic zone with preferred tariff and access rates." It also shapes the Board of Peace's reconstruction-first programming, such as the $2.45 billion, 66-programme, six-month plan of 24 Sep 2026. — [Wikipedia: Gaza peace plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan); [Wikipedia: Board of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Peace); [PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/trumps-board-of-peace-proposes-2-45-billion-blueprint-to-begin-gaza-reconstruction)
- **Critique of Gaza "economic" schemes (2025–26).** Carnegie's Zaha Hassan argues that leaked Board-adjacent plans such as the "GREAT Trust," "Project Sunrise" and "New Rafah" monetize land and coastline. She says they risk forcing Palestinians to choose between temporary compounds and resettlement abroad. Many of these plans are unofficial and unsigned. — [Carnegie, 30 Jul 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/07/gaza-refugees-displacement-board-of-peace-trump-netanyahu-palestine-israel)

### Inferences
- As a standalone endgame, economic peace offers no settlement on sovereignty, borders, Jerusalem or refugees. In a game it fits best as a modifier on any interim state, raising Palestinian welfare and possibly lowering violence, with outcomes highly sensitive to Palestinian political buy-in and donor follow-through. Both the 2019 boycott and the 2026 funding gap show that buy-in and money do not follow automatically.
- Donor reluctance to fund rebuilding that may be destroyed again is a recurring constraint (Arab plan 2025, Board of Peace 2026; see section 6).

### Gaps
- No verified transcript of a 2008 Netanyahu Herzliya speech was found. The 2008 quotes come via TPM (2012).
- The JMCC fact sheet tracing "economic peace" to Moshe Dayan was not available (page not found).
- No public-opinion data was found on the 2019 workshop.

## 4. The Trump "Peace to Prosperity" political plan ("Vision for Peace", 28 January 2020)

### Takeaway
The plan offered a conditional, demilitarized Palestinian state on roughly 70% of the West Bank plus Gaza and Negev swap areas, with territory joined by roads, tunnels and bridges. Israel would get sovereignty over the Jordan Valley and essentially all settlements. Jerusalem would remain Israel's undivided capital, with a Palestinian capital in areas beyond the barrier (Kafr Aqab, eastern Shuafat, Abu Dis). There would be no refugee return to Israel, and a possible transfer of the Arab "Triangle" towns to Palestine. The PA, the Arab League and the OIC rejected it, as did the settler Yesha Council and 94% of Palestinians polled. Israel's planned annexation under the plan was suspended in August 2020 in exchange for UAE normalization.

### Cited Findings
**Origin, date, proponents**
- Trump and Netanyahu unveiled the plan at the White House on 28 Jan 2020, with no Palestinian representatives present. It was developed from Nov 2017 under Jared Kushner, with Jason Greenblatt, Dina Powell and Ambassador David Friedman. — [Wikipedia: 2020 Trump Israel–Palestine plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)

**Core terms (quoted from the primary document, [White House PDF](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Peace-to-Prosperity-0120.pdf))**
- **Territory and settlements.**
  - "Approximately 97% of Israelis in the West Bank will be incorporated into contiguous Israeli territory, and approximately 97% of Palestinians in the West Bank will be incorporated into contiguous Palestinian territory."
  - "Land swaps will provide the State of Palestine with land reasonably comparable in size to the territory of pre-1967 West Bank and Gaza."
  - Israel "will not have to uproot any settlements." Israeli enclaves inside Palestinian territory become part of Israel and are linked by transport routes.
  - Palestinian enclaves inside Israel get Palestinian citizenship and civil administration, under Israeli security responsibility.
  - (Borders section, pp. 12–13)
- **Jordan Valley.** "The Jordan Valley, which is critical for Israel's national security, will be under Israeli sovereignty." Palestinian agricultural enterprises would continue under Israeli licences or leases. (p. 13)
- **Swaps near Gaza and Negev.** The plan provides for "allocating for the Palestinians Israeli territory close to Gaza (as depicted on the conceptual map)" for infrastructure and future towns. Press reports describe uninhabited Negev land near the Egyptian border, with one industrial and high-tech zone and one residential and agricultural zone, as compensation for about 15 settlements left as enclaves inside Palestine. — [Primary PDF p. 13](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Peace-to-Prosperity-0120.pdf); [Ynet on Negev communities' concerns](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/SkQc5X1MI)
- **Triangle.** "Land swaps provided by the State of Israel could include both populated and unpopulated areas." The plan names the Triangle Communities: Kafr Qara, Ar'ara, Baha al-Gharbiyye, Umm al Fahm, Qalansawe, Tayibe, Kafr Qasim, Tira, Kafr Bara and Jaljulia. It "contemplates the possibility, subject to agreement of the parties," that the borders be redrawn so they "become part of the State of Palestine," with residents' civil rights "subject to the applicable laws and judicial rulings of the relevant authorities." (p. 13)
- **Connectivity.**
  - "High-speed transportation links (such as the West Bank/Gaza connection)."
  - Two access roads across the Jordan Valley to Jordan, subject to Israeli security requirements.
  - "Tunnels and overpasses" linking enclaves.
  - Access to designated facilities at Haifa and Ashdod ports until Palestine builds its own port.
  - Israel retains sovereignty over territorial waters.
  - (pp. 12–14)
- **Jerusalem.**
  - "Jerusalem will remain the sovereign capital of the State of Israel, and it should remain an undivided city."
  - The Palestinian capital "should be in the section of East Jerusalem located in all areas east and north of the existing security barrier, including Kafr Aqab, the eastern part of Shuafat and Abu Dis, and could be named Al Quds."
  - Arab residents inside the barrier could choose Israeli citizenship, Palestinian citizenship, or continued Israeli permanent residency.
  - Holy sites keep "the same governance regimes that exist today" and the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif status quo "should continue uninterrupted." The text also says "People of every faith should be permitted to pray on the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif."
  - (pp. 16–18)
- **Refugees.**
  - "There shall be no right of return by, or absorption of, any Palestinian refugee into the State of Israel."
  - Eligibility is limited to UNRWA-registered refugees as of the plan's release date.
  - Three options: absorption into Palestine "subject to limitations"; integration in host countries with their consent; or "5,000 refugees each year, for up to ten years (50,000 total refugees)" in willing OIC member states.
  - A "Palestinian Refugee Trust" with two trustees would distribute whatever compensation is raised.
  - Jewish refugees from Arab countries and Israel's absorption costs "must also be addressed."
  - (Refugees section)
- **Security.**
  - Israel "will maintain overriding security responsibility" for Palestine.
  - "The State of Palestine shall be fully demilitarized and remain so."
  - Wikipedia's summary adds that Israel would control borders, airspace and the electromagnetic spectrum.
  — [Primary PDF](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Peace-to-Prosperity-0120.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)
- **Gaza conditions.** Before implementation in Gaza: (1) "the Palestinian Authority or another national or international body acceptable to the State of Israel is in full control of Gaza"; (2) "Hamas, PIJ, and all other militias and terror organizations in Gaza are disarmed"; (3) "Gaza is fully demilitarized." (p. 25)
- **Conditions for statehood.** These are "to be determined to have occurred by the State of Israel and the United States, jointly... after consultation with the Palestinian Authority":
  - a constitution and rule of law with free press and free elections
  - creditworthy financial institutions meeting IMF criteria
  - an end to incitement and to "pay-for-slay" programs
  - civilian and law-enforcement control over all territory, and demilitarization
  
  Wikipedia's summary adds: recognizing Israel as the Jewish state, not joining international organizations without Israeli consent, and dropping ICC and ICJ actions. Statehood would come no earlier than up to four years into implementation. — [Primary PDF pp. 33–34](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Peace-to-Prosperity-0120.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)
- **Settlement freeze during talks.** In West Bank areas "not contemplated by this Vision to be part of the State of Israel," Israel would not build or expand settlements or enclaves and would freeze most demolitions (Section 22, "Conduct During Negotiations"). Netanyahu pledged no new settlements in those areas for four years. — [Primary PDF](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Peace-to-Prosperity-0120.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)
- **Size.** Press reporting estimated the Palestinian area at about 70% of the West Bank, with Israel annexing about 30%. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2020/01/28/trump-middle-east-peace-plan-palestinian-state/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)

**Reactions**
- **Palestinians.** Abbas called it the "slap of the century" and told the UNSC in Feb 2020 that the proposed state looked like "Swiss cheese." Saeb Erekat called it the "fraud of the century." Hamas rejected it. On 9 Jun 2020 PM Shtayyeh proposed a counter-plan for a demilitarized state with one-to-one swaps. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)
- **Arab and Muslim bodies.** The Arab League unanimously rejected the plan on 1 Feb 2020, and the OIC on 3 Feb 2020.
  - Jordan insisted on 1967 lines with an East Jerusalem capital.
  - Saudi Arabia praised US efforts but urged direct talks.
  - The UAE called it "an important starting point."
  - Egypt called for "careful consideration."
  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan); [Ynet on OIC](https://ynetnews.com/article/Hyfy5OHzI)
- **Europe and the UN.** EU High Representative Borrell (4 Feb 2020) said annexation steps "could not pass unchallenged." Guterres said he could accept only a plan respecting UN resolutions and international law. Fifty former European prime ministers and foreign ministers said it would create an apartheid-like situation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)
- **Israel.** Netanyahu welcomed the plan and moved to annex the Jordan Valley and the settlements. The Yesha Council opposed it because it envisaged a Palestinian state. Gantz rejected the 1 July 2020 annexation start date. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2020/01/28/trump-middle-east-peace-plan-expands-israeli-territory-offers-path-n1124606)
- **Arab citizens of Israel and the Triangle.** Thousands protested and Triangle mayors rejected transfer. Jo-Ann Mort (NYRB, 3 Feb 2020) called it a peacetime forced population transfer. She cites legal scholar Yuval Shany: a state "cannot strip its own nationals of their citizenships against their will." She traces the idea to Avigdor Lieberman's 2004 plan. — [NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2020/02/03/the-trump-netanyahu-plan-to-force-arab-population-transfer/); [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/world/mena/thousands-of-arabs-protest-in-israel-against-trump-peace-plan-1.972752)
- **US politics.** Leading 2020 Democratic candidates called the plan a "smokescreen" for annexation, and more than 185 House Democrats opposed annexation. J Street criticized the plan and AIPAC praised it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)

**Public opinion**
- **PCPSR (5–8 Feb 2020, n=1,270).** 94% of Palestinians opposed the plan and 4% supported it. Only 39% supported the two-state concept (59% opposed), and 64% favoured a return to armed struggle in response. The Washington Institute describes a 94% "categorical" rejection among West Bank respondents, which may be a different cut of the data. — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/poll-94-percent-of-palestinians-oppose-us-peace-plan/); [Israel Hayom](https://www.israelhayom.com/2020/02/12/poll-94-of-palestinians-reject-trump-plan-64-favor-return-to-armed-struggle); [Washington Institute](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/palestinian-public-rejects-trump-plan-prefers-calm-practical-response)
- **Settlers.** A June 2020 survey reported that 56% of Jewish settlers supported the plan (headline only). — [i24 News](https://www.i24news.tv/en/news/israel/diplomacy-defense/1592408992-israel-56-of-jewish-settlers-support-trump-peace-plan-new-survey-finds)

**Implementation and outcome**
- A joint US–Israel mapping committee was formed on 15 Feb 2020. Annexation did not begin on 1 July 2020. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel–Palestine_plan)
- The UAE–Israel deal announced on 13 Aug 2020 was tied to postponing annexation. Netanyahu later said annexation was only postponed, and the plan was effectively shelved as diplomacy shifted to the Abraham Accords. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords); [MEI backgrounder (title)](https://mei.edu/backgrounder/abraham-accords/)
- Its DNA survives in the 2025 Gaza plan: conditional statehood "pathway," PA reform conditions, demilitarization, and Israeli security perimeters (section 6).

### Inferences
- On the spectrum, the plan is formally a "two-state" outcome but sits at the far Israeli end. Sovereignty is heavily conditioned, Israel and the US jointly judge the readiness criteria, and Israel keeps overriding security control. For game design it can be treated as a "conditional state with vetoes": the Palestinian state exists only once Israeli/US-adjudicated thresholds are met, which creates an endogenous veto point.
- The Triangle clause is an unusual mechanism, a "population-for-territory" swap of existing citizens, with high domestic legitimacy costs inside Israel. A model should treat it as near-infeasible without consent.

### Gaps
- The academic and former-official critiques the brief named (Kurtzer, Indyk, Ross, Elgindy, Thrall) were not retrieved this session.
- IDI polling on the 2020 plan was not found.
- The exact share of territory (about 70% Palestinian) is a press estimate. The document itself gives only "reasonably comparable" swap language and a conceptual map.

## 5. Regional normalization frameworks (Abraham Accords and accessions; Israel–Saudi talks; I2U2/IMEC)

### Takeaway
The Abraham Accords (2020) normalized Israel's relations with the UAE, Bahrain, Morocco and Sudan without resolving the Palestinian question. In practice they reversed the Arab Peace Initiative's sequence of statehood first, normalization later. The only new accession since 2020 is Kazakhstan (announced 6 Nov 2025). Somaliland pledged to join after Israel recognized it in Dec 2025. Saudi normalization, the "crown jewel," remains frozen through November 2026. Riyadh insists on a Palestinian state on 1967 lines or an "irreversible pathway" to one, and a Saudi source said on 6 Nov 2026 that normalization is off the table while Netanyahu's government remains. The IMEC/I2U2 corridors were designed partly as normalization accelerants and conflict-management tools, and they have been largely stalled since October 2023.

### Cited Findings
**Abraham Accords: dates and terms**
- The UAE–Israel deal was announced 13 Aug 2020, tied to postponing West Bank annexation. Bahrain's deal followed on 11 Sep 2020, and both were signed at the White House on 15 Sep 2020. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- Sudan agreed to normalize on 23 Oct 2020. The US removed it from the state-sponsors-of-terrorism list and provided a $1.2 billion bridge loan to clear World Bank arrears. Sudan signed the declaration on 6 Jan 2021, but normalization remained unratified as of 2024. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- Morocco's deal was announced 10 Dec 2020 alongside US recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara. Some sources date the formal signing to 22 Dec 2020. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- Kazakhstan's accession was announced 6 Nov 2025 after a Trump–Netanyahu–Tokayev call and formalized in early 2026. It was the first accession of Trump's second term. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords); [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-873025)
  - Atlantic Council commentators noted that Kazakhstan already had normal relations with Israel, making accession "mostly a costless formality." — [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/new-atlanticist/experts-react-kazakhstan-will-join-the-abraham-accords-heres-what-that-means-for-the-us-the-middle-east-and-central-asia/)
- Israel recognized Somaliland on 26 Dec 2025, and Somaliland pledged to join the Accords. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- In July 2025 the administration reportedly sought to add Syria, Lebanon and Saudi Arabia. Syria's president later said Syria would not join. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- On 25 May 2026 Trump called on Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Turkey and Pakistan to join "immediately," ahead of any US agreement with Iran. — [Times of Israel liveblog, 25 May 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/after-trump-call-saudi-source-says-no-normalization-with-israel-without-irreversible-pathway-to-palestinian-state/)

**Accords and the Palestinian question**
- **Rationale and terms.** The accords set no Palestinian terms beyond the annexation postponement. Critics say they did little for Palestinians, and a Fatah official called them "one of the reasons" for October 7. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- **Leverage on annexation.** In early Sept 2025 the UAE publicly warned Israel that West Bank annexation would undermine the Accords. Irish Times analysis called the intervention one that "carries real weight." — [Irish Times, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/middle-east/2025/09/04/why-the-uaes-intervention-in-west-bank-carries-real-weight)
- Secretary of State Rubio called a Knesset annexation bill "counterproductive" (Oct 2025). — [Wikipedia: Gaza peace plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan)
- **Effects of the Gaza war.** Bahrain recalled its ambassador on 2 Nov 2023. Israeli defence firms were excluded from the 2025 Dubai Airshow. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- **Economic and security content.** Israeli defence exports to normalized states reached $791 million in 2020. The $3 billion "Abraham Fund" was never funded. A 2021–22 Jordan–UAE–Israel solar/water letter of intent was pulled from the COP28 agenda over Gaza. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)

**Public opinion (Arab states)**
- In a Nov 2022 poll, 76% of Saudis viewed the Accords negatively.
- In a Washington Institute poll of 14 Nov–6 Dec 2023, 96% of Saudis said Arab states should cut ties with Israel, and only 16% said Hamas should accept two states on 1967 lines.
— [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords); [Wikipedia: Israel–Saudi Arabia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel–Saudi_Arabia_relations)
- In a Washington Institute survey of Aug 2025, 99% of Saudi respondents called normal relations with Israel a negative step. Positive Saudi views of the Accords fell from 41% (2020) to 20% (2023) to 13% (2025). — [INSS, Yoel Guzansky, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/saudi-israel-2026/)

**Israel–Saudi normalization**
- In 2023 Saudi Arabia sought US security guarantees, help with a civilian nuclear program and looser arms limits. It suspended talks after October 7. On 9 Jan 2024 Ambassador Khalid bin Bandar told the BBC that normalization was still wanted after the war, but only with a Palestinian state. — [Wikipedia: Israel–Saudi Arabia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel–Saudi_Arabia_relations)
- In Sept 2024 MBS said the Kingdom "will not establish diplomatic relations with Israel without" a Palestinian state with East Jerusalem as its capital. — [Wikipedia: Israel–Saudi Arabia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel–Saudi_Arabia_relations)
- **INSS assessment (Feb 2026).**
  - Normalization is "off the agenda." Riyadh sees "more risks than opportunities," and its 1967/East Jerusalem condition is "unequivocal and non-negotiable."
  - Israel would need to offer "a clear and binding pathway" to a Palestinian state, and the current Israeli government "lacks the desire" for a deal.
  - MBS reportedly told US lawmakers in 2024 that his normalization push put his life at risk and that any deal needs "a real path" to a state.
  - Revival would require "a fundamental change in circumstances," such as political change in Israel.
  - Saudi Arabia co-led with France the Sept 2025 UNGA initiative on recognizing Palestine (the New York Declaration), which referenced the "right of return" and endorsed UNRWA.
  — [INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/saudi-israel-2026/)
- **"Irreversible pathway" (25 May 2026).** After Trump's call, a Saudi source said Riyadh would normalize only if an "irreversible pathway" to Palestinian statehood is established. — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/after-trump-call-saudi-source-says-no-normalization-with-israel-without-irreversible-pathway-to-palestinian-state/)
- **Nuclear linkage (Aug 2026).** Per the WSJ, as relayed by Middle East Monitor (a partisan outlet), Trump sent the US–Saudi civil nuclear cooperation agreement to Congress with the condition that it will not take effect unless Saudi Arabia joins the Abraham Accords. Energy Secretary Chris Wright and Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman had signed it the previous month. A Saudi source rejected the linkage and reiterated that the 1967/East Jerusalem position is "clear, firm and unwavering." — [Middle East Monitor, 27 Aug 2026](https://www.middleeastmonitor.com/20260827-saudi-arabia-reaffirms-no-normalisation-with-israel-without-palestinian-state/)
- **After the Israeli election (6 Nov 2026).** A Saudi source told KAN that normalization "cannot happen while" Netanyahu is in power: "As long as Netanyahu is in power with the current government, normalization is not on the agenda." The source said Riyadh wants the issue "closed via a two-state solution." In Oct 2025 a royal-family source had told Channel 12 that normalization could follow a new Israeli government that accepts two states. On 25 Oct 2026 Saudi Arabia signed a joint statement calling Israel "the fundamental source of instability." (Anonymous-sourced.) — [Jerusalem Post, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-914042)

**I2U2 and IMEC as conflict-management tools**
- **I2U2** (India, Israel, UAE, US) was formed in 2022 and is described as IMEC's conceptual precursor. — [Wikipedia: IMEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India–Middle_East–Europe_Economic_Corridor)
- **IMEC origin.** IMEC was announced at the G20 in New Delhi on 9 Sep 2023. The MoU was signed by India, the US, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, France, Germany, Italy and the EU. Israel and Jordan are on the route but did not sign. The route is a maritime leg from India to the Gulf, then rail across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Israel to Haifa (owned by India's Adani Group), then shipping to Europe. The MoU is a declaration of intent without a funding plan. — [Wikipedia: IMEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India–Middle_East–Europe_Economic_Corridor)
- **Rationale.** The US and Israel saw IMEC as a vehicle for the Accords and for Saudi normalization. The Biden administration reportedly hoped the economic ties could underpin a Saudi–Israeli defence arrangement. Netanyahu called it a "blessing" at UNGA (Sept 2024). — [Wikipedia: IMEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India–Middle_East–Europe_Economic_Corridor)
- **Status.**
  - The Gaza war "significantly stalled" IMEC and effectively paralyzed the Saudi–Jordan–Israel rail segment. Houthi attacks deterred private investment.
  - Non-Israeli legs progressed: India–UAE framework agreements (2024), a Marseille summit (June 2025), a Trieste summit (2025), Trump–Modi endorsement (Feb 2025: "one of the greatest trade routes in all of history"), and the EU–India trade deal (Jan 2026).
  - Turkey's Erdoğan opposes the route and promotes an Iraq "Development Road." Jordan lacks functioning rail.
  — [Wikipedia: IMEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India–Middle_East–Europe_Economic_Corridor)

### Inferences
- Normalization has worked as a substitute for resolution between 2020 and 2023, and as a lever on Israeli behaviour: the 2020 annexation freeze and the Sept 2025 UAE red line. It has not driven a final-status deal. In a model it fits best as an external "reward track" with thresholds. Small states join cheaply (Kazakhstan); the decisive actor (Saudi Arabia) sets a high Palestinian threshold that the incumbent Israeli government has rejected.
- The Saudi threshold appears to have hardened over time: from 2023's undefined "Palestinian component," to "1967 borders and East Jerusalem," to an "irreversible pathway." Gulf public opinion constrains rulers, with 96–99% negative in Saudi polls. Israeli government change (section 7) is the variable Saudi sources explicitly name.
- Corridor projects (IMEC) are best modelled as long-lag economic payoffs contingent on normalization and Red Sea security, not as independent drivers.

### Gaps
- MBS's 18 Nov 2025 White House visit (reported Strategic Defense Agreement, F-35 sale approval, and his remarks linking Accords accession to a "clear path" to two states) could not be verified with a fetched source this session.
- Trade-volume figures for Israel–UAE trade and the Negev Forum's status were not retrieved.
- No independent confirmation was found of the date Kazakhstan's accession was formalized "in early 2026," or of any other 2026 accessions beyond the Somaliland pledge.
- I2U2's founding summit details (July 2022) and any 2025–26 I2U2 activity were not retrieved.

## 6. Post–October 2023 Gaza governance plans (Netanyahu "day after"; Arab League/Egypt plan; Trump "Riviera"; Blair GITA; Trump 20-point plan; UNSCR 2803 and implementation through Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
Plans converged in 2025 on a formula: no Hamas governing role, a technocratic Palestinian committee, international oversight, an international stabilization force, phased IDF withdrawal tied to disarmament, and a conditional, undated "pathway" to statehood. UNSCR 2803 (17 Nov 2025) endorsed that formula. By November 2026, implementation is largely stalled at the Phase 1/Phase 2 boundary:
- The Board of Peace exists and has a 30 July 2026 disarmament roadmap and a $2.45 billion six-month plan.
- The technocratic committee (NCAG) has never entered Gaza and works from Cairo.
- The ISF is a few hundred troops at most and lacks a base.
- Israel holds about 58–70% of Gaza behind the "Yellow Line."
- Hamas accepts disarmament "in principle," but the sequencing dispute (withdrawal first vs. disarmament first) is unresolved.
- Roughly 1,580 Palestinians have been reported killed since the October 2025 ceasefire (Gaza MoH, via a single-source newsletter).

### Cited Findings

**6a. Trump's Feb 2025 "Riviera" proposal (context)**
- On 4 Feb 2025 Trump said the US would "take over" and "own" Gaza and redevelop it as the "Riviera of the Middle East," relocating about two million Palestinians. He later said he would "recommend" rather than enforce it (21 Feb). — [Wikipedia: Gaza Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Riviera)
- A 38-page "GREAT Trust" prospectus assumed about 25% (around 500,000 people) would leave permanently, with $5,000 per person in incentives. — [Wikipedia: Gaza Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Riviera)
- Egypt, Jordan, the PA, Hamas, Saudi Arabia and others rejected the proposal. Netanyahu, Defence Minister Katz and opposition leader Lapid supported it. 15% of Americans supported a US takeover and 62% opposed. — [Wikipedia: Gaza Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Riviera)

**6b. Arab League/Egypt reconstruction plan (Cairo Extraordinary Arab Summit, 4 March 2025)**
- **Cost and phases.** The plan was costed at about $53 billion with no displacement of Palestinians:
  - six months of early recovery, with temporary housing for 1.5 million people at seven sites
  - a two-year first stage costing $20 billion
  - a 2.5-year second stage costing $30 billion
  — [Wikipedia: Gaza Riviera, citing Reuters and UPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Riviera); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/arab-summit-focus-egypts-alternative-trumps-gaza-riviera-2025-03-04/); [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2025/03/05/Israel-reject-Arab-plan-for-Gaza/8641741180895/)
- **Governance.** An interim committee of "independent, professional Palestinian technocrats" would govern Gaza and prepare for the PA's return. Hamas said it would not field candidates but wanted a say over the committee's members and agenda. Abbas offered elections. — [Reuters via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Riviera)
- **Reactions.**
  - Israel's foreign ministry called the plan "outdated" and objected to its reliance on the PA.
  - The White House said Gaza is "currently uninhabitable" and that "President Trump stands by his vision."
  - Envoy Witkoff said it had "compelling features."
  - The OIC and several European states endorsed it.
  - The NYT noted that funding was unclear because Gulf donors fear rebuilding only to see Gaza destroyed again.
  — [Wikipedia: Gaza Riviera (citing UPI, Reuters, Politico EU, Ynet, NYT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Riviera)

**6c. Tony Blair / Gaza International Transitional Authority (GITA), 2025**
- **Origin.** The Tony Blair Institute began work in July 2025. Blair met Trump and Kushner at the White House on 27 Aug 2025, and the Times of Israel first reported the proposals on 18 Sep 2025. — [Wikipedia: GITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_International_Transitional_Authority)
- **Structure.**
  - GITA as the "supreme political and legal authority," with an international board issuing binding decisions.
  - Five oversight commissioners (humanitarian, reconstruction, legal, security, PA liaison).
  - A Palestinian technocratic Executive Authority under a board-appointed Chief Executive.
  - The existing five governorates and 25 municipalities, and a Judicial Board led by an Arab jurist.
  - A Property Rights Unit guaranteeing return for those who leave temporarily.
  - An Arab-led ISF and vetted civil police.
  
  It was modelled on UN transitional administrations (Eastern Slavonia, Kosovo, East Timor, Cambodia, West New Guinea). The Trump 20-point plan incorporated its core. — [Wikipedia: GITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_International_Transitional_Authority)

**6d. Trump's 20-point "Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict" (29 Sep 2025) and ceasefire**
- **Key points** (paraphrased from the published text):
  - Gaza becomes a "deradicalized terror-free zone."
  - War ends on acceptance, with the IDF pulling back to an agreed line.
  - All hostages are returned within 72 hours.
  - Israel releases 250 life-sentence prisoners and 1,700 Gazans detained after 7 Oct 2023. It returns the remains of 15 Gazans per deceased hostage.
  - Amnesty and safe passage for Hamas members who decommission.
  - Aid at least at January 2025 levels via the UN and Red Crescent; Rafah opens under the Jan 2025 mechanism.
  - A "technocratic, apolitical Palestinian committee" overseen by a "Board of Peace" chaired by Trump, with Tony Blair among its members, until PA reforms are complete.
  - An economic plan and a special economic zone.
  - "No one will be forced to leave Gaza."
  - No Hamas governing role; demilitarization verified by independent monitors, with buy-back.
  - Regional guarantees of compliance.
  - A temporary International Stabilization Force that trains vetted Palestinian police and secures borders.
  - "Israel will not occupy or annex Gaza." IDF withdrawal by demilitarization milestones, keeping a security perimeter.
  - If Hamas delays, the plan proceeds in IDF-to-ISF handed-over areas.
  - Interfaith dialogue.
  - **Point 19:** as redevelopment advances and PA reform is carried out, "conditions may finally be in place for a credible pathway to Palestinian self-determination and statehood, which we recognize as the aspiration of the Palestinian people."
  - **Point 20:** a US-convened Israeli–Palestinian dialogue on a "political horizon."
  — [Wikipedia: Gaza peace plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan)
- **Timeline.**
  - 3 Oct 2025: Hamas accepted the release of all hostages and the handover of administration to technocrats, but not disarmament.
  - 8–9 Oct 2025: phase one agreed and signed at Sharm el-Sheikh, with the US, Qatar, Turkey and Egypt as mediators.
  - 10 Oct 2025: ceasefire took effect.
  - 13 Oct 2025: 20 living hostages released, and an international summit held at Sharm el-Sheikh without Netanyahu.
  - 26 Jan 2026: the IDF said it had recovered the last hostage's body.
  — [Wikipedia: Gaza peace plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan); [ABC News, 13 Oct 2025](https://abcnews.go.com/International/trump-world-leaders-gather-egypt-ceasefire-deal-signing/story?id=126477199)
- **Reactions.**
  - The PA supported the plan but was not party to the negotiations.
  - PIJ and the PFLP criticized it.
  - Smotrich called it "a historic missed opportunity."
  - Netanyahu warned on 12 Oct 2025 that the "military campaign is not over."
  - Critics included Rashid Khalidi, Noura Erakat and Diana Buttu.
  - Amnesty (27 Nov 2025) said Israel continued to commit genocide. A UN inquiry (June 2026) said Israel was deliberately targeting children.
  - France, Germany, the UK, Italy, Spain, Canada, Russia, the UAE, Egypt, Turkey, Qatar, Jordan, Indonesia and Pakistan supported the plan.
  — [Wikipedia: Gaza peace plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan); [Guardian, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jun/23/israel-deliberately-targeting-gaza-children-to-commit-genocide-un-inquiry-finds)
- Qatari diplomat Majed al-Ansari warned of Gaza sliding into "no war, no peace" (31 Oct 2025). — [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/oct/31/gaza-no-war-no-peace-qatari-diplomat-majed-al-ansar-international-force-israeli-withdrawal)

**6e. UN Security Council Resolution 2803 (17 Nov 2025)**
- **Vote.** Adopted 13–0, with Russia and China abstaining, at the 10,046th meeting. It "endorses the Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict." — [UNSCR.com](https://unscr.com/en/resolutions/2803); [UN meeting record S/PV.10046](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4093696/files/S_PV.10046-EN.pdf)
- **Board of Peace and ISF.** The resolution welcomes the Board of Peace "as a transitional administration" coordinating reconstruction. It authorizes a temporary ISF "under unified command acceptable to the BoP," with personnel contributed "in close consultation and cooperation" with Egypt and Israel. — [UN News, 17 Nov 2025](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166391)
- **ISF tasks** (per US Ambassador Waltz): stabilize security, support demilitarization, dismantle terrorist infrastructure, decommission weapons, protect civilians. — [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166391)
- **Other provisions.** The resolution allows an NCAG ("Palestinian technocratic, apolitical committee") for day-to-day administration and empowers the Board to "train and provide support to the vetted Palestinian police forces." The US draft of 3 Nov 2025 sought a two-year mandate. — [Wikipedia: Gaza Strip under Resolution 2803](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_under_Resolution_2803); [Times of Israel on draft](https://www.timesofisrael.com/draft-un-resolution-would-grant-us-and-partners-two-year-mandate-to-govern-gaza/)
- **Duration.** The Board's UN mandate expires 31 Dec 2027 (Carnegie). EU High Representative Kallas noted that the Board's charter omits "the time limit of 2027 set in the UN resolution." — [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/07/gaza-refugees-displacement-board-of-peace-trump-netanyahu-palestine-israel); [Wikipedia: Board of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Peace)
- **Explanations of vote.**
  - Russia (Nebenzia) said the Council was "giving its blessing to a US initiative on the basis of Washington's promises" and "complete control" to the Board.
  - China (Fu Cong) cited "skimpy details" on the structure of the ISF and the Board.
  - Algeria said peace is impossible "without justice for the Palestinian people."
  - Pakistan said important recommendations were omitted.
  
  The PA welcomed the resolution. Hamas called it "foreign guardianship." Netanyahu welcomed it, citing "full demilitarization, disarmament, and the deradicalization of Gaza." Russia had tabled a rival draft that omitted the Board. — [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166391); [Wikipedia: UNSCR 2803](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_2803); [Al Jazeera, Hamas](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2025/11/17/live-hamas-rejects-foreign-guardianship-of-gaza-before-un-vote)
- **Reporting.** The Board's first progress report to the UNSC on 15 May 2026 blamed Hamas's unwillingness to disarm. — [Wikipedia: Board of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Peace)

**6f. Board of Peace (BoP): structure and funding**

**6i. Disarmament and withdrawal sequencing (core blocker)**
- **March 2026 Board plan.** Mladenov's plan phases disarmament over eight months:
  - Weeks 1–2: full halt to operations, and NCAG entry.
  - Days 16–90: heavy weapons removed, first from IDF-held areas, then from Hamas-held areas; tunnels destroyed before day 90.
  - After certification: gradual IDF withdrawal to the perimeter; weapons collection by day 251; then full reconstruction and lifting of dual-use restrictions.
  
  Palestinian analysts called it "political surrender." — [Al Jazeera, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/26/details-revealed-of-board-of-peace-plan-for-gaza-disarmament)
- Hamas rejected a disarmament plan in April 2026 (BBC, 14 Apr), and an impasse followed (JPost, 4 May). — [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cn4vvxxg8lgo); [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-895007)
- **Roadmap of 30 July 2026** ([Board of Peace](https://boardofpeace.org/gaza-roadmap)):
  - All parties reaffirm the Trump plan and UNSCR 2803.
  - A timetable is due within 14 days of approval, and each phase is gated on verification by an International Verification Committee (IVC).
  - Factions transfer civil and security functions to the NCAG.
  - "One Authority, One Law, One Weapon": heavy weapons, production sites, depots and tunnels are decommissioned under the NCAG with ISF support, linked to Israeli withdrawal. "No weapons shall be transferred or handed to Israel or non-Palestinian parties," and the end state is "only NCAG shall hold, store or control weapons in Gaza."
  - The ISF separates forces, does not police, and trains police.
  - Phased withdrawal comes with "a commitment not to force anyone to leave the Strip."
  - The NCAG may assess legitimate obligations up to $400 million over three years.
  - Implementation creates conditions for "a credible pathway towards achieving Palestinian self-determination and statehood."
  - The PA is not named.
- **INSS on the roadmap (Udi Dekel, 3 Aug 2026).**
  - Trump called it a "historic agreement." Disarmament is expected to take 6–8 months, with Israeli withdrawal reciprocal by stage, a buyback, amnesty, and limits on targeted killings.
  - INSS sees Hamas's acceptance as "a calculated strategy of delay." It estimates Hamas at about 30,000 armed operatives, rebuilding while regional attention was on Iran.
  - Israel controls about 65% of Gaza. Egypt had blocked about 5,000 vetted police candidates from training on its soil.
  - INSS recommends Israel "express support in principle" and let the NCAG and ISF enter first between the Yellow and Orange Lines.
  — [INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/hamas-disarmament-agreement/)
- **Positions.**
  - Hamas negotiator Ghazi Hamad called the roadmap "difficult and painful" and said disarmament would not start until Israel halts operations, increases aid and withdraws. A Hamas official said "not a single bullet will leave the Gaza Strip."
  - An Israeli political source said there would be "no withdrawal whatsoever" without genuine disarmament.
  - Axios (16–17 Aug 2026) reported that Hamas told Kushner it would demilitarize, and that Kushner urged Netanyahu to test it.
  — [France 24, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260731-hamas-agrees-to-disarm-under-trump-plan); [FDD LWJ](https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2026/07/board-of-peace-announces-hamas-disarmament-agreement-idf-reports-32-gaza-ceasefire-violations-july-10-31.php); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/16/kushner-hamas-gaza-disarmament-egypt-israel); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/17/kushner-netanyahu-hamas-gaza)

### Inferences
- The 20-point plan and UNSCR 2803 created an interim state that the game designer can model as "international trusteeship-lite": nominal international and technocratic governance, with real control split between the IDF (about 58–70% of territory) and Hamas (the rest). The key state variables are:
  1. disarmament stage, verified by the IVC
  2. IDF line position
  3. NCAG physical presence in Gaza
  4. ISF troop numbers
  5. donor funds actually disbursed (versus pledged)
  6. casualty rate
- The "Yellow Line" de facto partition is the current equilibrium. Both main parties' revealed preferences (J Street) suggest it is sticky. It resembles the pre-2023 "management" model, now with Israeli territorial occupation of most of Gaza.
- The statehood "pathway" across the 2020 plan, the 2025 plan, UNSCR 2803 and the July 2026 roadmap is consistently conditional and undated, with no named arbiter of "readiness" in the 2025–26 texts. By contrast, Israel and the US are the named arbiters in the 2020 plan. That ambiguity is a modellable veto point.
- Hard deadlines include the Board's UN mandate (31 Dec 2027) and the end of Trump's term (Jan 2029). Absent renewal, these are natural "phase-change" triggers in a game timeline.

### Inferences
- **Spectrum placement** (from least to most change in sovereignty, for the report writer):
  1. Status quo/"mowing the grass" and the pre-Oct 7 Concept: no change.
  2. Economic peace: welfare change, no sovereignty change.
  3. "Shrinking the conflict": autonomy expansion, no state.
  4. Regional normalization without a Palestinian component (2020 Accords): external change only.
  5. Gaza trusteeship-lite under UNSCR 2803 with a conditional, undated pathway: interim international governance.
  6. Trump 2020 "Vision": a conditional demilitarized state on about 70% of the West Bank with Israeli overriding security.
  7. Saudi-conditioned normalization requiring an "irreversible pathway" or 1967 borders: closest to classic two states.
- **Game-design levers.**
  - Israeli government composition, as of Nov 2026 a hung Knesset with a possible Eisenkot minority government relying on Arab support. This affects Saudi normalization (sources tie it to Netanyahu's exit) and Gaza withdrawal (INSS notes withdrawal was politically hard before the elections).
  - Palestinian leadership legitimacy: Hamas and Fatah are both declining, "none of the above" is at 33%, and elections are postponed.
  - Donor disbursement versus pledges (Board of Peace: $17 billion pledged against an initially empty World Bank account).
  - ISF troop delivery (20,000 pledged; roughly 200–500 approved or present).
  - The Iran conflict, which diverts attention and resources.
- Rejection is the modal Palestinian response to plans drafted without Palestinian participation (2019 workshop boycott; 94% rejection in 2020; 24% trust in the Board in 2026). Arab-state endorsement (2025) did not translate into Palestinian public trust.
