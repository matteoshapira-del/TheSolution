## 1. Historical binationalism: Brit Shalom and Ihud (1925–1948), the 1939 White Paper's single-state vision, the Arab unitary-state position, and the 1947 UNSCOP minority (federal) plan

### Takeaway
Binationalism began as a small Jewish intellectual current: Brit Shalom from 1925, then Ihud from 1942, led by Magnes and Buber. It sought one state shared by Jews and Arabs on the principle of political parity, whatever their relative numbers. In the same period, Britain's 1939 White Paper envisaged one independent Palestine state with a permanent Arab majority, Arab Palestinian leaders demanded a unitary Arab-majority state, and the UNSCOP minority (India, Iran, Yugoslavia) proposed a federal state. All were overtaken by the UN partition resolution of 29 November 1947 and the war of 1948.

### Cited Findings
**Brit Shalom (1925 to the early or mid 1930s)**
- Brit Shalom was founded in 1925 by Jewish Zionist intellectuals. Founders and supporters included Arthur Ruppin, Martin Buber, Hugo Bergmann, Hans Kohn, Gershom Scholem, Henrietta Szold and Israel Jacob Kligler. Albert Einstein voiced support. Judah L. Magnes helped write the programme but never formally joined. It advocated a binational state in which Jews and Arabs would have "equal rights", and it published the monthly *Sheifoteinu* and ran Arabic courses for Jews — [Wikipedia: Brit Shalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Shalom_(political_organization))
- It became "quite unpopular" after the 1929 riots. The Wikipedia article says it "disintegrated by mid 1936". The Jewish Virtual Library (Encyclopaedia Judaica) dates it 1925–1933 and calls it "the first body to advocate bi-nationalism" — [Wikipedia: Brit Shalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Shalom_(political_organization)); [Jewish Virtual Library: Bi-nationalism](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism)
- The historian Joel Beinin notes that Brit Shalom never had more than a few hundred adherents and no social or political base — [Beinin, "Intellectuals, Socialist Capitalists and Binationalism in Mandate Palestine" (BJPA)](https://www.bjpa.org/content/upload/bjpa/bein/Beinin%20Intellectuals%20Socialist%20Capitalists%20and%20Binationalism%20in%20Mandate%20Palestine.pdf)
- Core binationalist idea, as the Encyclopaedia Judaica entry puts it: since both peoples claimed the land, it "should become the state of both", with relations based on equality regardless of numbers. Variants ranged from federal or confederal schemes to community-based (non-territorial) arrangements that avoided partition — [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism)

**Hashomer Hatzair**
- The Marxist-Zionist Hashomer Hatzair movement adopted binationalism in 1929. It submitted "The Case for a Bi-National State" to the Anglo-American Committee of Inquiry in 1946. Mordekhai Bentov and Aharon Cohen kept supporting the idea until after 1948 — [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism)

**Ihud (1942)**
- Ihud was formed in August 1942 within the League for Jewish-Arab Rapprochement and Cooperation. Members included Magnes, Buber, Haim Margolis-Kalvaryski, Moshe Smilansky, Henrietta Szold and Justice Joseph Moshe Valero. It rejected an independent Jewish state as "ruinous" — [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism)
- Ihud was a binational response to the 1942 Biltmore programme, which demanded a Jewish commonwealth. Its goal was "political parity", with Jewish immigration regulated by the parity principle, joint organs of government, and districts drawn on a communal basis — [Wikipedia: Ihud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihud); [Encyclopedia.com: Binationalism](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/binationalism)
- Magnes, Buber and Smilansky testified for Ihud before the Anglo-American Committee in March 1946. Ihud and the League also appeared before UNSCOP in 1947. Committee members were "impressed", but neither body convinced them that a binational solution was feasible. The partition resolution of 29 November 1947 "effectively ended" binationalist activity — [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism)
- After May 1948, Magnes reluctantly abandoned binationalism and advocated confederation. Hannah Arendt regarded Magnes's binationalism as an erroneous version of federalism — [Wikipedia: Ihud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihud)
- Ihud revived in the early 1950s, publishing *Ner* until 1964, and then turned to dialogue and to defending the civil rights of Israel's Arab minority — [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism)

**The 1939 White Paper (British government)**
- The White Paper declared that "it is not part of their policy that Palestine should become a Jewish State". It envisaged "the establishment within 10 years of an independent Palestine State" in treaty relations with the UK, "in which Arabs and Jews share government in such a way as to ensure that the essential interests of each community are safeguarded" — [Avalon Project: British White Paper of 1939](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brwh1939.asp)
- Immigration: about 75,000 Jewish immigrants over five years (10,000 a year plus 25,000 refugees), to "bring the Jewish population up to approximately one third of the total population". After that, no further immigration "unless the Arabs of Palestine are prepared to acquiesce in it". The High Commissioner would get "general powers to prohibit and regulate transfers of land" — [Avalon Project](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brwh1939.asp)

**The Arab unitary-state position (1946–47)**
- At the London Conference (opened 9 September 1946 with Arab state delegations only), the Arab side proposed a single unitary Palestinian state with a permanent Arab majority. It would become independent after a short transition of two to three years and have a democratic constitution and an elected legislature. Jews who had acquired Palestinian citizenship would have full civil rights, and naturalisation would require ten years' residence. The British proposal was rejected by the Jewish Agency, the Palestine Arab Higher Executive and the Arab states — [Wikipedia: London Conference of 1946–1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Conference_of_1946%E2%80%931947); [Hansard, HC Deb 18 Feb 1947](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1947/feb/18/palestine-conference-government-policy)
- The Palestinian Arab leadership "refused to testify" before UNSCOP. UNSCOP met Arab state representatives in Lebanon instead — [Encyclopedia.com: UNSCOP](https://encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/united-nations-special-committee-palestine-1947-unscop)
- A search-result excerpt from an encyclopedia entry says no UNSCOP member endorsed the unitary Arab state recommended by the Arab Higher Committee (AHC) — [Wikipedia (mirror): UNSCOP](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/UNSCOP). Note: the AHC position (a unitary Arab state) and the UNSCOP minority plan (federal) are different proposals and are often conflated.

**The UNSCOP minority (federal) plan (31 August 1947)**
- Proposed by India, Iran and Yugoslavia: an independent federal state after a transition of no more than three years under an authority designated by the General Assembly, with Arab and Jewish constituent regions and Jerusalem as the federal capital. The majority instead proposed partition with economic union and Jerusalem as a *corpus separatum* — [IsraelEd: UNSCOP report](https://israeled.org/unscop-report/); [Encyclopedia.com: UNSCOP](https://encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/united-nations-special-committee-palestine-1947-unscop); [FRUS 1947 vol. V, editorial note](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1947v05/d796)
- The General Assembly adopted Resolution 181 (based on the majority plan) on 29 November 1947 by 33–13–10. The Jewish Agency accepted it and the AHC rejected it — [Encyclopedia.com: UNSCOP](https://encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/united-nations-special-committee-palestine-1947-unscop); [IsraelEd](https://israeled.org/unscop-report/)

**Demography of the period**
- In 1948, 957,000 Palestinians were displaced, out of 1.4 million living in about 1,300 villages and towns (PCBS figures, May 2025) — [WAFA/PCBS, 12 May 2025](https://english.wafa.ps/Pages/Details/157272)
- More than 700,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled in 1948 (HRW) — [HRW, *A Threshold Crossed*, 2021](https://www.hrw.org/report/2021/04/27/threshold-crossed/israeli-authorities-and-crimes-apartheid-and-persecution)
- Note: the 1948 displacement figures differ by source.

### Inferences
- **Two historical one-state traditions:**
  - *Parity binationalism* (Brit Shalom/Ihud): two collectives with equal political weight whatever their numbers, and capped or negotiated Jewish immigration.
  - *Majoritarian unitary statehood* (1939 White Paper, the Arab 1946 plan): one state with a permanent Arab majority, minority civil rights for Jews, and immigration halted or subject to Arab consent.
  - The federal minority plan sat between these. Later one-state proposals inherit one or the other logic: ODSC and Judt lean "civic-plus-collective-rights", while the PLO 1968 Charter and Hamas lean majoritarian or exclusionary.
- **Failure pattern for a game model:** binationalism lacked a mass constituency on either side (a few hundred adherents, rejected by the Biltmore mainstream and by the Arab leadership) and was overtaken by partition and war. A designer could model this as an endgame that is "admired by external arbiters, unfeasible without domestic coalitions".

### Gaps
- No independently sourced data were found on Arab interlocutors of Brit Shalom/Ihud or on Arab responses to them (e.g., the 1946 killing of Fawzi Darwish al-Husseini, who reportedly co-signed an agreement with Ihud). Search budget ran out.
- The exact institutional design of the UNSCOP minority plan (bicameral federal legislature, division of powers, citizenship, immigration during transition) could not be retrieved. Check UN doc A/364 (UNSCOP report, ch. VII).
- The Avalon page does not show the White Paper's issue date. It is commonly dated 17 May 1939 (Cmd. 6019), but this was not verified here.

## 2. One democratic (secular) state: the PLO slogan (1969–74), Said, Judt, Tilley, Abunimah, the ODSC (Haifa, 2018–), and diaspora positions

### Takeaway
The "democratic state" idea moved from a PLO/Fatah slogan of 1969–74 (a non-sectarian state replacing Israel) to an intellectual proposal of 1999–2006 (Said, Judt, Tilley, Abunimah) and then to an organised campaign, the ODSC, founded in 2018. The ODSC calls for one constitutional democracy between the river and the sea, full implementation of refugee return with property restitution or compensation, constitutionally protected collective (national, ethnic, religious) rights, and "decolonisation" of Zionist structures. It does not provide for Jewish national self-determination in a separate polity. Support in polls is a minority on both sides and has fallen since 2020.

### Cited Findings
**PLO/Fatah "democratic secular state" phase (1969–1974)**
- A 1997 *Palestine-Israel Journal* article describes a "secular democratic state phase" from 1969 to 1974. At the 1971 Palestine National Council (PNC), "at the behest of Fatah's leadership", a democratic state "became official policy". It is described as a "non-sectarian secular state in which all Jewish residents who had come to Palestine prior to 1947 would become citizens". The author calls it an "unrealistic concession" that "rejected the fact that Israel existed as a Jewish entity" — [Palestine-Israel Journal vol. 4 no. 2 (1997)](https://pij.org/articles/482). Attribution of this specific PIJ article's author was unclear in retrieval.
- The same article says the 12th PNC (Cairo, 1974) called for a "national authority" in "any part of Palestine" liberated, and the 13th PNC (1977) called for a "national state" on "soil of the homeland". It reads these as steps toward acceptance of two states — [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/482)
- Historian Rashid Khalidi is quoted as saying the PLO shifted in 1969 toward a secular democratic state for Muslims, Christians and Jews, replacing Israel. A critic says the idea was first explicitly proposed in 1969 by the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine (DFLP) and "discreetly but effectively backed" by Fatah. Benny Morris argued the PNC never amended the National Charter to adopt the "secular democratic state" goal — [AIJAC, "The Myth of a Secular Palestine" (search excerpt; page blocked to fetch)](https://aijac.org.au/australia-israel-review/the-myth-of-a-secular-palestine/)
- Discrepancy: the 1968 Charter's Article 6 recognises as Palestinians only "Jews who had normally resided in Palestine until the beginning of the Zionist invasion". The 1971 formulation reportedly extended citizenship to Jews who arrived before 1947 — [Avalon: PLO Charter 1968](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/plocov.asp); [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/482)

**Edward Said (NYT Magazine, 10 January 1999, "The One-State Solution")**
- The essay could not be fetched — [NYT URL](https://www.nytimes.com/1999/01/10/magazine/the-one-state-solution.html)
- Per secondary citation, Said wrote in 1999 that "After 50 years of Israeli history, classic Zionism has provided no solution to the Palestinian presence". He saw no other way than to speak about "sharing the land that has thrust us together", democratically and with equal rights for each citizen — [Wikipedia: One-state solution (citing Said, Al-Ahram Weekly 1999)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)
- In a 2000 *Haaretz* interview, Said said the fate of Jews in a binational state "worries me a great deal" — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)

**Tony Judt ("Israel: The Alternative", NYRB, 23 October 2003)**
- "Israel, in short, is an anachronism." Judt proposed "a single, integrated, binational state of Jews and Arabs, Israelis and Palestinians" as the alternative to an ethnically cleansed Greater Israel, and wrote that "Israel today is bad for the Jews" — [NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2003/10/23/israel-the-alternative/)
- He argued that settlements had probably already doomed partition, that Arabs would soon be a majority between the river and the sea (forcing a choice between a Jewish state and a democracy), and that strong American leadership would be needed — [NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2003/10/23/israel-the-alternative/)

**Virginia Tilley**
- Tilley published "The One-State Solution" in the *London Review of Books* (late 2003) and a book of the same title (2005). She argued that settlements had made two states impossible and that the international community should accept one state as the de facto reality. Tilley and Judt both acknowledged that a unitary democratic state was currently utopian — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)

**Ali Abunimah (*One Country: A Bold Proposal to End the Israeli-Palestinian Impasse*, 2006)**
- The book revives the idea of one state shared by two peoples. It draws on South Africa and, to a lesser extent, Northern Ireland as reconciliation precedents, and seeks a single democratic state on equal citizenship that addresses "the legitimate concerns" of Jewish Israelis. Abunimah co-founded and directs The Electronic Intifada (2001) and wrote *The Battle for Justice in Palestine* (Haymarket, 2014) — [Wikipedia: Ali Abunimah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abunimah)

**The One Democratic State Campaign (ODSC), Haifa, 2018–**
- The ODSC was launched in 2018 by activists in Israel and the Palestinian territories and is based in Haifa — [InfluenceWatch (critical profile)](https://www.influencewatch.org/organization/one-democratic-state-campaign-odsc/); [Mondoweiss](https://mondoweiss.net/2018/08/political-democratic-palestine). It describes itself as "a Palestinian-led initiative" — [ODSC Q&A](https://onestatecampaign.org/en/qa/)
- Political programme (published 15 August 2018), ten points — [Mondoweiss: "The political program of the Campaign for One Democratic State in Historic Palestine"](https://mondoweiss.net/2018/08/political-democratic-palestine):
  1. One constitutional democracy over the whole area "between the Mediterranean Sea and the Jordan River", belonging to all its citizens.
  2. Equal citizenship, with naturalisation open to those who wish to immigrate.
  3. Full implementation of return for refugees and their descendants, with help to rebuild and reintegrate. The state will "restore to the refugees their private and communal property and/or compensate them".
  4. No discriminatory laws; freedom of movement and residence anywhere; civil marriage alongside religious marriage.
  5. Constitutional protection of national, ethnic, religious, class and gender associations. "No group or collectivity will have any privileges"; "all languages, arts and cultures can flourish and develop freely".
  6. Shared civil institutions.
  7. "A creative and long-term redistributive economic policy".
  8. International law, ratification of human-rights treaties, and "collective security" under the UN Charter.
  9. Regional alliance with progressive Arab forces.
  10. A place within global anti-colonial movements.
- Website framing: "decolonisation", meaning dismantling "Zionist structures of control and domination". Settlements "are illegal and violate basic Palestinian human and national rights", and "their precise fate will be decided by the '67' refugees and the current inhabitants of the occupied West Bank". Transitional justice is "a constitutional measure that will (re)address and correct existing inequalities". The strategy is to "collapse Israel's apartheid regime and force Israelis to accept a transition to a democratic state" through "a tripartite alliance" of the Palestinian movement, "anti-Zionist Jews" and international solidarity. Two-state and confederation variants are rejected: "Both are simply variations of the two-state solution" — [ODSC Q&A](https://onestatecampaign.org/en/qa/); [ODSC home](https://onestatecampaign.org/en/)
- What the programme leaves unspecified: how to adjudicate refugee claims to property now occupied by Israeli Jews; Jerusalem; the army and security forces; any Jewish national self-determination beyond "collective rights" — [ODSC Q&A](https://onestatecampaign.org/en/qa/); [Mondoweiss](https://mondoweiss.net/2018/08/political-democratic-palestine)
- Related but distinct documents:
  - The 2007 Haifa Declaration (by Palestinian citizens of Israel) called for return and a just two-state solution, not one state — [Wikipedia: Haifa Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa_Declaration)
  - Adalah's 2007 "Democratic Constitution" for Israel recognises refugee return based on UNGA Resolution 194 — [Adalah, Democratic Constitution (PDF)](https://www.adalah.ORG/Public/files/democratic_constitution-english.pdf)

**Diaspora positions**
- Jewish Voice for Peace (2007 position paper) "does not advocate for a one- or two-state solution at this time". It said "the people living in Israel-Palestine are the ones who must decide on their own political formations" and backed any outcome respecting the national rights of both peoples. At the time it judged one state to lack a viable plan with majority support and to risk sectarian violence — [JVP, "One State or Two?" (2007)](http://www.jewishvoiceforpeace.org/jewish-voice-for-peace-on-one-state-or-two)
- The ADL (a critic) reports that JVP adopted an explicitly anti-Zionist position by 2019, and that members voted in July 2025 to restructure the organisation, a process completed by early 2026 — [ADL profile](https://www.adl.org/education/resources/profiles/jewish-voice-for-peace)
- New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Israel should exist with equal rights for all, under either a one-state or a two-state solution — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)

**Critiques**

*Zionist and Israeli critiques*
- Benny Morris argues one state is not viable because of Arab unwillingness to accept a Jewish national presence, and that a binational state would be authoritarian, with a persecuted Jewish minority. He writes that "secular democratic Palestine" "objectively serves merely as camouflage for the goal of a Muslim Arab–dominated polity to replace Israel" — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)
- Shlomo Ben-Ami called it "ivory tower nonsense", a "South Africa situation without a South Africa solution". Amos Elon said the result would more likely resemble Zimbabwe than post-apartheid South Africa. Gershom Gorenberg predicted economic collapse as Israeli Jewish high-tech workers emigrated — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)

*Liberal and pro-two-state critiques*
- Hussein Ibish warned of protracted civil war. Shaul Arieli and Shany Mor argued settlements are less entrenched than one-staters claim. Norman Finkelstein and Noam Chomsky argued one state is less realistic than dismantling some settlements — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)

*Palestinian-official usage*
- PA negotiators Ahmed Qurei (2004) and Saeb Erekat (2009) invoked a binational state as the fallback if talks failed, i.e. as leverage rather than a programme — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)

*Critiques of the ODSC*
- InfluenceWatch frames the campaign as a vehicle to dissolve Israel — [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/organization/one-democratic-state-campaign-odsc/)

### Inferences
- **Endgame description for a game model:** "One Democratic State (ODS)": a single citizenship and parliament; collective cultural rights instead of national sovereignty for either people; full refugee return and restitution; settlements' fate decided by the 1967 refugees and current inhabitants; economic redistribution. Its preconditions in ODSC's own theory are the "collapse" of the current regime under external pressure and a cross-national alliance. Neither exists today.
- **Constituency is small and soft:** single-digit to low-teen first-choice support among Palestinians (2025–26); 14% among Israeli Jews (2024), with high overlap with two-state supporters. The ODS position draws its strongest relative support from Palestinian citizens of Israel (44–56%).
- **Key fault lines a designer could parameterise:**
  1. Whether Jewish Israelis keep collective national (not merely cultural) rights.
  2. The scale and mechanics of return and property restitution.
  3. Control and integration of the security forces.
  4. Whether the transition is negotiated or imposed.

## 3. The "one-state reality" analysis (Lustick 2019; Barnett, Brown, Lynch & Telhami 2023) and the apartheid framing (B'Tselem 2021, HRW 2021, Amnesty 2022, ICJ 2024)

### Takeaway
This school is descriptive, not prescriptive. It argues that a single regime already governs everyone between the river and the sea under different legal tiers, so the operative question is equality within that regime rather than partition. B'Tselem, HRW and Amnesty applied the legal label "apartheid", each with a different scope. The ICJ's July 2024 advisory opinion found a breach of CERD Article 3 (racial segregation and apartheid) without saying which. Israel's governments have rejected the apartheid framing as false and antisemitic. The US State Department rejected the HRW and Amnesty conclusions.

### Cited Findings
**Ian Lustick, *Paradigm Lost: From Two-State Solution to One-State Reality* (University of Pennsylvania Press, 2019)**
- Lustick, a former two-state advocate, argues that the two-state paradigm has failed and can no longer come out of negotiations, and that a one-state reality already exists between the river and the sea. He focuses on "what is" rather than "what should be", blames chiefly Israel's overwhelming power and the Israel lobby's influence on US policy, and draws on the history of paradigm collapse in science — [Wikipedia: Paradigm Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm_Lost)
- Reception:
  - Lisa Anderson in *Foreign Affairs* called the argument compelling: whatever promise it once held, the two-state solution "is now dead". She added that it is hard to know what should replace it — [Foreign Affairs review](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/node/1126841)
  - Jeffrey Herf (*Middle East Quarterly*) rejected the book, arguing that Palestinians, not Israelis, rejected prior state offers — [MEQ review](https://www.meforum.org/middle-east-quarterly/book-reviews/paradigm-lost-from-two-state-solution-to-one)
  - Seth Anziska's review doubts Lustick's reading of Israeli right-wing one-statism and says that, while not explicitly advocating one state, Lustick "comes pretty close" — [Anziska review (UCL)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10162806/1/Anziska_Review%20of%20Lustick%20Paradigm%20Lost%20Journal%20of%20Modern%20Jewish%20Studies%20Anziska.pdf)
  - An INSS review by Pnina Sharvit Baruch is also available — [INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Adkan24.4Eng_130-133.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Not an endgame but a "default state":** in game terms, the one-state reality is the status-quo attractor that other endgames must beat. It has four exits:
  1. Toward equality (ODS or Israeli-right citizenship).
  2. Toward formal annexation without rights (file 01).
  3. Back toward partition.
  4. Toward expulsion or "maximalist" outcomes.
- **The legal framing raises costs without fixing an endgame:** the apartheid/CERD findings create external pressure (sanctions, litigation, delegitimisation) whose remedy is equality or ending the occupation. They do not specify one state over two. This makes them a pressure variable rather than a terminal state.
- **Differences in scope matter for modelling:**
  - B'Tselem: whole river-to-sea regime.
  - HRW: apartheid finding for the OPT.
  - Amnesty: Israel, the OPT and refugees.
  - ICJ: West Bank/East Jerusalem separation, under CERD Article 3.

- No 2025–26 Israeli government statements specifically rebutting the apartheid charge were retrieved. Nor were later reports (e.g., B'Tselem's 2025 genocide report or the 2025 UN Commission of Inquiry findings), because the search budget ran out.

## 4. Israeli-right one state with citizenship (Elitzur, Hotovely, Rivlin, Arens): a contrast to annexation without rights

### Takeaway
A minority strand of the Israeli right, prominent around 2008–2014, proposed annexing the West Bank, not Gaza, with gradual Israeli citizenship for Palestinians. Its premise was that a Jewish majority would survive, based on contested low Palestinian population estimates. It differs from Palestinian one-state models on every core issue: a Jewish nation-state stays constitutionally entrenched, there is no refugee return, Gaza is excluded and settlements stay. Israeli Jewish opinion has since moved toward annexation *without* equal rights (42% in 2024).

### Cited Findings
**Main proponents**
- Uri Elitzur (former Yesha Council head and Netanyahu's chief of staff in the late 1990s) wrote in the settler journal *Nekuda* in 2008, calling for annexation and a gradual process toward citizenship and rejecting "half-measures" such as autonomy or Jordanian arrangements — [+972, Noam Sheizaf, 12 May 2014](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/); [Haaretz piece described on +972](https://www.972mag.com/the-israeli-right-going-one-state-my-haaretz-piece/)
- Likud MK Tzipi Hotovely held a Knesset conference, "Alternatives to Two States", in May 2009 and later called for granting citizenship to Palestinians "in gradual fashion" — [+972 (Sheizaf)](https://www.972mag.com/the-israeli-right-going-one-state-my-haaretz-piece/). She has also argued that Jordan was originally meant to be the Arab state and called for annexing the West Bank — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)
- Knesset Speaker Reuven Rivlin (Likud), April 2010, as reported by Haaretz: "I would rather see Palestinians as citizens of this State than partition the land." — [+972, 30 Apr 2010](https://www.972mag.com/knesset-speaker-goes-one-state/). Rivlin also floated a dual-parliament model, which the +972 author likens to a confederation — [+972 (2014)](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/)
- As president in 2017, Rivlin was reported as backing sovereignty with equal rights. This rests on the headline only; the article was not retrievable — [Forward](https://forward.com/fastforward/362943/israels-president-backs-one-state-solution-with-equal-rights-forpalestinia/)
- Others: Moshe Arens and Uri Ariel supported versions of one state, typically excluding Gaza. Naftali Bennett proposed annexing only Area C (about 60% of the West Bank). Caroline Glick (*The Israeli Solution*, 2014) argued annexation would not erode the Jewish majority much, citing estimates that she said showed Palestinian population figures were inflated — [Wikipedia: One-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)

**Model terms as described by proponents interviewed in 2010 and 2014** — [+972 (2014)](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/)
- Gradual unilateral annexation over 5–25 years: Area C, then B, then A.
- Blue Israeli ID cards with full rights, subject to security vetting. Some proposed loyalty oaths.
- A Basic Law defining Israel as a Jewish state enacted first.
- No return of refugees.
- Gaza excluded, as a separate entity.
- The Knesset electoral system largely unchanged.
- Demographic premise: Yoram Ettinger's estimate of 1.5 million West Bank Palestinians, excluding East Jerusalem. The +972 author notes official figures are much higher.

**Critics within the right and centre**
- Dani Dayan, Adi Mintz and Danny Danon opposed Elitzur. Dayan proposed expanding personal freedoms without political rights, i.e. annexation without citizenship — [+972 (2014)](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/)
- Moshe Arens worried that Israel's treatment of its Arab citizens would prevent fair absorption of the new population — [+972 (2014)](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/)
- A *Jerusalem Report* piece called Elitzur's plan "delusional" — [Jerusalem Report/JPost](https://jpost.com/jerusalem-report/the-two-state-one-state-hora)

**Public opinion: annexation and equal rights**
- In Dec 2022, 37% of Israeli Jews supported "one non-democratic state ruled by Israel without equality for Palestinians", compared with 20% for one equal state. If Israel annexed the West Bank, 71% of Israeli Jews (and 66% of Palestinians) said Palestinians should *not* demand the vote. 78% of Palestinians said they would not take part in Knesset elections, while 58% of Israeli Jews expected they would — [PCPSR/TAU Jan 2023](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Press%20Release_Eng%20_Joint%20Poll%2024JAN2023.pdf)
- In July 2024, 42% of Israeli Jews supported "annexation of the West Bank without equal rights for Palestinians", the highest ever recorded in the Pulse and double the 21% for two states. 14% supported one equal state — [PCPSR/TAU Sept 2024](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- In 2024, 24% of Israeli Jews said Israel's own aim is to annex all the land and expel Palestinians, and 17% said it is to annex without full rights — [PCPSR/TAU Sept 2024](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Endgame description for a game model:** "Greater Israel with citizenship", a Jewish-nation-state constitution plus individual equality for West Bank Palestinians.
  - It is internally unstable: it depends on a Jewish majority, which the 2025 data put at about parity between the river and the sea, or a clear majority only if Gaza is excluded.
  - It would likely drift either toward ODS-type demands, as Palestinian voters use the franchise, or toward rights restrictions (file 01).
- **Thin and shrinking constituency:** the citizenship variant had prominent but few backers. Polling shows the right's base preferring the no-rights variant. A designer could model it as a "transitional branch" rather than a stable terminal state.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–26) statements from Hotovely (now ambassador to the UK) or other figures endorsing citizenship were found. The Forward 2017 Rivlin article and the JC article on Hotovely could not be retrieved.
- The Bennett-Zimmerman "American-Israel Demographic Research Group" estimates and DellaPergola's rebuttals were not retrieved.

## 5. Palestinian and Islamist maximalism: PLO Charter 1964/1968 and the 1996–98 revisions; Hamas 1988 and 2017; Palestinian Islamic Jihad; expulsion and emigration implications; post–7 October rhetoric

### Takeaway
Maximalist programmes envisage a single Arab/Palestinian (secular-national) or Islamic state over all of Mandate Palestine.
- **Who counts as a Jewish citizen:** citizenship for Jews is limited by date of arrival (PLO 1964/1968) or framed as life "under the wing of Islam" (Hamas 1988). The 2021 Hamas-sponsored "Promise of the Hereafter" conference discussed which Jews would be killed, prosecuted, allowed to leave or kept.
- **PLO revision:** the PLO voted in 1996 and 1998 to nullify the anti-Israel articles, though their legal status is still disputed.
- **Hamas 2017:** the document retains "from the river to the sea" and non-recognition while calling a state on the 1967 lines a "formula of national consensus".
- **Polling:** about a third of Palestinians support "one Palestinian state without equal rights for Jews" (2024). Support for Hamas and armed struggle fell in 2025–26.

### Cited Findings
**PLO Charter (militant/national movement document)**
- 1964 Article 7: "Jews of Palestinian origin are considered Palestinians…" The 1968 text narrowed this to those who had resided in Palestine "until the beginning of the Zionist invasion" — [Wikipedia: Palestinian National Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Covenant)
- 1968 Charter (PNC, 1–17 July 1968) — [Avalon: Palestinian National Charter 1968](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/plocov.asp):
  - Art. 2: "Palestine, with the boundaries it had during the British Mandate, is an indivisible territorial unit."
  - Art. 6: "The Jews who had normally resided in Palestine until the beginning of the Zionist invasion will be considered Palestinians."
  - Art. 9: "Armed struggle is the only way to liberate Palestine. This it is the overall strategy, not merely a tactical phase."
  - Art. 19: partition and Israel's establishment are "entirely illegal".
  - Art. 20: the Balfour Declaration and the Mandate are "null and void".
  - Art. 21: rejection of all alternatives to total liberation.
  - Art. 22: Zionism is "organically associated with international imperialism".
  - Art. 33: amendment requires a two-thirds majority of total PNC membership.
- Revisions:
  - The PNC voted in Gaza on 24 April 1996 (504–54–14) to abrogate provisions contradicting the September 1993 Arafat–Rabin letters and mandated a legal committee to redraft — [Wikipedia: Palestinian National Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Covenant); [UN UNISPAL](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-188599)
  - The White House welcomed it as revoking the sections calling for Israel's destruction — [Clinton White House archive, 24 Apr 1996](https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1996/04/1996-04-24-press-secretary-on-palestinian-covenant-amendment.html)
  - Arafat's letter of 22 January 1998 listed Articles 6–10, 15, 19–23 and 30 as nullified in full, and parts of Arts. 1–5, 11–14, 16–18, 25–27 and 29 as nullified insofar as they conflicted with peace with Israel — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Covenant)
  - On 14 December 1998 in Gaza, PNC and PLC members reaffirmed this by show of hands. Clinton thanked them for rejecting "fully, finally and forever" the passages calling for Israel's destruction — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Covenant); [CS Monitor, 10 Dec 1998](https://www.csmonitor.com/1998/1210/121098.intl.intl.1.html)
  - Disputes: critics cite the Article 33 two-thirds rule and the absence of a new text. PLO spokesman Marwan Kanafani said: "This is not an amendment. This is a license to start a new charter." A Fatah document called the Covenant "frozen, not annulled". The redraft "still remains uncompleted". Israel's Likud government formally accepted the abrogation after 1998 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Covenant)

**Hamas Charter (1988; militant group)** — [Avalon: Hamas Covenant 1988](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/hamas.asp)

- Art. 7 quotes the hadith: "The Day of Judgement will not come about until Moslems fight the Jews (killing the Jews)… when the Jew will hide behind stones and trees".

  - Palestinians on the PA/PLO's long-term aim: 26% chose "conquer the state of Israel and destroy much of the Jewish population" and 14% "regain control over the pre-1948 Palestine". 47% chose the moderate 1967-territory options. 55% of Gazans and 32% of West Bankers chose the hardline views.
  - Israeli Jews: 64% believe Palestinians aim to "conquer Israel and destroy much of the Jewish population", up from 40% in 2022.
  - 89% of Palestinians and 87% of Israeli Jews see themselves as rightful owners of all the land between the sea and the river.
  - Source: [PCPSR/TAU Sept 2024](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- Support for Hamas's 7 October decision: 72% (Dec 2023), 71% (Mar 2024), 67% (Jun 2024), 54% (Sep 2024), 50% (May 2025), 53% (Oct 2025; WB 59%, Gaza 44%). "Armed struggle" as the most effective means: 41% (Oct 2025) — [PCPSR Poll 96](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Poll%2096%20press%20release%20FINAL%20ENGLISH%2028%20Oct%202025.pdf)
- Aug 2026: support for armed struggle fell to 27% and negotiations rose to "a little less than half". 72% oppose Hamas disarming before full Israeli withdrawal from Gaza — [PCPSR Poll 97](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)
- Oct 2026 (7–11 Oct): Hamas party identification is 19%, down from 35% ten months earlier, and Fatah 22%. Legislative elections are set for 28 November 2026, with a "Joint Hamas-Dahlan List" polling 16–23% depending on the model. 74% oppose disarming Hamas before full withdrawal. Negotiations are preferred (42%) over armed struggle (33%), and 53% support two states on 1967 borders — [PCPSR October 2026 Election Poll](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)

### Inferences
- **Endgame description for a game model:** "Liberation / single Arab-Islamic state", in two variants.
  1. *Secular-national* (PLO 1968): Mandate borders, Arab identity, citizenship for "pre-invasion" Jews only.
  2. *Islamist* (Hamas 1988, PIJ): Islamic sovereignty, Jews and Christians tolerated under Islamic rule.
  - In both, many post-1917/1948 Jewish Israelis lose citizenship or face pressure to emigrate. That is the "Jewish departure" mechanism, documented textually in the PLO's 1968 Art. 6 and in the 2021 Hamas conference recommendations.
  - Required preconditions are military defeat of Israel or its collapse.
- **Hamas 2017 is a "phased" posture:** an interim 1967-line state is acceptable as a "consensus formula" while recognition and the river-to-sea claim are retained. A game could model this as a state-on-1967-lines interim branch that does not end the conflict.
- **Calibration for modelling:** about one-third of Palestinians (2024) support a Palestinian-dominated unequal single state. This is about the same as Israeli Jewish support for an Israeli-dominated unequal state (37–42%). Both "supremacist" one-state variants have larger constituencies than the equal one-state model on both sides.

### Gaps
- The search budget ran out before documenting specific "return to where they came from" or "go back to Poland/Europe" slogans and their sources, or Hamas leaders' 2023–26 statements rejecting two states (e.g., Mashal). Hamas's January 2024 "Our Narrative" document and its 2025–26 positions on the US ceasefire and disarmament plan were also not covered.
- No scholarly survey of emigration and expulsion themes in Palestinian discourse was retrieved (e.g., Morris, Matthias Küntzel, or Palestinian critics).
- The text of the 1964 Charter's Article 7 is quoted only partially. The PLO's own dating of "the beginning of the Zionist invasion" (often said to be 1917) was not verified.

## 6. Full implementation of the refugees' right of return (UNGA 194; UNRWA figures; Abu Sitta's feasibility studies) and its demographic implications

### Takeaway
Full return is a core term of the ODS programme, the 2017 Hamas document and the PLO's position, and Amnesty frames its denial as part of an apartheid system. Israel reads Resolution 194 as a non-binding recommendation, conditional on living "at peace". About 6 million people are registered with UNRWA. Salman Abu Sitta argues return is physically feasible because most depopulated village lands are sparsely settled. Arithmetically, adding refugees now outside the territory would produce a clear Palestinian majority between the river and the sea, which is why Zionist critics treat full return as equivalent to ending a Jewish-majority state.

### Cited Findings
**Resolution 194 and its interpretation**
- UNGA Resolution 194, para. 11 (11 December 1948; vote 35–15–8): "refugees wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so", with compensation for those choosing not to return — [Wikipedia: UNGA Resolution 194](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_194)
- Israeli interpretation: no obligation is created ("should be permitted"; conditional on living at peace), and any compensation is tied to a peace settlement. Palestinian representatives initially rejected 194, but the PLO later treated it as legal authority. Abbas calls it the basis of a "just solution", and Hanan Ashrawi calls return non-negotiable — [Wikipedia: Resolution 194](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_194)

**Registered refugees**
- UNRWA serves "over 6 million refugees across the five fields" — [EUAA COI Query Response, April 2026](https://www.euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2026-04/2026_04_EUAA_COI_Query_Response_Q16_Occupied_Palestinian_Territory_OPT_Jordan_Lebanon_Syria_UNRWA.pdf)
- More than 5.9 million are registered as refugees — [Wikipedia: UNRWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNRWA)
- A 2022 figure of 6.65 million "eligible persons" covers a broader category — [MOPAN](https://www.mopan.org/en/our-work/performance-evidence/unrwa.html)
- Jordan hosted 2.4 million registered refugees (about 40% of the total) as of October 2025. Gaza had about 2.4 million people before the 2023 war, of whom 1.6 million were refugees — [EUAA](https://www.euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2026-04/2026_04_EUAA_COI_Query_Response_Q16_Occupied_Palestinian_Territory_OPT_Jordan_Lebanon_Syria_UNRWA.pdf)
- HRW: descendants of the 1948 refugees number over 5.7 million — [HRW 2021](https://www.hrw.org/report/2021/04/27/threshold-crossed/israeli-authorities-and-crimes-apartheid-and-persecution)

**Proposals that include return**
- ODS programme: full return of refugees "and their descendants", with property restitution and/or compensation — [Mondoweiss/ODSC](https://mondoweiss.net/2018/08/political-democratic-palestine)
- Hamas 2017, Arts. 12–13 — [MEE](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/hamas-2017-document-full)
- Adalah constitution (based on 194) — [Adalah](https://www.adalah.ORG/Public/files/democratic_constitution-english.pdf)
- Amnesty describes the denial of return as a means of fragmenting Palestinians — [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/02/israels-system-of-apartheid/)

**Salman Abu Sitta's feasibility case (advocacy research; Palestine Land Society)**
- He argues return is physically workable and desirable for peace — [Abu Sitta, PRRN/McGill paper](https://prrn.mcgill.ca/research/papers/abusitta.htm)
- Using 1994 data, he divides Israel into zones — [Abu Sitta, "Is the Right of Return Feasible?" (PLS)](https://www.plands.org/en/books-reports/books/palestine-right-of-return/is-the-right-of-return-feasible):
  - **"Area A"**: 1,683 km² (8% of Israel), holding 68% of Jews.
  - **"Area B"**: 1,318 km² (7%), holding 10% of Jews. In his words, "78% of the Jews in Israel live in 15% of Israel".
  - **"Area C"**: 17,325 km², "essentially the land of Palestinian refugees", with only 154,000 rural Jews, at about 4% of the central density.
- Modelled return of refugees from Lebanon, then Gaza, would raise Area C density by 17% and then 32%, which he says leaves Jews above 50% in Area C in both scenarios. Proposed mechanisms: phased return (Lebanon first, then Gaza), return to original sites, possible voluntary relocation of some rural Jews, and mapping from the 1945–46 aerial survey and land records — [PLS](https://www.plands.org/en/books-reports/books/palestine-right-of-return/is-the-right-of-return-feasible)
- In a 2026 piece he says 246 village lands have no Jewish residents and 272 have fewer than 5,000 Jews — [Abu Sitta, "Right of Return" (2026)](https://www.plands.org/en/articles-speeches/articles/2026/right-of-return)
- A *Falastin* article citing work with Zochrot says 77% of 536 depopulated villages are not built over. It notes that "not built over" does not mean the land is unused — [Falastin (issuu)](https://issuu.com/paccusa/docs/vol_4_issue_3_final_draft_6614f8fdc2dcec/26)

**Maximalist variant**
- The 2021 Hamas conference envisaged a "law of return" with absorption centres near the Lebanese, Syrian and Jordanian borders and identity cards — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/hamas-organized-confab-in-2021-to-plot-administration-of-liberated-palestine/)

**Israeli policy trajectory**
- The Knesset's UNRWA ban took effect on 30 January 2025. UNRWA cut about 600 staff and salaries by 20% in January 2026 amid funding shortfalls — [EUAA](https://www.euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2026-04/2026_04_EUAA_COI_Query_Response_Q16_Occupied_Palestinian_Territory_OPT_Jordan_Lebanon_Syria_UNRWA.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Demographic arithmetic** (author's inference from the cited figures):
  - About 7.4 million Palestinians and about 7.4 million Israeli Jews were between the river and the sea in mid-2025 (section 7).
  - Many registered refugees already live there: about 1.6 million in Gaza before the war, plus West Bank refugees.
  - Return *into the territory* therefore adds mainly refugees from Jordan (2.4 million registered), Lebanon and Syria, plus non-registered diaspora. Of the about 15.2–15.5 million Palestinians worldwide, about 7.8 million live outside historic Palestine (PCBS).
  - If even the Jordan-registered population returned, the river-to-sea population would be roughly 9.8 million Palestinians versus about 7.4–7.8 million Jews/"Jews and others", a Palestinian majority of roughly 55–57%.
  - Return of those refugees into pre-1967 Israel specifically, as distinct from a future Palestinian state, is what turns Israel's Arab share of about 21% into parity or a majority.
- **For a game model:** "full return" is a separable, toggleable term.
  - Under ODS it is internal resettlement plus inflow.
  - Under two states it could be capped or directed to the Palestinian state (not covered here).
  - Under maximalism it is combined with departure or exclusion of Jews.
  - It is the single variable most strongly correlated with Israeli Jewish rejection.

### Gaps
- An official UNRWA December 2025 total from its Refugee Registration Information System (RRIS) was not retrieved; UNRWA's own site returned 403. Lebanon and Syria registered totals were not captured.
- No polling on refugees' actual intention to return was retrieved (e.g., the PSR 2003 refugee survey). Nor were critiques of the inheritance of refugee status (e.g., Einat Wilf and Adi Schwartz, *The War of Return*), Israeli-government counter-arguments about Jewish refugees from Arab countries, or the 1948–2026 property legal framework (Absentees' Property Law).

## 7. Cross-cutting data: who lives between the river and the sea, and how support for each endgame compares (for spectrum placement)

### Takeaway
By 2025, Palestinians and Jews between the river and the sea were roughly equal in number, at about 7.4 million each (PCBS, citing Israeli CBS). This parity underlies every one-state argument: binationalists and ODS supporters say equality is unavoidable, Israeli critics say a democratic single state means the end of a Jewish-majority state, and the Israeli-right citizenship model fails without excluding Gaza. Polls show the equal-rights one state is a minority option on both sides, below two states among Palestinians and below annexation-without-rights among Israeli Jews.

### Cited Findings

The West Bank held about 3.4 million and Gaza about 2.1 million, down about 10% on earlier 2025 estimates. There were 15.2 million Palestinians worldwide, about 7.8 million of them outside historic Palestine (6.5 million in Arab countries) — [WAFA/PCBS, 12 May 2025](https://english.wafa.ps/Pages/Details/157272)

### Inferences
- **Spectrum placement**, from the "most Palestinian-maximal" pole toward the centre:
  1. Islamist liberation state (Hamas 1988, PIJ).
  2. Secular-national Arab state with limited Jewish citizenship (PLO 1968).
  3. Full return plus ODS (one person, one vote; collective cultural rights; restitution).
  4. Liberal binational or one state with communal guarantees (Judt; Brit Shalom/Ihud parity).
  5. Federal single state (UNSCOP minority, 1947).
  6. Then, crossing to the Israeli side of the one-state cluster: Israeli-right one state with gradual citizenship (Elitzur, Hotovely, Rivlin) — Jewish constitution, no return, Gaza excluded.
  7. Beyond that, annexation without rights and transfer (file 01).
  - The "one-state reality" is not a point on the spectrum but the current baseline from which these branch.
- **Suggested state variables for a game:**
  1. Demographic balance (Gaza included or excluded; return toggle).
  2. Franchise (none, gradual, full).
  3. Collective rights (none, cultural, parity, national).
  4. Refugee return (none, symbolic, capped, full).
  5. Settlements and property (retained, adjudicated, restituted).
  6. Security forces (Israeli, integrated, Palestinian).
  7. International legal pressure (ICJ/CERD/apartheid framing).
  8. Domestic constituency for each option (the poll shares above).
