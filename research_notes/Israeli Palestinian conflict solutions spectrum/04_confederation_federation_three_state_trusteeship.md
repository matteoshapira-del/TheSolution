# The "Hybrid Middle": Confederation, Federation/Cantons, Jordanian Confederation, Three-State Solutions, and International Trusteeship (dossier current to early November 2026)

Scope note: Five families of proposals sit between "two fully separate states" and "one state". Each is a separate key question below. A sixth section covers placement on the spectrum and modelling, mostly as inference. Every factual bullet carries a URL. Where only a secondary summary was available (for example a Wikipedia digest of a primary text), this is stated. Corrections to the brief: the A Land for All co-founder is **Meron Rapoport** (Wikipedia spells it "Rappaport"), not "Meron Rabinovich". The main Scheindlin–Waxman academic piece found is from **2016**, not 2022 (details in section 1).

## 1. Israeli–Palestinian confederation: A Land for All ("Two States, One Homeland"), the Beilin–Husseini Holy Land Confederation, and EU-style confederal ideas

### Takeaway
Confederal plans keep **two sovereign states, roughly on the 1967 lines**, but **separate citizenship from residency**. Citizens of one state, settlers included, may live in the other as permanent residents, with open or partly open borders, a shared or "partially open" Jerusalem and joint institutions. The most detailed text is the Beilin–Husseini Holy Land Confederation, 2nd edition, July 2025. Its key numbers: 2.5% land swaps, 105 settlements and about 194,000 settlers left under Palestinian sovereignty, and a reciprocal residency quota. A Land for All gained new political traction in 2025–26: Standing Together adopted its framework, and a party named after it launched in June 2026. Public support is still a minority view: in July 2024, 20% of Israelis (12% of Jews) and 35% of Palestinians backed confederation.

### Cited Findings

#### A Land for All (ALFA): origin, proponents, status
- Founded in **2012** under joint Israeli–Palestinian leadership. It grew out of meetings between Israeli journalist Meron Rapoport ("Rappaport" on Wikipedia) and Palestinian activist Awni al-Mashni ("Al-Mahshni"). Journalists, scholars and activists from both sides later joined to draft shared principles. It was previously called "Two States, One Homeland". — [Wikipedia: A Land for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization))
- Current co-CEOs are May Pundak (Israeli Jew) and Dr. Rula Hardal (Palestinian citizen of Israel). The group is a member of the Alliance for Middle East Peace (ALLMEP). — [Wikipedia: A Land for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization)); [ALLMEP members](https://www.allmep.org/about-us-allmep/members/)
- Won the "Outstanding Peace Support" award of the Luxembourg Peace Prize in 2021. — [Luxembourg Peace Prize](https://luxembourgpeaceprize.org/laureates/outstanding-peace-support/2021-a-land-for-all-two-states-one-homeland/)
- Rapoport set out the idea in a Guardian op-ed, "Could this be the idea that secures peace for Israelis and Palestinians?" — [Guardian op-ed (PDF copy)](https://www.b8ofhope.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Could-this-be-the-idea-that-secures-peace-for-Israelis-and-Palestinians-Meron-Rapoport-The-Guar.pdf)

#### ALFA core terms (as summarised on Wikipedia; the primary site could not be retrieved, see Gaps)
- **Borders and sovereignty:** one territorial unit (the "homeland") holding two sovereign democratic states on the pre-1967 Green Line, linked in a confederation with an open border. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization))
- **Political, not demographic, separation:** sovereignty is political, not territorial or demographic. The movement "insists that this separation be only political – not geographic nor demographic." It draws explicitly on the EU model of open borders and free movement. — [American Prospect, 2023](https://prospect.org/world/2023-11-17-israel-palestine-land-for-all/); [MyJewishLearning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/2020/11/25/a-land-for-all/)
- **Movement and residence:** all the land's people would have the right to visit, study, work and live as permanent residents anywhere in the shared homeland. — [MyJewishLearning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/2020/11/25/a-land-for-all/)
- **Citizenship vs residency / voting:** citizens of one state living in the other vote in the national elections of their state of citizenship. Settlers in Palestine stay Israeli citizens and vote in Israel. Settlers can become residents of Palestine if they accept Palestinian law, with local voting rights. Settlement expansion stops and any further relocation is phased. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization))
- **Refugees:** Palestine could grant refugees a right of return to its territory, with a gradual option to reside in Israel. Diaspora Jews keep the right to immigrate to Israel. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization))
- **Jerusalem:** an open, shared city and capital of both states. One of the earliest agreed principles was Jerusalem as "an open and shared city". Wikipedia's summary adds management involving international authorities and religious representatives. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization)); [search digest of ALFA principles via Wikipedia/MyJewishLearning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/2020/11/25/a-land-for-all/)
- **Joint institutions:** shared bodies for water, epidemics, tourism and finance. The two states decide jointly which matters to share. Disputes go to a joint Israeli–Palestinian assembly or a joint human rights court. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization))
- **Security:** each state is fully responsible inside its own territory with its own forces, and there is no Israeli military presence in Palestine. The plan provides for close cooperation on intelligence and policing, keeps border checks, and includes a demilitarisation commitment. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization))
- **Equality provisions inside Israel:** repeal of discriminatory laws, better land access and planning for Palestinian citizens of Israel, and recognition of Bedouin villages in the Negev. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization))

#### ALFA developments 2023–2026
- 2023: featured in New York Times coverage of new peace plans from younger activists. — [NYT, 16 Nov 2023](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/11/16/world/middleeast/israel-palestinians-new-peace-plans.html)
- 2025: presented at the UN High-Level Conference on the Question of Palestine and the Two-State Solution. Wikipedia cites remarks by Zeid Ra'ad Al Hussein of The Elders. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_(organization)); [The Elders](https://theelders.org/news/two-state-solution-needs-global-affirmation)
- **November 2025:** Standing Together, a mass Jewish–Arab movement in Israel, adopted the ALFA framework. — [Forward, Dec 2025](https://forward.com/opinion/787894/israel-apartheid-peace-movement/)
- **June 2026:** Standing Together launched a political party, **Makom Lekulanu** ("a place for us all"), whose name echoes ALFA's Hebrew name. — [Forward, July 2026](https://forward.com/opinion/835250/israeli-politics-standing-together-palestinian/)

#### Holy Land Confederation (HLC): Yossi Beilin and Hiba Husseini
- **Authors and process:** Yossi Beilin (former Israeli justice minister, Geneva Initiative co-founder) and Hiba Husseini (former legal adviser to Palestinian negotiators). The Tel Aviv-based Economic Cooperation Foundation (ECF) coordinated the work. It was presented to a senior US official and to UN Secretary-General Guterres on **11 Feb 2022**. — [IDN-InDepthNews, 16 Feb 2022](https://indepthnews.net/will-a-holy-land-confederation-help-resolve-the-long-simmering-palestinian-israeli-dispute/)
- **Editions:** an unofficial draft permanent-status proposal, developed 2020–2022. The original document came out at the end of 2021. A **2nd edition was completed in July 2025**, revising the chapters on reconciliation, security and Jerusalem and addressing October 7. — [ECF issue page](https://ecf.org.il/issues/issue/1541); [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Framing:** "cohabitation" of two sovereign states rather than a "divorce". The confederation is "a facilitator for the two-state solution", not a substitute for it. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf); [IDN](https://indepthnews.net/will-a-holy-land-confederation-help-resolve-the-long-simmering-palestinian-israeli-dispute/)
- **Borders:** Palestine covers the West Bank and Gaza with **land swaps of 2.5%**, equal in size and value, limited to "first-line" settlements as in the 2003 Geneva Accord. Israel annexes **21 West Bank settlements (247,044 Israelis)** and **8 East Jerusalem Jewish neighbourhoods (200,979 Israelis)**. **105 settlements (193,565 Israelis)** stay under Palestinian sovereignty. Israel would cede swap land in the Gaza envelope, the south-west Judean Desert and the Valley of Springs. A Palestinian-administered **West Bank–Gaza corridor** is included. Palestine would cover 22.5% of Mandatory Palestine (6,205 km²). — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
  - Conflicting description: NPR/AP coverage in Nov 2023 described the plan as involving no land swaps. — [KUNC/NPR, 8 Nov 2023](https://www.kunc.org/2023-11-08/2-negotiators-with-peace-plan-experience-have-a-plan-to-kickstart-peace-talks). The plan text itself specifies 2.5% swaps (PDF above).
- **Phasing:** after signature, a Palestinian state is created immediately on Areas A and B. The confederation is established at the end of a **30-month** implementation period. A timetable sets further "liberalisation" steps, including fully opening Jerusalem, which either state may defer. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Settlers / residency quota:** settlers in the 105 settlements may stay as **permanent residents of Palestine, keeping Israeli citizenship**, or relocate. No evacuation is required, though small settlements may be merged. In return, **an equal number of Palestinians may settle in Israel as permanent residents**. Palestinians keep the right to fill this quota later if parity is not reached. This is separate from, and additional to, refugee arrangements. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Residents' rights:** permanent residents have equal rights with citizens "in every possible respect, except for participation in national elections". They vote in their citizenship state's elections, with arrangements to vote at their place of residence, and may vote in local elections. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Refugees:** builds on Geneva Accord Article 7. Each refugee chooses a "Permanent Place of Residence": the Palestinian state, swapped areas, third countries, current host countries, or a **limited option in Israel "commensurate with the average number of refugees absorbed by other countries"**, at Israel's sovereign discretion, with priority for surviving 1948 refugees. Compensation runs through an international fund, host countries are remunerated, UNRWA's functions pass to host governments, and the refugee definition is aligned with UNHCR's. The plan cites about 5.5 million registered refugees. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Jerusalem:** two capitals, Yerushalayim and Al-Quds, with two municipalities. Palestine gets the Arab neighbourhoods, the Muslim and Christian Quarters and part of the Armenian Quarter. The **Old City (about 1 km²) becomes a "confederal and open entity"** with delegated authority and joint confederal security and police. Elsewhere there are regulated crossings at first; later the whole city comes under joint management with free flow of people, goods and capital. The plan explicitly rejects a Clinton-style "harsh division" of the Old City. A joint committee handles planning, holy places and natural resources. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Security:**
  - The confederation has **no army of its own**; each state keeps its forces, as in the EU.
  - **External threats:** Israel takes "the lead role" at first, with third-party involvement. Palestine accepts initial limits on weapons systems.
  - **International forces** are stationed in Palestine, with limited Israeli forces integrated into them. A joint Israeli–Palestinian–third-party force guards Palestine's borders with Jordan and Egypt. Israel monitors the crossings remotely, with no personnel on site.
  - **Air:** the Israeli Air Force covers air defence over the whole confederation, and a joint civilian air-traffic centre is set up.
  - **Counter-terrorism:** a joint Israeli–Palestinian force may operate inside Palestine in agreed emergencies for 3–5 years.
  - **Settlers' security:** passes gradually to Palestine over 3–5 years, with international monitoring of settlements. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Economy:** a phased replacement of the Paris Protocol customs union with a free-trade agreement, and a Confederal Economic and Social Committee. **No Palestinian currency at first**; the confederation uses a modified shekel that also reflects the Palestinian economy. Free movement of goods, workers, people and capital is phased in. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Institutions and exit:** each state keeps its own government and courts. Joint institutions may come "in time". Any transfer of authority requires constitutional legislation. Disputes go to negotiation, mediation or arbitration. Other states, such as Jordan, could join later. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)
- **Reception:** AP said the plan contains "several controversial proposals, and it's unclear if it has any support among leaders on either side". It has not been endorsed by the Israeli government or the PA. — [IDN](https://indepthnews.net/will-a-holy-land-confederation-help-resolve-the-long-simmering-palestinian-israeli-dispute/); [KUNC/NPR](https://www.kunc.org/2023-11-08/2-negotiators-with-peace-plan-experience-have-a-plan-to-kickstart-peace-talks)
- The authors continued presenting the plan in 2026, for example at "Peace is Possible" at Michigan State University, April 2026. — [MSU event](https://polisci.msu.edu/news-events/events/peace20260421.html)

#### EU-style confederal scholarship (Scheindlin, Waxman and others)
- Dov Waxman and Dahlia Scheindlin, in a Guardian op-ed and the fuller article "Confederalism: A Third Way for Israel–Palestine" (*The Washington Quarterly*, **2016**), proposed "two-state solution 2.0". It means two sovereign states with an open border, freedom of movement and residency, separation of citizenship from residency, and limited shared governance beyond security. — [Guardian piece via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/hope-fades-for-a-two-state-solution-is-there-another-path-to-middle-east-peace); [Mondoweiss, May 2016](https://mondoweiss.net/2016/05/proposal-confederated-without/); [Jewish Independent](https://thejewishindependent.com.au/panel-picks/confederalism-a-third-way-for-israel-palestine)
- Under their model, refugees who wish to return could live in Israel as residents but exercise full citizenship rights, such as national voting, in Palestine. Shared authorities would cover water, climate and public health. — [Guardian via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/hope-fades-for-a-two-state-solution-is-there-another-path-to-middle-east-peace)
- Scheindlin, "The confederation alternative for Israel and Palestine", The Century Foundation, 3 Feb 2020. She also wrote "An Israeli-Palestinian Confederation Can Work" in *Foreign Policy*. — [IFA catalogue record](https://discovery.ifa.de/vufind/Record/1690276711); [FP piece via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/an-israeli-palestinian-confederation-can-work)
- Mondoweiss criticised the 2016 proposal for offering no mechanism to make Israel accept it or change current policy. — [Mondoweiss](https://mondoweiss.net/2016/05/proposal-confederated-without/)
- Other venues: a Forward editorial essay, "It's time to talk seriously about a Confederation of Israel and Palestine" (Jodi Rudoren, 2022) — [Forward](https://forward.com/opinion/504687/its-time-to-talk-seriously-about-a-confederation-of-israel-and-palestine); a peer-reviewed analysis by Shemer-Kunz (2023) — [Frontiers in Political Science](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpos.2023.981237); Harvard PON event "An Israeli-Palestinian Confederation: Opportunities and Challenges" — [PON](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/events/confederation-negotiated-option); Chatham House event "Pathways to peace: exploring Israeli-Palestinian confederation" — [Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/events/all/research-event/pathways-peace-exploring-israeli-palestinian-confederation)
- A Columbia SIPA capstone sponsored by the CIA assessed a confederal model. It flagged the asymmetry between Israel, sovereign since 1948, and a never-sovereign Palestine. — [Columbia SIPA](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/reimagining-israelpalestine-assessing-confederal-future)
- Aslı Bâli's chapter "Stuck Together" (Cambridge UP volume on federalism in MENA) critically reviews confederal proposals. — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/federalism-and-decentralization-in-the-contemporary-middle-east-and-north-africa/stuck-together/E03080586ED32C3602E560E6A49EEC18)

#### Critiques of confederation
- Shira Efron and Evan Gottesman ("Is Israeli-Palestinian confederation all it's hyped up to be?", +972, 3 May 2021) raised these points. Wikipedia's reference list credits the piece to a different author, "Konrad". — [+972](https://www.972mag.com/confederation-israel-palestine-obstacles/)
  - **Asymmetry:** Palestine would be the junior partner. They quote a Palestinian view: "Under the confederacy Israel will keep all its power. We will continue being the weak side." They warn of East German-style decline in poorer Palestinian areas.
  - **Settlers:** leaving settlements in place raises Palestinian safety fears and legitimacy problems.
  - **Security:** Israel is unlikely to share control of external borders, given its resistance even to an international force in the West Bank.
  - **Residency:** non-citizen Palestinian residents in Israel raise Israeli security concerns and create unequal status.
  - **Institutions:** joint courts and bodies would pair a democracy with a non-democratic system.
  - **Public opinion:** a 2021 RAND study found "relatively low rates of acceptance".
  - **Feasibility:** confederation is "more ambitious" than either alternative.
- The Palestinian one-state/binational camp (Bashir Bashir, Leila Farsakh) argues for moving "beyond partition", the premise underlying the two-state model. Farsakh's *Rethinking Statehood in Palestine* (2021) and the Bashir–Farsakh co-edited *The Arab and Jewish Questions* (Columbia UP, 2020) take this line. No source found addresses confederation specifically (see Gaps). — [Harvard Crimson, 2010](https://thecrimson.com/article/2010/11/9/solution-state-bashir-farsakh); [Columbia UP listing](https://www.abbeys.com.au/book/the-arab-and-jewish-questions-geographies-of-engagement-in-palestine-and-beyond-9780231199209.do); [Wikipedia: Leila Farsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leila_Farsakh)

#### Public opinion on confederation (PSR / Tel Aviv University "Palestinian-Israeli Pulse")
- **Oct 2020:** confederation supported by 30% of Israelis and 29% of Palestinians. — [PSR/TAU joint poll press release, Oct 2020](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Press%20Release_Eng%20_Joint%20Poll%2026%20Oct%202020.pdf)
- **Jan 2023 release (Dec 2022 fieldwork):** Palestinian support at 22%, on a "mostly consistent downward trajectory from 2016". Israeli support 28%. Most of the five confederation components (free movement, citizenship/residency, Jerusalem, joint civic institutions and so on) failed to reach 30% among Israeli Jews or Palestinians. The exception: half of Israeli Jews backed joint civic institutions. — [TAU Pulse Jan 2023](https://resolution.tau.ac.il/pulse-jan-23); [Summary report PDF](https://resolution.m.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/resolution/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2024%20Jan%202023.pdf)
- **July 2024 (latest wave found):**
  - Confederation: **20% of all Israelis** (down from 29% in 2022), **12% of Israeli Jews**, **52% of Israeli Arabs**, and **35% of Palestinians** (up from 22%).
  - Two-state support: 40% of Palestinians (+7) and 21% of Israeli Jews, the lowest since comparable questions began in the early 1990s.
  - Partners: PSR (Ramallah) and TAU's International Program in Conflict Resolution and Mediation.
  - Method: Palestinian n=1,270 face-to-face (17–20 July 2024), margin of error ±3.5%; Israeli online panels, margin of error ±3.34%. — [PSR press release](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/989); [Summary report PDF](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- July 2024: 44% of Palestinians and 34% of Israeli Jews supported no solution at all. — [PSR Critical Policy Brief 3/2024](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/993)

### Inferences
- In confederation the key game variable is **decoupling residency from citizenship**. It lets settlers stay without annexation and lets some refugees live in Israel without becoming citizens, the two hardest trade-offs in classic partition. The HLC turns this into a **1:1 settler-for-Palestinian residency quota**, which a game could model as a tunable parameter.
- The HLC is **two states with confederal add-ons**, close to the Geneva Accord: hard sovereignty, Israeli-led external defence, and phased, reversible opening. ALFA is closer to an **open-border union**, with free residence for all, joint courts and assemblies, and no Israeli military presence in Palestine. On a spectrum, the HLC sits just "one-state-ward" of Geneva-style two states, and ALFA sits further toward one state.
- Polling shows an **ethnic divide inside Israel** (52% of Arab citizens vs 12% of Jews in 2024) and a **rise among Palestinians** after Oct 7 (22% to 35%). The model's constituency is therefore mainly Palestinian citizens of Israel and part of the Palestinian public, which matters for any "coalition-building" mechanic.
- The 2025–26 adoption by Standing Together and the founding of Makom Lekulanu are the first steps toward party-political representation for the confederal idea in Israel. No electoral results were found (see Gaps).

## 2. Federation and cantonisation: Swiss-style cantons, right-wing federal and "emirates" schemes, Belgian and Bosnian analogies, and "parallel states"

### Takeaway
"Federation" covers ideologically opposite designs:
- **Single sovereign, Israeli-led cantonal federations** (the Federation Movement's 30-canton plan; Kedar's "Palestinian Emirates" city-states; the 2025 Hebron sheikhs' "Emirate of Hebron"). These keep the IDF as the only army and keep settlements.
- **Shared-sovereignty designs** such as LeVine and Mossberg's "parallel states", where two states overlap across the whole land.

Belgium and Bosnia (Dayton) come up mainly as cautionary analogies. No major Israeli party has adopted a federal plan. Endorsements of the emirates idea are limited to Economy Minister Nir Barkat.

### Cited Findings

#### Federation Movement (30 Swiss-style cantons)
- Israel and the West Bank would be divided into **30 autonomous cantons** under a federal constitution guaranteeing rights. Co-chair **Emanuel Shahaf**, described by Ynet as a former Mossad official, argues the current situation "is already a federation, though an unfair and illogical one". — [Ynetnews analysis (Robert Swift)](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/b1qst0uru)
- Core terms per Ynet: **West Bank Arabs receive Israeli citizenship** and Israeli law applies. **Settlements remain**. The **IDF is the only military**. **Gaza stays outside**, likely as a separate state with an airport and port. Refugee concerns would be handled by negotiating citizenship in Arab host states. **No Israeli party has endorsed it.** Critics include the PA (expected rejection), parts of the right (fear of a Palestinian influx) and parts of the left (suspicion it legitimises settlements and could be imposed unilaterally). — [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/b1qst0uru)
- A 2019 report described the plan as 30 Swiss-style cantons, **20 Jewish-majority and 10 Arab-majority**, each sending elected representatives to a federal government in Jerusalem. — [Intermountain Jewish News, 23 Jul 2019](https://www.ijn.com/alternative-two-state-solution/)

#### Israeli right-wing "cantonal" and "emirates" schemes
- **Palestinian Emirates Plan / "Eight-State Solution"** (Mordechai Kedar, Bar-Ilan University, promoted since about 2012): Palestinian society would be broken into city-based emirates ruled by local clans, each with self-rule over a city and its surroundings, including Gaza, Jenin, Nablus and Ramallah. — [Wikipedia: Palestinian Emirates Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan); [Voz/JNS](https://voz.us/en/world/250707/26620/the-palestinian-emirates-plan-an-israeli-academic-s-proposal-as-an-alternative-to-the-two-state-solution.html)
- **"Emirate of Hebron" (July 2025):** five Hebron sheikhs led by Sheikh Wadee' al-Jaabari proposed that Hebron break away from the PA, form its own emirate and join the Abraham Accords. Kedar reportedly brought the sheikh to Economy Minister Nir Barkat. — [JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/the-revolutionary-plan-of-five-hebron-sheikhs); [IJN](https://www.ijn.com/palestinian-sheikhs-float-idea-emirate-instead-of-state/)
- Barkat publicly endorsed the emirates framework as a viable solution for Judea and Samaria. — [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/413472)
- Palestinian responses were largely negative. Hamas denounced the initiative as normalisation, and critics called it "Israel's New Partition Plan". — [NoonPost](https://www.noonpost.com/en/363682/); [Jerusalem Post](https://jpost.com/middle-east/article-901903)
- The Times of Israel noted that there is no Emirate of Hebron in practice. — [CIJR citing ToI](https://isranet.org/?p=36378)
- **Contrast case, not federal:** former US ambassador David Friedman's 2024 plan. It envisages Israeli sovereignty over all of Judea and Samaria, with Palestinians given permanent residency and travel documents but no Knesset vote, through an Israel–US–Abraham Accords agreement. — [JNS](https://www.jns.org/feature/future-of-judea-samaria-former-us-envoy-unveils-sovereignty-plan)

#### Belgian and Bosnian (Dayton) analogies
- Dayton is "the most frequently cited template for ending an intractable ethno-national war" and is "invoked, loosely and often" for Israel–Palestine. One analysis argues the analogy breaks down because Israel–Palestine is an occupation (one sovereign armed side, one stateless side). It adds that territorial-entity models may codify displacement. — [Palestine-Israel Journal](https://pij.org/articles/2372)
- Harvard's Robert Mnookin reports that Palestinian intellectuals proposed a single **binational federal state modelled on Belgium**, with language communities and autonomous regions. He notes that Israel plus the territories is roughly Belgium's size. — [Harvard PON](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/research_projects/meni/professor-mnookin-explores-the-ethnic-conflicts-between-israelis-and-palestinians-and-flemings-and-walloons/)

#### "Parallel states" (Mark LeVine and Mathias Mossberg, eds., *One Land, Two States*, UC Press, 2014)
- An edited volume by historian Mark LeVine (UC Irvine) and former Swedish diplomat Mathias Mossberg. It proposes **dividing sovereignty without dividing the land**: two states overlap across the whole territory, and each governs its own nationals. — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/978-0-520-27913-1); [UCI News, 30 Jul 2014](https://news.uci.edu/2014/07/30/uci-professor-edits-book-offering-solution-to-israeli-palestinian-conflict)
- Government functions would be divided "in layers": some joint, some separate, all "harmonized". The editors argue the peace process has failed and reject one state on the grounds that majorities on both sides oppose it. — [Æther (Air University) review](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Aether-ASOR/Book-Reviews/Article-Display/Article/1185218/one-land-two-states-israel-and-palestine-as-parallel-states/)
- The editors argued that a parallel-states structure "or some form thereof, may in fact be the only way to preserve a two-state solution". — [Tablet](https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/news/articles/defining-the-one-land-two-state-solution)
- Kirkus called it a visionary approach "so daring that it could actually work". Campus Watch (Middle East Forum) framed it critically as "provocative". — [Kirkus](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/mark-levine/one-land-two-states); [Campus Watch](https://www.meforum.org/campus-watch/mark-levine-provocative-uc-irvine-professor?p=95)

### Inferences
- For modelling, "federation" must be split into at least two endgames:
  - **(a) Israeli-sovereign cantonal federation:** one army (the IDF), settlements stay, Palestinians get local self-rule and in some versions Israeli citizenship. This sits near the one-state end, with a "Greater Israel with autonomy" flavour, except where full citizenship is granted.
  - **(b) Shared or parallel sovereignty:** two states with territorial overlap. This sits between confederation and binational one state.
- The emirates/Hebron line is a **fragmentation** model (many sub-state Palestinian units, no Palestinian state). It is functionally the opposite of the confederal models: it dissolves the PA rather than federating it.
- The Belgium and Bosnia analogies serve in the literature mainly to show the **costs** of ethnic federalism (paralysis, entrenched division), not as templates.

### Gaps
- No federal scheme by **Martin Sherman** was found. His published proposals, as recalled but not verified in this session, centre on funded emigration ("humanitarian paradigm"), not federation. No **Yoram Hazony** federal proposal was found either. Neither should be described as a federal proposal without further sourcing.
- The "Federation Plan / Sovereignty for the Jewish People and the Palestinian people within a federation" named in the brief was not found as a distinct document. The Federation Movement's 30-canton plan is the closest match located. Its founding date and full founder list were not established. The Ynet article is undated.
- A Yesha Council-era "autonomous cantons" concept was mentioned in search snippets, but no dated primary source was found.
- No polling on cantonal or emirates models was found.

## 3. Jordanian–Palestinian confederation

### Takeaway
Jordan–Palestine union ideas run from Jordan's 1950 annexation of the West Bank, through Hussein's 1972 "United Arab Kingdom" federation plan and the **11 February 1985 Hussein–Arafat (Amman) Accord** (self-determination "within a Jordanian-Palestinian confederation"), to the 1988 disengagement. They resurfaced in 2018, when Abbas said Trump's team had floated a confederation. Jordan's long-standing official line is that confederation is "not on the table" and could be considered **only after** an independent Palestinian state exists. The line is driven by fears of Jordan becoming the Palestinian "alternative homeland". Palestinian support was 29% in 2018.

### Cited Findings
- **Background:** Jordan annexed the West Bank and East Jerusalem in April 1950 and held them until June 1967. — [Wikipedia: King Hussein's federation plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein%27s_federation_plan)
- **1972 "United Arab Kingdom" plan (15 Mar 1972):** a federation of two federal districts, the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan and a Palestinian district in the West Bank, to take effect after peace with Israel and the West Bank's return. It was rejected by Israel, the PLO and most Arab states. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein%27s_federation_plan); [FRUS editorial note](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v23/d280)
- **Hussein–Arafat Amman Accord (11 Feb 1985):** called for Palestinian self-determination "within the framework of a Palestinian state and a Jordanian-Palestinian confederation". It also called for a joint Jordanian–Palestinian approach to negotiations and the "termination of the Israeli occupation". — [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/the-hussein-arafat-accord); [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/politics/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/jordanian-palestinian-accord); [JTA](https://www.jta.org/archive/hussein-and-arafat-reportedly-reach-accord-on-joint-framework-for-negotiations-with-israel)
  - Reactions: the PLO Executive Committee voiced "reservations". The US was cautious. Israeli PM Peres responded in a muted way, renewing his call for Hussein to negotiate directly. — [JTA](https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-continues-to-maintain-low-profile-on-hussein-arafat-accord); [MERIP](https://merip.org/?p=8564)
  - Collapse: Hussein suspended the effort in **February 1986**, citing the PLO's failure to accept UNSC Resolutions 242 and 338. The PLO Executive Committee abrogated the accord in Algiers on **22 April 1987**. — [Harvard Crimson, 20 Feb 1986](https://thecrimson.com/article/1986/2/20/hussein-quits-joint-plo-peace-effort); [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/politics/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/jordanian-palestinian-accord)
- **1988 disengagement (31 Jul 1988):** Hussein announced full legal and administrative disengagement from the West Bank. Jordan kept its custodianship of the Muslim holy sites in Jerusalem. — [Israel Education Center](https://israeled.org/jordan-out-of-west-bank/); [Speech text PDF](https://israeled.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/8-1988-7-July-31-Speech-by-Jordanian-King-Hussein-on-Jordan%E2%80%99s-Separation-from-West-Bank.pdf)
- **2018 revival:**
  - Abbas told Israeli peace activists in September 2018 that Jared Kushner and Jason Greenblatt had asked him about a confederation with Jordan. He said he would accept only if **Israel were also part of it**. Greenblatt declined to discuss specifics. — [JTA](https://jta.org/?p=1590154); [Israel Hayom](https://www.israelhayom.com/2018/09/03/us-floats-palestinian-jordanian-confederation-as-part-of-peace-plan-abbas-claims/); [The Hill](https://digital-release.thehill.com/?p=404778)
  - Jordan's government spokeswoman said confederation was "not on the table" and could not be discussed. Anadolu reported that Jordan rejected the "US-proposed confederation". — [Jordan Times](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/jordan-palestine-confederation-issue-%E2%80%98not-table%E2%80%99-%E2%80%94-govt); [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/jordan-rejects-us-proposed-confederation-with-palestine/1244862); [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/5340089)
  - King Abdullah was reported to call such a confederation a red line. — [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/251661)
  - Israeli officials distanced the government from the idea. — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/Arab-Israeli-Conflict/Official-Israel-not-behind-Jordanian-Palestinian-Confederation-idea-566412)
- **Jordan's rationale:**
  - Jordan rejects "any form of confederation" before an independent Palestinian state exists. It fears the kingdom could be "transformed into a Palestinian state", and memories of the PLO's 1970–71 "state within a state" persist.
  - Former PM Abdul Salam Majali argued that a confederation, with a joint government and parliament, should come only after Palestinian independence. — [Arab News, "Why Jordan rejects a confederation with Palestine"](https://arabnews.pk/node/1366951/middle-east)
- **Current official line:** FM Ayman Safadi repeatedly states there is "no alternative" to an independent Palestinian state on the 4 June 1967 lines with East Jerusalem as its capital. — [Jordan Times](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/safadi-says-establishing-palestinian-state-only-guarantee-regional-peace); [Jordan Times](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/two-state-solution-%E2%80%98sole-path%E2%80%99-peace-%E2%80%94-fm)
- **Palestinian opinion (PSR, Sept 2018):** 29% supported a Palestinian confederation with Jordan. A confederation including Israel was opposed by **75%**. PSR said opposition to a Jordan confederation had fallen about 10 points from several years earlier. — [PSR poll, 20 Sept 2018](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/737)
  - Earlier PSR findings put support above 40% over the preceding decade, including for confederation "now or later after the establishment of a Palestinian state" (2007). — [PSR](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/163)
  - A JMCC poll (date not confirmed) found 61% opposed. — [JMCC](https://www.jmcc.org/Documentsandmaps.aspx?id=880)
- **Israeli right and Israeli proposals:**
  - Some on the Israeli right favour a Palestinian–Jordan confederation as a way to avoid full Palestinian statehood. — [JNS/Israel Hayom](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/abbas-us-floats-palestinian-jordanian-confederation-as-part-of-peace-plan)
  - Giora Eiland proposed that Jordan "take charge" of Palestine in a West Bank–East Bank–Gaza federal arrangement. — [The JC](https://www.thejc.com/news/world/giora-eiland-jordan-should-take-charge-of-palestine-hg1czhif); [IJN](https://www.ijn.com/former-israel-national-security-adviser-proposes-west-bank-east-bank-gaza-merger/)
  - Historian Benny Morris (2009) proposed a joint Jordanian–Palestinian state. — [Wikipedia: Three-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution)
- The Beilin–Husseini HLC leaves open Jordan joining "aspects" of the confederation later by mutual agreement. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)

### Inferences
- The **sequencing condition** is the crucial modelling rule: Jordan and most Palestinian actors accept confederation only as a **post-statehood** choice between two sovereign states. The Israeli right and some US officials have floated it as a **substitute** for statehood, which Jordan treats as an existential threat ("Jordan is not Palestine").
- In a game, a Jordan-confederation endgame plausibly requires: (1) a prior Palestinian state; (2) Jordanian consent, a veto actor; (3) demographic and Hashemite-legitimacy safeguards. Without (1), it collapses into the "Jordanian option" variant of the three-state solution (section 4).

### Gaps
- No Jordanian official statement from 2025–2026 specifically on confederation was found. The 2018 "not on the table" line and Safadi's two-state statements are the latest located.
- No Palestinian polling on a Jordan confederation after 2018 was found.
- The verbatim text of the 1985 accord's confederation clause and its five principles was not retrieved from a primary archive (the JVL text was not fetched in full).

## 4. "Three-state solution": Egypt–Jordan return (Bolton, Eiland) vs. the de facto West Bank–Gaza split

### Takeaway
"Three states" has two meanings:
- **(A) Israel plus Egypt (taking Gaza) plus Jordan (taking most of the West Bank):** the "Egyptian–Jordanian option". John Bolton championed it from January 2009. Giora Eiland's regional land-swap variant would enlarge Gaza into northern Sinai. Egypt and Jordan have consistently rejected both, most forcefully since October 2023 (Sisi: no "liquidation of the Palestinian cause", no relocation to Sinai; Egypt "will not govern Gaza").
- **(B) Israel plus a PA-run West Bank plus Hamas-run Gaza:** the de facto situation since 2007. Post-2025 Gaza arrangements under Resolution 2803 run Gaza through a separate technocratic committee rather than the PA.

### Cited Findings

#### (A) Egyptian–Jordanian "three-state" option
- Definition: the "Egyptian–Jordanian solution" or "Jordan–Egypt option" returns West Bank administration to Jordan and Gaza to Egypt, restoring the 1949–67 arrangement. — [Wikipedia: Three-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution)
- **John Bolton**, Washington Post, 5 Jan 2009: the "most attractive prospect" is to attach the Palestinian communities to contiguous Arab states. Gaza goes to Egypt, and Jordan resumes sovereignty over the parts of the West Bank not incorporated into Israel. He framed this as a "three-state solution". — [Washington Post, 5 Jan 2009](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2009/01/04/AR2009010401434.html); [JTA](https://www.jta.org/2009/01/06/culture/bolton-three-state-solution)
- After Oct 7, Bolton called the two-state solution "dead" and again promoted three states. — [Audioboom interview](https://audioboom.com/posts/8776585-john-bolton-criticizes-the-two-state-solution-as-a-dead-idea-post-october-7th-proposing-a-thr); [Jordan News](https://jordannews.jo/Section-20/Middle-East/John-Bolton-Proposes-Three-State-Solution-as-an-Alternative-to-the-Two-State-Solution-for-the-Palestinian-Issue-39389)
- Critiques: Foreign Policy ran "Bolton's zombie idea". — [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/?p=14890). The NYT reported in Jan 2009 that Egypt and Jordan were apprehensive about reassuming control. — [NYT, 12 Jan 2009](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/01/12/world/middleeast/12egypt.html)
- Other proponents listed: Binyamin Elon (annexation, with Palestinians as Jordanian citizens), Aryeh Eldad, Daniel Pipes ("a uniquely sober way"), Ian Bremmer (2007, "a difficult plan whose time has come") and a New York Sun editorial (2007). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution); [Pipes](https://www.danielpipes.org/6110/solving-the-palestinian-problem)
- **Giora Eiland** (former head of Israel's National Security Council):
  - He endorsed a regional approach in a Washington Institute Policy Focus (Sept 2008). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution)
  - The **Sinai swap**, dating to about 2004: Egypt transfers about **600 km² of northern Sinai** to enlarge Gaza. Israel transfers about **200 km² in the Negev** to Egypt. Egypt gets a land link or tunnel to Jordan. Sources differ on the figures: some give 600 km² for the Negev transfer, and one cites "some 1,600 km" of Sinai. — [Daily Alert summary of Haaretz](https://www.dailyalert.org/rss/Mainissues.php?id=32085); [Worldcrunch](https://worldcrunch.com/the-endless-war-1/egypt-sinai-palestinians-gaza/); [Jerusalem Post](https://jpost.com/opinion/know-comment-revolutionizing-the-peace-process-375131)
  - His later paper "Regional Alternatives to the Two-State Solution" argued that Jordan and Egypt should replace the PA as Israel's Arab negotiating partners. — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/international/eiland-current-two-state-solution-untenable/article-115416); [IJN](https://www.ijn.com/former-israel-national-security-adviser-proposes-west-bank-east-bank-gaza-merger/)
  - The Egyptian response was "less than lukewarm". Uri Avnery called the plan "no worse than the other utopias… also no better". — [Gush Shalom](https://gush-shalom.org/history_en/en-channels-avnery-1149931512)
- **Egyptian rejection:**
  - On 18 Oct 2023 Sisi said: "We reject the liquidation of the Palestinian cause and any relocation to Sinai." He warned that Sinai would become "the base for launching operations against Israel" and suggested Israel could move Gazans to the Negev instead. — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1781978); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2393366); [BusinessDay](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/world/africa/2023-10-18-egypt-rejects-influx-of-palestinians-into-sinai-as-security-risk/)
  - Sisi repeated that Egypt rejects displacement "into Sinai or any other place" in April 2024. — [Africanews](https://www.africanews.com/2024/04/26/egypt-rejects-any-displacement-of-palestinians-into-sinai-or-any-other-place-president-el-/)
  - In February 2025 Egypt rejected Yair Lapid's proposal that Egypt run Gaza for 15 years, calling it "unacceptable". — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2591728/middle-east); [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=828889)
  - Egypt has said it "will not govern Gaza" and that Palestinians must govern themselves. — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/un%20security%20council/252024/egypt-rejects-us-plan-for-foreign-control-in-gaza-insists-palestinians-must-govern-themselves)
- **Jordanian rejection:**
  - Jordan opposes granting citizenship to more Palestinians, for demographic and political reasons. It summoned Israel's ambassador in 2009 over a Knesset bill on the "Jordanian option". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution); [JPost archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20200128211751/https:/www.jpost.com/Israel/Jordan-summons-Israeli-ambassador-on-bill)
  - Both Egypt and Jordan rejected the 2025 proposal to relocate Gazans to their territory. — [NPA Syria](https://npasyria.com/en/?p=122039); [Jordan News (Safadi)](https://www.jordannews.jo/Section-109/News/Safadi-Jordan-rejects-displacement-plans-of-Palestinians-39411)
- December 2025: Steve Bannon floated a different "three-state" idea that includes a Christian state in the Levant. — [Media Matters](https://www.mediamatters.org/steve-bannon/steve-bannon-calls-creation-christian-state-levant-0); [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/steve-bannon-calls-christian-state-middle-east)

#### (B) De facto three entities (post-2007 split)
- After the 2007 Fatah–Hamas conflict, "three-state solution" was also used for the de facto division: Israel, a PA-run West Bank and Hamas-run Gaza. Some analysts argue this makes two states obsolete. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution)
- In 2012 there were reports that Hamas was weighing a declaration of Gaza's independence with Egyptian backing. — [Al Arabiya (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20121014181327/http://english.alarabiya.net/articles/2012/07/31/229595.html)
- **2025–26 institutionalisation of separate Gaza governance:**
  - Resolution 2803 has Gaza administered by a "Palestinian technocratic, apolitical committee", the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG). Chief Commissioner Ali Shaath took office on 14 Jan 2026, and the committee is temporarily based in Cairo. The PA welcomed the resolution.
  - Hamas still controls the area west of the "Yellow Line", slightly under half of Gaza. — [Wikipedia: Gaza Strip under Resolution 2803](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_under_Resolution_2803); [NCAG](https://www.ncag.ps)

### Inferences
- Meaning (A) is effectively an **"no Palestinian state" endgame**: Palestinian sovereignty is absorbed into neighbouring Arab states. It needs Egypt and Jordan as willing actors, and both have explicitly refused for decades. In a game it should face a near-absolute veto from those actors unless their core security, demographic and regime-legitimacy concerns change drastically.
- Meaning (B) is a **default or drift state**, not a negotiated endgame. A game could treat it as the attractor that results when no deal is reached. The 2025–26 Gaza architecture (NCAG, Board of Peace, ISF) could either deepen the split or, if PA reform conditions are met, reconnect Gaza and the West Bank.

### Gaps
- Eiland's 2008 Washington Institute Policy Focus (marked as a dead link) and his BESA "Regional Alternatives" paper were not retrieved in primary form. The Sinai figures rest on press summaries and are inconsistent.
- No formal Egyptian government statement addressing Eiland's plan by name was found.
- No polling on the Egypt–Jordan "three-state" option was found.

## 5. International trusteeship or UN administration: corpus separatum, Holy Basin regimes, and the 2025–26 Board of Peace / ISF in Gaza

### Takeaway
International administration has three historical layers:
1. **The 1947 corpus separatum** for Jerusalem (Resolution 181): UN Trusteeship Council administration, a non-local Governor, demilitarisation, a 10-year review and a referendum.
2. **Holy Basin proposals:** Olmert's 2008 five-nation non-sovereign trusteeship, revived in the 2024 Olmert–al-Kidwa plan, and the JOCI "special regime" with an international Chief Administrator.
3. **The live Gaza experiment under UNSC Resolution 2803 (17 Nov 2025):** a Trump-chaired **Board of Peace** as transitional administration until end-2027, a Palestinian technocratic committee and an **International Stabilization Force**. Hamas calls it "international guardianship". Critics compare it to a League of Nations mandate. By late 2026 implementation was slow: ISF deployment was limited and conditioned by Israel.

### Cited Findings

#### 1947 corpus separatum (UNGA Resolution 181, Part III)
- Jerusalem was to be "a corpus separatum under a special international regime", administered by the UN, with the **Trusteeship Council** as Administering Authority. Its boundaries ran from Abu Dis (east), Bethlehem (south), 'Ein Karim/Motsa (west) and Shu'fat (north). — [Res. 181 text, Avalon Project](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/res181.asp)
- Key terms:
  - A **Governor** appointed by the Trusteeship Council, not a citizen of either state.
  - **Demilitarised and neutral**, with a special police force recruited from outside Palestine.
  - An elected **Legislative Council** (universal suffrage, proportional representation) subject to the Governor's veto, and an independent judiciary.
  - Membership in the Palestine Economic Union, with Arabic and Hebrew as official languages.
  - Residents become city citizens unless they opt for a state's citizenship.
  - Holy places are a special concern of the Governor.
  - In force for **10 years**, then reviewed by the Trusteeship Council, with residents able to vote by **referendum** on changes. — [Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/res181.asp)

#### Holy Basin international or special regimes
- **Olmert's offer (16 Sept 2008):** a **non-sovereign trusteeship** of five states (Saudi Arabia, Jordan, the Palestinian state, Israel and the US) over the Holy Basin: the Old City, Mount of Olives and City of David. "No one country will have exclusive political sovereignty." — [Middle East Forum, quoting Olmert](https://www.meforum.org/olmert-details-his-offer-to-abbas); [Times of Israel, hand-drawn map](https://www.timesofisrael.com/hand-drawn-map-shows-what-olmert-offered-for-peace/)
- **Olmert–al-Kidwa document (2024):** Ehud Olmert and Nasser al-Kidwa published a plan that again proposes a five-state trusteeship for the Old City. They met Pope Francis to promote it. — [Al Majalla](https://en.majalla.com/node/322135/documents-memoirs/al-majalla-publishes-olmert-kidwa-peace-proposal); [Catholic Register](https://catholicregister.org/faith/item/37255-pope-francis-meets-former-israeli-and-palestinian-officials-promoting-peace)
- **Jerusalem Old City Initiative (JOCI, University of Windsor, Track II):**
  - Proposes an Old City **"special regime"** set up at the direction of both states, headed by a **Chief Administrator who is neither Israeli nor Palestinian**, appointed by a governance board for a fixed renewable term.
  - It covers the area within and including the walls, above and below ground, and "will not prejudice the claims of either party regarding sovereignty". — [JOCI governance document, June 2013](https://www.uwindsor.ca/jerusalem-old-city-initiative/sites/uwindsor.ca.jerusalem-old-city-initiative/files/governance_discussion_document_june_2013.pdf); [INSS, Feb 2010](https://www.inss.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/3-2010-Feb-The-Jerusalem-Old-City-Initiative.pdf); [MEI](https://mei.edu/publication/israeli-palestinian-peace-special-regime-option-old-city-jerusalem/)
- The Jerusalem Institute's Historic Basin study discussed a special regime for the holy places alongside Israeli control of the basin, with Palestinian cultural autonomy. — [Jerusalem Institute](https://jerusaleminstitute.org.il/en/publications/the-historic-basin-of-jerusalem-problems-and-possible-solutions-concluding-remarks/)
- Contrast: the HLC (2025) proposes a **confederal, bilateral** Old City authority instead of an international one. — [HLC 2nd ed. PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)

#### Gaza: Resolution 2803, Board of Peace (BoP), NCAG, ISF
- **Adoption and mandate:**
  - Resolution 2803 was adopted **17 Nov 2025, 13–0, with Russia and China abstaining**. It endorsed the US "Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict" and welcomed the BoP as a **transitional administration with international legal personality**.
  - It authorised a temporary **ISF** under unified command acceptable to the BoP, tasked with stabilisation and "demilitarizing the Gaza Strip", working with vetted Palestinian police. — [UN press via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/11/mil-251117-unsc02.htm); [UN press SC/16225](https://press.un.org/en/2025/sc16225.doc.htm); [Resolution text](https://undocs.org/S/RES/2803(2025)); [Chatham House explainer](https://www.chathamhouse.org/2025/11/what-security-council-resolution-2803-and-what-does-it-mean-trump-gaza-plan)
  - EU High Representative Kaja Kallas noted that the resolution limits the mandate to **2027**, gives Palestinians a say and refers only to Gaza. She said the BoP charter does not reflect these limits. — [Guardian, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/feb/13/dispute-future-of-gaza-trump-board-of-peace-eu-un); [Wikipedia: Board of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Peace)
- **Board of Peace structure:**
  - Formed 15 Jan 2026; charter signed 22 Jan 2026 at Davos. Trump is chairman with no term limit (independent of his presidency), sole power to invite members and the right to nominate a successor.
  - **Permanent membership costs US$1 billion**; otherwise members serve three-year terms. About 25–27 states have joined. France, Spain, Ireland, Sweden, the UK, Germany, Norway, the EU and others declined or held back.

### Inferences
- The BoP differs from UNMIK and UNTAET in three modelling-relevant ways:
  - It is **not a UN organ**: it is chaired by a head of state and funded by pay-to-join members.
  - Its security force is **small and conditioned by a party to the conflict** (an Israeli veto over contributors and deployment zone).
  - It administers only **part** of the territory, with Hamas holding about half of Gaza.
- For a game, "international administration" is best treated as a **transitional state with exit conditions** (PA reform, disarmament, a 2027 sunset) rather than an endgame. It can branch toward a reunified Palestinian state, a long-term protectorate or reversion to the status quo.
- For Jerusalem, international or special-regime options are mostly **"carve-outs" inside two-state or confederal deals** (Holy Basin, Old City). They are not freestanding solutions, except for the corpus separatum, which neither party has accepted since 1948.

### Gaps
- Events after about mid-October 2026 were not found: whether ISF troops actually entered Gaza, the outcome of Israel's late-2026 election and its effect on the BoP, and any renewal or amendment of Res. 2803.
- No authoritative comparative legal study of the BoP against UN Charter Chapter XII (trusteeship) law was found. Johnson's Lawfare piece predates the BoP.
- Tony Blair's earlier "Gaza International Transitional Authority" (GITA) concept was not sourced in this session.
- No polling of Gazans on the BoP/NCAG arrangement was found.

## 6. Cross-model synthesis: placing the hybrids on a two-state ↔ one-state spectrum and modelling them as distinct endgames

### Takeaway
The hybrids differ along a small set of variables: who holds sovereignty, whether the territory is partitioned, whether residency is decoupled from citizenship, whether settlers stay, who controls security, and whether an outside actor holds authority. Measured by public opinion, all are minority options. Confederation is the only one with tracked polling, at 20% of Israelis and 35% of Palestinians in July 2024. Third-state options (Egypt, Jordan) face explicit vetoes from those states.

### Cited Findings
- Confederation support has stayed below two-state support in every PSR/TAU wave, and both have stayed minority positions. — [PSR/TAU July 2024](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/989); [PSR/TAU Oct 2020](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Press%20Release_Eng%20_Joint%20Poll%2026%20Oct%202020.pdf)
- July 2024 rival options: 33% of Palestinians backed one state with limited rights for Jews, and 25% one democratic state. 42% of Israeli Jews backed annexation without equal rights, and 14% one democratic state. — [PSR/TAU July 2024](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/989)
- HLC: "a facilitator for the two-state solution". — [HLC PDF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf). Parallel states: "the only way to preserve a two-state solution". — [Tablet](https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/news/articles/defining-the-one-land-two-state-solution). Both sets of proponents frame their models as two-state variants, not alternatives.
- Jordan accepts confederation only after Palestinian statehood. — [Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/1366951/middle-east). Egypt "will not govern Gaza". — [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=828889)

### Inferences
Suggested ordering from "hard partition" toward "one state", based on the cited terms:
1. **Geneva-style two states** (baseline, not covered here).
2. **Holy Land Confederation:** two sovereign states, 2.5% swaps, about 194,000 settlers stay as residents of Palestine, a reciprocal Palestinian residency quota in Israel, a confederal Old City, Israeli-led external defence, and phased, reversible opening.
3. **A Land for All / Scheindlin–Waxman confederation:** two states on 1967 lines, open borders, free residence for all, joint assembly and human rights court, a shared open Jerusalem.
4. **Parallel states (LeVine and Mossberg):** two states overlapping the whole land, sovereignty split by function and person rather than territory.
5. **Israeli-sovereign cantonal federation (Federation Movement):** one sovereign and one army, 30 cantons, citizenship offered to West Bank Palestinians, Gaza separate.

Off-axis endgames, which do not sit on the simple two-to-one line:
- **Fragmentation:** Kedar's emirates or the Hebron emirate. No Palestinian state; Israeli control over the spaces between city-states.
- **Externalisation:** the Bolton/Eiland three-state option. Palestinian territory is absorbed by Egypt and Jordan, or Gaza is enlarged into Sinai.
- **Jordanian–Palestinian confederation:** two-state-plus-Jordan if it follows statehood; externalisation if it replaces statehood.
- **International administration:** a transitional overlay (BoP/ISF in Gaza) or a carve-out (Holy Basin trusteeship, the corpus separatum).

Possible game variables per endgame:
- Sovereignty locus: 1, 2, many or external.
- Territorial partition: yes/no.
- Residency–citizenship decoupling: yes/no, with an HLC-style quota.
- Settler status: evacuated, annexed, or resident under Palestinian law.
- Refugee channel: Palestine-only, limited residency in Israel, or full return.
- Jerusalem: divided, open shared, confederal Old City, or international.
- Security: Israeli-led, joint, or international force.
- Veto players: Israel, PA, Hamas, Jordan, Egypt, the US/BoP.

### Gaps
- No single public-opinion series measures support for federation/cantons, Jordanian confederation (after 2018), the three-state option or international administration in comparable wording. Cross-model comparisons of support cannot be made from the sources found.
- No economic modelling (GDP or fiscal impacts) of any hybrid model was found beyond the HLC's qualitative economic chapter.
