# The Two-State Solution and Its Negotiated Variants (1937 to November 2026)

Research notes compiled 6 November 2026. Scope: each major two-state framework or offer, what it said on the core issues, why it failed, current public opinion, and current settlement figures. Tone is descriptive; claims are attributed. Where sources conflict, both are given. Method note: the web-search budget ran out partway through. The 2024–2026 sections therefore rely partly on Wikipedia pages that cite Reuters, BBC, NYT and Al Jazeera; those underlying URLs are listed where the page gave them.

## 1. Origins: Peel (1937), UNGA 181 (1947), UNSC 242 (1967) and 338 (1973), PLO 1988

### Takeaway
Partition into a Jewish and an Arab state was first proposed by Britain in 1937. The UN set it out in more detail in 1947, with Jerusalem as an international zone. The Jewish leadership accepted partition (with reservations) and the Arab leadership rejected it both times. After 1967 the framework became "land for peace" under UNSC 242. The PLO formally accepted partition and 242 in 1988, which made the 1967 lines the reference baseline for every later negotiation.

### Cited Findings
**Peel Commission (report 7 July 1937)**
- Chaired by Lord Peel. It judged the Mandate unworkable and recommended partition. The Jewish state would get about one-fifth of the territory (most of the coast, the Jezreel Valley, the Galilee). The Arab state would be joined to Transjordan. Jerusalem and a corridor to Jaffa would stay under a permanent British mandate — [Wikipedia: Peel Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission)
- It proposed a population exchange modelled on Greece–Turkey 1923, estimated at up to 225,000 Arabs and 1,250 Jews — [Wikipedia: Peel Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission)
- At the 20th Zionist Congress (August 1937), delegates rejected the proposed borders but authorised the executive to negotiate a better plan. Ben-Gurion and Weizmann backed the principle. The Arab Higher Committee rejected partition and demanded an independent state, as did the Bloudan Conference (September 1937). The Woodhead Commission (1938) shelved the plan, mainly over the forced transfer it required — [Wikipedia: Peel Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission)
- The report's diagnosis: "an irrepressible conflict has arisen between two national communities within the narrow bounds of one small country" — [Wikipedia: Peel Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission)

**UNGA Resolution 181 (II), 29 November 1947**
- Vote: 33 for, 13 against, 10 abstentions, 1 absent (a two-thirds majority was required) — [Wikipedia: UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine)
- Land: Jewish state 15,264 km² (56.47%); Arab state 11,592 km² (42.88%); Jerusalem–Bethlehem international zone (corpus separatum) 176 km² (0.65%). The two states were to be in economic union — [Wikipedia: UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine)
- Population (UNSCOP 1945 data): the Jewish state would hold about 498,000 Jews (55%) and 407,000 Arabs and others (45%); the Arab state about 725,000 Arabs (99%) and 10,000 Jews. A Sub-Committee 2 revision gave 509,780 Arabs and 499,020 Jews in the Jewish state, so Arabs would have been a slight majority there. The two counts conflict — [Wikipedia: UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine)
- The Jewish Agency accepted, reluctantly. The Arab Higher Committee, the Arab League and Arab governments rejected it on self-determination grounds. It was never implemented: civil war became the 1948 war, during which about 85% of the Palestinians living in what became Israel fled or were expelled — [Wikipedia: UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine)
- The 1949 Armistice lines (the Green Line) became Israel's de facto borders — [Wikipedia: Two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution)

**UNSC Resolution 242 (22 November 1967) and 338 (1973)**
- Operative text: "Withdrawal of Israeli armed forces from territories occupied in the recent conflict"; "Termination of all claims or states of belligerency" and the right of every state "to live in peace within secure and recognized boundaries"; and "a just settlement of the refugee problem" — [Wikipedia: UNSC 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242); text at [undocs.org S/RES/242](https://undocs.org/S/RES/242(1967))
- The English text has no "the" before "territories"; the French reads "des territoires occupés". Lord Caradon, the chief drafter, said the 1967 lines were not meant to be permanent. US drafter Arthur Goldberg said 242 "neither commands nor prohibits total withdrawal" and left the extent to direct negotiations — [Wikipedia: UNSC 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242)
- The PLO rejected 242 in 1967 because it ignored "the existence of the Palestinian people and their right of self-determination". The 1974 Ten-Point Program still wanted a framework "other than that of resolution 242". In 1993 Israel and the PLO adopted 242 and 338 as their terms of reference — [Wikipedia: UNSC 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242)
- Resolution 338 was the 1973 (Yom Kippur War) ceasefire resolution, which took in 242. The Road Map and Sinai II later cited it — [Wikipedia: UNSC 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242); [Wikipedia: UNSC 338](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_Resolution_338)

**PLO Declaration of Independence (15 November 1988, Algiers)**
- Proclaimed by Arafat at the 19th Palestinian National Council (PNC) and written by Mahmoud Darwish. The PNC adopted it 253–46–10. It cites UNGA 181 as a basis of international legitimacy — [Wikipedia: Palestinian Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Declaration_of_Independence)
- The accompanying PNC political communiqué called for negotiations on the basis of 242, which Wikipedia calls "the Historic Compromise" — [PNC communiqué (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20010420191313/http://www.al-bab.com/arab/docs/pal/pal4.htm); [Wikipedia: UNSC 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242)
- On 14 December 1988 in Geneva, Arafat clarified the PLO position. The US accepted this as meeting its conditions and opened a dialogue — [JVL: Arafat statement, 14 Dec 1988](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Terrorism/plotstate1.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Declaration_of_Independence)
- 75 states had recognised Palestine by mid-December 1988 and 93 by February 1989. UNGA Resolution 43/177 acknowledged the proclamation and replaced "PLO" with "Palestine" in the UN system, by 104–2 (US, Israel) with 44 abstentions — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Declaration_of_Independence)
- Later milestones: in 1974 the UNGA affirmed Palestinian self-determination. At Fez in 1982 the PLO mainstream committed to mutual recognition with a state in the West Bank and Gaza and East Jerusalem as capital. On 29 November 2012 the UNGA made Palestine a "non-member observer state" (138–9; the article gives 46 abstentions) — [Wikipedia: Two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution)
- Hamas's 2017 charter accepted a state on the 4 June 1967 lines without recognising Israel — [Wikipedia: Two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution)

### Inferences
- The baseline for Palestinian territory fell from about 43% of Mandate Palestine (181) to the 22% outside the Green Line (West Bank, East Jerusalem, Gaza). Palestinians describe their 1988 acceptance as the "historic compromise", and Palestinian negotiators later argued that any swaps had to be measured from that 22%. This is an inference from the 181 and 1967 figures, not a quoted source.
- The 242 drafting ambiguity ("territories" vs "the territories") explains why later offers argue over percentages and swaps rather than over the principle of withdrawal.

### Gaps
- The adoption date of UNSC 338 (22 October 1973) and its text were not fetched directly.
- The exact wording of Arafat's 14 December 1988 statement (renouncing terrorism and recognising Israel's right to exist) was not read; only the JVL page title was seen.
- The 2012 abstention count: the Wikipedia summary gives 46, which may conflict with UN records (not checked).

## 2. Oslo I (1993) and Oslo II (1995): the interim framework and Areas A/B/C

### Takeaway
Oslo was an interim, phased framework. It created the PA and a patchwork of jurisdictions, and left every core issue (borders, Jerusalem, refugees, settlements, security) to permanent-status talks that were due to begin by May 1996. Area C, about 60% of the West Bank, stayed under full Israeli control and is where nearly all settlements sit. The interim map became the long-term map.

### Cited Findings
- The Declaration of Principles (Oslo I) was signed on 13 September 1993 in Washington by Israel and the PLO. Letters dated 9 September 1993 concerned the PLO Covenant and recognition — [Wikipedia: Oslo II Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord)
- Oslo II was signed in Taba on 24 September 1995 and in Washington on 28 September 1995 (Rabin, Arafat, witnessed by Clinton). It set a transitional period "not exceeding five years", with permanent-status talks to begin no later than 4 May 1996. The aim was to implement 242 and 338 — [Wikipedia: Oslo II Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord)
- **Area A:** full PA civil and security control. About 3% of the West Bank at first, about 18% by 2013; eight cities, no settlements — [Wikipedia: West Bank Areas in the Oslo II Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord), citing [B'Tselem](https://www.btselem.org/area_c/what_is_area_c)
- **Area B:** Palestinian civil control with joint security control. About 23–25% at first, about 22% by 2013; around 440 villages — [Wikipedia: Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord)
- **Area C:** full Israeli civil and security control. About 72–74% at first; about 61% officially after the 1998 Wye River Memorandum; about 63% by 2013 including settlements, outposts and "state land". Area C is contiguous, while A and B form 165 non-contiguous enclaves — [Wikipedia: Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord)
- Area C population: about 150,000 Palestinians (2015) or about 300,000 (a 2014 UN figure; the two figures conflict), and about 400,000 Israelis in 135 settlements plus more than 100 outposts. Settlers numbered about 1,000 in 1972 and about 110,000 in 1993 — [Wikipedia: Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord)
- Oslo II planned three further redeployments over 18 months, transferring much of Area C. Israel withdrew from only about 2% of the land designated at Wye, and re-entered all areas in Operation Defensive Shield (2002) — [Wikipedia: Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord)
- The 1997 Hebron agreement split the city into H1 (Palestinian) and H2 (Israeli) — [Wikipedia: Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord)

### Inferences
- For game design: Oslo is the "interim" state of the board, not an endgame. Its A/B/C split (18/22/60) is the natural starting map for any two-state model. Where a final border runs largely determines which Area C settlements are annexed or evacuated.
- The missed redeployments, and the fact that the permanent-status deadline (May 1999) passed without a deal, are the background to the 2000 summit.

### Gaps
- Primary text of Oslo II Annex I was not read. The Wikipedia Oslo II article is tagged as needing citations.
- No first-hand Israeli or Palestinian critiques of Oslo (e.g. Edward Said, Likud opposition) were retrieved.

## 3. Camp David (July 2000), the Clinton Parameters (December 2000) and Taba (January 2001)

### Takeaway
Camp David was the first leader-level permanent-status summit. It ended without agreement, mainly over sovereignty on the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif, plus refugees and territory. Clinton then set out bridging "Parameters": 94–96% of the West Bank, Arab neighbourhoods of Jerusalem to Palestine, and a refugee solution limited to the Palestinian state plus Israeli discretion. Both sides accepted them "with reservations". At Taba the gaps narrowed further (Israel asked for about 6% annexation with about 3% compensation), but talks were overtaken by Israel's February 2001 election. Who was to blame for the failure is still disputed.

### Cited Findings
**Camp David (11–25 July 2000)**
- Clinton, Barak and Arafat. Israeli negotiators included Gilead Sher and Shlomo Ben-Ami; Palestinians included Mahmoud Abbas and Saeb Erekat; US mediators included Dennis Ross. It ended without agreement, with a Trilateral Statement of principles — [Wikipedia: 2000 Camp David Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit); [Trilateral Statement, US State Dept](https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/nea/rls/22698.htm)
- **Reported Israeli offer (accounts differ, since most proposals were oral):**
  - Territory: a state on about 73% of the West Bank at first, rising to about 92% over 10–25 years, including a 1% swap, plus all of Gaza. Palestinians counted it as about 86%.
  - Jerusalem: Israeli sovereignty over the Temple Mount and Western Wall areas, with Palestinian "custodianship". Some Arab neighbourhoods would get only civil autonomy.
  - Refugees: up to 100,000 returns on humanitarian or family-reunification grounds, with an international fund of about $30 billion.
  - Security: a demilitarised state; Israeli control of airspace; Israeli radar stations; an international force in the Jordan Valley.
  - [Wikipedia: Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)
- **Palestinian position:** full withdrawal under 242, open to 1:1 swaps; sovereignty over East Jerusalem and its holy sites, with the Jewish Quarter and Western Wall under Israel; the right of return, but with mechanisms to steer most refugees away from Israel (for example, a pilot of 2,000 per year from Lebanon) — [Wikipedia: Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)
- **Main sticking point:** Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif sovereignty — Barak insisted on Israeli sovereignty, Arafat on Palestinian — [Wikipedia: Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)
- **Competing narratives:**
  - Clinton blamed Arafat ("I am a failure, and you made me one"). Dennis Ross (*The Missing Peace*) blamed Arafat's unwillingness to close off claims, especially the right of return.
  - Robert Malley and Hussein Agha argued the Israeli offer did not meet core Palestinian needs and that Arafat had made significant concessions — [Malley & Agha, "Camp David: The Tragedy of Errors", NYRB, 9 Aug 2001](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/archives/2001/aug/09/camp-david-the-tragedy-of-errors/); [Malley, NYT, 8 July 2001](https://www.nytimes.com/2001/07/08/opinion/fictions-about-the-failure-at-camp-david.html)
  - Clayton Swisher held the US and Israel at least as responsible.
  - Ben-Ami said Camp David itself was not a missed Palestinian opportunity, but faulted the Palestinian response to the Clinton Parameters and Taba.
  - Sher and Abed Rabbo both criticised the preparation.
  - [Wikipedia: Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)
- The summit's failure is described as one of the main triggers of the Second Intifada — [Wikipedia: Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)

**Clinton Parameters (presented 23 December 2000)**
- **Territory:** a Palestinian state on 94–96% of the West Bank plus all of Gaza. Israel annexes blocs holding about 80% of settlers. Israel cedes land equal to 1–3% in swaps. Contiguity required, annexation minimised. No map — [Wikipedia: Clinton Parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)
- **Security:**
  - The IDF withdraws over 36 months and an international force replaces it.
  - A small Israeli presence at fixed Jordan Valley locations for a further 36 months.
  - Three Israeli early-warning stations, reviewed every 10 years.
  - Palestinian sovereignty over airspace, with Israeli training and operational arrangements.
  - A "non-militarized state" with a "strong security force".
  - Israeli emergency deployment to pre-agreed areas with notice to the international force.
  - [Wikipedia: Clinton Parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)
- **Jerusalem:** Arab neighbourhoods Palestinian, Jewish neighbourhoods Israeli, including in the Old City. Israeli sovereignty over the Western Wall; Palestinian sovereignty over the Haram/Temple Mount, with an Israeli "symbolic" stake; shared arrangements on excavation — [Wikipedia: Clinton Parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)
- **Refugees:**
  - Palestinians give up an unlimited return to Israel; Israel acknowledges the suffering caused by the 1948 war.
  - An international commission implements the solution.
  - Refugees may go to the Palestinian state, stay in host countries, resettle in third countries, or enter Israel in limited numbers if Israel agrees.
  - Both sides agree UNGA 194 has been implemented.
  - [Wikipedia: Clinton Parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)
- **End of conflict and claims;** a UNSC resolution would declare 242/338 implemented — [Wikipedia: Clinton Parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)
- **Responses:** Israel's cabinet voted 10–2 on 28 December 2000 to accept, with reservations (Barak's 20-page letter on Temple Mount sovereignty and refugees). Arafat gave qualified agreement with reservations on 2 January 2001. The White House said on 3 January that "both sides have now accepted the president's ideas with some reservations" — [Wikipedia: Clinton Parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)

**Taba (21–27 January 2001)**
- Ben-Ami (Israel); Erekat with Abed Rabbo and Dahlan (Palestinians). EU envoy Miguel Moratinos wrote a "non-paper" that both sides reportedly regarded as fair — [Wikipedia: Taba Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit); [Moratinos non-paper (archived UNISPAL)](https://web.archive.org/web/20140203064550/https://unispal.un.org/UNISPAL.NSF/0/CEA3EFD8C0AB482F85256E3700670AF8)
- **Territory:** both sides accepted the 4 June 1967 lines as the basis. Israel lowered its annexation demand to about 6% with about 3% compensation (leaving Palestinians about 97%); the Palestinians proposed about 3% with equal compensation. Gaza settlements to be evacuated; safe passage between Gaza and Hebron — [Wikipedia: Taba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit)
- **Jerusalem:** both accepted the Clinton neighbourhood principle and an open city; Israel would recognise Jerusalem as capital of both states. The extent of the Western Wall area was disputed; the Haram/Temple Mount was unresolved — [Wikipedia: Taba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit)
- **Refugees:** both cited 242 and 194. Israel listed return options (Israel, swapped land, Palestine, host country, third country). UNRWA to be phased out over about five years. Israel raised claims of Jewish immigrants from Arab countries; Palestinians raised property restitution, which Israel rejected — [Wikipedia: Taba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit)
- **Security:** Israel asked for three early-warning stations, a non-militarised state, a unified air-control system, and withdrawal over 36 months plus 36 months in the Jordan Valley with an international force. The Palestinians accepted arms limits but called 36 months too long — [Wikipedia: Taba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit)
- The joint statement (27 January 2001) said the sides "have never been closer". Sharon defeated Barak on 6 February 2001 and did not resume talks; on 8 February Barak said the ideas were not binding — [Wikipedia: Taba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit); [Joint statement (archived MFA)](https://web.archive.org/web/20040701082504/http://www.mfa.gov.il/MFA/MFAArchive/2000_2009/2001/1/Israeli-Palestinian%20Joint%20Statement%20-%2027-Jan-2001)
- Arafat said in June 2002 that he accepted the Clinton plan; the Israeli government said it was no longer on the table — [Guardian, 22 June 2002](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2002/jun/22/israel)

### Inferences
- Clinton/Taba is where the "standard two-state package" took shape: about 94–97% plus swaps, Jerusalem split by demography, a non-militarised state with an international force, and refugees resolved mostly outside Israel. Geneva, Olmert and Kerry are all variations on it.
- The decisive variable at Camp David was holy-site sovereignty, not percentages. A game model could treat Haram/Temple Mount sovereignty as a binary "deal-breaker" card with intermediate options: divided sovereignty, deferred sovereignty, or international/no sovereignty.

### Gaps
- The verbatim Clinton Parameters text was not fetched. The two alternative Haram/Western Wall formulations and the refugee "right of return to historic Palestine / to their homeland" options are commonly reported, but the Wikipedia summary did not confirm them, so they are unverified here.
- Specific Israeli refugee numbers at Taba (reported in some accounts) were not confirmed.
- The Camp David refugee figure (up to 100,000) comes from the Wikipedia summary; other accounts give lower numbers.

## 4. The Arab Peace Initiative (2002; re-endorsed 2007, 2013, 2017 and later)

### Takeaway
The API offers collective Arab normalisation with Israel in return for full withdrawal to the 4 June 1967 lines (including the Golan), a Palestinian state with East Jerusalem as capital, and an "agreed" just solution for refugees under UNGA 194. The 2013 Arab League clarification accepted "comparable and mutually agreed minor swaps". Israeli governments have welcomed its normalisation logic while rejecting its terms as a basis for talks.

### Cited Findings
- Adopted unanimously at the Beirut summit on 27–28 March 2002 — [Wikipedia: Arab Peace Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative); text at [JVL](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/politics-wing/peace-process/israeli-palestinian-negotiations-historical-agreements-and-frameworks/the-arab-league-ldquo-peace-plan-rdquo-march-2002)
- **Asks of Israel:**
  - "Full Israeli withdrawal from all the territories occupied since 1967, including the Syrian Golan Heights" to the 4 June 1967 lines, and from remaining Lebanese territory.
  - "Achievement of a just solution to the Palestinian Refugee problem" under UNGA 194.
  - "A Sovereign Independent Palestinian State" on the territories occupied since 4 June 1967, with East Jerusalem as capital.
  - [JVL text](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/politics-wing/peace-process/israeli-palestinian-negotiations-historical-agreements-and-frameworks/the-arab-league-ldquo-peace-plan-rdquo-march-2002)
- **Arab commitments in return:** consider the conflict ended and "enter into a peace agreement with Israel", "provide security for all the states of the region", and establish normal relations. The text also rejects "all forms of Palestinian patriation" (resettlement) that conflict with host-country circumstances — [JVL text](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/politics-wing/peace-process/israeli-palestinian-negotiations-historical-agreements-and-frameworks/the-arab-league-ldquo-peace-plan-rdquo-march-2002)
- The refugee clause says the solution is "to be agreed upon in accordance with the UN General Assembly Resolution No 194". This compromise wording avoids the term "right of return" — [Wikipedia: API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative)
- **Re-endorsements:** Riyadh, March 2007 (unchanged; the Hamas delegate abstained); 30 April 2013 (the League accepted "comparable and mutual agreed minor swaps" based on the 4 June 1967 line); 2017 summit — [Wikipedia: API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative)
- **Israeli reactions:**
  - The Sharon government called it a "non-starter" (2002).
  - Olmert called it a "revolutionary change" but would "never accept" refugee return to Israel.
  - Netanyahu said in 2015 that "the general idea... is a good idea", while objecting to the Golan and refugee clauses; in 2018 he rejected it as a basis for negotiation.
  - [Wikipedia: API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative); [Times of Israel, 2015](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-endorses-general-idea-behind-arab-peace-initiative/)
- **Critiques:** Israeli critics (Joel Singer; Caroline Glick, who called it "a recipe for Israel's destruction") said it asked Israel to act without demanding an end to terror. Hezbollah's Mohammad Raad said the option could no longer be promoted. Hamas positions varied: al-Zahar in 2006 said "We will never recognize the Arab initiative"; Barhum in 2007 gave neither yes nor no — [Wikipedia: API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative)
- The PA and Hamas called the 2020 UAE and Bahrain normalisation deals a "betrayal" that weakened the API's land-for-normalisation sequence — [Wikipedia: API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative)
- The API is the reference for the 2024 Saudi–Norwegian "Global Alliance for the Implementation of the Two-State Solution", launched 26 September 2024 with about 90 countries' representatives — [Wikipedia: Two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution); [Saudi Gazette on the Global Alliance](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/664863)

### Inferences
- The API is the regional "payoff" layer of the two-state family. In game terms it is a reward multiplier (normalisation with 22 states) attached to a bilateral deal, not a deal in itself. The 2020 Abraham Accords showed normalisation could partly happen without that sequence, which reduced its value as leverage.

### Gaps
- Arab League summit texts from 2023–2026 reaffirming the API were not retrieved.
- Saudi Arabia's stated condition (normalisation only with a Palestinian state) was not sourced here.

## 5. The Quartet Road Map (2003) and the Geneva Initiative (2003)

### Takeaway
The Road Map was a performance-based, three-phase sequence: security and reform first, then a state with provisional borders, then permanent status by 2005. It stalled in Phase I. Geneva was an unofficial but very detailed model treaty that filled in the Clinton/Taba template: 1:1 swaps of under 3%, Ariel and Efrat outside Israel, Palestinian sovereignty on the Haram with a multinational presence, a refugee cap tied to third-country intake, a multinational force, and staged withdrawal.

### Cited Findings
**Road Map (final text 30 April 2003)**
- Based on Bush's 24 June 2002 speech and presented by the Quartet (US, EU, UN, Russia). "Performance-based and goal-driven" — [Wikipedia: Road map for peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_map_for_peace)
- **Phase I:** mutual recognition; ceasefire; PA reform and elections; IDF return to positions of 28 September 2000; settlement freeze and removal of outposts built since 2001.
- **Phase II (2003):** an international conference and a state with provisional borders.
- **Phase III (2004–2005):** permanent status on borders, Jerusalem, refugees and settlements; end of conflict; Arab–Israeli peace.
- [Wikipedia: Road map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_map_for_peace)
- Israel's cabinet approved it on 25 May 2003 with 14 reservations, including:
  - dismantling of militant groups before any progress;
  - US-only monitoring;
  - a demilitarised state, with Israeli control of borders, airspace and the electromagnetic spectrum;
  - recognition of Israel as a Jewish state and no refugee return to Israel.
  - Abbas rejected the reservations.
  - [Wikipedia: Road map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_map_for_peace)
- Endorsed by UNSC Resolution 1515 (November 2003) — [Wikipedia: Road map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_map_for_peace)
- **Why it stalled:**
  - The June 2003 hudna collapsed within weeks.
  - By the end of 2003 neither the PA's security obligations nor Israel's withdrawal and freeze obligations had been met.
  - Sharon announced unilateral Gaza disengagement in April 2004. Bush's reply letter accepted that large settlement blocs would be reflected in a final deal, which Palestinians saw as a shift in US policy.
  - [Wikipedia: Road map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_map_for_peace)

**Geneva Initiative (finished 12 October 2003; launched 1 December 2003)**
- Led by Yossi Beilin and Yasser Abed Rabbo (Jamal Zakut also named). Non-binding; neither government was obligated — [Wikipedia: Geneva Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Initiative)
- **Borders:** based on the 4 June 1967 lines (citing 242/338) with 1:1 swaps of "equal quality and quantity". The map border is "final and indisputable" — [Geneva Accord summary](https://geneva-accord.org/the-accord/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Initiative)
- **Settlements:**
  - Israel annexes under 3% of the West Bank with an equal exchange.
  - Annexed: East Jerusalem settlements, Gush Etzion (not Efrat), Ma'ale Adumim, Giv'at Ze'ev, the Modi'in settlements, Alfei Menashe and the area south of Qalqilya, and a strip to Latrun.
  - Ariel and Efrat are explicitly not annexed.
  - Swap land would come from west of Hebron and along Gaza's eastern border.
  - Evacuated settlements stay intact for Palestinian use, with their value credited toward refugee compensation.
  - A corridor between Gaza and the West Bank would be under Israeli sovereignty and Palestinian administration.
  - [Palestine-Israel Journal](https://pij.org/articles/16); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Initiative)
- **Withdrawal timetable:** first phase within 9 months, full withdrawal within 30 months; Israeli forces stay in the Jordan Valley under the Multinational Force (MF) for 36 more months — [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/16)
- **Security:**
  - A "non-militarized" state with a "strong security force".
  - The MF handles external defence and border observation.
  - Two Israeli early-warning stations for 15 years.
  - Indefinite Israeli air-force training use of Palestinian airspace.
  - A joint security committee meeting at least monthly.
  - [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/16); [Geneva Accord summary](https://geneva-accord.org/the-accord/)
- **Jerusalem:**
  - Jewish neighbourhoods Israeli, Arab neighbourhoods Palestinian; mutually recognised capitals.
  - The Old City stays open "as one whole". Al-Haram al-Sharif is Palestinian, with a multinational presence on the compound; the Jewish Quarter and Western Wall are Israeli.
  - No digging or construction on the Haram/Temple Mount without both parties' approval; Jewish visitors have access.
  - An Old City Policing Unit and an Implementation and Verification Group (IVG).
  - [Geneva Accord summary](https://geneva-accord.org/the-accord/); [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/16); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Initiative)
- **Refugees:**
  - Based on 194, 242 and the API.
  - Five residency options: Palestine; areas transferred from Israel; host country; third country; Israel. Entry to Israel is at Israel's discretion and capped by reference to the average number taken by third countries.
  - Compensation for refugeehood and lost property through an international commission; a two-year window to choose.
  - UNRWA ends five years after the commission starts work.
  - Refugee claims are deemed fulfilled.
  - [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/16); [Geneva Accord summary](https://geneva-accord.org/the-accord/)
- **Recognition:** Palestinians recognise the Jewish people's right to statehood and Israel as its "national home"; Israel recognises Palestine as the Palestinian people's national home. "End of conflict. End of all claims." — [Geneva Accord summary](https://geneva-accord.org/the-accord/); [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/16)
- Annexes covering security, borders, water, environment, economy and Jerusalem were released in September 2009 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Initiative)
- **Reactions:**
  - Arafat praised a "brave initiative" but approved it only in principle.
  - The Sharon government condemned it.
  - Supporters included Bush, Powell, Clinton and 58 former world leaders.
  - Palestinian critics objected to trading the right of return for statehood.
  - A 2010 poll found about 49% Palestinian and 52% Israeli support.
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Initiative)
- **Critiques (PIJ):** sovereignty limits risk "almost turning the Palestinian state into an international protectorate"; few refugee returns. A December 2003 PSR poll found 19% Palestinian support and 44% opposition; once the terms were explained, 39% support and 58% opposition, with the strongest opposition on refugees (72%) and sovereignty limits (76%) — [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/16)

### Inferences
- Geneva is the most fully specified "maximal compromise" in the two-state family and is the best template for a parameter-driven game variant. The Road Map is a "process" variant: sequencing and conditionality, with the provisional-borders state as an intermediate node.
- The Road Map's Israeli reservations (Jewish-state recognition, demilitarisation, airspace and spectrum control) prefigure the later Netanyahu-era conditions and the 2020 Trump plan.

### Gaps
- The commonly cited Geneva land figure (about 2.2%) and the number of settlers evacuated were not confirmed; PIJ says only "less than 3%".
- The 2009 Geneva annexes (water allocations, detailed MF composition) were not read. The official geneva-accord.org text page gave principles only.
- Geneva's water arrangements are a gap, as are water and airspace terms for most frameworks.

## 6. Annapolis (2007) and the Olmert offer to Abbas (2008)

### Takeaway
After the Annapolis launch (November 2007), PM Ehud Olmert made the most far-reaching Israeli offer on record. Territory: about 6.3–6.8% annexed for about 5.5–5.8% swapped plus a safe passage (figures vary by source). Jerusalem: a Holy Basin with no sovereignty, under a multinational body. Refugees: about 5,000 over five years on humanitarian grounds. Abbas did not accept it. He says he was not allowed to keep or study the map, and the process ended with Olmert's departure and changed leadership.

### Cited Findings
- **Annapolis (November 2007):** the PLO, Israel and the US agreed on a two-state outline for negotiations; no agreement followed — [Wikipedia: Two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution)
- **Palestinian summary of the "package" made on 31 August 2008** (Palestine Papers; based on Erekat's notes of 9 September 2008):
  - Israel annexes 6.8% of the West Bank, including Gush Etzion (with Efrat), Ma'ale Adumim, Giv'at Ze'ev, Ariel and all East Jerusalem settlements (with Har Homa), in exchange for the equivalent of 5.5% of Israeli territory. Percentages are on Israeli calculations, excluding East Jerusalem and No-Man's-Land.
  - A "safe passage" under Israeli sovereignty and Palestinian control, not counted in the percentages.
  - A Bethlehem–Ramallah road bypassing East Jerusalem.
  - No mention of the Jordan Valley.
  - [Summary of Olmert's "Package" Offer, Palestine Papers (PDF hosted by INSS)](https://www.inss.org.il/he/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/systemfiles/summary%20of%20Ehud%20Olmert%20Package%20offer%20to%20Abu%20Mazen.pdf)
- **Jerusalem (same summary):**
  - East Jerusalem divided along Clinton lines, except the "Holy Basin" (about 0.04% of the West Bank per Olmert), whose sovereignty is deferred.
  - The Holy Basin to be negotiated bilaterally with the involvement of the US, Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Egypt, without power to impose.
  - [Palestine Papers summary (INSS PDF)](https://www.inss.org.il/he/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/systemfiles/summary%20of%20Ehud%20Olmert%20Package%20offer%20to%20Abu%20Mazen.pdf)
- **Refugees (same summary):**
  - Israel acknowledges the suffering of, but not responsibility for, the refugees (with mention of Jewish suffering).
  - Israel takes in 1,000 refugees a year for five years on "humanitarian" grounds; family reunification continues.
  - Compensation, not restitution, is the only remedy.
  - The package "apparently made no mention of security".
  - [Palestine Papers summary (INSS PDF)](https://www.inss.org.il/he/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/systemfiles/summary%20of%20Ehud%20Olmert%20Package%20offer%20to%20Abu%20Mazen.pdf)
- **Olmert's map of 16 September 2008 (Israeli and press accounts):**
  - Israel annexes 6.3% and compensates with 5.8% plus a corridor. Olmert's office said its map differed from the version Haaretz later published — [search-result summary of multiple reports incl. Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/olmert-offered-to-swap-land-for-peace-report-says-1.554909); [Middle East Forum](https://www.meforum.org/more-details-of-olmert-offer-disclosed)
  - The Jewish Virtual Library gives: Israel retains about 6.4% (Olmert later said 4.9%) and cedes 5.8% of Israeli land (areas near Afula–Tirat Tzvi, Lachish, the Judean desert, the Gaza periphery).
  - Jerusalem: the Holy Basin (Old City, Mount of Olives, City of David, part of Silwan) under no sovereignty, "jointly administered by five nations" (Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Palestine, Israel, US).
  - Refugees: 1,000 a year for five years; an international compensation fund; recognition of Jews displaced from Arab countries.
  - Security: no army or air force; an international (possibly NATO) force on the Jordan border; no foreign armies or military pacts; Israeli airspace access.
  - Sparsely populated settlements evacuated.
  - Framed as "end of conflict and end of claims".
  - [JVL: Ehud Olmert's Peace Offer](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/ehud-olmert-s-peace-offer)
- **Map revealed in 2025:** a version of the 2008 map became public in February 2025 via a BBC documentary. Separately, a sketch Abbas drew of the offer has been published — [Israel Hayom, 24 Feb 2025](https://israelhayom.com/2025/02/24/unseen-2008-peace-map-reveals-path-not-taken); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/olmerts-2008-palestinian-state-map-revealed-for-first-time/); [Times of Israel: hand-drawn map](https://www.timesofisrael.com/hand-drawn-map-shows-what-olmert-offered-for-peace/)
- **Palestinian counter-position:** accounts say Palestinians discussed swaps of about 1.9% against Olmert's 6.3%; Israeli Foreign Ministry sources said the Palestinians would exchange only 1–2% — [Middle East Forum](https://www.meforum.org/more-details-of-olmert-offer-disclosed)
- **Why it failed:**
  - Olmert brought one copy of the map to the 16 September 2008 meeting and Abbas could not keep it. Abbas said he needed to consult advisers before a follow-up meeting, which never took place — [St. Louis Jewish Light (JTA)](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/abbas-says-he-rejected-08-peace-offer-because-no-time-to-review-details)
  - Abbas said a symbolic refugee intake did not resolve an issue affecting millions of descendants — [search summary of Fox News/AP reporting](https://www.foxnews.com/world/palestinian-leader-shines-new-light-on-failed-2008-peace-talks)
  - Olmert: "To this day, I cannot understand why the Palestinian leadership did not accept..." (Washington Post, 17 July 2009, quoted by JVL) — [JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/ehud-olmert-s-peace-offer)
- Abbas later reportedly asked Trump (2017) to base talks on the 2008 Olmert maps — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/abbas-told-trump-to-base-peace-talks-on-2008-maps-report/)

### Inferences
- The swap ratio in the Olmert offer (annex 6.3–6.8% / compensate 5.5–5.8%) brackets the Israeli ask. The Palestinian ask (about 1.9–3%, 1:1) marks the other end. For modelling, a "land swap %" slider from about 2% to about 7% covers the whole negotiated range from Geneva/Taba-Palestinian to Olmert/Taba-Israeli.
- The Holy Basin "no sovereignty / multinational trusteeship" device is the main Olmert innovation on Jerusalem. It works as an intermediate option between divided and Israeli sovereignty.

### Gaps
- Primary confirmation was not obtained for the reasons Olmert left office or how they affected Abbas's calculation (Olmert's corruption investigation and 2009 departure, the December 2008 Gaza war). Excluded for lack of a fetched source.
- The figures conflict (6.3/5.8 vs 6.8/5.5 vs 6.4 vs 4.9), and no single authoritative version exists.

## 7. The Kerry framework (2013–2014), the Kerry principles (2016) and the 2020 Trump plan as a contrasting variant

### Takeaway
The nine months of US-mediated talks (July 2013–April 2014) produced a draft US "framework". Its draft language included borders "based on the 1967 lines with mutual agreed swaps", recognition of Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people, a Palestinian capital in Jerusalem (later drafts), and refugee compensation with Israeli discretion over admissions. Talks collapsed over prisoner releases, settlement announcements, the PA joining UN conventions and the Fatah–Hamas unity deal. Kerry later published six principles (December 2016). The 2020 Trump plan marks the opposite end of the "two-state" label: about 30% annexation, and conditional, limited Palestinian sovereignty.

### Cited Findings
- **Talks:** began 29 July 2013, with a nine-month deadline of 29 April 2014. Negotiators: Tzipi Livni and Yitzhak Molcho (Israel); Saeb Erekat and Mohammad Shtayyeh (PLO); Martin Indyk and Frank Lowenstein (US) — [Wikipedia: 2013–14 talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%932014_Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_talks)
- **Prisoner releases:** Israel agreed to release 104 pre-Oslo prisoners in four tranches (13 August, 29 October and 30 December 2013). The fourth was withheld on 28 March 2014 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%932014_Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_talks). The New Republic reports Netanyahu had meant about 80 prisoners, not 104 — [New Republic, July 2014](https://newrepublic.com/article/118751/how-israel-palestine-peace-deal-died)
- **Framework draft language (New Republic reconstruction):**
  - Borders "negotiated based on the 1967 lines with mutual agreed swaps"; an Israeli official said public knowledge of this would have caused "a political earthquake".
  - Recognition of Israel as "the nation-state of the Jewish people", inserted over Abbas's objections.
  - A Palestinian capital in Jerusalem in a later draft; Netanyahu had accepted only vague wording on "Palestinian aspirations".
  - Refugees: compensation for 1948 refugees and for Jews from Arab countries; the refugee problem to be "solved within the new Palestinian state"; Israel admits some refugees on humanitarian grounds at its "sole discretion".
  - Security: Netanyahu sought a long-term IDF presence in the Jordan Valley; Abbas demanded full withdrawal within three years. Gen. John Allen's plan (early warning, monitoring at crossings, US technology) was liked by Livni and most security officials; Ya'alon called it worthless.
  - Netanyahu said he did not intend "to evacuate any settlements"; his office floated settlers staying under Palestinian sovereignty.
  - [New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/118751/how-israel-palestine-peace-deal-died); see also [JTA, 29 Jan 2014](https://www.jta.org/2014/01/29/united-states/report-kerry-framework-includes-shared-jerusalem-jewish-state-recognition)
- **Reported percentages:** about 80% of settler homes redesignated as Israeli. Palestinians reportedly sought Israeli annexation of about 3% and Israel about 10% — [Wikipedia, citing Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%932014_Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_talks)
- **Jordan Valley (leaked accounts):** an Israeli presence along the Jordan for 4–10 years in some accounts; these come from partisan outlets — [search summary of reports incl. Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/177643)
- **Abbas:** in August 2013 he said the right of return would likely have to be waived. In March 2014 he rejected recognising Israel as a Jewish state, citing the 1988 and 1993 recognitions. At the 17 March 2014 White House meeting, Obama urged him not to "quibble"; Abbas gave no formal response — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%932014_Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_talks); [New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/118751/how-israel-palestine-peace-deal-died)
- **Collapse:**
  - The fourth prisoner tranche was withheld (28 March 2014).
  - 708 units in Gilo/East Jerusalem were announced around 1 April; Peace Now counted nearly 14,000 settler homes approved during the talks.
  - Abbas signed 15 international conventions on 1 April 2014.
  - The Fatah–Hamas unity deal of 23 April 2014 led Israel to suspend talks the next day.
  - Indyk: "We definitely tested the proposition... And it wasn't enough."
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%932014_Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_talks); [New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/118751/how-israel-palestine-peace-deal-died); [BBC, 23 Apr 2014](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-27128902)
- **Kerry's six principles (28 December 2016, after UNSC 2334 passed 14–0 with a US abstention on 23 December 2016):**
  1. Borders based on the 1967 lines with mutually agreed equivalent swaps.
  2. The UNGA 181 vision of "two states for two peoples, one Jewish and one Arab", with mutual recognition and equal rights.
  3. A just, agreed, realistic refugee solution that does not "affect the essential character of Israel".
  4. Jerusalem as the recognised capital of both states, with protected access to holy sites.
  5. Israel's security needs met, the occupation fully ended, and a sovereign non-militarised Palestine.
  6. End of conflict and all claims, normalisation and regional security in line with the API.
  - Netanyahu rejected the speech; Abbas said he was ready to resume talks if settlement building halted.
  - [Wikipedia: John Kerry Parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry_Parameters); [UN press release on 2334](https://press.un.org/en/2016/sc12657.doc.htm); [Haaretz on Kerry's principles (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20180217025555/https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/kerry-s-peace-principles-jerusalem-would-be-capital-of-two-states-1.5479186)
- **2020 Trump "Peace to Prosperity" plan (28 January 2020), the minimalist variant:**
  - Netanyahu announced intended annexation of the Jordan Valley and settlements, and later planned about 30% of the West Bank.
  - A Palestinian state after up to four years, conditional on compliance, with Israel controlling borders, airspace, the electromagnetic spectrum and security. Netanyahu called it "conditional, limited sovereignty".
  - A capital in areas east and north of the barrier (Kafr Aqab, eastern Shuafat, Abu Dis).
  - Conditions: disarm Hamas and Islamic Jihad; recognise Israel as a Jewish state; drop ICC/ICJ actions; end "prisoner and martyr payments"; total demilitarisation.
  - About $50 billion in economic investment.
  - The PLO rejected it. PM Shtayyeh proposed a counter-plan with 1:1 swaps (June 2020).
  - [Wikipedia: 2020 Trump plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_plan)
  - Annexation was suspended after the August 2020 Israel–UAE agreement — [Wikipedia: Proposed annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Israeli_annexation_of_the_West_Bank)

### Inferences
- The Kerry framework is the only US-drafted text that combined "1967 lines with swaps" with "Jewish nation-state" recognition. It is the clearest example of the trade-off at the centre of the US approach after 2009.
- The 2020 Trump plan stretches the "two-state" label to its minimalist limit: a state in name, with Israeli security sovereignty. Any game "two-state family" should include it as the low end of the sovereignty spectrum, sitting between a full two-state deal and autonomy or annexation endgames.

### Gaps
- No primary text of the 2014 US framework exists publicly. Details rest on leaks and reconstructions (New Republic; Indyk interviews).
- The Kerry December 2016 speech transcript on state.gov could not be retrieved (site error). The principles are from Wikipedia and Haaretz summaries.
- 2020 plan specifics (exact Negev swap areas, refugee numbers) were not confirmed from the fetched text.

## 8. Recent revivals (2024–November 2026): PA positions, recognitions, the New York Declaration, UNGA/UNSC votes, Knesset resolutions and moves on the ground

### Takeaway
Since 2024 the two-state framework has gained diplomatic weight while losing ground on the ground. About 157 of 193 UN members now recognise Palestine (France, UK, Canada, Australia, Portugal and others in September 2025). The UNGA endorsed the French–Saudi New York Declaration 142–10–12 on 12 September 2025. UNSC 2803 (November 2025) mentions a conditional "pathway" to statehood. In the opposite direction, the Knesset voted against Palestinian statehood (July 2024, 68–9) and for West Bank sovereignty (July 2025, 71–13; October 2025 preliminary reading 25–24). The Israeli government approved record settlement planning, E1 and land-registration measures (2025–2026) that critics call de facto annexation.

### Cited Findings
**Recognitions**
- 2024 recognitions: Barbados (19 April), Jamaica (22 April), Trinidad and Tobago (2 May), Bahamas (7 May), Ireland, Norway and Spain (28 May, coordinated), Slovenia (4 June), Armenia (21 June) — [Wikipedia: International recognition of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine)
- 2025 recognitions:
  - Mexico (formalisation dated variously 2023/2025).
  - Canada, Australia, UK and Portugal on 21 September 2025.
  - France, Monaco, Luxembourg, Malta and Andorra on 22 September 2025.
  - San Marino on 23 September 2025.
  - [Wikipedia: International recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine); [NPR, 22 Sep 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/22/nx-s1-5550376/some-major-western-countries-announce-the-recognition-of-a-palestinian-state); [Dawn, 21 Sep 2025](https://www.dawn.com/news/1943686)
- **Count:** 157 of 193 UN member states (about 81%) as of September 2025 — [Wikipedia: International recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine).   - France announced on 24 July 2025 that it would recognise at the UNGA.

  - No new recognitions dated to 2026 were listed.
  - Germany, Italy, Japan, South Korea and the US still do not recognise; Italy's position is contingent on Hamas conditions.
  - [Wikipedia: International recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine)

**UN votes**

On 10 May 2024, UNGA Resolution ES-10/23 found Palestine qualified for membership, gave it extra rights (seating, proposing items, no vote) and asked the UNSC to reconsider — [Wikipedia: International recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine)

**Trump Gaza plan and UNSC 2803**
- The 20-point plan was released on 29 September 2025; the ceasefire took effect on 10 October 2025.

### Inferences
- The 2025 recognitions and the New York Declaration moved the two-state model from a negotiated endgame toward an international "outside-in" variant. In that variant, statehood is recognised first and borders, security and Gaza governance are filled in by a multilateral mechanism (stabilisation mission, PA reform, the Board of Peace) rather than by bilateral final-status talks. A game could model this as a separate track with its own success conditions: Hamas disarmament, PA elections, Israeli acceptance.
- Israeli formal positions (Knesset 2024 and 2025 votes) and Israeli administrative measures (E1, land registration) are moving away from the 1967-lines template, while US policy (Trump 2025) has so far opposed formal annexation. This makes "de facto annexation without formal sovereignty" the status-quo trajectory against which two-state variants compete.

- No reliable source was found for the status of the Abbas-pledged elections in 2026, a PA interim constitution, or a new PA prime minister or vice-president (beyond al-Sheikh's title).
- The Saudi position on normalisation and statehood in 2025–2026, and the vote counts for the Ma'ale Adumim sovereignty bill (October 2025), were not confirmed.
- No 2026 tally of recognising states from a primary or wire source was found.

## 9. Public opinion: Israeli Jews, Arab citizens of Israel and Palestinians

### Takeaway
Support for two states in principle fell sharply after 7 October 2023 among Israeli Jews (21% in the July 2024 Pulse, from 42% in 2020) and dipped and then partly recovered among Palestinians. The latest PCPSR poll (October 2026) has 53% support for a 1967-lines state. Arab citizens of Israel remain the most supportive group (72%, Pulse 2024). Belief that coexistence is possible is at record lows (Pew 2026: 17% of Israelis, 21% of West Bank/East Jerusalem Palestinians). Results depend heavily on question wording, and detailed packages with incentives can produce majorities.

### Cited Findings

- **Two-state support:**
  - Israeli Jews: 21% (34% Dec 2022; 42% Sept 2020).
  - Palestinians: 40% (33% in 2022; 43% in 2020).

### Inferences
- In game terms, "public acceptance" should be a separate variable for each of three constituencies:
  - Israeli Jews: low, about 16–27% depending on wording.
  - Arab citizens of Israel: high but with high non-response; about 23% on coexistence, 72% on support.
  - Palestinians: middling and volatile, about 33–53% depending on wording and on whether "1967 lines with East Jerusalem" is specified.
- Package design and incentives (the Pulse experiments) can shift these by 20–35 points.
- The gap between support for a 1967 state and belief that it is achievable (PPC 2026: 65.6% would accept one, but 83.6% think it unlikely in the near future) suggests that feasibility, not preference, is now the main constraint on Palestinian opinion.

## 11. Cross-framework comparison: core-issue values for a matrix and game model

### Takeaway
The negotiated two-state variants form a fairly narrow band on most issues. Borders: the 1967 lines plus 2–7% swaps. Jerusalem: split by demography, with an Old City and holy-sites mechanism. Security: non-militarised, with an international force and phased IDF withdrawal. Refugees: return mainly to Palestine, plus a small capped number to Israel at its discretion. They differ in degree: annexation %, holy-site sovereignty formula, timetable and refugee cap. The 2020 Trump plan and the 2025 "outside-in" recognition track sit outside this band at opposite ends.

### Cited Findings
- **Land annexed by Israel / swap:**
  - Camp David: final Palestinian share about 92% including a 1% swap (reported), which implies about 8–9% annexed (my arithmetic) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)
  - Clinton: 4–6% annexed, 1–3% swap — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)
  - Taba: Israel about 6% / about 3%; Palestinians about 3% at 1:1 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit)
  - Geneva: under 3%, 1:1 — [PIJ](https://pij.org/articles/16)
  - Olmert: 6.3% / 5.8% (Israeli account) or 6.8% / 5.5% (Palestinian summary) — [INSS-hosted PDF](https://www.inss.org.il/he/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/systemfiles/summary%20of%20Ehud%20Olmert%20Package%20offer%20to%20Abu%20Mazen.pdf); [JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/ehud-olmert-s-peace-offer)

### Inferences
- **Suggested game variants for a "two-state" endgame family**, ranked by how far they move toward the Palestinian position:
  1. "Geneva-max": under 3% at 1:1; Haram Palestinian with an MF; refugee cap at the third-country average.
  2. "Clinton/Taba": 4–6% annexed; demographic Jerusalem; 36+36 months.
