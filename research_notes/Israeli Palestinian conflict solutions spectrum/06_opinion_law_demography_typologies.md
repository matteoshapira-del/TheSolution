# Cross-cutting baseline for all solution models: public opinion, international law, demography and territory, typologies, and drivers (current to early November 2026)

Research note for the report writer. The research ran 6 November 2026. The web-search budget for the session ran out before every lead could be checked. Remaining gaps are listed under each question. Where a figure comes from a secondary outlet, an advocacy group or an aggregator, the note says so. ## 1. Public opinion (2023–2026 and long-run trends): two states, confederation, one equal state, one unequal state, annexation, transfer, armed struggle vs negotiations

### Takeaway
Since 2023, Israeli Jews and Palestinians have moved in opposite directions. Jewish-Israeli support for two states reached record lows: 21% in the July 2024 joint Pulse, 16% of Jews in Pew 2025–26 saying peaceful coexistence with a Palestinian state is possible, and 27% of all Israelis in Gallup 2025. In the 2024 Pulse, more Israeli Jews backed annexation without equal rights for Palestinians (42%) than backed two states. Palestinian support for two states has recovered since its 2022–23 low. It is about 40–44% when the concept is asked without detail and 53–59% when the question specifies a state on the 1967 borders (PCPSR 2026, Arab Barometer 2025). A majority on both sides still thinks two states are impractical. Arab citizens of Israel are the only group with consistent majority support for two states (72% in 2024) and with large minorities supporting confederation or one equal state.

### Inferences
- **Proposed scoring baseline for the game (late 2024–2026 data):**

  | Option | Israeli Jews | Arab citizens of Israel | Palestinians (West Bank and Gaza) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Two states | ~16–27% | ~70% | 40–44% generic; 53–59% on 1967 borders |
  | Confederation | ~12% | ~52% | ~7–35% depending on pollster |
  | One equal state | ~14% | ~49% | ~6–25% |
  | One unequal state / annexation without rights | ~42% | — | 33% (Palestinian-dominated mirror) |

  Armed struggle among Palestinians swings between 27% and 41% across 2025–26 PCPSR polls.
- Detail and framing move numbers a great deal:
  - "1967 borders" adds 10–15 points among Palestinians.
  - A regional peace and normalisation frame wins about 62–65% on both sides.
  - Single incentives can flip the package to a majority (see Q5).

  A game could model a "framing/package" multiplier rather than fixed preferences.
- Polls agree that perceived feasibility is low: about 60% or more on each side say two states are no longer practical. Support and perceived practicality move together (PCPSR's repeated finding). This suggests modelling "feasibility" as a hidden state variable that drives support.
- Large undecided or "reject all" blocs (44% of Palestinians, 34% of Israeli Jews in 2024) mean many people do not yet hold a fixed position.

## 2. International law: UNSC 242/338/2334 and post-2023 resolutions; UNGA 181/194; Geneva IV Art. 49; ICJ 2004 and 2024 opinions; UNGA ES-10/24; the ICJ genocide case; developments to 2026

### Takeaway
The legal baseline is set by UN resolutions and ICJ opinions. Advisory opinions and General Assembly resolutions are not binding in themselves. The baseline consists of:
- the "land for peace" formula with "secure and recognized boundaries" (242/338);
- the illegality of settlements and non-recognition of changes to the 4 June 1967 lines except by agreement (2334);
- the ICJ's 2024 finding that Israel's continued presence in the territory occupied in 1967 is unlawful and must end "as rapidly as possible," with settlement activity ceasing and settlers evacuated;
- a refugee formula of return and compensation (194);
- the 1947 partition template of two states with an international regime for Jerusalem (181).

From 2025 to 2026, diplomacy added mass recognition of Palestine (about 157 UN members by late Sept 2025), the "New York Declaration" (142 votes) and UNSC 2803, which endorsed the Trump Gaza plan and a conditional "pathway" to statehood. On the ground, Israel's February 2026 measures in the West Bank were described by critics and UN bodies as de facto annexation. Israel described them as administrative. The UNGA 12-month deadline passed in September 2025 without compliance or enforcement.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational texts (all available in the UN Official Document System)**
- **UNGA 181 (II), 29 Nov 1947.** Partition of Mandatory Palestine into independent Arab and Jewish states, with Jerusalem as a corpus separatum under a special international regime and an economic union. — [A/RES/181(II)](https://docs.un.org/en/A/RES/181(II))
- **UNGA 194 (III), 11 Dec 1948, para. 11.** "Refugees wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date, and … compensation should be paid for the property of those choosing not to return." The resolution also created the UN Conciliation Commission for Palestine. — [A/RES/194(III)](https://docs.un.org/en/A/RES/194(III))
- **UNSC 242, 22 Nov 1967.**
  - Emphasises "the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war."
  - Calls for "withdrawal of Israel armed forces from territories occupied in the recent conflict." The English text has no "the" before "territories," which has long been the subject of interpretive dispute.
  - Calls for termination of belligerency and the right of every state "to live in peace within secure and recognized boundaries."
  - Calls for "a just settlement of the refugee problem."
  - — [S/RES/242(1967)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/242(1967))
- **UNSC 338, 22 Oct 1973.** Ceasefire; implementation of 242 "in all of its parts"; negotiations "between the parties concerned under appropriate auspices." — [S/RES/338(1973)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/338(1973))
- **UNSC 478 (1980)** declared Israel's 1980 Basic Law, which proclaimed "Jerusalem, complete and united" as its capital, "null and void." — [Wikipedia: East Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jerusalem)
- **UNSC 2334, 23 Dec 2016** (14–0, with the US abstaining).
  - Settlements have "no legal validity" and are a "flagrant violation under international law."
  - Demands that Israel "immediately and completely cease all settlement activities in the occupied Palestinian territory, including East Jerusalem."
  - The Council "will not recognize any changes to the 4 June 1967 lines, including with regard to Jerusalem, other than those agreed by the parties through negotiations."
  - Calls on states to "distinguish … between the territory of the State of Israel and the territories occupied since 1967."
  - Calls for preventing violence against civilians, including acts of terror, and incitement. — [S/RES/2334(2016)](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2334(2016))
- **Fourth Geneva Convention, Art. 49.**
  - Para. 1 prohibits "individual or mass forcible transfers, as well as deportations of protected persons from occupied territory … regardless of their motive."
  - Para. 6: "The Occupying Power shall not deport or transfer parts of its own civilian population into the territory it occupies." This paragraph is the legal basis for the illegality of settlements. Israel contests its application. — [ICRC IHL database, GC IV Art. 49](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/gciv-1949/article-49)
- **ICJ Wall Advisory Opinion, 9 July 2004.** The Court found that the wall's route inside the occupied territory, including in and around East Jerusalem, is contrary to international law; that the Fourth Geneva Convention applies; and that settlements were established in breach of international law. It found that Israel must cease construction, dismantle the wall and make reparation, and that states must not recognise the illegal situation. — [ICJ case 131](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/131)

**ICJ Advisory Opinion, 19 July 2024 (Legal Consequences arising from the Policies and Practices of Israel in the OPT, including East Jerusalem)**
- Findings in the operative part:
  1. Israel's continued presence in the Occupied Palestinian Territory is unlawful (11–4; dissenting: Vice-President Sebutinde and Judges Tomka, Abraham and Aurescu).
  2. Israel must end that presence "as rapidly as possible."
  3. Israel must cease immediately all new settlement activity and evacuate all settlers.
  4. Israel must make reparation to all persons affected.
  5. All states must not recognise the situation as legal and must not render aid or assistance in maintaining it.
  6. International organisations, including the UN, must likewise not recognise it.
  7. The UNGA and UNSC should consider the "precise modalities" of ending the presence.
  - — [IHL Centre summary](https://www.ihlcentre.org/news/summary-icj-advisory-opinion-19-july-2024); [UNRIC](https://unric.org/en/international-justice-the-icj-demands-the-end-of-israels-presence-in-the-occupied-palestinian-territory/); [Leigh Day analysis](https://www.leighday.co.uk/news/blog/2024-blogs/icj-declares-israel-s-continued-presence-in-the-occupied-palestinian-territory-is-unlawful-and-should-come-to-an-end-as-rapidly-as-possible/); [ICJ case 186](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/186)
- Fourteen separate opinions or declarations were appended, including Vice-President Sebutinde's dissent. — [ICJ case 186](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/186)
- The Times of Israel summarised the Court as having found that Israel had already de facto annexed large parts of the West Bank. — [Times of Israel, 19 Feb 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/)

**UNGA ES-10/24 (18 Sept 2024) and the 12-month deadline**
- Vote: 124 in favour, 14 against (including Israel and the US), 43 abstentions.
- The resolution demanded that Israel end its unlawful presence within 12 months. It is non-binding. The Secretary-General's implementation report is A/79/588. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2571833); [SG report A/79/588](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4069921/files/A_79_588-EN.pdf); [A/RES/ES-10/24](https://docs.un.org/en/A/RES/ES-10/24)
- The deadline passed around 18 Sept 2025 without Israeli compliance or any formal UN enforcement step. Advocacy groups called this a failure of enforcement. — [Bernama/Anadolu](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php/sports/world/news.php?id=2468983); [Law for Palestine](https://law4palestine.org/join-us-the-ungas-12-month-deadline-to-end-the-israeli-occupation-has-passed-what-comes-next-after-non-compliance/)

  - Condemns the 7 October 2023 attack.
  - Demands that Hamas release the hostages, disarm and give up control of Gaza.
  - Calls for the PA to govern all Palestinian territory, via a transitional committee in Gaza.
  - Sets out a time-bound path (The National: 15 months) to a demilitarised, sovereign Palestinian state. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/middle-east/un-general-assembly-backs-saudi-french-new-york-declaration-for-israeli-palestinian-peace-excluding-hamas-2615074); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2025/09/12/un-general-assembly-votes-in-favour-of-a-future-palestinian-state-but-without-hamas)
- **Recognition wave (21–22 Sept 2025).** The UK, Canada, Australia and Portugal recognised Palestine on 21 Sept, followed by France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Malta, Monaco and Andorra at the UNGA. Reported total: about 157 of 193 UN members. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2025/09/21/uk-recognises-palestine-to-boost-two-state-solution); [Free Press Journal (157 list)](https://www.freepressjournal.in/world/which-are-the-157-countries-that-recognise-palestine-as-a-state-heres-the-full-list); [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/09/22/who-recognises-the-state-of-palestine-who-doesnt-and-why-does-it-matter)
- **UNSC 2803 (17 Nov 2025).** Adopted 13–0, with China and Russia abstaining.
  - Endorses Trump's 20-point "Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict" (29 Sept 2025).

### Inferences
- For the comparison matrix, each solution model can be scored for consistency with five legal anchors:
  1. 1967 lines as the baseline, with agreed swaps (2334, ICJ 2024);
  2. no settlements, or evacuation or agreed retention (Art. 49(6), ICJ 2024);
  3. Palestinian self-determination (ICJ 2024);
  4. refugees: return and/or compensation (194);
  5. no forcible transfer (Art. 49(1)).

  Annexation without rights and transfer-based outcomes would conflict with all five. Two-state and confederation models built on 1967 lines with swaps align most closely. A one equal state model satisfies self-determination only if both peoples consent.
- The record from 2024 to 2026 suggests a widening gap between legal and diplomatic consensus (ICJ, UNGA, recognitions) and conditions on the ground (Israeli measures, settlement growth), with enforcement weak. In game terms, "international legitimacy" and "facts on the ground" could be separate meters.

## 3. Demographic and territorial facts

### Takeaway
Israel's population was about 10.24 million in April 2026 (CBS), of whom 21.1% are Arab citizens. PCBS estimates about 3.43 million Palestinians in the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and about 2.13 million in Gaza at end-2025. Between the river and the sea, Jews plus "others" (about 7.8 million) and Palestinians/Arabs (about 7.4 million per PCBS) are close to numerical parity. Estimates of Jewish refugees from Arab countries come mainly from advocacy groups and are about 850,000.

### Inferences
- **Demographic parity.** Combining CBS (7.79m Jews and others) with PCBS (about 7.4m Palestinians in historic Palestine) suggests rough parity between the river and the sea. ## 4. Core final-status issues and scholarly typologies of solutions ("from one extreme to the other")

### Takeaway
The core issues are: borders and territory; security; Jerusalem and the holy sites; refugees; settlements; water and resources; mutual recognition and end of claims; and citizenship, residency and freedom of movement. The most systematic published typologies treat these issues as parameters and arrange solutions along an integration–separation axis:
- the unitary single state;
- a single state with Palestinian autonomy;
- a federation;
- a confederation (two states with open borders and shared institutions);
- classic two states.

The joint Pulse adds an axis for equality of rights: one equal state versus one unequal state. A two-axis spectrum, territorial arrangement against rights and equality, lets the report run from one extreme (exclusive control with transfer) to the other (the mirror-image exclusive Palestinian state) without conflating "one state" options that are opposites on rights.

### Cited Findings
- **INSS Memorandum No. 217 (Pnina Sharvit Baruch, Dec 2021).**
  - Examines four "one-state family" models: (1) a unitary state from the river to the sea; (2) an Israeli state containing a Palestinian autonomous area; (3) a federal state with Jewish and Palestinian districts; (4) a confederation of two states on Green Line borders, with no physical barrier, free movement and joint confederal institutions.
  - Assesses them on about 17 parameters: territory, settlements and Jerusalem; citizenship and residency; governmental authority and Palestinian participation; freedom of movement; refugees; security, social, economic and civil aspects; Jewish character; democratic and liberal character; implications for Israel's Arab citizens and the PA; Gaza; implementation and feasibility.
  - Notes that two states remain the leading framework internationally. — [INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/one-state-models/)
- **Joint Pulse operationalisation (PCPSR/TAU).** The Pulse tests three frameworks side by side: two states, one democratic state with equal rights, and one state in which one side dominates and the other lacks equal rights. It tests confederation separately. Its detailed two-state package fixes positions on every core issue:
  - demilitarisation;
  - the Green Line with equal swaps;
  - 100,000 refugees reunified in Israel;
  - divided Jerusalem and a holy-sites split;
  - democratic states;
  - a regional peace framework;
  - US and Arab guarantees;
  - end of conflict and claims. — [Joint Pulse summary (PDF)](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- **Confederation proposals.**
  - Omar M. Dajani and Limor Yehuda (Foreign Affairs, Sept 2024) argue that confederation is "at its core, a two-state solution." Each state controls areas such as education, foreign affairs, law enforcement, welfare and taxation, while joint bodies handle cross-border matters.
  - The Beilin/Husseini "Holy Land Confederation" is a parallel Israeli-Palestinian proposal.
  - A 2015 Lawfare analysis warned that building a confederation might be even harder than a two-state deal. — [Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/israel/two-state-solution-can-work); [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/middle-east/news/two-state-confederation-palestinian-israeli-public-figures-draw-up-proposal-2956336); [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/confederation-israel-palestine-principles-effective-design-and-continuing-challenges)
- **"One-state reality" school.** Michael Barnett, Nathan Brown, Marc Lynch and Shibley Telhami (eds.), *The One State Reality: What Is Israel/Palestine?* (Cornell UP, 2023), and their Foreign Affairs essay (May 2023), argue that a single state already exists de facto, with unequal rights. They treat this as the baseline from which any "solution" departs. Critics, such as the Washington Institute, called the essay "tendentious." — [Lynch, Abu Aardvark](https://abuaardvark.substack.com/p/israels-one-state-reality); [Washington Institute critique](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/no-state-solution)

### Inferences
**Proposed ordering for the report.** This is a synthesis of mine, not a published typology. It runs on two axes: territorial arrangement, and equality and self-determination. From one extreme to the other:
1. Greater Israel with population transfer or "voluntary emigration." Polled in Israel (Sorek/Hazkani; JPPI). Conflicts with Art. 49(1).
2. Annexation without equal rights: a single state with Jewish political dominance. 42% of Israeli Jews supported this in the 2024 Pulse.
3. Annexation of Area C or the settlement blocs with Palestinian autonomy or enclaves in Areas A and B (INSS model 2). Includes the current "de facto sovereignty" policy and status-quo "conflict management."
4. A limited-sovereignty or demilitarised Palestinian state with Israeli security control, such as the 2020 Trump plan or the 2803 "conditional pathway." Covered by other researchers.
5. Classic two states on the 1967 lines with swaps (Clinton, Geneva, Olmert, Pulse package).
6. Two-state confederation (Dajani and Yehuda; A Land for All; Holy Land Confederation).
7. Federation (INSS model 3).
8. One democratic or binational state with equal rights (INSS model 1, democratic version).
9. The mirror extreme: a single Palestinian state with limited rights for Jews. 33% of Palestinians supported this in the 2024 Pulse.

Regional or "Jordanian option" frames cut across positions 3–6 and can be shown as an overlay.

**Comparison axes**, each derivable from the INSS parameters and the Pulse package:
- **Borders:** the 1967 line with swaps, partial annexation, or none.
- **Security:** demilitarisation, Israeli overriding control, international forces.
- **Jerusalem:** divided, shared, international, or Israeli sovereignty.

## 5. Conditions and drivers that make each solution more or less likely (for endgame logic)

### Takeaway
Polling and expert sources point to six drivers that can be measured.
1. **Perceived feasibility.** Support falls when people believe a solution cannot happen.
2. **Violence and security.** War produced a long-term hardening among Israeli Jews and a short-term rise in Palestinian support for armed struggle. Palestinian support for armed struggle fell from 41% to 27% between 2025 and August 2026, then rose again to 33% in October 2026.
3. **Leadership and legitimacy.** Palestinians are disillusioned with both Hamas and Fatah. Israel's 27 Oct 2026 election produced no majority, and Arab parties won a record 19 seats.
4. **Incentives and packaging.** Single targeted incentives or a regional-normalisation frame can produce majorities.
5. **External pressure.** Recognitions, the ICJ, UNSC 2803 and US positions have shaped the agenda, but enforcement has been weak.
6. **Demography and facts on the ground.** Generational drift to the right among Israeli Jews, settlement growth, de facto annexation steps and Gaza's territorial compression.

### Cited Findings
- **Feasibility.** PCPSR repeatedly finds Palestinian support for two states linked to perceived practicality. In August 2026, 58% said two states were impractical because of settlements and 64% saw little or no chance of a state within five years. — [PCPSR Poll 97](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014); [Joint Pulse summary (PDF)](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- **Incentives (2024 Pulse).**
  - All six one-sided incentives offered to Israeli Jews raised support for the package from 25% to a majority, up to 60%. Examples: Palestinian textbook reform 60%; recognition of Israel as the state of the Jewish people with equal rights for Arab citizens 59%; ongoing security cooperation 57%; compensation for Jews from Arab countries 56%; consolidation of armed factions 51%.
  - Four of six incentives offered to Palestinians produced majorities, up to 55%. Examples: release of all prisoners 66%; labour access to Israel 65%; an end to Israeli military presence 54%; refugee compensation and rehabilitation 54%.
  - Paired incentives were weaker, though a joint textbook-incitement pair raised Israeli-Jewish support from 25% to 61%. — [Joint Pulse summary (PDF)](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- **Violence expectations (2024 Pulse).**
  - 52% of Israeli Jews and nearly 60% of settlers expect a two-state accord to increase violence. One-third of Israeli Jews think re-occupying Gaza would reduce violence.
  - 42% of Palestinians think an accord would lower violence (50% in Gaza, 37% in the West Bank), and 83% expect re-occupation to raise it.
  - 65% of Arab Israelis expect an accord to lower violence. — [Joint Pulse summary (PDF)](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- **"Breakthrough" moment (2024 Pulse).** Asked whether the war creates an opportunity for peace like that after the 1973 war, 41% of Palestinians (54% in Gaza), 52% of Arab Israelis and 23% of Israeli Jews agreed. — [PCPSR node 989](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/989)
- **Generational and religious demography in Israel.** Two-state support is 8% among Jews aged 18–35 and 38% among Jews 55+. Young Jews are more religious: among Jews aged 18–34, 32% are religious or Haredi, against 14% among those 55+. Haredi and national-religious support is 1–3%. — [Joint Pulse summary (PDF)](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)
- **Israeli politics (Oct–Nov 2026).**
  - The 27 Oct 2026 election produced no clear majority. Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats, with about 840,000 votes, 64% more than in 2022.
  - With the Arab parties, Gadi Eisenkot's anti-Netanyahu bloc would have 67 seats. Without them, the options are a unity deal or another election.
  - Lieberman rules out Arab partners. Eisenkot's coalition conditions include recognising Israel as a Jewish and democratic state.
  - Likud alleged fraud, which the election committee chair rejected. — [Al Jazeera, 3 Nov 2026](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/3/palestine-weekly-election-deadlock-meets-west-bank-sieges-gaza-killings)
- **Palestinian leadership.** 44% say neither Hamas nor Fatah deserves to lead. 82% in the West Bank want Abbas to resign. Faction ID in Oct 2026: Hamas 19%, Fatah 22%, "none" 33%. New independent lists poll strongly: the Masri list and Barghouti-linked lists at 22% each among decided likely voters. Elections were expected in late November 2026. — [PCPSR Poll 97](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014); [PCPSR Oct 2026](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)
- **Arab Barometer panel interpretations (Princeton, 2026).** Amaney Jamal described a "governance paradox": the PA is distrusted and Hamas is the only alternative retaining some trust. Shikaki said Gazans are angrier at Hamas than West Bankers are. Panelists agreed opinion is driven by events on the ground. — [Princeton Alumni Weekly](https://paw.princeton.edu/article/princeton-spia-event-dissects-new-polling-data-palestinian-attitudes)
- **External pressure.**
  - On the legal and diplomatic side: the 2024 ICJ obligations, ES-10/24, about 157 recognitions and the 142-vote New York Declaration.
  - The 12-month deadline passed without compliance.
  - UNSC 2803 ties any statehood "pathway" to PA reform and Gaza redevelopment, and ties IDF withdrawal to demilitarisation benchmarks. — [Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php/sports/world/news.php?id=2468983); [JVL on 2803](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/analysis-and-implications-of-unsc-resolution-2803)
- **Disarmament as a veto point.** 72–74% of Palestinians oppose Hamas disarming before full Israeli withdrawal. — [PCPSR Oct 2026](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)
- **Facts on the ground.** The 2025 settlement approvals were a record (41 new settlements; about 28,000 units). The 2026 Area C land registration is projected to take about 30 years and converts unregistered land to state land. Settler attacks hit record monthly levels in Oct 2026. — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/); [Al Jazeera](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/3/palestine-weekly-election-deadlock-meets-west-bank-sieges-gaza-killings)
- **Expert outlook.** Only about 3% of surveyed Middle East scholars saw two states as probable within ten years. Most expected a one-state reality with unequal rights if two states fail. — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/academic-experts-believe-that-middle-east-politics-are-actually-getting-worse)
- **Gaza governance trust.** 24% of Palestinians trust the Board of Peace (Aug 2026). — [PCPSR Poll 97](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)
- **Israeli anxieties.** IDI (Nov 2025) found that most Israelis expect Palestinian terrorism to stay the same or increase if a Palestinian state were declared, and 81% see a low chance of PA reform. — [JVL aggregation of IDI](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/israeli-polls-regarding-peace-with-the-palestinians)

### Inferences
**Suggested endgame-scoring variables and directions.** These are an inference from the findings above.

| Driver | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Violence level** (attacks, war) | Raises support for exclusionary or annexation outcomes among Israeli Jews. Raises Palestinian armed-struggle support in the short run, though Palestinian two-state support rose after 2023. Sustained calm plus credible security guarantees raises the value of separation models. |
| **Leadership change** (new Israeli coalition; Palestinian elections producing a new, legitimate leadership) | Probably the largest swing factor. The 2026 Israeli result shows Arab parties as kingmakers, and Palestinians favour new independent lists. |
| **Feasibility signal** (settlement freeze or expansion, recognitions, a credible timeline) | Shifts Palestinian support by roughly 10–15 points. Settlement growth lowers feasibility for two states and confederation and raises the unequal-one-state "default." |
| **Incentive packages** (textbooks, security cooperation, prisoners, labour access, refugee compensation, recognition of Israel as the Jewish state with equal rights) | Each can add about 25–35 points among the initially opposed, as measured in the Pulse. |
| **Regional normalisation** (Saudi track) | Pushes both publics above 60% for a two-state regional peace. |
| **External enforcement** (sanctions, conditionality, the ISF) | Has so far been weak. It could be modelled as low-probability but high-impact. |
| **Demographic drift** (younger, more religious Jewish electorate; parity river-to-sea) | Long-run pressure toward a rights crisis in any single-state outcome. Hardens Jewish-Israeli opinion against separation. |

**Gaza as gatekeeper.** Disarmament sequencing (72–74% of Palestinians oppose disarming first) and Israeli control of 53–70% of the Strip are likely gatekeepers for any comprehensive model.
