## Q1. Population transfer, "voluntary emigration" and "encouraged migration" (historical and current)

### Takeaway
"Transfer" runs from the 1937 Peel Commission and the Zionist debates of the 1930s–40s, through Kahane/Kach (banned) and Moledet ("transfer by agreement"), to proposals made after 7 October 2023: the Intelligence Ministry's Sinai paper, Smotrich and Ben-Gvir's "voluntary emigration", Trump's February 2025 "Gaza Riviera", the Defense Ministry's 2025 emigration directorate, and Ben-Gvir's 2026 campaign plan to move 1.86 million Gazans out over seven years. Egypt, Jordan, the Arab League and the UN rejected it outright. The UN and human-rights bodies call it forcible transfer, deportation or ethnic cleansing. By late 2026 it had not happened at scale: about 10,500 people left Gaza in the year to September 2026 (most for medical reasons) and about 6,000 returned. Even so, it is still a live platform of Israeli far-right parties.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Historical (1937–1990s)
- **Peel Commission (1937):** The Commission recommended partition together with a transfer of about 225,000 Arabs out of the area allotted to the proposed Jewish state — [Wikipedia, "1937 Ben-Gurion letter"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Ben-Gurion_letter); [Wikipedia, "Peel Commission"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission).
- The Jewish Agency reportedly submitted a confidential transfer plan to the Commission in March 1937. In August 1937 Colonial Secretary Ormsby-Gore reportedly said force was ruled out and that Arabs were expected to move voluntarily — [Vridar blog summarising archival literature](https://vridar.org/2014/04/21); [Vridar](https://vridar.org/2014/04/26/compulsory-arab-transfer-necessary-for-a-jewish-state/). *(Secondary blog; verify against Morris or Masalha.)*
- **Ben-Gurion and the historiography:** Benny Morris cites a 1937 Ben-Gurion diary entry supporting "compulsory transfer" and argues that Ben-Gurion and Weizmann saw partition as a stepping stone — [Mondoweiss, quoting Morris](https://mondoweiss.net/2018/10/zionists-solution-cleansing/) (advocacy outlet); [Wikipedia, "Peel Commission"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission). Efraim Karsh and Shabtai Teveth dispute that Ben-Gurion backed expulsion, and the translation of the line in the 5 October 1937 letter to his son Amos is itself disputed — [Wikipedia, "1937 Ben-Gurion letter"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Ben-Gurion_letter).
- **Kahane/Kach (1970s–1994):** In 1985 the Knesset amended Basic Law: The Knesset and the Elections Law so that lists inciting racism could be disqualified. Under that amendment Kach was disqualified on 18 October 1988 from the 12th Knesset election — [Jewish Virtual Library, "Kach"](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/kach-political-party); [IDI, "Kach"](https://en.idi.org.il/israeli-elections-and-parties/parties/kach/); [Wikipedia, "Kach"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach). Israel later outlawed Kach and Kahane Chai as terrorist organisations in 1994, after the Cave of the Patriarchs massacre — [Wikipedia, "Kach"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach).
- **Moledet / Rehavam Ze'evi (from 1988):** A 1988 JTA analysis said Ze'evi favoured "a more genteel, negotiated 'transfer'" worked out in a peace agreement, possibly including exchanges with Jews from Arab lands. The same piece reported calls to disqualify Moledet under the law that barred Kahane — [JTA archive](https://www.jta.org/archive/news-analysis-kahanes-ideas-may-live-on-but-his-movement-is-moribund).
- **Earlier public opinion:** In Pew's 2014–15 survey (published March 2016; 5,601 face-to-face interviews), 48% of Israeli Jews agreed that "Arabs should be expelled or transferred from Israel." Agreement ran from about 54% to 71% among Haredi, religious and "traditional" Jews and was about 36% among secular Jews — [Ahram/AFP](https://english.ahram.org.eg/WorldCup/News/190484.aspx); [J. Weekly](https://jweekly.com/2016/03/11/study-nearly-half-of-israeli-jews-want-arabs-expelled/). Critics said the wording did not say whether "Arabs" meant citizens or Palestinians in the occupied territories, or whether transfer would be voluntary — [The JC](https://thejc.com/comment/analysis/do-israeli-jews-really-want-to-expel-arabs-the-huge-holes-in-the-pew-survey-1.61102); [GetReligion](https://getreligion.org/getreligion/2016/3/13/flawed-pew-survey-question-produces-flawed-coverage-of-how-many-israeli-jews-want-arabs-out).

#### B. After 7 October 2023: the Intelligence Ministry concept paper
- **Date and origin:** A 10-page paper dated 13 October 2023 from the Intelligence Ministry (headed by Gila Gamliel, Likud), produced by its policy division. Calcalist first reported it on 24 October 2023, and Local Call/+972 published it in full on 28 October 2023. The ministry confirmed it was authentic — [+972 Magazine †](https://www.972mag.com/intelligence-ministry-gaza-population-transfer/); [Wikipedia, "Policy paper: Options for a policy regarding Gaza's civilian population"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policy_paper:_Options_for_a_policy_regarding_Gaza%27s_civilian_population).
- **Core terms:** It recommended to "evacuate the civilian population to Sinai". The stages were air strikes focused on the north, a ground operation across the whole Strip, movement into Egypt with no return, tent cities and then permanent cities in northern Sinai, and "a sterile zone of several kilometers" inside Egypt. It rejected the alternatives of PA rule (called the most dangerous because it could lead to a Palestinian state) and a "local Arab authority" — [+972 †](https://www.972mag.com/intelligence-ministry-gaza-population-transfer/).
- **Messaging and burden-sharing:** The paper recommended telling Gazans there was "no hope of returning" ("whether or not that is true"), presenting the plan to the West as a humanitarian step that would mean "fewer casualties", and naming Greece, Spain and Canada as possible host countries. It asked the US to press Egypt, Turkey, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE to take people or fund the plan — [+972 †](https://www.972mag.com/intelligence-ministry-gaza-population-transfer/).
- **Official response:** Netanyahu's office told AP it was a non-binding "concept paper" and that the Intelligence Ministry does not make policy — [AP via NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/an-israeli-ministry-in-a-concept-paper-proposes-transferring-gaza-civilians-to-egypts-sinai/4817621/).

#### C. Smotrich and Ben-Gvir's "voluntary emigration" (2023–2026)
- On 2 January 2024 State Department spokesman Matthew Miller called the two ministers' calls for Gazans to emigrate and for resettling Gaza "inflammatory and irresponsible", a rare US rebuke of named Israeli ministers. Ben-Gvir answered that Israel is "not another star in the [US] flag" — [France 24](https://go.france24.com/en/middle-east/20240103-us-condemns-far-right-israeli-ministers-call-for-palestinians-to-emigrate-from-gaza); [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/us-condemns-israeli-ministers-call-for-palestinians-to-emigrate-/7422095.html); [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/ben-gvir-us-gaza-rebuke-we-are-not-star-your-flag). Smotrich repeated his support afterwards — [JNS](https://www.jns.org/after-us-condemnation-smotrich-repeats-support-for-resettling-gazans/).
- **2026 campaign platform (current):** Ben-Gvir (Otzma Yehudit) proposed a dedicated emigration ministry. His target is 250,000 departures in the first year, about 1.11 million within three years and about 1.86 million within seven. He said: "This is a free choice; we are not forcing anyone to leave, but there is encouragement for this" — [Algemeiner, 11 Sep 2026 †](https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/09/11/10500-palestinians-left-gaza-past-year-new-data-show-amid-israeli-ministers-renewed-voluntary-emigration-push/); [Middle East Monitor, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.middleeastmonitor.com/20260907-ben-gvir-proposes-ministry-to-promote-voluntary-emigration-from-gaza/); [i24NEWS (headline: "Ben Gvir unveils a 'voluntary emigration' party plan for 1.86 million Gazans")](https://www.i24news.tv/en/news/israel-election-s-2026/artc-itamar-ben-gvir-unveils-a-voluntary-emigration-party-plan-for-1-86-million-gazans).
- In 2026 Defense Minister Katz said there is "no real solution for Gaza in the end without this migration". He said Israel would help Gazans leave "by sea, by air, by every way possible" if countries would take them, and claimed 80% of Gazans want to leave. Foreign Minister Sa'ar said: "We are not planning to expel or to deport anyone from Gaza" — [Algemeiner †](https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/09/11/10500-palestinians-left-gaza-past-year-new-data-show-amid-israeli-ministers-renewed-voluntary-emigration-push/). A Palestinian outlet reported that Katz called the displacement plan "frozen" but "not cancelled" — [Palestinian Information Center (Hamas-affiliated)](https://english.palinfo.com/reports/2026/09/07/369700/) *(unverified elsewhere)*.

#### D. Trump's "Gaza Riviera" / US takeover proposal (February 2025)
- **Origin:** On 4 February 2025, at a press conference with Netanyahu, Trump proposed that the US "take over" Gaza and turn it into the "Riviera of the Middle East", with Gaza's population relocated to Egypt, Jordan and elsewhere — [Dubai Eye/AFP](https://dubaieye1038.com/news/international/trump-vows-to-take-over-gaza-and-create-riviera-of-the-middle-east); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/02/05/trump-gaza-plan-reaction-israel-middle-east).
- **Reactions:** Saudi Arabia rejected it outright. Russia, China, France and the UK reaffirmed support for two states. UN Secretary-General Guterres said "it is essential to avoid any form of ethnic cleansing". Abbas said Palestinians would not give up their land — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/world/trump-gaza-world-reaction-1.7450855); [KSL/AP](https://www.ksl.com/article/51245502). On 5 February 2025 UN High Commissioner Volker Türk said "any forcible transfer in or deportation of people from occupied territory is strictly prohibited". On 24 February he told the Human Rights Council to "resist any normalization of unlawful conduct, including proposals for annexation or forced transfer" — [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/un-gaza-deportation-occupied-land-strictly-prohibited); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2589097); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1894542).
- **Walk-back:** In a later Fox News interview Trump appeared to accept that Egypt's and Jordan's refusal made the idea unworkable — [AP via Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/?p=338848). On 12 March 2025, next to Irish PM Micheál Martin, he said "nobody's expelling any Palestinians". Egypt, Hamas and the PLO welcomed the remark — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2593400); [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/breaking-news/article-845772).
- **"GREAT Trust" prospectus (Aug 2025):** The Washington Post reported a 38-page plan circulating in the administration, drawn up by the Israelis who set up the Gaza Humanitarian Foundation. It offered each departing Gazan $5,000 cash, four years of rent subsidy and one year of food, plus "digital tokens" for land. It estimated that each departure would save the trust $23,000. A source called it "not prescriptive" — [Al-Monitor/Reuters](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2025/08/post-war-gaza-plan-sees-relocation-population-digital-token-palestinian-land); [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/552238.aspx).
- **Formal US shift (29 Sep 2025):** Trump's 20-point Gaza plan says no one will be forced to leave Gaza, that those who leave can return, and that people will be encouraged to stay — [Arab News (White House text)](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2617207); [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/574538/what-s-in-donald-trump-s-20-point-plan-to-end-the-war-in-gaza).

#### G. Legal assessment (transfer)
- **Fourth Geneva Convention, Art. 49(1):** individual or mass forcible transfers and deportations of protected persons from occupied territory are prohibited "regardless of their motive" — [ICRC IHL database, GCIV Art. 49](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/gciv-1949/article-49) [text not fetched]. The UN and many observers apply Art. 49 to the Trump and Israeli proposals — [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/un-gaza-deportation-occupied-land-strictly-prohibited).
- **When "voluntary" is genuinely voluntary:** customary IHL treats displacement as forced where people lack genuine choice, including under coercive conditions — [ICRC Customary IHL, Rule 129](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/customary-ihl/v1/rule129) [text not fetched]. Critics argue that consent given amid destruction, famine and blockade is not free — [Al Majalla](https://en.majalla.com/node/324944/politics/israel%E2%80%99s-%E2%80%98voluntary%E2%80%99-migration-policy-gaza-anything); [Common Dreams](https://www.commondreams.org/news/israeli-security-cabinet-approves-plan-for-mass-ethnic-cleansing-of-gaza) (advocacy). Israeli ministers say the programme complies with international law — [search-summary of JNS/TPS coverage](https://www.jns.org/israel-establishes-new-body-to-assist-gazans-seeking-to-emigrate/).
- **Rome Statute:** Art. 7(1)(d) lists "deportation or forcible transfer of population" as a crime against humanity. Art. 8(2)(b)(viii) makes it a war crime for an occupying power to transfer its own civilians into occupied territory, or to deport or transfer all or part of the occupied population "within or outside this territory" — [Rome Statute (ICC)](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf) [text not fetched].
- **Human Rights Watch (14 Nov 2024):** found that Israel's mass forced displacement of more than 90% of Gaza's people (1.9 million) amounts to war crimes and crimes against humanity, and that in some areas it amounts to "ethnic cleansing". It found the displacement likely intended to be permanent, for buffer zones — [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/11/14/israels-crimes-against-humanity-gaza). NGO Monitor called the report one-sided — [NGO Monitor](https://ngo-monitor.org/in-the-media/opinion-human-rights-hypocrisy-forced-displacement-in-hamas-out/).
- **ICJ (19 July 2024):** in the West Bank context, the Court found that Israeli policies leave Area C Palestinians "little choice but to leave" and breach the Art. 49(1) ban on forcible transfer — [ICJ summary †](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176).

#### H. Public opinion (transfer)
- **Penn State / Geocartography (fieldwork March 2025, published in Haaretz May 2025; 1,005 Jewish Israelis; authors Tamir Sorek and Shay Hazkani):** 82% of Jewish Israelis supported expelling Gazans, against 45% in a 2003 survey. 56% supported expelling Palestinian citizens of Israel, against 31% in 2003 — [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/poll-huge-majority-israelis-back-gaza-ethnic-cleansing); [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/live-blog/live-blog-update/us-poll-82-percent-israelis-support-expulsion-palestinians-gaza). Critics say the forced-choice format inflated the figure: an aChord poll that offered a neutral option saw 26% choose it — [Haaretz rebuttal reposted by JFJFP](https://jfjfp.com/?p=188520).
- **Tel Aviv University Peace Index (March 2025):** reportedly found 62% of Israeli Jews supported "evacuating Palestinians from Gaza, even by force" — cited in [JFJFP repost of Haaretz](https://jfjfp.com/?p=188520) *(not confirmed from the primary Peace Index)*.
- **Gazans' own stated intentions (PCPSR, May 2025, about 1,270 respondents):** about 43% of Gazans said they were ready to emigrate after the war, and roughly half said they would seek Israel's help to leave — [FDD summary](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2025/05/07/large-minority-wants-to-leave-gaza-as-hamas-popularity-declines-new-poll-of-palestinians-finds/); [AFP via Free Malaysia Today](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/world/2025/05/07/almost-half-of-gazans-willing-to-leave-survey-finds). Another write-up gives a different breakdown (38% temporary relocation, 14% permanent emigration) — [JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/half-of-gazans-would-leave-if-given-opportunity). An April 2026 survey found 73% of Palestinians think emigration harms their society — [PNN](https://english.pnn.ps/news/48162) *(pollster not confirmed)*.

### Inferences
- **Position on the spectrum:** this is the far maximalist pole. It aims at "maximum land, minimum Arabs" by removing population rather than ruling it.
- **"Voluntary" is the key contested variable.** Proponents present incentives and exit options as humanitarian choice. The UN, HRW, the ICJ's reasoning on Area C and the ICRC customary-law test treat consent under coercive conditions as forcible transfer. A game model could tie "voluntariness" to the level of humanitarian pressure in Gaza, with legal and diplomatic costs rising as pressure rises.
- **Feasibility depends on receiving states, and so far none has agreed.** Egypt and Jordan treat it as an existential security threat, no large host has signed up, and actual departures in 2025–26 were in the thousands, mostly medical, not the hundreds of thousands proposed. The US formally dropped coercive relocation in the September 2025 plan. The idea now survives mainly as an Israeli far-right party platform (RZP and Otzma together won 13 of 120 seats in October 2026; see Q6).
- **Endgame modelling:** prerequisites are a host state, US acquiescence, sustained Israeli coalition support and Arab-state acquiescence. Failure modes are ICC/ICJ exposure, collapse of Abraham Accords normalisation, destabilisation of Egypt or Jordan, and Palestinian refusal.

## Q2. Annexation / "Greater Israel" with limited or no Palestinian citizenship

### Takeaway
Annexation proposals range from partial (Bennett's Area C plan, 2012; the Trump 2020 roughly 30% model; Smotrich's 82% map, 2025) to full (Glick, 2014; the Sovereignty Movement; Smotrich's 2017 Decisive Plan). They differ mainly on what Palestinians receive: citizenship (Bennett for Area C residents; Glick in phases), residency without national voting (Smotrich), or emigration. Formal annexation has been blocked repeatedly by the US: the 2020 suspension for UAE normalisation, Trump's September 2025 "will not allow", and Vance's October 2025 rebuke. The Knesset has passed only declaratory or preliminary votes. Meanwhile the ICJ (2024), B'Tselem and Yesh Din describe an annexation that is already proceeding de facto, and in February 2026 the cabinet extended Israeli land registration and enforcement in the West Bank.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Smotrich's "Decisive Plan" (Tochnit HaHachra'a), 2017
- **Origin:** published by MK Bezalel Smotrich in the journal *HaShiloach* in 2017 and adopted unanimously at a National Union / religious-Zionist conference in September 2017 — [HaShiloach (English) †](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/); [Jewish Press, 13 Sep 2017](https://jewishpress.com/news/israel/religious-secular-in-israel-israel/religious-zionist-political-conference-unanimously-adopts-aggressive-sovereignty-policy-paper/2017/09/13).
- **Rationale:** "The national ambitions of the Jewish People and the Arabs of the Land of Israel are in conflict"; there is "room for only one expression of national self-determination west of the Jordan River." Two states are called "unfeasible" — [HaShiloach †](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/).
- **Territory and settlement:** "the application of full Israeli sovereignty to the heartland regions of Judea and Samaria," new towns and "hundreds of thousands of additional settlers" — [HaShiloach †](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/).
- **Options offered to Palestinians:**
  1. Those who "forgo their national aspirations can stay here and live as individuals in the Jewish State". They would live under "regional municipal administrations lacking national characteristics" in six regions (Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, Jericho, Nablus, Jenin), would vote for local bodies but not for the Knesset "at the first stage", and might later vote for the Jordanian parliament. Full citizenship "will be possible to consider" later, conditioned on loyalty and military or national service.
  2. Those who keep national ambitions "will receive aid to emigrate", with "generous grants" as a "parting grant".
  3. Those who keep fighting will be "dealt with by the security forces with a strong hand" — [HaShiloach †](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/).
- **Critics:** commentators call it a blueprint for apartheid or a "second Nakba" — [Mondoweiss, 2017](https://mondoweiss.net/2017/09/rightist-smotrich-apartheid/); [The New Arab](https://english.alaraby.co.uk/analysis/how-smotrichs-west-bank-plan-actualises-second-nakba). +972 argues it still "defines the horizon of Israeli politics" — [+972](https://www.972mag.com/smotrich-decisive-plan-israeli-liberals-maximalism/).
- **Current form (Sept 2025):** On 3 September 2025 Smotrich, now Finance Minister and also a minister in the Defense Ministry, presented a map for annexing 82% of the West Bank, leaving six Palestinian population centres as enclaves, under the principle of "maximum land, minimum population" — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/smotrich-proposes-annexing-82-of-west-bank-in-bid-to-prevent-palestinian-state/?lang=en); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2614044); [Truthout](https://truthout.org/articles/israels-smotrich-vows-to-annex-west-bank-maximum-land-minimum-population). #### G. "De facto annexation": ICJ, B'Tselem, Yesh Din
- **ICJ Advisory Opinion (19 July 2024):** Israel's policies amount to annexation of large parts of the OPT, notably East Jerusalem and Area C. Seeking sovereignty over occupied territory violates the prohibition on the use of force and the principle of non-acquisition of territory by force. Settlements breach GCIV Art. 49(6). Israel's continued presence is unlawful (11–4) and must end "as rapidly as possible" (11–4). Israel must cease new settlement and evacuate settlers (14–1). States must not recognise or aid the situation (12–3). Security concerns do not override the prohibition — [ICJ †](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176).
- **B'Tselem (12 Jan 2021),** "A regime of Jewish supremacy from the Jordan River to the Mediterranean Sea: This is apartheid": treats Israel and the OPT as a single regime — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2021/1/12/this-is-apartheid-rights-group-slam-israeli-rule); [JTA](https://www.jta.org/2021/01/14/israel/israel-is-an-apartheid-state-leading-israeli-human-rights-group-says). Israeli officials and NGO Monitor reject the apartheid label — [NGO Monitor](https://www.ngo-monitor.org/?p=41445).
- **Yesh Din legal opinion (9 July 2020):** concludes that the crime against humanity of apartheid is being committed in the West Bank. It describes "gradual"/"creeping legal annexation" and warns that formal annexation would merge the legal regimes and strengthen the case that Israel as a whole is an apartheid state — [Yesh Din †](https://www.yesh-din.org/en/the-occupation-of-the-west-bank-and-the-crime-of-apartheid-legal-opinion/).
- **UN Charter Art. 2(4)** prohibits the threat or use of force against the territorial integrity of any state — [UN Charter, Ch. I](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-1) [text not fetched]. The ICJ applied the related non-acquisition rule to the OPT — [ICJ †](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176).

### Inferences
- **Spectrum placement (least to most maximalist):** Bennett (Area C plus citizenship for a small population, PA autonomy remains) → Trump 2020 (about 30%, conditional Palestinian statelet) → Smotrich 2025 (82%, six enclaves) → Glick (all of the West Bank, phased residency then citizenship) → Smotrich 2017 and the Sovereignty Movement (all of the West Bank, no national vote, emigration incentives, force against resisters).
- **The core trade-off is democracy versus demography versus territory.** Granting citizenship erodes the Jewish majority (see Q6). Withholding it is what B'Tselem, Yesh Din and the ICJ's discrimination findings describe as apartheid or systemic discrimination. Proponents try to escape the dilemma through disputed demographic counts (Glick), conditional citizenship (Smotrich), or a small population figure (Bennett).
- **Feasibility:** de jure annexation has repeatedly hit a US veto (2020, 2025) and UAE/Saudi normalisation red lines. De facto measures (land registration, enforcement in Areas A and B, settlement approvals) continue without a formal declaration. A model should probably treat formal and creeping annexation as separate tracks with different costs.
- **2026 politics:** the October 2026 election left Netanyahu's bloc at 53 seats, so whether the sovereignty agenda survives depends on coalition formation (see Q6).

## Q3. Autonomy plans: the Allon Plan (1967), Begin's autonomy plan and the Camp David framework (1978), and "autonomy-plus"

### Takeaway
Autonomy models give Palestinians self-administration over people, not sovereignty over land, while Israel keeps security control and, in most versions, its claim to the territory. All of them are historical: the Allon Plan was never formally adopted, and the Camp David autonomy talks of 1979–82 failed. Their logic survives in current proposals: Smotrich's "regional municipal administrations", Bennett's expanded PA autonomy, Kedar's emirates, and the Trump 2020 "limited sovereignty" concept. Critics, among them the historian Seth Anziska, describe autonomy as "state prevention."

### Cited Findings
- **Allon Plan (July 1967, Yigal Allon):** Israel would annex most of the Jordan Valley, from the river to the eastern slopes of the hill ridge, plus East Jerusalem and the Etzion bloc. Settlements and army bases would be placed to include "as few Arabs as possible". The populated hill country, with a corridor via Jericho, would go to Jordan. It was put to several cabinets but never officially adopted — [Wikipedia, "Allon Plan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allon_Plan); [Indiana University Press, *Negotiating Arab-Israeli Peace*, Appendix 18](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/negotiating-arab-israeli-peace-third-edition-appendices/section/3daff194-5d77-47a8-9030-dcaa86e1234e). It was the basis for Labor-era settlement in the Jordan Valley and sits within the "Jordanian option" (Q4).
- **Begin's autonomy plan (presented to the Knesset 28 Dec 1977; shown to Carter on 16 Dec and Sadat at Ismailia on 25 Dec), 26 points:**
  - "The administration of the military rule in Judea, Samaria and the Gaza Strip will be abolished."
  - An elected 11-member Administrative Council (seat in Bethlehem) would run departments such as education, health and agriculture.
  - Security and public order would stay with Israel, and the IDF would remain.
  - Residents would have "the free option to receive either Israeli or Jordanian citizenship".
  - "Israeli residents will be entitled to purchase land and settle".
  - Refugee entry would be "in a reasonable volume", decided by an Israel–Jordan–Council committee.
  - Israel would assert its sovereignty claim but "leave the question of sovereignty in those areas open", with re-examination after five years — [Jewish Virtual Library (plan text) †](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Peace/AutonomyPlan.html); [JTA archive](https://www.jta.org/archive/begin-presents-autonomy-plan); [FRUS 1977–80, vol. IX, doc. 150](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v09Ed2/d150); [Palestine Studies, JPS](https://digitalprojects.palestine-studies.org/jps/abstract/38522).
- **Camp David "Framework for Peace in the Middle East" (17 Sep 1978):** kept autonomy as "self-government" but recast it as a five-year transitional arrangement rather than a permanent solution — [Cairo Review (Joel Singer)](https://www.thecairoreview.com/essays/developing-the-concept-of-palestinian-autonomy/). The full text provides for a freely elected "self-governing authority (administrative council)" and final-status negotiations to start no later than the third year of the transition — [Avalon Project, Camp David Accords](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/campdav.asp) [text not fetched]. Palestinians were not parties and refused to join the autonomy talks. US negotiators Robert Strauss and Sol Linowitz could not bridge the gaps on self-government, settlements and East Jerusalem. Jordan and Saudi Arabia did not back the Accords — [US State Dept Office of the Historian †](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1977-1980/camp-david).
- **Critiques:** Palestinian leaders rejected the Begin plan, and critics saw it as entrenching Israeli control, while others saw it as a possible step toward statehood — [Cairo Review](https://www.thecairoreview.com/essays/developing-the-concept-of-palestinian-autonomy/); [BICOM](https://www.bicom.org.uk/?p=30723). Seth Anziska's "Autonomy as State Prevention: The Palestinian Question after Camp David, 1979–1982" argues that autonomy was designed to prevent statehood — [mirror of Anziska 2017](https://scott-garden.vercel.app/arab-israeli-conflict/camp-david/anziska-autonomy-state-prevention2017) *(unofficial mirror)*.
- **Village Leagues (1978–early 1980s) as a precedent:** In 1981 Menahem Milson, head of the Civil Administration, argued for building rural, non-PLO local leadership — [Commentary, Milson, "How to Make Peace with the Palestinians"](https://www.commentary.org/articles/menahem-milson/how-to-make-peace-with-the-palestinians/). Palestinian critics, and Fatah-aligned Hebron families in 2025, invoke the Village Leagues as the template of collaborationist local rule — [Wikipedia, "Palestinian Emirates Plan" †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan); [Al-Bushra (Palestinian advocacy essay)](https://www.al-bushra.org/palestine/nizar/).
- **"Autonomy-plus" variants (current or recent):** Bennett proposed devolving "more autonomy to the PA" in a "post-Oslo" era while annexing Area C — [Algemeiner, 2014](https://www.algemeiner.com/2014/05/15/israels-bennett-floats-plan-to-exercise-israeli-sovereignty-devolve-more-autonomy-to-pa-in-post-oslo-era-video/). Smotrich's 2017 plan offers six regional municipal administrations "lacking national characteristics" — [HaShiloach †](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/). Ayelet Shaked proposed a "confederation" of Jordan, Gaza and the Palestinian parts of the West Bank — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/shaked-touts-confederation-of-jordan-gaza-and-parts-of-west-bank/) *(date not verified)*.

### Inferences
- **Spectrum placement:** autonomy is the "softest" form of Israeli-maximalist territorial control, softer than annexation-with-residency. It separates people from land: Palestinians govern themselves, Israel keeps security and the land question, and the settlement right is explicit in Begin's plan. Camp David moved it toward an interim stage with final status left open, which reappeared in the Oslo interim model.
- **Feasibility:** it has failed historically for lack of Palestinian partners (PLO rejection, Village Leagues delegitimised) and because Arab states would not back it. For a game, autonomy could be an interim state that drifts toward either statehood or annexation depending on settlement growth and legitimacy variables.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the full Camp David text or the Allon Plan's original cabinet documents. The Avalon URL is cited from reference knowledge.
- No dedicated sources were retrieved on the Village Leagues' rise and fall (1978–84) or Jordan's 1982 treason decree against members.
- No current polling on autonomy as an endgame was found.

## Q4. The Jordanian option and "Jordan is Palestine"

### Takeaway
The Labor "Jordanian option", returning most of the populated West Bank to Hashemite Jordan, peaked with the 1987 Peres–Hussein London Agreement. Shamir rejected that agreement, and the option was effectively ended by Jordan's 31 July 1988 disengagement. "Jordan is Palestine" is a different idea, associated with Likud figures such as Sharon and Shamir from the late 1970s: Jordan as the Palestinian state, sometimes with transfer implied. Jordan rejects both "alternative homeland" ideas absolutely, and in 2025 it treated Gaza and West Bank displacement as a direct threat. Today both are historical, though variants (Smotrich's Jordanian-parliament voting, Shaked's confederation) still appear on the right.

### Cited Findings
- **Labor's Jordanian option (1967–1988):** after 1967 Labor leaders opposed a Palestinian state and sought territorial compromise with Jordan. The option stopped being official policy when Likud won in 1977, but Labor kept advocating it — [Wikipedia, "Jordanian option"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_option); [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/jordanian-option). In 1980 Labor said it would hand most of the West Bank and Gaza to Jordan if it won — [Christian Science Monitor, 22 Sep 1980](https://csmonitor.com/1980/0922/092240.html).
- **London Agreement (11 April 1987):** an informal Peres–Hussein understanding on a UN-hosted international conference based on UNSC Resolutions 242 and 338, which "would not impose a solution or veto any agreement" — [ECF, English text](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/M00147_Peres–HusseinLondonAgreement-EnglishText_0.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peres%E2%80%93Hussein_London_Agreement). PM Shamir had authorised the meeting but rejected the result, fearing an imposed settlement. Brookings says this effectively ended the Jordanian option. The conference idea resurfaced only at Madrid in 1991 — [ECF](https://ecf.org.il/issues/issue/159); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/former-israeli-prime-minister-yitzhak-shamir-a-stalwart-of-israeli-conservatism/).
- **Jordan's disengagement (31 July 1988):** King Hussein cut legal and administrative ties with the West Bank, except custodianship of the Muslim holy sites in Jerusalem, and halted a $1.3 billion West Bank development plan — [UNISPAL, speech text](https://www.un.org/unispal/?p=202739); [IsraelEd](https://israeled.org/jordan-out-of-west-bank/). Contemporary reporting quoted him: "Jordan is not Palestine" — [New York Review of Books, Oct 1988](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1988/10/13/the-turning-point/). US analysts partly read it as a tactical move — [CSM, 2 Aug 1988](https://www.csmonitor.com/1988/0802/ajord.html). Shamir said the Jordanian option was dead; Peres said it was alive — [CSM, 2 Aug 1988](https://www.csmonitor.com/1988/0802/opra.html).
- **"Jordan is Palestine":** the slogan was revived by Likud figures including Sharon and Shamir from the late 1970s, alongside anti-PLO policy. In some formulations it implied large-scale transfer of West Bank Palestinians to Jordan — [Oxford, "Prelude to the Accord: Likud, Labour and the Palestinians"](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~ssfc0005/Prelude%20to%20the%20Accord%20Likud,%20Labour%20and%20the%20Palestinians.html); [MERIP, "Ariel Sharon and the Jordan Option" (2005)](https://www.merip.org/2005/03/ariel-sharon-and-the-jordan-option); [MERIP, "No Jordan Option" (2004)](https://www.merip.org/2004/06/no-jordan-option/). In the 1950s–60s Jordan itself used "Jordan is Palestine and Palestine is Jordan" to mean integration — [Wikipedia, "Jordanian option"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_option).
- **Jordanian positions (current):** King Abdullah II calls the "substitute homeland" idea an "illusion": "Jordan is Jordan and Palestine is Palestine" — [Royal Hashemite Court](https://kingabdullah.jo/en/node/47206); [Jordan Times](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/substitute-homeland’-illusion-minds-rumour-mongers-—-king); [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/articles/0,7340,L-4491702,00.html); [King Abdullah: "Jordan will never be an alternative homeland"](https://kingabdullah.jo/en/node/37560). He has stated three "noes" (to displacement, to an alternative homeland, to liquidating the Palestinian cause) — [Jordan Times opinion](https://jordantimes.com/opinion/shehab-al-makahleh/king-abdullahs-stand-against-alternative-homeland-narrative-palestinians). In January 2025 FM Safadi said "Jordan is for Jordanians, Palestine is for Palestinians" — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/58/1262/539295/War-on-Gaza/War-on-Gaza/All-talk-about-alternative-homeland-for-Palestinia.aspx). When Trump floated moving Gazans in February 2025, the King rejected "any attempts to annex land and displace Palestinians" — [KSL/AP](https://www.ksl.com/article/51245502).
- **Residual right-wing variants:** Smotrich's 2017 plan lets Palestinian residents eventually vote for the Jordanian parliament — [HaShiloach †](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/). Shaked's "confederation" idea links Jordan, Gaza and the Palestinian parts of the West Bank — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/shaked-touts-confederation-of-jordan-gaza-and-parts-of-west-bank/).

### Inferences
- **Spectrum placement:** the Labor Jordanian option is a territorial-compromise model, closer to the centre: it returns land and people to an Arab sovereign. "Jordan is Palestine" is maximalist: it denies any Palestinian state west of the Jordan and implies transfer or political outsourcing to Amman.
- **Feasibility:** Jordan's consent is a hard veto. It disengaged legally in 1988 and frames any "alternative homeland" as a threat to its regime and demography. A game should treat Jordan's stability as a state variable that displacement or annexation can push toward crisis.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable figure, in this session, for the share of Jordan's population of Palestinian origin (frequently cited, but contested).
- The "declaration of war" phrasing sometimes attributed to Jordanian officials about displacement was not verified.
- I did not confirm the date of the Shaked confederation proposal.

## Q5. Emirates / clan model: Kedar's "Palestinian emirates" and the 2025 Hebron sheikhs

### Takeaway
Mordechai Kedar (Bar-Ilan) has argued since 2012 for eight clan-based Palestinian city-state "emirates": Gaza, Jenin, Nablus, Ramallah, Jericho, Tulkarm, Qalqilya and Arab Hebron. Israel would keep the rest of the land and overall security. In July 2025 the idea had its most concrete test, when five Hebron sheikhs led by Wadee' al-Jaabari wrote to Israel's economy minister proposing a breakaway "Emirate of Hebron" that would recognise Israel as the Jewish state and join the Abraham Accords. Large parts of the Ja'abari clan, Hebron's families, the PA, Fatah and Hamas rejected it. It had no visible follow-through by late 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Kedar's plan (2012– ):** He argues Middle Eastern societies are organised by tribe and clan and that Western-style nation-states fail where rival groups are combined. The proposed emirates are Gaza, Jenin, Nablus, Ramallah, Jericho, Tulkarm, Qalqilya and the Arab parts of Hebron. Each would run its own affairs and security, possibly coordinating through a council, while Israel keeps overall security between the enclaves. Rural areas would fall under "alternative administrative arrangements" coordinated with Israel — [Wikipedia, "Palestinian Emirates Plan" †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan); [Foreign Policy Association blog, 2014](https://fpa.org/dr-mordechai-kedar-the-eight-state-solution-is-the-best/). The emirates would not be contiguous and their borders are not fixed. The Gulf emirates are the stated model — [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/192908). Counting Israel, it is sometimes called the "nine-state solution" — [NJ Jewish News / Times of Israel](https://njjewishndev.timesofisrael.com/israeli-academic-offers-nine-state-solution/). Kedar restated the plan after 7 October 2023 — [San Diego Jewish World](https://www.sdjewishworld.com/?p=178079).
- **Gaps in the design:** critics note that it bypasses Jerusalem, refugees and borders — [Wikipedia †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan). The summary found does not say whether rural Palestinians outside the emirates would receive Israeli citizenship or residency.
- **Critiques:** critics say it fragments Palestinian national identity under Israeli dominance and risks clan violence, and compare it to South African Bantustans — [Wikipedia †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan). One critic calls it an "orientalist" blueprint for dismantling the Palestinian cause — [Vision PD](https://vision-pd.org/en/?p=528908) *(Palestinian think-tank)*. It has had little international support beyond some Israeli and American right-wing circles — [Wikipedia †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan).
- **Hebron sheikhs' letter (reported by the Wall Street Journal, early July 2025; the letter was reportedly dated 24 March 2025):** Sheikh Wadee' al-Jaabari and four other sheikhs (most of them anonymous) pledged peace, "zero tolerance" for terrorism and recognition of Israel as the Jewish state. They proposed that Hebron leave the PA, become an emirate and join the Abraham Accords, replacing Oslo, which they said had "only brought damage, death, economic disaster and destruction". They asked for work permits for Israel and a joint industrial zone. The letter went to Economy Minister Nir Barkat, who had met them more than ten times since February 2025 and said: "Who in Israel is going to say no?" — [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/b11n2tvsgg); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/the-revolutionary-plan-of-five-hebron-sheikhs); [Jewish News](https://www.jewishnews.co.uk/hebron-chiefs-propose-joining-abraham-accords/); [Wikipedia †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan). Samaria Regional Council head Yossi Dagan was pictured with sheikhs behind the plan — [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/411395).
- **Rejection:** Hebron families and leaders issued a joint statement backing the PLO and PA — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/549368.aspx); [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/hebron-residents-and-leaders-denounce-local-sheikhs-plan-declare-independence-and-recognise). A Ja'abari family statement disowned the relative and backed a state with Jerusalem as its capital — [Jewish News](https://www.jewishnews.co.uk/hebron-chiefs-propose-joining-abraham-accords/). The PA, speaking through former Hebron governor Arif Jaabari, framed it as an "Israeli plan" — [Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2025/07/10/hebron-residents-want-to-live-in-peace-with-israel-palestinian-authority-calls-it-an-israeli-plan/). Hamas denounced it as normalisation, and Fatah-aligned families compared it to the Village Leagues — [Wikipedia †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan). Al-Jaabari's car was reportedly torched — [JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/palestinians-torch-car-of-sheikh-who-led-plan-for-hebron-emirate). One analyst told JTA that al-Jaabari was the strongest figure in the clan, though rivalries persist — [JTA](https://www.jta.org/?p=1886287). A Lebanese outlet called it a "short-lived" dream — [This is Beirut](https://thisisbeirut.com.lb/articles/1320177/hebron-s-short-lived-tribal-emirate-dream).
- **Supporters' framing:** pro-Israel commentators present it as proof that "the Oslo delusion is finally cracking" and a model for clan-based peace — [JNS (Avi Abelow)](https://www.jns.org/opinion/avi-abelow/hebron-arab-leaders-back-joining-the-abraham-accords); [JNS (Shoshana Bryen)](https://www.jns.org/opinion/shoshana-bryen/the-al-jabari-clan-fixing-the-post-colonial-world).

### Inferences
- **Spectrum placement:** a fragmentation variant of autonomy. It is maximalist on territory (rural land and Area C would effectively be Israeli) but formally offers local "sovereignty" to urban enclaves. It is closely related to Smotrich's six regional administrations and the 82% map.
- **Feasibility:** low as of 2026. It depends on clan elites willing to break with the PA and absorb high personal risk, on Israeli and Abraham Accords state buy-in, and on overcoming Palestinian national opposition. No Abraham Accords state endorsed the Hebron proposal. A model could treat "clan defection" as a local event that the PA or Hamas can reverse, with legitimacy costs for Israel.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 coverage of any progress on the Hebron emirate. Its current status is presumed dormant but not confirmed.
- The original WSJ article (Elliot Kaufman) was not fetched. The number and identity of the signatories are unverified.
- No independent academic evaluation of Kedar's plan was found. Most coverage is advocacy, for or against.

## Q6. Cross-cutting: legal framework, demography, public opinion, and political context (November 2026)

### Takeaway
Under the ICJ's 2024 opinion and the main treaty texts, both transfer and annexation are unlawful: GCIV Art. 49, Rome Statute Arts. 7(1)(d) and 8(2)(b)(viii), and the UN Charter rule against acquiring territory by force. Demographically, roughly 7.3–7.8 million Palestinians/Arabs and about 7.8 million Jews and others live between the river and the sea, near parity, which is why annexation proposals depend on denying citizenship, emigration, or disputed counts. The 27 October 2026 Israeli election left Netanyahu's bloc (including Smotrich's and Ben-Gvir's parties) at 53 seats, short of a majority. That makes the political path for maximalist endgames uncertain.

### Cited Findings

#### Legal framework
- GCIV Art. 49(1) bans forcible transfers and deportations from occupied territory "regardless of their motive". Art. 49(6) bars the occupier from transferring its own civilians into occupied territory — [ICRC, GCIV Art. 49](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/gciv-1949/article-49) [text not fetched]; the ICJ applied both paragraphs to the West Bank — [ICJ †](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176).
- Rome Statute Art. 7(1)(d) (deportation or forcible transfer as a crime against humanity) and Art. 8(2)(b)(viii) (transfer of the occupier's population, and deportation or transfer of the occupied population, as a war crime) — [Rome Statute](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf) [text not fetched].
- ICJ (19 July 2024): occupation does not confer sovereignty, annexation violates the non-acquisition rule, security concerns do not override it, and states may not recognise or aid the situation — [ICJ †](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176).
- UN statements: Türk (Feb 2025) said forcible transfer and deportation are "strictly prohibited". Guterres said "avoid any form of ethnic cleansing" — [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/un-gaza-deportation-occupied-land-strictly-prohibited); [KSL/AP](https://www.ksl.com/article/51245502).

#### Demography (with dates)
- **Israel (CBS, eve of Rosh Hashanah 5787, September 2026):** about 10.305 million, of whom about 7.8 million (78.3%) are Jews and others and nearly 2.2 million (21.7%) are Arabs. Growth was about 1.2% — [i24NEWS](https://www.i24news.tv/en/news/israel/society/artc-israel-s-population-reaches-10-3-million-ahead-of-rosh-hashanah); [Times of Israel (French)](https://fr.timesofisrael.com/israel-compte-103-millions-dhabitants-a-lapproche-de-rosh-hashana/). The September 2025 figure was 10.148 million, 78.5% Jews and others and 21.5% Arabs — [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/syvhwmujee). *Note: CBS figures include East Jerusalem Palestinians and West Bank settlers.*
- **West Bank and Gaza (PCBS, end-2025):** State of Palestine 5,557,096, made up of 3,430,062 in the West Bank and 2,127,034 in Gaza — [PCBS press release, end-2025](https://www.pcbs.gov.ps/portals/_pcbs/PressRelease/Press_En_PalPeopleEnd2025E.pdf); [PCBS Nakba 2026 report](https://pcbs.gov.ps/media/3hqlbiio/nakba2026e.pdf). Gaza's population fell by about 6% after the war began, and by 254,000 in 2025 according to one report — [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/arab-world/5097128-gaza-population-down-6-start-war-says-palestinian-statistics-bureau?page=1); [Ground News aggregation](https://ground.news/article/gazas-population-declined-by-254-000-people-in-2025_ec1268). Mid-2025 total: about 5.5 million — [WAFA](https://english.wafa.ps/Pages/Details/157272). The UN estimate for mid-2025 is 5.59 million — [Worldometer/UN DESA](https://ipv6.worldometers.info/world-population/state-of-palestine-population/). There are about 15.5 million Palestinians worldwide — [Arab News/PCBS](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2643317).
- **East Jerusalem:** the Jerusalem Institute for Policy Research put Jerusalem at about 1.05 million at end-2024, 60% Jewish and 40% Arab, which implies roughly 400,000 or more Arab residents, mostly in East Jerusalem — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-896263). A Palestinian estimate gives about 331,000 inside the barrier (mid-2025) and notes definitional discrepancies — [Pal-Think](https://palthink.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/The-Future-of-Jerusalem-Amid-Political-and-Regional-Changes.pdf).
- **Settlers:** more than 750,000, including about 250,000 in East Jerusalem (2025) — [EEAS 2025 report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/palestine-occupied-palestinian-territory-west-bank-and-gaza-strip/2025-report-israeli-settlements-occupied-west-bank-including-east-jerusalem-reporting-period-january_en).

#### Political context (current)
- **Israeli election of 27 October 2026 (preliminary CEC figures, 28 Oct):**
  - Netanyahu bloc, 53 seats: Likud 26 (27 in other reports), Otzma Yehudit 7, Religious Zionism 6, Shas 7, UTJ 7.
  - Opposition without Arab parties, 48: Yashar/Eisenkot 21, B'Yachad/Bennett–Lapid 12, Democrats 8, Yisrael Beytenu 7.

Eisenkot refuses to serve under Netanyahu — [Jerusalem Post †](https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-913122); [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-913237); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/israel/israels-final-election-results-show-neither-netanyahu-rival-can-immedi-rcna606290); [Latin Times](https://www.latintimes.com/israel-election-netanyahu-coalition-talks-600895); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Israeli_legislative_election).

### Inferences
- **Demographic arithmetic (my calculation from the sources above; overlaps and definitions differ):** about 7.8 million Jews and others in CBS data (including about 0.75 million settlers). Arabs/Palestinians come to about 2.2 million in CBS data (including about 0.4 million in East Jerusalem), plus 3.43 million in the West Bank and 2.13 million in Gaza, for about 7.3–7.8 million. PCBS West Bank figures may include the Jerusalem governorate, so there is a risk of double counting. The result is near parity between the river and the sea.
  - Full annexation with citizenship (Glick) would create a binational electorate: excluding Gaza, Palestinians/Arabs would be roughly 40% of citizens.
  - Area C-only annexation (Bennett) adds tens to a few hundred thousand Palestinians, depending on whose count is used.
  - Annexation without citizenship (Smotrich, Sovereignty Movement) is what critics call apartheid.
  - Transfer is the only path in these proposals that changes the ratio decisively, which explains why annexation and "emigration" proposals are often paired.
- **Political feasibility as of November 2026:** the far-right parties that carry transfer and full annexation (RZP plus Otzma, 13 seats) are in a bloc short of a majority. The opposition's largest lists (Eisenkot, Bennett) have not, in the sources found, endorsed transfer. Externally, the US (Trump 2025: no annexation; 20-point plan: no forced departure), the UAE (red line), Egypt and Jordan (hard vetoes) and the ICJ framework all act as constraints. De facto annexation through land registration and settlement continues regardless of these formal blocks.
- **For a game designer:** the actors with veto power are the US president, the Israeli coalition arithmetic, Egypt and Jordan (for transfer), the UAE and Saudi Arabia (normalisation), Palestinian institutions and clans, and international courts (legal-cost multipliers). Key state variables are the Jewish share of voters, settler population, Gaza humanitarian pressure, and Jordan's internal stability.

- Whether a government had formed by 6 November 2026 is unknown. Sources describe deadlock and possible new elections.
- The official Israeli legal position on Art. 49's applicability (the "disputed territories" argument) and on "voluntary" emigration was not retrieved from a primary Israeli government source.
- I did not research UNSC Resolution 2803 (November 2025) or the Board of Peace mandate in Gaza as constraints on transfer.
