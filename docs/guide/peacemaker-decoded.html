Two numbers decide the peace

PeaceMaker casts you as the Israeli Prime Minister or the Palestinian President. You pick one action a turn, the region reacts, and the news tells you how it went. Under the photos and headlines, the outcome comes down to two scores on the track below. This guide lays out every rule the game files contain.

Score track for both scores. Every game starts at 0. Milestones fire after 2 turns at or above 30, 50 and 70. You win after 4 turns with both scores at 95 or more, and lose after 4 turns with either one at −50 or below.
01 · Structure

Stages of a game

Stage 1

Set up

Choose a side (or Random) and a difficulty: Calm, Tense or Violent. Pick a language: English, Hebrew or Arabic.

Stage 2

Briefing

An intro video and a welcome letter from your office. Then an inciting incident, a violent news event shown as a red pin on the map.

Stage 3

Governing

Turn after turn: choose one action, watch time pass, read the outcome, then open every news pin before you can act again.

Stage 4

Milestones

At 30, 50 and 70 on both scores a cut-scene marks progress: Gaza first, then a Gaza–West Bank rail link, then the West Bank.

Stage 5

Ending

A win or loss video, then an end report that rates the violence during your term, then the credits.

02 · The loop

What happens in one turn

  1. You choose an action

    Open a category (Security, Politics, Construction; the Palestinian side also has Financing), go down the sub-menus, and read the two opposing advisors if you like. Greyed-out items are on cooldown or not unlocked yet.

  2. The outcome table picks a result

    Each action has an ordered list of results, such as repetition, success, sub-optimal, delay, refusal and failure. They're checked top to bottom, and the first whose conditions hold is used. Most conditions compare one meter against a noisy threshold.

  3. Some results end here

    “You just did this” (repetition), financing replies and refusals appear at once and cost no turn. Pick something else.

  4. Time passes

    A clock animates the days. Cooldowns and delayed effects tick, and the turn counter goes up.

  5. You read the result

    A news-style report appears, with a photo, a headline and a sound cue. Its meter changes apply now.

  6. The world responds

    The AI actors run in a fixed order: militants, publics, governments, the press, settlers, the other leader. At most three of them make news in a turn. Their stories land as pins on the map.

  7. Scores update, milestones are checked

    Both scores are recomputed from the meters. If a milestone, win or loss condition has held long enough, it fires.

  8. You open every pin

    Each pin's effects apply when you open it. The action menu unlocks only after the last one. Then the game autosaves.

Cooldowns

Each channel (the army, the Palestinian president, the UN…) has a hidden cooldown. Using it blocks that channel for about two turns.

Noisy thresholds

A check like “sympathy ≥ 50 ± 10” draws a random bar between 40 and 60. Below 40 it always fails, above 60 it always passes, and in between the odds rise steadily.

Three-story cap

Once three AI actors have made news in a turn, the rest skip it. On violent turns, actors late in the list rarely get heard.

Turns are weeks

The code calls a turn a week. A skilled player wins in about 21–29 turns.

05 · Choices

Every action, every result

Each entry lists the possible results in the order the game checks them, with the conditions and the meter changes. “rand(50±10)” means a random bar between 40 and 60.

06 · Opponents

The world: scripted actors

The other side, your own factions and the world are simple scripted actors. They run every turn in this order. Each reads one or two meters, rolls dice, and either does something or sleeps.

07 · Feedback

News and events

1,350 events, 574 story types

Every result names a story type (a tag such as hamas, anti-israel, attack). The game picks one of that tag's written variants at random, up to 11 of them, so the same event reads differently each time.

On the map

World events appear as pins on 27 locations, from Ramallah, Gaza and Jerusalem to settlements, Sderot and Beersheba. Red pins are violent or bad news, white pins are the rest.

Real photography and video

1,096 news photos, a documentary video for each milestone and ending, and sound cues by mood: sirens, crowds, cheers, cash registers, construction.

Context tools

A 15-era history timeline from 1920 to the present, a 12-step tutorial, and two advisors on every decision: the Hawk and the Dove for Israel, a National and a Foreign advisor for Palestine.

08 · Measured

Difficulty and balance

Difficulty changes one thing: how long the AI actors sleep. Calm gives the most breathing room, Violent the least. Odds, effects and scoring are the same at every level. We re-ran the game's rules headlessly to see what that means in practice.

  • The sides are very unequal. Random play as Israel loses about 9 games in 10, usually to “The Third Intifada” around turn 15. Random play as Palestine wins most games.
  • Difficulty barely matters to a skilled player: it shifts the win by about one turn.
  • A few actions dominate. For Israel: seeking the government's backing for withdrawal, asking the PA for anti-militant action, restraining settler violence. For Palestine: housing, a power plant, health care, law-and-order speeches, then declaring independence once world opinion is high.
  • Winning and stability are separate. Skilled Israeli wins end with an Extreme violence rating (median 152–218). Skilled Palestinian wins end Low (18–38).
09 · Fine print

Quirks in the shipped game

Endings ignore what you built

The Palestinian side tracks control of airspace, coast, water, borders and holy sites, and whether a state was declared. No ending checks any of it.

Settlers don't count

The in-game help says the settlers' council feeds the Israeli score. The formula leaves it out.

Free money

Financing replies are meant to cost the funder's goodwill, but those changes are never applied. The water treatment plant's cost is misspelled in the data, so it's free.

Double-speed cooldowns

Cooldowns and delayed effects tick twice per turn, once after your move and once after the AI's.

An inverted menu

The Gaza border starts open, but the menu offers to open it, not to close it.

Debug tools left in

Hidden menu items add or subtract 10 on every meter, crash the game, or reset the database. There are also tables for logging every turn for research.

10 · Next

What this means for The Solution

PeaceMaker models legitimacy, not arrangements. “Two-State Solution” is the name of a score threshold. The Solution's multiple endgames need the arrangements themselves in the simulation.
