# Law, demography and vetoes narrow every endgame

Every long-term solution proposed for the Israeli–Palestinian conflict is blocked in November 2026 by at least one actor with a veto. That holds from population transfer and annexation without rights at the Israeli-maximalist pole to a single Palestinian state with limited or no rights for Jews at the opposite pole. The options with the largest popular constituencies are the ones that breach international law. The negotiated two-state band is the only family that fits the legal anchors set by UN Security Council Resolutions 242 and 2334 and the International Court of Justice's July 2024 advisory opinion. Its terms are the 1967 lines with about 2–7% land swaps, Jerusalem divided by neighbourhood with a special regime for the holy sites, a non-militarised Palestine, and refugee return mainly to Palestine. It has gained diplomatic weight: about 157 states recognise Palestine, and the General Assembly endorsed the New York Declaration 142–10. Yet only 16–27% of Israeli Jews support it, the Israeli government rejects Palestinian statehood, and record settlement growth and the February 2026 West Bank land-registration decisions are eroding its territorial basis.

At the Israeli end, 42% of Israeli Jews backed annexation without equal rights in 2024, and transfer has entered party platforms. Both breach the Fourth Geneva Convention and the ban on acquiring territory by force, and both face vetoes from Washington, Cairo, Amman and the Gulf. At the Palestinian end, about a third of Palestinians back a Palestinian-dominated state in which Jews lack full rights. That outcome presupposes Israel's military defeat and would itself require expulsion or disenfranchisement. The hybrids (confederation, federation and trusteeship) and one democratic state are minority options everywhere except among Arab citizens of Israel.

A short list of variables separates the endgames:
- near-parity of about 7.4–7.8 million Jews and "others" and 7.4 million Palestinians between the river and the sea;
- the franchise granted to Palestinians;
- the fate of about 750,000 settlers;
- refugee return;
- sovereignty over the holy sites;
- security control;
- the consent of veto players, starting with the hung 53–48–19 Knesset elected on 27 October 2026.

For "The Solution", the evidence argues for starting the game in the current hybrid: de facto annexation in the West Bank, an Israeli-held majority of Gaza behind the "Yellow Line", and a stalled international transition. Each named endgame is then a departure from that default.

**International law supplies a common yardstick, even though Israel rejects how it is applied.**
- **UN Security Council resolutions.** Resolution 242 (1967) stresses "the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war" and every state's right to "secure and recognized boundaries" ([UN](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/242%281967%29)). Resolution 2334 (2016) says settlements have "no legal validity". It adds that the Council "will not recognize any changes to the 4 June 1967 lines… other than those agreed by the parties" ([UN](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2334%282016%29)).
- **Treaty law.** Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention bans forcible transfers and deportations from occupied territory "regardless of their motive". It also bars an occupier from moving its own civilians in ([ICRC](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties/gciv-1949/article-49)). The Rome Statute lists deportation or forcible transfer as a crime against humanity, and an occupier's transfer of its own population as a war crime ([ICC](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf)).
- **The ICJ's advisory opinion of 19 July 2024** ([ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176)):
  - Israel's continued presence in the territory occupied in 1967 is **unlawful (11–4)** and must end "as rapidly as possible".
  - Israel must stop new settlement activity and **evacuate settlers (14–1)**.
  - Israeli policies leave Area C Palestinians "little choice but to leave", in breach of Article 49(1).
  - States must not recognise or aid the situation (12–3).
  - A "near-complete separation" of settler and Palestinian communities breaches Article 3 of the Convention on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination, which condemns "racial segregation and apartheid". The Court did not say which of the two it found ([ICJ, Judge Brant](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204171); [Verfassungsblog](https://verfassungsblog.de/apartheid-or-systemic-discrimination/)).
- **Israel's response.** Netanyahu called the opinion "a decision of lies" and said "the Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land". Israel formally rejected it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_Consequences_arising_from_the_Policies_and_Practices_of_Israel_in_the_Occupied_Palestinian_Territory,_including_East_Jerusalem)).
- **Enforcement.** Advisory opinions and General Assembly resolutions do not bind on their own. The Assembly's 12-month deadline for withdrawal (ES-10/24) passed in September 2025 with no compliance and no enforcement ([Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php/sports/world/news.php?id=2468983)).
- **The apartheid label.** B'Tselem (2021), Human Rights Watch (2021) and Amnesty (2022) apply it with different territorial scopes. Israel and the US State Department reject their conclusions ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/report/2021/04/27/threshold-crossed/israeli-authorities-and-crimes-apartheid-and-persecution); [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/02/israels-system-of-apartheid/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_and_apartheid)).
- **The genocide case.** South Africa's case is years from judgment, with written pleadings scheduled into 2029 ([South African Presidency](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/presidency-notes-icj-order-south-africa-and-israel-case-02-jun-2026)).

**Public opinion has moved in opposite directions since 2023, and large undecided blocs mean it is not fixed.**
- **The last joint Palestinian–Israeli Pulse** (PCPSR and Tel Aviv University, July 2024) found two-state support at **21% of Israeli Jews, 40% of Palestinians and 72% of Arab citizens of Israel**. A record **42% of Israeli Jews backed annexing the West Bank without equal rights**. Meanwhile 33% of Palestinians backed a Palestinian state in which Jews lack full rights ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).
- **Pew (2026):** only **16% of Jewish Israelis and 21% of West Bank Palestinians** believe Israel and a Palestinian state can coexist peacefully ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/15/many-israelis-and-palestinians-say-they-cannot-coexist-peacefully-and-explain-why/)).
- **Israel Democracy Institute (September 2025):** almost three-quarters of Jewish Israelis say Palestinians have no right to a state of their own ([IDI](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61845)).
- **Palestinian support rises when the question names the 1967 borders.** PCPSR found **56% (August 2026) and 53% (October 2026)** backing a state on those lines, even though 58% say settlements have made two states impractical ([PCPSR Poll 97](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014); [PCPSR October 2026](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)).

**2025–2026 widened the gap between diplomacy and conditions on the ground.**
- **Abroad.** The UK, France, Canada, Australia and others recognised Palestine in September 2025. The General Assembly endorsed the Franco-Saudi New York Declaration 142–10–12 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine); [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/un-overwhelmingly-endorses-call-to-establish-palestinian-state-without-hamas/)).
- **The Knesset.** It voted 68–9 against a Palestinian state in July 2024 and 71–13 for a declaratory sovereignty motion in July 2025 ([The JC](https://thejc.com/news/israel/knesset-votes-68-9-for-resolution-against-palestinian-state-mp3qdn7k); [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-862007)).
- **The cabinet's West Bank decisions of February 2026.** It opened land purchases to Israelis, extended Israeli enforcement into Areas A and B, and restarted Area C land registration for the first time since 1967. Smotrich called these steps "de facto sovereignty". The UN human rights office called registration an exercise of sovereignty that is prohibited in occupied territory ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/); [UN Palestine](https://palestine.un.org/en/node/294287)).
- **Gaza.** The October 2025 ceasefire under Trump's 20-point plan and Resolution 2803 created a Board of Peace and an International Stabilization Force. By November 2026 the technocratic committee had still not entered Gaza, and the order of disarmament and withdrawal was unresolved ([Gaza Biweekly](https://jaser2.substack.com/p/gaza-biweekly-nineteen-seats); [INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/hamas-disarmament-agreement/)).
- **The Israeli election.** Gadi Eisenkot received 67 recommendations, including those of the Arab parties. Netanyahu refuses to govern with those parties ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Israeli_legislative_election); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/2/arab-parties-back-eisenkot-whats-the-latest-in-israels-coalition-talks)).
- **Saudi Arabia.** A Saudi source told Israel's public broadcaster KAN that normalisation "is not on the agenda" while Netanyahu's government remains ([Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-914042)).
- **Palestinian elections.** Abbas postponed the 28 November 2026 legislative elections to 11 September 2027 ([PCPSR](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)).
- **Iran.** A war with Iran in 2026, which overlapped Pew's fieldwork and the 7 April ceasefire, diverted attention, and Indonesia paused its stabilisation-force commitment "amid the Iran crisis" ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/15/many-israelis-and-palestinians-say-they-cannot-coexist-peacefully-and-explain-why/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Peace)).

The report orders proposals in five families, running from Israeli sovereignty over all the land to one state over all the land. The ordering follows who holds sovereignty over the territory. A second axis cuts across it: whether the non-sovereign people get equal political rights. That second axis is why the "one state" label covers opposites, such as annexation without rights and one democratic state. It is also why the two far poles mirror each other: each gives one people sovereignty over all the land and removes or disenfranchises the other ([INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/one-state-models/); [Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).

## Family 1 — Israeli sovereignty over all the land: maximal territory meets the demographic wall

These proposals share Bezalel Smotrich's premise that there is "room for only one expression of national self-determination west of the Jordan River" ([HaShiloach](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/)). They differ on what happens to the 5.5 million Palestinians of the West Bank and Gaza: removal, disenfranchisement, or gradual citizenship. The demographic arithmetic explains why annexation and "emigration" proposals so often come together. Transfer is the only mechanism in these plans that decisively changes the population ratio.

### Transfer and "voluntary emigration" remain a far-right platform that no host state accepts

**Origin and proponents.**
- **Before 1948.** Transfer has a long pedigree and a revived present. The 1937 Peel Commission paired partition with moving about 225,000 Arabs out of the proposed Jewish state ([Wikipedia: Peel Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission)). Historians still dispute how far Ben-Gurion endorsed compulsory transfer. Benny Morris cites a 1937 diary entry; Efraim Karsh and Shabtai Teveth contest that reading ([Wikipedia: 1937 Ben-Gurion letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Ben-Gurion_letter)).
- **Kach and Moledet.** Meir Kahane's Kach was disqualified from the 1988 election under a 1985 anti-racism amendment and outlawed as a terrorist organisation in 1994 ([JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/kach-political-party); [Wikipedia: Kach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach)). Rehavam Ze'evi's Moledet advocated "a more genteel, negotiated 'transfer'" ([JTA](https://www.jta.org/archive/news-analysis-kahanes-ideas-may-live-on-but-his-movement-is-moribund)).
- **The Intelligence Ministry paper.** After 7 October 2023 the idea moved closer to government. A concept paper dated 13 October 2023 recommended moving Gaza's civilians into tent cities and then permanent cities in northern Sinai, and telling them there was "no hope of returning". Netanyahu's office called it a non-binding paper ([+972 Magazine](https://www.972mag.com/intelligence-ministry-gaza-population-transfer/); [AP via NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/an-israeli-ministry-in-a-concept-paper-proposes-transferring-gaza-civilians-to-egypts-sinai/4817621/)).
- **Smotrich and Ben-Gvir.** Their calls for "voluntary emigration" and for resettling Gaza drew a rare named rebuke from the State Department in January 2024 ([France 24](https://go.france24.com/en/middle-east/20240103-us-condemns-far-right-israeli-ministers-call-for-palestinians-to-emigrate-from-gaza)).
- **Trump's "Riviera" and the "GREAT Trust".** On 4 February 2025 Trump proposed a US "takeover" of Gaza as the "Riviera of the Middle East", with its population relocated ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/02/05/trump-gaza-plan-reaction-israel-middle-east)). A related "GREAT Trust" prospectus offered each departing Gazan $5,000 plus rent and food subsidies ([Al-Monitor/Reuters](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2025/08/post-war-gaza-plan-sees-relocation-population-digital-token-palestinian-land)).
- **The emigration directorate.** Israel's security cabinet created a Defence Ministry "Directorate for Voluntary Transition of Gaza Residents" on 23 March 2025 ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-establishes-new-body-to-assist-gazans-seeking-to-emigrate/)).
- **Ben-Gvir's 2026 platform.** For the 2026 election he proposed an emigration ministry with a target of **250,000 departures in the first year and about 1.86 million within seven years**. He insisted that "we are not forcing anyone to leave, but there is encouragement for this" ([Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/09/11/10500-palestinians-left-gaza-past-year-new-data-show-amid-israeli-ministers-renewed-voluntary-emigration-push/)).

**Core terms.**
- **Territory.** Israel keeps all of it. The population ratio changes through departure.
- **Destinations.** Gaza is the main target. The proposed destinations are Sinai, Jordan and distant third states; Somaliland, South Sudan and DR Congo have been discussed ([Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/09/11/10500-palestinians-left-gaza-past-year-new-data-show-amid-israeli-ministers-renewed-voluntary-emigration-push/)).
- **Security, Jerusalem, settlements.** Security stays Israeli and Jerusalem does not come up. Settlements expand, and Smotrich's and Ben-Gvir's versions add resettlement of Gaza.
- **Refugees and rights.** The refugee problem grows rather than shrinks, because departure is meant to be permanent. Those who leave lose any political stake.

The variants differ mainly in how much consent they claim, from Kach's open compulsion to the incentive schemes. That claim is the contested legal variable.

**Legal and ethical assessment: forcible transfer is prohibited and can be criminal.**
- **The rules.** Article 49(1) applies "regardless of their motive". Customary humanitarian law treats displacement as forced when people lack genuine choice ([ICRC Rule 129](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/customary-ihl/v1/rule129)). Deportation or forcible transfer is a crime against humanity under Rome Statute Article 7(1)(d) ([ICC](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf)).
- **UN statements.** UN High Commissioner Volker Türk said in February 2025 that "any forcible transfer in or deportation of people from occupied territory is strictly prohibited". Secretary-General Guterres warned against "any form of ethnic cleansing" ([The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/un-gaza-deportation-occupied-land-strictly-prohibited); [KSL/AP](https://www.ksl.com/article/51245502)).
- **Human Rights Watch** found in November 2024 that the displacement of more than 90% of Gazans amounted to war crimes and crimes against humanity, and in some areas to ethnic cleansing. NGO Monitor called the report one-sided ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/11/14/israels-crimes-against-humanity-gaza); [NGO Monitor](https://ngo-monitor.org/in-the-media/opinion-human-rights-hypocrisy-forced-displacement-in-hamas-out/)).
- **Competing views on consent.** Israeli ministers say the programme complies with international law because departures are voluntary ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-establishes-new-body-to-assist-gazans-seeking-to-emigrate/)). Critics answer that consent given amid destruction, famine and blockade is not free ([Al Majalla](https://en.majalla.com/node/324944/politics/israel%E2%80%99s-%E2%80%98voluntary%E2%80%99-migration-policy-gaza-anything)).

**This report flags transfer as a breach of international law in any form not based on genuinely free individual choice.**

**Public support.** Israeli Jewish support is high but sensitive to wording.
- **Penn State/Geocartography** (March 2025; Tamir Sorek and Shay Hazkani) reported **82% support for expelling Gazans** and 56% for expelling Palestinian citizens of Israel. Sources disagree on whether the 82% refers to Jewish Israelis or all Israelis. Critics argue the forced-choice format inflated it: when an aChord poll offered a neutral option, 26% chose it ([The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/poll-huge-majority-israelis-back-gaza-ethnic-cleansing); [Haaretz via JFJFP](https://jfjfp.com/?p=188520)).
- **JPPI** found that about eight in ten Jewish Israelis welcomed Trump's relocation idea in February 2025. A month later, support for it as a "practical solution" had fallen from 43% to 32% ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/poll-most-israeli-jews-support-trumps-gaza-relocation-proposal); [JPPI](https://jppi.org.il/en/?p=22165)).
- **Gazans.** PCPSR found in May 2025 that about 43% were ready to emigrate after the war. Other write-ups break the figure down differently ([FDD](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2025/05/07/large-minority-wants-to-leave-gaza-as-hamas-popularity-declines-new-poll-of-palestinians-finds/); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/half-of-gazans-would-leave-if-given-opportunity)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: negligible at scale.**
- **Departures so far.** In the year to September 2026 about 10,500 people left Gaza: 3,015 patients, 5,220 companions and about 2,300 for study, family or residency. About 6,000 returned, and no country agreed to take Gazans at scale ([Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/09/11/10500-palestinians-left-gaza-past-year-new-data-show-amid-israeli-ministers-renewed-voluntary-emigration-push/)).
- **Arab vetoes.** Egypt's president called displacement an injustice "in which we cannot participate". Jordan rejected any "alternative homeland". The Arab League adopted a $53 billion reconstruction plan that keeps Gazans in place ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2025/01/30/egypts-firm-stance-against-palestinian-displacement-amid-u-s-proposals/); [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/58/1262/539295/War-on-Gaza/War-on-Gaza/All-talk-about-alternative-homeland-for-Palestinia.aspx); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/middle-east/2025/03/04/arab-leaders-agree-to-egyptian-plan-for-gaza-reconstruction-avoiding-palestinian-resettlement)).
- **The US position.** By March 2025 Trump was saying "nobody's expelling any Palestinians". His September 2025 plan states that no one will be forced to leave Gaza and that those who leave may return ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2593400); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2617207)).
- **The South Africa flight.** In November 2025 a charter flight carried 153 Gazans to Johannesburg; Haaretz reported it was coordinated with the Defence Ministry's emigration bureau. South Africa's foreign minister called it part of a plan to "clear" Gaza ([AP via ADN](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2025/11/14/south-africa-investigates-origins-of-plane-that-arrived-with-more-than-150-palestinians-from-gaza/); [AP](https://www.messenger-inquirer.com/ap/ap_international_news/south-africa-foreign-minister-says-charter-flights-part-of-a-plan-to-clear-palestinians-out/article_743cfc80-db42-537e-9136-2700cd97d230.html)).
- **Israeli politics.** Otzma Yehudit and Religious Zionism won 13 of 120 seats, inside a bloc short of a majority ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/with-all-votes-counted-netanyahus-bloc-at-53-seats-eisenkots-48-arab-parties-19/)). Even inside government the line is split: Defence Minister Katz says there is "no real solution for Gaza in the end without this migration", while Foreign Minister Sa'ar says "we are not planning to expel or to deport anyone" ([Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/09/11/10500-palestinians-left-gaza-past-year-new-data-show-amid-israeli-ministers-renewed-voluntary-emigration-push/)).

The main critique beyond the legal one comes from Carnegie's Zaha Hassan. She argues that development schemes linked to the Board of Peace could converge with the emigration aim by leaving Gazans to choose between temporary compounds and resettlement abroad ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/07/gaza-refugees-displacement-board-of-peace-trump-netanyahu-palestine-israel)).

### Annexation without equal rights draws 42% of Israeli Jews but meets a US veto

**Origin and proponents.**
- **Smotrich's "Decisive Plan" (2017)** is the most developed blueprint. A religious-Zionist conference adopted it unanimously that September ([HaShiloach](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/); [Jewish Press](https://jewishpress.com/news/israel/religious-secular-in-israel-israel/religious-zionist-political-conference-unanimously-adopts-aggressive-sovereignty-policy-paper/2017/09/13)). It calls for "full Israeli sovereignty" over Judea and Samaria and "hundreds of thousands of additional settlers". It then offers Palestinians three paths ([HaShiloach](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/)):
  - Those who "forgo their national aspirations" may stay as individuals under six regional municipal administrations "lacking national characteristics": Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, Jericho, Nablus and Jenin. They would vote locally but not for the Knesset "at the first stage", perhaps for Jordan's parliament instead. Citizenship would be "possible to consider" later, conditional on loyalty and military or national service.
  - Those who keep national ambitions would receive grants to emigrate.
  - Those who fight would be dealt with "with a strong hand".
- **The Sovereignty Movement** of Yehudit Katsover and Nadia Matar seeks sovereignty over all of Judea and Samaria "without compromises or withdrawals". In a movement-aligned interview Matar rejected citizenship and endorsed large-scale emigration ([Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/405207); [Oz Sheri interview](https://ozsheri.substack.com/p/sovereignty-is-in-the-air-a-conversation)).
- **David Friedman's 2024 plan.** The former US ambassador proposes Israeli sovereignty over all of Judea and Samaria under an Israel–US–Abraham Accords agreement. Palestinians would get permanent residency and travel documents but no Knesset vote ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/feature/future-of-judea-samaria-former-us-envoy-unveils-sovereignty-plan)).
- **Dani Dayan.** The settler leader earlier proposed widening personal freedoms without political rights ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/)).

Across these texts Jerusalem stays undivided under Israel, there is no refugee return, Gaza is usually left out, and security is wholly Israeli.

**Legal and ethical assessment.**
- **Annexation.** Annexing occupied territory breaches the UN Charter's ban on the use of force and the rule against acquiring territory by force. The ICJ applied that rule in 2024, and states must not recognise such an annexation ([ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176); [UN Charter](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-1)).
- **Settlements.** Expanding them conflicts with Article 49(6).
- **Disenfranchisement.** Withholding national voting rights from an annexed population on national-ethnic lines is what B'Tselem, Yesh Din and HRW describe as apartheid. Yesh Din's 2020 legal opinion warned that formal annexation would merge the legal regimes and strengthen the case that Israel as a whole is an apartheid state ([Yesh Din](https://www.yesh-din.org/en/the-occupation-of-the-west-bank-and-the-crime-of-apartheid-legal-opinion/)). Israeli officials reject the apartheid label ([NGO Monitor](https://www.ngo-monitor.org/?p=41445)).
- **Commentary.** Critics call the Decisive Plan a blueprint for apartheid or a "second Nakba" ([Mondoweiss](https://mondoweiss.net/2017/09/rightist-smotrich-apartheid/)). +972 argues it still "defines the horizon of Israeli politics" ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/smotrich-decisive-plan-israeli-liberals-maximalism/)).

**This report flags formal annexation and the disenfranchisement of an annexed population as breaches of international law.**

**Public support.** Among Israeli Jews this option has the most support of any:
- **42% in July 2024**, double the two-state figure ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).
- **December 2022:** 37% backed "one non-democratic state ruled by Israel without equality for Palestinians", and 71% said Palestinians should not demand the vote after annexation ([PCPSR/TAU](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Press%20Release_Eng%20_Joint%20Poll%2024JAN2023.pdf)).
- **INSS, December 2024:** Israeli Jews split three ways. 24% would annex the whole West Bank, 26% only the settlements, and 29% oppose any annexation ([JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/israeli-views-on-the-establishment-of-a-palestinian-state)).
- **Direct Polls, June 2026** (commissioned by the Sovereignty Movement, an advocacy group): 59% backed some form of sovereignty ([Israel365](https://israel365news.com/?p=418654)).
- **Palestinians:** 78% said in 2022 that they would not take part in Knesset elections even if offered the vote ([PCPSR/TAU](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Press%20Release_Eng%20_Joint%20Poll%2024JAN2023.pdf)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: formal annexation is blocked; de facto annexation is advancing.**
- **The formal track.**
  - The 2020 attempt was suspended in exchange for normalisation with the UAE ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2020/08/13/netanyahu-uae-deal-annexation-plans-suspension)).
  - In late 2025 Trump said Israel "would lose all of its support from the United States" if it annexed. Vice-President Vance called the October 2025 preliminary Knesset vote (25–24) "a very stupid" political stunt.
  - The UAE called annexation a "red line" for the Abraham Accords ([KSL/AP](https://www.ksl.com/article/51394247/vance-says-israeli-vote-on-west-bank-annexation-was-an-insult-opposed-by-trump); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/23/vance-criticises-israeli-parliament-vote-on-west-bank-annexation); [Jewish News](https://www.jewishnews.co.uk/uae-warns-israel-that-west-bank-annexation-could-derail-abraham-accords/)).
- **The executive track.** It moved ahead regardless, through the February 2026 land-registration and enforcement decisions. In August 2026 civilian enforcement in the West Bank passed from the army to the Israel Police, a step critics call a "spearhead of annexation" ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/cabinet-oks-new-west-bank-land-registration-process-critics-decry-de-facto-annexation/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/16/spearhead-of-annexation-israel-hands-west-bank-rule-to-civilian-police)).
- **The election.** The October 2026 vote left the annexationist parties inside a 53-seat minority bloc.

### Annexation with phased citizenship is the right's minority bet on a Jewish majority

**Origin and proponents.** A minority strand of the Israeli right, prominent between 2008 and 2014, argued that democracy and Greater Israel can coexist.
- Uri Elitzur, a former Yesha Council head, called in 2008 for annexation with a gradual path to citizenship.
- Likud MK Tzipi Hotovely called for citizenship "in gradual fashion".
- Knesset Speaker Reuven Rivlin said in 2010: "I would rather see Palestinians as citizens of this State than partition the land" ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/the-israeli-right-going-one-state-my-haaretz-piece/); [+972](https://www.972mag.com/knesset-speaker-goes-one-state/)).
- Moshe Arens and Uri Ariel backed versions that excluded Gaza.
- Caroline Glick's *The Israeli Solution* (2014) proposed sovereignty over all of Judea and Samaria, with permanent residency first and citizenship later ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution); [Jewish News](https://www.jewishnews.co.uk/israeli-solution-time-two-state-plan-ditched/)).

**Core terms.** Proponents interviewed by +972 described the following ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/)):
- unilateral annexation over 5–25 years, first Area C, then B, then A;
- blue Israeli identity cards with full rights after security vetting;
- a Basic Law entrenching the Jewish state, enacted first;
- no refugee return;
- Gaza excluded;
- settlements kept.

**Legal and ethical assessment.** The model rests on a **contested demographic premise**. It relied on Yoram Ettinger's estimate of 1.5 million West Bank Palestinians excluding East Jerusalem. PCBS counts 3.43 million including it ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/); [PCBS](https://www.pcbs.gov.ps/portals/_pcbs/PressRelease/Press_En_PalPeopleEnd2025E.pdf)), and critics cite Sergio DellaPergola's projections against Glick ([Jewish News](https://www.jewishnews.co.uk/israeli-solution-time-two-state-plan-ditched/)). The model removes the disenfranchisement problem but not the annexation problem. Without agreement it still conflicts with the non-acquisition rule, and it offers individual rights without Palestinian national self-determination.

**Critiques.** Inside the right, Dayan and Danny Danon opposed Elitzur. Arens worried that Israel's treatment of its Arab citizens would prevent fair absorption. A *Jerusalem Report* writer called the plan "delusional" ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/what-is-the-israeli-rights-one-state-vision/); [Jerusalem Report](https://jpost.com/jerusalem-report/the-two-state-one-state-hora)).

**Public support.** No poll tracks this variant directly. The right's base prefers the no-rights version, and only 14% of Israeli Jews back one state with equal rights ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: very low.** Glick was still urging Israel to "annex everything" in August 2026 ([Jewish Link](https://jewishlink.news/caroline-glick-tells-teaneck-annex-everything/)), but the constituency is thin. The model is also unstable as an endpoint. With Palestinians forming about 40% of the electorate even without Gaza, it would likely drift either toward demands for a binational state or back toward rights restrictions ([INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/one-state-models/)).

## Family 2 — Israeli control with limited Palestinian self-rule: autonomy over people, not land

These models give Palestinians self-administration over people while Israel keeps security control and, in most versions, the land. The idea runs from Begin's 1977 autonomy plan through Smotrich's enclaves to the 2026 extension of Israeli enforcement into Areas A and B.

### Autonomy plans from Allon to Camp David offered self-government with sovereignty left open

**Origin and core terms.**
- **The Allon Plan (1967)** would have annexed most of the Jordan Valley, East Jerusalem and the Etzion bloc, siting settlements to include "as few Arabs as possible". The populated hill country would have gone back to Jordan. It was never formally adopted but shaped Labor-era settlement ([Wikipedia: Allon Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allon_Plan)).
- **Menachem Begin's 26-point autonomy plan (December 1977)** ([JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Peace/AutonomyPlan.html)):
  - abolishes the military administration;
  - creates an elected 11-member Administrative Council, seated in Bethlehem, for civil affairs;
  - keeps "security and public order" with Israel;
  - gives residents "the free option to receive either Israeli or Jordanian citizenship";
  - entitles Israelis "to purchase land and settle";
  - admits refugees "in a reasonable volume", as decided by an Israel–Jordan–Council committee;
  - leaves sovereignty "open", for review after five years.
- **The 1978 Camp David Framework** recast autonomy as a five-year transition toward final-status talks ([Cairo Review](https://www.thecairoreview.com/essays/developing-the-concept-of-palestinian-autonomy/)). Palestinians were not parties and refused to join. The 1979–82 talks foundered on self-government, settlements and East Jerusalem ([US Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1977-1980/camp-david)).

**Critiques.** Israel's Village Leagues of the late 1970s and early 1980s grew out of Menahem Milson's case for a rural, non-PLO leadership. Palestinians still cite them as the template for collaborationist local rule ([Commentary](https://www.commentary.org/articles/menahem-milson/how-to-make-peace-with-the-palestinians/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan)). The historian Seth Anziska calls the autonomy design "state prevention" ([Anziska, unofficial mirror](https://scott-garden.vercel.app/arab-israeli-conflict/camp-david/anziska-autonomy-state-prevention2017)).

**Legal assessment.** It depends on what autonomy leads to. As an interim step toward agreed final status it mirrors Oslo. As a permanent arrangement combined with Israeli land claims and continued settlement, it collides with the ICJ's 2024 findings on annexation and Article 49(6) ([ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176)).

**Public support.** No current poll measures autonomy as an endgame.

### Enclave and emirate models break Palestinian self-rule into city islands

Three newer proposals turn autonomy into a territorial formula.
- **Naftali Bennett's "Israel Stability Initiative" (2012)** would annex Area C, about 60% of the West Bank and all the settlements, and offer full citizenship to the Palestinians living there. PA autonomy would expand in Areas A and B, and Gaza would stay out. Its population count is contested: Bennett estimated about 50,000 Palestinians in Area C; OCHA put the figure at 150,000 ([Palestine Studies](https://chronology.palestine-studies.org/node/2172); [Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2014/05/15/israels-bennett-floats-plan-to-exercise-israeli-sovereignty-devolve-more-autonomy-to-pa-in-post-oslo-era-video/)). Bennett's list won 12 seats in 2026, but no 2026 source shows him still advocating the plan.
- **Smotrich's map of 3 September 2025** would annex **82% of the West Bank** and leave six Palestinian population centres as enclaves, under the principle of "maximum land, minimum population" ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/smotrich-proposes-annexing-82-of-west-bank-in-bid-to-prevent-palestinian-state/?lang=en)).
- **Mordechai Kedar's "Palestinian Emirates" plan** (from 2012) proposes eight clan-based city-states on the Gulf model: Gaza, Jenin, Nablus, Ramallah, Jericho, Tulkarm, Qalqilya and Arab Hebron. Israel would keep the rural land and overall security between the non-contiguous enclaves. The plan sets aside Jerusalem, refugees and borders ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan); [FPA](https://fpa.org/dr-mordechai-kedar-the-eight-state-solution-is-the-best/)).

**The Hebron test case (July 2025).** The emirate idea had its only field test when the *Wall Street Journal* reported a letter from five Hebron sheikhs led by Wadee' al-Jaabari.
- **The proposal.** An "Emirate of Hebron" that would recognise Israel as the Jewish state and join the Abraham Accords. Economy Minister Nir Barkat, who had met the sheikhs repeatedly, asked: "Who in Israel is going to say no?" ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/b11n2tvsgg); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/the-revolutionary-plan-of-five-hebron-sheikhs)).
- **The response.** Hebron's families issued a joint statement backing the PLO, the Ja'abari family disowned its relative, and the PA called it an "Israeli plan". Hamas denounced it as normalisation, and al-Jaabari's car was reportedly torched ([Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/549368.aspx); [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/hebron-residents-and-leaders-denounce-local-sheikhs-plan-declare-independence-and-recognise); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/israel-news/palestinians-torch-car-of-sheikh-who-led-plan-for-hebron-emirate)).
- **The outcome.** Barkat endorsed the emirates framework, but the *Times of Israel* noted that no Emirate of Hebron exists in practice ([Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/413472); [CIJR citing ToI](https://isranet.org/?p=36378)).

**Legal and ethical assessment.** Annexing Area C or 82% of the West Bank is annexation of occupied territory and breaches the same rules as full annexation. The ICJ has already found that Israeli policies in Area C and East Jerusalem amount to annexation ([ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176)).

**Critiques.** Critics compare the enclaves to South Africa's Bantustans and warn of clan violence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Emirates_Plan)). A Palestinian think-tank calls the emirates idea an "orientalist" blueprint for dismantling the national cause ([Vision PD](https://vision-pd.org/en/?p=528908)).

**Public support.** No poll measures these models.

**Feasibility as of November 2026: low as a declared endgame.** No Abraham Accords state endorsed the Hebron proposal. Even so, the February 2026 extension of Israeli enforcement into Areas A and B moves the de facto map toward this family ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/)).

### The Jordanian option died in 1988, and Amman treats any revival as a threat

**Origin and proponents.** Two different ideas share the label.
- **Labor's Jordanian option (1967–1988)** sought territorial compromise with Hashemite Jordan instead of a Palestinian state. It peaked with the **Peres–Hussein London Agreement of 11 April 1987** on a UN-hosted conference. Prime Minister Shamir rejected the agreement, which effectively ended the option ([ECF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/M00147_Peres%E2%80%93HusseinLondonAgreement-EnglishText_0.pdf); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/former-israeli-prime-minister-yitzhak-shamir-a-stalwart-of-israeli-conservatism/)). King Hussein's **disengagement of 31 July 1988** cut Jordan's legal and administrative ties to the West Bank but kept its custodianship of Jerusalem's Muslim holy sites ([UNISPAL](https://www.un.org/unispal/?p=202739)).
- **"Jordan is Palestine"** was revived by Likud figures, Sharon and Shamir among them, from the late 1970s. It cast Jordan as the Palestinian state, and some formulations implied mass transfer ([MERIP](https://www.merip.org/2005/03/ariel-sharon-and-the-jordan-option); [Oxford](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~ssfc0005/Prelude%20to%20the%20Accord%20Likud,%20Labour%20and%20the%20Palestinians.html)).
- **Remnants on the right.** Smotrich's plan lets Palestinians eventually vote for Jordan's parliament, and Hotovely has argued that Jordan was meant to be the Arab state ([HaShiloach](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)).

**Core terms (Allon-style version).** Israel gets the Jordan Valley, East Jerusalem and the settlement blocs. The populated areas and their residents go to Jordan, with Jordanian citizenship. Refugees are absorbed east of the river, and Jordan keeps a role at the holy sites.

**Legal assessment.** A Jordanian option imposed without Palestinian consent sits badly with the Palestinian right to self-determination that the ICJ affirmed ([ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176)). Any "Jordan is Palestine" variant that involves transfer would breach Article 49(1).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: negligible, because Jordan holds a hard veto.** King Abdullah calls the "substitute homeland" idea an "illusion" and says "Jordan is Jordan and Palestine is Palestine". Foreign Minister Safadi said in January 2025 that all talk of an alternative homeland "is rejected" ([Royal Hashemite Court](https://kingabdullah.jo/en/node/47206); [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/58/1262/539295/War-on-Gaza/War-on-Gaza/All-talk-about-alternative-homeland-for-Palestinia.aspx)).

### Conflict management, "shrinking" and economic peace put off the end state indefinitely

**Origin and proponents.** Conflict management treats the end state as deferred without limit.
- **"Mowing the grass."** Efraim Inbar and Eitan Shamir of Bar-Ilan University coined the term in the *Journal of Strategic Studies* (2013–14). It describes periodic short operations that degrade Hamas and buy "a period of quiet" without a political settlement. They warned it could become outdated if non-state actors grew stronger ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowing_the_grass)).
- **Qatari cash.** Under Netanyahu the strategy came with Qatari cash for Hamas-run Gaza: about $15 million a month by 2018 and about $30 million by 2021, according to a *New York Times* investigation. In the words of former intelligence official Yossi Kuperwasser, the logic was that Hamas should be "strong enough to rule Gaza" but "weak enough to be deterred" ([Irish Times/NYT](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/middle-east/2023/12/12/buying-quiet-inside-the-israeli-plan-that-propped-up-hamas)).
- **The contested Netanyahu quote.** A widely quoted 2019 remark attributed to Netanyahu says that whoever wants to thwart a Palestinian state should support "bolstering Hamas". It rests on press reports, not a recording, and Netanyahu calls the claim that he built up Hamas "ridiculous" ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/for-years-netanyahu-propped-up-hamas-now-its-blown-up-in-our-faces/); [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/h1cqppnba)).
- **Post-7 October findings.** The Shin Bet's 7 October probe named the policy of "calm" and Qatari funds among the factors that let Hamas build up ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/report-shin-bet-probe-tied-oct-7-directly-to-pms-policy-of-sending-qatari-money-to-gaza-may-be-another-reason-hes-firing-bar/)). A 2026 *TIME* investigation, as summarised by Ynet, reports that nearly $2 billion was moved with Israeli help ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/l9qc4pft1)).

**Softer variants.** Three softer variants share the premise of deferral.
- **"Shrinking the conflict."** Micah Goodman's *Catch-67* (2017) proposed reducing friction through permits, roads, trade and more self-rule, without withdrawal or a state. The Bennett–Lapid government of 2021 used the idea as coalition glue. Goodman put it this way: "There is no consensus in the government about the end game, but they can agree about the game." The government issued 15,000 new work permits while giving tentative approval to about 2,000 settlement units ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/); [+972](https://www.972mag.com/bennett-saar-goodman-shrinking-conflict/)).
- **Economic peace.** Netanyahu described it in 2009 as "not a substitute for political peace" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2009/7/11/full-text-of-netanyahus-speech)). It became the $50 billion, one-million-job prospectus of the June 2019 Bahrain workshop, which left out borders, Jerusalem and refugees. The PA boycotted the workshop, and the related Abraham Fund never received money ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/6/26/us-led-bahrain-meeting-on-palestine-all-the-latest-updates); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)).
- **Normalisation without a Palestinian component.** The 2020 Abraham Accords with the UAE, Bahrain, Morocco and Sudan, joined by Kazakhstan in 2025, reversed the Arab Peace Initiative's sequence of statehood first and normalisation second ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)).

**Core terms.** These are the status quo:
- no change in sovereignty;
- full IDF security control;
- continued settlement;
- Jerusalem and refugees unaddressed;
- Palestinian political rights limited to the PA.

**Legal assessment.** Indefinite management conflicts with the ICJ's 2024 finding that the occupation itself is unlawful and must end "as rapidly as possible" ([ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176)).

**Critiques.** A former Netanyahu aide called the 2021 period "just a lull between wars". Dahlia Scheindlin warned it perpetuates "the paradigm of Israel giveth and Israel taketh away" ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2021/09/15/israels-new-plan-is-to-shrink-not-solve-the-palestinian-conflict-heres-what-that-looks-like/)). +972 calls it "managing the occupation" ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/bennett-saar-goodman-shrinking-conflict/)).

**Public support.** No poll measures conflict management as such. Its implicit constituency is the bloc that rejects every option or is undecided: 34% of Israeli Jews and 44% of Palestinians in 2024 ([PSR policy brief](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/993)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026.** As a long-term solution it lost credibility on 7 October 2023. As a default, it remains the most likely state of the world, now in a harder form. J Street concluded in mid-2026 that "Hamas and Israel both seem to prefer the status quo" in Gaza ([J Street](https://jstreet.org/resources/nine-months-assessing-status-gaza-ceasefire)).

Regional normalisation has turned from a substitute for a settlement into a lever on one:
- INSS describes Riyadh's demand for a state on the 1967 lines with East Jerusalem as "unequivocal and non-negotiable" ([INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/saudi-israel-2026/)).
- In May 2026 a Saudi source set the bar as an "irreversible pathway" to statehood ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/after-trump-call-saudi-source-says-no-normalization-with-israel-without-irreversible-pathway-to-palestinian-state/)).
- 99% of Saudi respondents called normal relations with Israel a negative step in August 2025 ([INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/saudi-israel-2026/)).

## Family 3 — Partition: the family that fits the legal consensus and has the least support in Israel

Partition gives Palestinians sovereignty over part of the land, or, in the three-state variant, hands that part to Arab neighbours. The proposals run from a conditional state on about 70% of the West Bank to the 1967 lines with minor one-for-one swaps.

### Three-state options would hand Palestinian sovereignty to Egypt and Jordan, which refuse it

**Origin and proponents.** The **Egyptian–Jordanian "three-state" option** would restore the 1949–67 arrangement: Gaza to Egypt, and to Jordan the parts of the West Bank that Israel does not annex.
- **John Bolton** called it the "most attractive prospect" in January 2009, and revived it after 7 October, declaring two states "dead" ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2009/01/04/AR2009010401434.html); [Jordan News](https://jordannews.jo/Section-20/Middle-East/John-Bolton-Proposes-Three-State-Solution-as-an-Alternative-to-the-Two-State-Solution-for-the-Palestinian-Issue-39389)).
- **Other backers** of versions of the idea include Binyamin Elon, Aryeh Eldad, Daniel Pipes and Ian Bremmer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution)).
- **Giora Eiland's regional swap** would enlarge Gaza with about 600 km² of northern Sinai in return for Negev land for Egypt. Reported figures vary ([Daily Alert summary of Haaretz](https://www.dailyalert.org/rss/Mainissues.php?id=32085)).

**Core terms.** Security, refugees and citizenship pass to Cairo and Amman. Israel keeps the annexed parts of the West Bank and their settlements, and Jerusalem stays Israeli.

**Legal assessment.** The option rules out Palestinian statehood, which sits badly with Palestinian self-determination.

**Critiques.** *Foreign Policy* called it "Bolton's zombie idea" ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/?p=14890)).

**Public support.** No poll measures it.

**Feasibility as of November 2026: negligible.** Egypt and Jordan have refused for decades:
- On 18 October 2023 President Sisi rejected "the liquidation of the Palestinian cause and any relocation to Sinai" ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1781978)).
- In February 2025 Egypt called Yair Lapid's idea of a 15-year Egyptian administration of Gaza "unacceptable" ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2591728/middle-east)).
- Cairo says it "will not govern Gaza" ([Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=828889)).

**A second meaning.** "Three states" also describes the **de facto split since 2007**: Israel, a PA-run West Bank and Hamas-run Gaza. Resolution 2803 has now institutionalised it with a separate technocratic committee for Gaza ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_under_Resolution_2803)). That is a drift state, not a design.

### The 2020 Trump plan stretched "statehood" to a conditional minimum

**Origin.** Trump's "Peace to Prosperity" vision was unveiled on 28 January 2020 with no Palestinian participation. It is formally a two-state plan, at the far Israeli edge of the family ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_plan)).

**Core terms** (from the plan text unless noted; [White House PDF](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Peace-to-Prosperity-0120.pdf)):
- **Borders and settlements.** About 97% of West Bank Israelis would be inside Israel and about 97% of West Bank Palestinians inside Palestine. The Jordan Valley would come "under Israeli sovereignty", Israel would "not have to uproot any settlements", and Palestine would be compensated with Negev areas near Gaza. Press estimates put the Palestinian share at about 70% of the West Bank ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2020/01/28/trump-middle-east-peace-plan-palestinian-state/)).
- **Jerusalem and holy sites.** Jerusalem "will remain the sovereign capital of the State of Israel" and stay "undivided". The Palestinian capital would lie beyond the barrier, in Kafr Aqab, eastern Shuafat and Abu Dis. The status quo at the holy sites "should continue", yet the text also says "people of every faith should be permitted to pray" on the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif.
- **Refugees.** There is "no right of return… into the State of Israel". Refugees could go to Palestine, stay in host countries, or resettle in Organisation of Islamic Cooperation states at 5,000 a year for ten years.
- **Security.** Israel keeps "overriding security responsibility" over a "fully demilitarized" Palestine.
- **Conditions for statehood.** Israel and the US would jointly judge whether they are met.
- **The Triangle.** The plan "contemplates the possibility" of moving ten Arab "Triangle" towns inside Israel into Palestine.

**Legal and ethical assessment.**
- Annexing about 30% of the West Bank conflicts with the non-acquisition rule.
- On the Triangle clause, the legal scholar Yuval Shany objected that a state "cannot strip its own nationals of their citizenships against their will" ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2020/02/03/the-trump-netanyahu-plan-to-force-arab-population-transfer/)).
- Fifty former European leaders said the plan would create an apartheid-like situation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_plan)).

**Reception and public support.** Abbas called it the "slap of the century". The Arab League and the OIC rejected it, and the settlers' Yesha Council opposed it because it envisaged a Palestinian state ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_plan)). **94% of Palestinians opposed it** in February 2020 ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/poll-94-percent-of-palestinians-oppose-us-peace-plan/)).

**Feasibility.** The plan was shelved when Israel suspended annexation in return for UAE normalisation in August 2020 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2020/08/13/netanyahu-uae-deal-annexation-plans-suspension)). Its conditional, undated "pathway" to statehood resurfaces in the 2025 Gaza plan and Resolution 2803 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan)).

### The negotiated band from Clinton to Kerry spans 2–7% swaps and a holy-sites deal-breaker

**Origins.**
- **Peel (1937).** Partition began with the Peel Commission, which judged that "an irrepressible conflict has arisen between two national communities within the narrow bounds of one small country" ([Wikipedia: Peel Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission)).
- **UNGA Resolution 181 (1947)** allotted 56.47% of Mandate Palestine to a Jewish state, 42.88% to an Arab state, and 0.65% to an international Jerusalem–Bethlehem zone. The Jewish Agency accepted, Arab leaders rejected the plan, and the 1948 war followed ([Wikipedia: UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine)).
- **After 1967.** "Land for peace" under Resolution 242 became the frame. In 1988 the PLO accepted 242 and called for negotiations on that basis, a step known as the "historic compromise" ([Wikipedia: Palestinian Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Declaration_of_Independence); [Wikipedia: Resolution 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242)).
- **Oslo (1993–95)** created the PA and the patchwork of Areas A, B and C. It left every core issue to permanent-status talks, which never concluded ([Wikipedia: Oslo II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord)).

**The negotiated packages.** Permanent-status talks produced a narrow band of package deals.
- **Camp David (July 2000).** Israel reportedly offered a state on about 73% of the West Bank, rising to about 92%, with Israeli sovereignty over the Temple Mount and Palestinian "custodianship". The summit broke down mainly over sovereignty at the holy sites. Blame is still disputed: Clinton and Dennis Ross blamed Arafat, while Robert Malley and Hussein Agha argued the offer did not meet core Palestinian needs ([Wikipedia: Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit); [Malley & Agha, NYRB](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/archives/2001/aug/09/camp-david-the-tragedy-of-errors/)).
- **The Clinton Parameters (December 2000)** set the template. Both sides accepted them "with reservations" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Parameters)):
  - a Palestinian state on 94–96% of the West Bank plus Gaza;
  - annexed blocs holding about 80% of settlers, against 1–3% swaps;
  - Arab neighbourhoods of Jerusalem to Palestine and Jewish ones to Israel, with Palestinian sovereignty on the Haram and Israeli sovereignty on the Western Wall;
  - a non-militarised state with an international force, and IDF withdrawal over 36 months plus a further 36 at fixed Jordan Valley sites;
  - refugees to Palestine, host states or third states, or to Israel only with Israel's agreement;
  - an end of claims.
- **Taba (January 2001).** Israel lowered its demand to about 6% annexed with 3% compensation, and the Palestinians proposed about 3% at one-for-one. The joint statement said the sides "have never been closer", but Sharon's election ended the talks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba_Summit)).
- **The unofficial Geneva Initiative (2003)** filled in the template ([Geneva Accord](https://geneva-accord.org/the-accord/); [Palestine-Israel Journal](https://pij.org/articles/16)):
  - under 3% annexed at one-for-one, with Ariel and Efrat left outside Israel;
  - Palestinian sovereignty over the Haram, with a multinational presence;
  - a refugee option to Israel capped at the average number taken by third countries;
  - two Israeli early-warning stations for 15 years, plus a multinational force.
- **Ehud Olmert's 2008 offer** was the furthest-reaching on record ([INSS-hosted summary](https://www.inss.org.il/he/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/systemfiles/summary%20of%20Ehud%20Olmert%20Package%20offer%20to%20Abu%20Mazen.pdf); [JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/ehud-olmert-s-peace-offer); [JTA](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/abbas-says-he-rejected-08-peace-offer-because-no-time-to-review-details)):
  - Israel would annex 6.3% for 5.8% plus a safe passage by Israeli accounts. The Palestinian summary in the Palestine Papers gives 6.8% for 5.5%.
  - The Holy Basin would fall under no state's sovereignty, run by a five-nation body.
  - Israel would admit 1,000 refugees a year for five years.
  - The state would have no army or air force.
  - Abbas says he was not allowed to keep the map to study it. The process ended when Olmert left office.
- **The Kerry talks (2013–14)** drafted "1967 lines with mutual agreed swaps" alongside recognition of Israel as "the nation-state of the Jewish people". They collapsed over a withheld prisoner release, settlement announcements, the PA's accession to 15 international conventions and a Fatah–Hamas unity deal. Kerry restated the package as six principles in December 2016 ([New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/118751/how-israel-palestine-peace-deal-died); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry_Parameters)).

**Legal and ethical assessment: this family fits the legal anchors most closely.**
- **Borders and settlements.** Resolution 2334 allows changes to the 1967 lines that are "agreed by the parties", so annexing blocs by agreement fits within it. Evacuating the remaining settlements matches the ICJ's 2024 order ([UN](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/2334%282016%29); [ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176)).
- **Refugees: the contested point.** Resolution 194 says refugees "wishing to return… should be permitted to do so"; Israel reads this as non-binding and conditional ([Wikipedia: Resolution 194](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_194)). Caps of 5,000–100,000 meet Israel's demographic concern. Those who treat return as an individual right reject them: Hamas's 2017 document calls return non-negotiable, and Amnesty describes its denial as part of a system of apartheid ([Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/hamas-2017-document-full); [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/02/israels-system-of-apartheid/)).
- **Sovereignty limits.** Geneva's own critics warned that its limits on sovereignty risked "almost turning the Palestinian state into an international protectorate" ([Palestine-Israel Journal](https://pij.org/articles/16)).

### The Arab Peace Initiative and the "outside-in" track add regional and multilateral payoffs

**The Arab Peace Initiative (Beirut, 2002)** attaches a regional payoff to a bilateral deal. It offers full Arab normalisation in exchange for:
- withdrawal to the 4 June 1967 lines, including the Golan;
- a Palestinian state with East Jerusalem as its capital;
- an "agreed" just solution for refugees under Resolution 194.

In 2013 the Arab League accepted "comparable and mutually agreed minor swaps" ([JVL text](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/politics-wing/peace-process/israeli-palestinian-negotiations-historical-agreements-and-frameworks/the-arab-league-ldquo-peace-plan-rdquo-march-2002); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative)). Israeli responses ranged from Sharon's "non-starter" to Olmert's "revolutionary change". Netanyahu called the "general idea" good in 2015 but rejected it as a basis for talks in 2018 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Peace_Initiative); [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-endorses-general-idea-behind-arab-peace-initiative/)).

**The "outside-in" track.** Since 2024 a third track has reversed the negotiating sequence: recognise the state first, then build it through a multilateral mechanism.
- **The New York Declaration (July–September 2025)** sets a 15-month path to a demilitarised, sovereign Palestine that governs all its territory. It envisages a temporary UN-mandated stabilisation mission, and requires Hamas to end its rule in Gaza and hand its weapons to the PA ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2025_Conference_on_the_Implementation_of_the_Two-State_Solution); [Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/middle-east/2025/07/30/twostate-conference-sets-15month-timeline-for-creation-of-palestinian-state)).
- **Abbas's June 2025 letter** to Macron and Mohammed bin Salman said the state "has no intention to be a militarized State" and promised elections within a year ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2604005/middle-east)).
- **Opposition and holdouts.** The US called the conference "unproductive and ill-timed" ([State Department](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2025/07/united-states-rejects-a-two-state-solution-conference)). Germany, Italy, Japan and the US still withhold recognition, and Slovenia's new government announced plans to withdraw its 2024 recognition ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine)).
- **The September 2026 follow-up.** At the one-year review, Guterres said the conditions for a viable state were being "systematically erased" and that E1 would "deal a deathblow" to contiguity ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168401)).

### Two-state support is lowest exactly where implementation depends on it

**Public support.**
- **The Pulse's detailed package** drew **34% of Palestinians, 25% of Israeli Jews and 69% of Arab Israelis** in July 2024, down from peaks of 43% and 45% in 2017–18 ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)). The package comprised:
  - a demilitarised state;
  - the Green Line with equal swaps;
  - family reunification of 100,000 refugees in Israel;
  - a divided Jerusalem, with the Western Wall Israeli and the Haram Palestinian;
  - regional peace;
  - an end of claims.
- **Gallup (mid-2025)** found 27% of Israelis and 33% of West Bank and East Jerusalem Palestinians supporting a Palestinian state alongside Israel ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/695582/peace-distant-prospect-israelis-palestinians.aspx)).
- **Among Israeli Jews** support is 6% on the right, 1–3% among Haredi and national-religious voters, and **8% among those aged 18–35** ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).
- **Among Palestinians**, an April 2026 West Bank poll found 65.6% would accept a negotiated 1967 state, but only 20.5% thought Israelis would accept one ([Palestinian Peace Coalition](https://geneva-accord.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Perceptions-and-Attitudes-towards-the-Two-State-Solution-in.pdf)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: low in the near term.**
- **The Israeli government rejects the outcome.** Netanyahu declared "There will be no Palestinian state" at an E1 event in September 2025 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1941487)), and E1 received final approval for 3,401 units that August ([Peace Now](https://peacenow.org.il/en/summary-of-2025-in-settlements)).
- **The evacuation load has grown.** At Clinton's 80% bloc ratio, today's 541,000 West Bank settlers imply about 100,000–110,000 living outside annexed blocs, against about 40,000 in 2000. This is a calculation from CBS and registry figures, not a sourced estimate.
- **The Palestinian side.** Hamas's disarmament is unresolved, and 74% of Palestinians oppose disarming before a full Israeli withdrawal ([PCPSR](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)).
- **Swing factors.** These are external and electoral. Saudi normalisation is tied to a credible pathway and to Netanyahu leaving office, and Eisenkot's bloc holds 48 seats without the Arab parties ([Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-914042); [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/with-all-votes-counted-netanyahus-bloc-at-53-seats-eisenkots-48-arab-parties-19/)).

**Critiques.** They come from three directions:
- **Israeli security concerns.** 52% of Israeli Jews expect an accord to increase violence ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).
- **The one-state camp.** Tony Judt, Virginia Tilley and Ian Lustick argue that settlements have already ruled out partition ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2003/10/23/israel-the-alternative/); [Wikipedia: Paradigm Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm_Lost)).
- **Palestinian critics** object to trading refugee return for statehood ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Initiative)).

The failure modes that recurred between 2000 and 2014 are all still present: leadership time limits, disputes over sequencing, settlement and prisoner deals, violent spoilers, and sovereignty over the holy sites.

## Family 4 — Shared and layered sovereignty: residency separated from citizenship

### Confederation keeps two states but lets settlers and refugees live across the line

**Origin and proponents.** Confederation keeps two sovereign states, roughly on the 1967 lines, but separates citizenship from residence.
- **A Land for All** ("Two States, One Homeland") was founded in 2012 by the Israeli journalist Meron Rapoport and the Palestinian activist Awni al-Mashni.
- **Standing Together**, a mass Jewish–Arab movement, adopted A Land for All's framework in November 2025 and launched a party, **Makom Lekulanu**, in June 2026 ([Forward](https://forward.com/opinion/787894/israel-apartheid-peace-movement/); [Forward](https://forward.com/opinion/835250/israeli-politics-standing-together-palestinian/)).
- **The scholarly version** comes from Dahlia Scheindlin and Dov Waxman's "two-state solution 2.0" (2016), and from Omar Dajani and Limor Yehuda's *Foreign Affairs* essay (2024), which calls confederation "at its core, a two-state solution" ([Guardian via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/hope-fades-for-a-two-state-solution-is-there-another-path-to-middle-east-peace); [Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/israel/two-state-solution-can-work)).

**A Land for All's core terms** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Land_for_All_%28organization%29); [MyJewishLearning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/2020/11/25/a-land-for-all/); [American Prospect](https://prospect.org/world/2023-11-17-israel-palestine-land-for-all/)):
- **Borders.** Two democratic states on the Green Line, in an open-border confederation on the EU model.
- **Citizenship and residence.** Citizens of either state may live anywhere as permanent residents and vote in their own state.
- **Settlements.** Settlers may stay as Israeli citizens resident in Palestine under Palestinian law.
- **Refugees.** Palestine may grant refugees return to its territory, with a gradual option to live in Israel.
- **Jerusalem.** "An open and shared city" and the capital of both states.
- **Joint institutions.** Shared bodies for water, health and finance, plus a joint assembly and a human rights court.
- **Security.** Each state is fully responsible inside its own territory, with no Israeli military presence in Palestine.

**The Holy Land Confederation of Yossi Beilin and Hiba Husseini** (second edition, July 2025) is the most detailed text ([ECF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)):
- **Borders and settlements.** It swaps **2.5%** of the land. Israel annexes 21 West Bank settlements (247,044 Israelis) and eight East Jerusalem neighbourhoods (200,979). **105 settlements with 193,565 Israelis stay under Palestinian sovereignty**; their residents become permanent residents of Palestine and keep Israeli citizenship.
- **Reciprocal residency.** An equal number of Palestinians may settle in Israel as permanent residents.
- **Residents' rights.** Residents have equal rights "in every possible respect, except for participation in national elections".
- **Refugees.** They choose among Palestine, the swapped areas, host or third countries, or a limited option in Israel "commensurate with the average number of refugees absorbed by other countries".
- **Jerusalem.** Two capitals and two municipalities. The 1 km² Old City becomes a "confederal and open entity" with joint police.
- **Security.** The confederation has no army of its own. Israel leads external defence at first, international forces deploy in Palestine with limited Israeli participation, the Israeli Air Force provides air defence for the whole area, and a joint counter-terror force may operate in Palestine for 3–5 years.
- **Currency.** A modified shekel serves both states.

NPR/AP coverage in 2023 described the plan as having no swaps; the text contradicts that ([KUNC/NPR](https://www.kunc.org/2023-11-08/2-negotiators-with-peace-plan-experience-have-a-plan-to-kickstart-peace-talks)).

**Legal assessment.** Confederation stays within the framework of the 1967 lines with agreed changes. Letting settlers remain under Palestinian sovereignty by agreement departs from the ICJ's call to evacuate them, but it does so with consent ([ICJ](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/204176)).

**Critiques.**
- **Efron and Gottesman.** Shira Efron and Evan Gottesman list:
  - the asymmetry between a strong partner and one that has never been sovereign;
  - Palestinian fears about settlers staying;
  - Israeli reluctance to share control of borders;
  - the unequal status of residents who are not citizens;
  - the pairing of a democracy with a non-democratic system.

  They cite a RAND finding of "relatively low rates of acceptance" ([+972](https://www.972mag.com/confederation-israel-palestine-obstacles/)).
- **Lawfare** warned that building a confederation could be harder than a two-state deal ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/confederation-israel-palestine-principles-effective-design-and-continuing-challenges)).
- **The One Democratic State Campaign** dismisses confederation, along with two states, as "simply variations of the two-state solution" ([ODSC](https://onestatecampaign.org/en/qa/)).

**Public support.** It is a minority view: **12% of Israeli Jews, 20% of all Israelis, 52% of Arab Israelis and 35% of Palestinians** in July 2024. As a forced first choice, Palestinian support was 18% in October 2025 and 14% in August 2026 ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf); [PCPSR Poll 96](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Poll%2096%20press%20release%20FINAL%20ENGLISH%2028%20Oct%202025.pdf); [PCPSR Poll 97](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: low.** Confederation needs every condition of a two-state deal, plus enough trust to open the borders. It is, however, the only hybrid with a new party behind it in Israel.

### A Jordanian–Palestinian confederation is acceptable to Amman only after statehood

**Origin.**
- **The early plans.** Ideas of union run from King Hussein's 1972 "United Arab Kingdom" plan to the **Hussein–Arafat Amman Accord of 11 February 1985**. The accord called for self-determination "within the framework of a Palestinian state and a Jordanian-Palestinian confederation". Hussein suspended it in 1986 and the PLO abrogated it in 1987 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein%27s_federation_plan); [JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/the-hussein-arafat-accord); [Harvard Crimson](https://thecrimson.com/article/1986/2/20/hussein-quits-joint-plo-peace-effort)).
- **2018.** Abbas said Jared Kushner and Jason Greenblatt had floated a confederation with Jordan, and that he would accept only if Israel joined too. Jordan said the idea was "not on the table" ([JTA](https://jta.org/?p=1590154); [Jordan Times](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/jordan-palestine-confederation-issue-%E2%80%98not-table%E2%80%99-%E2%80%94-govt)).

**Positions.** Jordan will consider confederation **only after** an independent Palestinian state exists, because it fears the kingdom could be "transformed into a Palestinian state" ([Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/1366951/middle-east)). Giora Eiland and Benny Morris have proposed Jordanian-led versions as substitutes for statehood ([The JC](https://www.thejc.com/news/world/giora-eiland-jordan-should-take-charge-of-palestine-hg1czhif); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-state_solution)).

**Public support.** In 2018, 29% of Palestinians backed a confederation with Jordan, and 75% opposed one that included Israel ([PSR](https://pcpsr.org/en/node/737)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026.** As an option after statehood it is plausible but distant. As a substitute for statehood, Jordan's veto makes it negligible.

### Federation and "parallel states" divide into Israeli-led and genuinely shared designs

Federal designs come in opposite forms.
- **The 1947 UNSCOP minority plan** (India, Iran, Yugoslavia) proposed an independent federal state with Arab and Jewish regions and Jerusalem as the federal capital ([IsraelEd](https://israeled.org/unscop-report/)).
- **A Belgian-style binational federation**, with language communities and autonomous regions, has been proposed by Palestinian intellectuals. Harvard's Robert Mnookin notes that the combined territory is roughly the size of Belgium ([Harvard PON](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/research_projects/meni/professor-mnookin-explores-the-ethnic-conflicts-between-israelis-and-palestinians-and-flemings-and-walloons/)).
- **Bosnia's Dayton settlement** is cited mainly as a warning. One analysis argues the analogy breaks down because one side is a sovereign occupier ([Palestine-Israel Journal](https://pij.org/articles/2372)).
- **The Israeli Federation Movement's 30 Swiss-style cantons** ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/b1qst0uru); [Intermountain Jewish News](https://www.ijn.com/alternative-two-state-solution/)):
  - 20 Jewish-majority and 10 Arab-majority cantons under a federal constitution;
  - Israeli citizenship for West Bank Arabs;
  - settlements remain;
  - the IDF is the only army;
  - Gaza stays outside as a separate state;
  - no Israeli party has endorsed it. The right fears an influx of Palestinians, and the left suspects it legitimises settlements.
- **"Parallel states"** (Mark LeVine and Mathias Mossberg, 2014) would divide sovereignty without dividing the land. Two states would overlap across the whole territory, each governing its own nationals, with government functions layered as joint or separate. The editors say this "may in fact be the only way to preserve a two-state solution" ([UCI News](https://news.uci.edu/2014/07/30/uci-professor-edits-book-offering-solution-to-israeli-palestinian-conflict); [Tablet](https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/news/articles/defining-the-one-land-two-state-solution)).

**Legal assessment.** A federation with equal citizenship fits equality norms if both peoples consent. The Israeli-led cantonal version keeps the settlements and a single Israeli army.

**Public support.** No poll measures federation.

**Feasibility as of November 2026: very low.** No party or polling base supports any federal model.

### Trusteeship ranges from the 1947 corpus separatum to the Board of Peace in Gaza

International administration comes in three layers.
- **The corpus separatum (1947).** Resolution 181 made Jerusalem a separate entity under the UN Trusteeship Council, with a Governor from outside the region, demilitarisation, a special police force and a referendum after ten years ([Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/res181.asp)). Neither party has accepted it since 1948.
- **Holy Basin regimes** carve the Old City out of a two-state deal:
  - Olmert's 2008 non-sovereign trusteeship of five states: Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Palestine, Israel and the US ([Middle East Forum](https://www.meforum.org/olmert-details-his-offer-to-abbas));
  - the 2024 Olmert–al-Kidwa plan, which revives that five-state trusteeship ([Al Majalla](https://en.majalla.com/node/322135/documents-memoirs/al-majalla-publishes-olmert-kidwa-peace-proposal));
  - the Jerusalem Old City Initiative's "special regime", headed by a Chief Administrator who is neither Israeli nor Palestinian ([JOCI](https://www.uwindsor.ca/jerusalem-old-city-initiative/sites/uwindsor.ca.jerusalem-old-city-initiative/files/governance_discussion_document_june_2013.pdf)).
- **Gaza under Resolution 2803** is the live experiment.

**Resolution 2803 and the Board of Peace.** Adopted on 17 November 2025 by 13–0, with Russia and China abstaining, the resolution ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166391); [UN via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/11/mil-251117-unsc02.htm); [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/07/gaza-refugees-displacement-board-of-peace-trump-netanyahu-palestine-israel)):
- endorsed Trump's 20-point plan;
- welcomed the **Board of Peace** as a transitional administration with international legal personality;
- authorised a temporary **International Stabilization Force** to demilitarise Gaza;
- set mandates running to 31 December 2027.

The underlying plan's terms ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan)):
- no governing role for Hamas, and amnesty for members who decommission their weapons;
- a "technocratic, apolitical Palestinian committee" (the NCAG) to run day-to-day affairs;
- "no one will be forced to leave Gaza";
- "Israel will not occupy or annex Gaza", with IDF withdrawal tied to demilitarisation milestones;
- a "credible pathway to Palestinian self-determination and statehood" once PA reforms are done.

The Board's roadmap of 30 July 2026 adopts the principle "One Authority, One Law, One Weapon", with phased, verified decommissioning matched by Israeli withdrawal ([Board of Peace](https://boardofpeace.org/gaza-roadmap)). Trump chairs the Board with no term limit. Permanent membership costs $1 billion. The UK, France, Germany and others declined to join ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Peace)).

**Implementation has been thin.**
- **Money.** About $17 billion was pledged in February 2026, but the Board's World Bank account held no funds in May. A $2.45 billion, six-month plan followed in September ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/5ba3bd2c-0e0e-4306-84be-99d6d89b0d49); [PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/trumps-board-of-peace-proposes-2-45-billion-blueprint-to-begin-gaza-reconstruction)).
- **Troops.** 20,000 were pledged. Indonesia put its planned 8,000 on hold ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/05/28/israel-hamas-gaza-trump-indonesia-stabilization-force/be070cdc-5a4a-11f1-8a9d-afb1148204e1_story.html)). Israel's cabinet approved only a 200-troop pilot in a Rafah zone, on conditions that included no withdrawal before full disarmament and police armed only with "rubber batons and tasers" ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/cabinet-gives-initial-approval-for-international-force-in-gaza-with-some-200-troops/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/27/israels-approval-of-limited-gaza-force-a-calculated-manoeuvre-experts-say)). By late 2026 up to 500 Moroccan troops had been trained, according to a Board official. A single-source newsletter puts the force at about 500 from Morocco and Kosovo, with its base unbuilt pending Israeli approvals ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-official-to-toi-up-to-500-moroccan-troops-are-trained-for-gaza-force/); [Gaza Biweekly](https://jaser2.substack.com/p/gaza-biweekly-nineteen-seats)).
- **The committee.** The NCAG, chaired by Ali Shaath, had still never entered Gaza as of 1 November 2026 ([Gaza Biweekly](https://jaser2.substack.com/p/gaza-biweekly-nineteen-seats)).

**Critiques.**
- Hamas called the arrangement "foreign guardianship" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2025/11/17/live-hamas-rejects-foreign-guardianship-of-gaza-before-un-vote)).
- Critics compare it to League of Nations mandates ([Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/opinion/opinion-trumps-gaza-plan-a-blueprint-for-entrenching-colonial-power/3842044)).
- EU High Representative Kaja Kallas noted that the Board's charter omits the UN's 2027 time limit ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/feb/13/dispute-future-of-gaza-trump-board-of-peace-eu-un)).
- Comparative studies note that the UN mission in Kosovo was backed by about 50,000 KFOR troops, and that East Timor's UN administration fared better than Kosovo's ([Oxford Political Review](https://oxfordpoliticalreview.com/2024/08/24/the-strategic-dilemmas-of-un-transitional-administration-in-gaza/); [UNU-WIDER](https://www3.wider.unu.edu/sites/default/files/RB2013-Neotrusteeship%20in%20Post-Conflict%20States%20%E2%80%93%20Lessons%20from%20Kosovo%20and%20East%20Timor.pdf)).

**Public support.** Palestinian trust in the Board stood at 24% in August 2026 ([PCPSR](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)).

**Feasibility and endgame status.** As an endgame, trusteeship is a transitional state with exit conditions. Carnegie's Zaha Hassan expects the Board to dissolve after Trump leaves office in January 2029, which could leave Israel to inherit the administration ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/07/gaza-refugees-displacement-board-of-peace-trump-netanyahu-palestine-israel)).

## Family 5 — One state over all the land: equal citizenship or Palestinian dominance

### The "one-state reality" thesis describes the baseline rather than proposing an endgame

This school argues that a single regime already governs everyone between the river and the sea, with tiered rights.
- **The argument.** Ian Lustick's *Paradigm Lost* (2019) and *The One State Reality* (2023), edited by Michael Barnett, Nathan Brown, Marc Lynch and Shibley Telhami, argue that "a one-state arrangement is not a future possibility; it already exists", and that Palestinians are "permanently treated as a lower caste" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm_Lost); [Abu Aardvark](https://abuaardvark.substack.com/p/israels-one-state-reality)).
- **The objection.** Robert Satloff of the Washington Institute called the argument "breathtakingly tendentious" for ignoring the hard borders between Israel, Gaza and the West Bank ([Washington Institute](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2009/01/04/AR2009010401434.html)).
- **Expert opinion.** Most surveyed Middle East scholars accept the diagnosis. Only about 3% saw two states as probable within ten years, and most expected "a one-state reality akin to apartheid" if partition fails ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/academic-experts-believe-that-middle-east-politics-are-actually-getting-worse)).

The thesis calls for equality but prescribes no particular design. For the game, it is the starting condition.

### Binationalism and the 1947 federal plan never found a mass constituency

**Parity binationalism.** It began as a Jewish intellectual current.
- **Brit Shalom** (1925) included Arthur Ruppin, Martin Buber, Hugo Bergmann and Gershom Scholem. **Ihud** (1942) was led by Judah Magnes and Buber.
- Both sought one state built on political **parity** between Jews and Arabs regardless of numbers, with Jewish immigration regulated by that principle. Hashomer Hatzair adopted binationalism in 1929 ([Wikipedia: Brit Shalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Shalom_%28political_organization%29); [JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism); [Wikipedia: Ihud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihud)).
- Brit Shalom never had more than a few hundred adherents ([Beinin](https://www.bjpa.org/content/upload/bjpa/bein/Beinin%20Intellectuals%20Socialist%20Capitalists%20and%20Binationalism%20in%20Mandate%20Palestine.pdf)). The UN Special Committee on Palestine was "impressed" but unconvinced ([JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/bi-nationalism)).

**Majoritarian one-state proposals** ran in parallel.
- **The 1939 White Paper.** Britain envisaged an independent Palestine within ten years, with Jewish immigration capped so that Jews would be about a third of the population ([Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brwh1939.asp)).
- **The 1946–47 London Conference.** Arab states proposed a unitary state with a permanent Arab majority. Jews who held citizenship would have full civil rights, and naturalisation would require ten years' residence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Conference_of_1946%E2%80%931947)).

Partition and war overtook all of these proposals in 1947–48.

### One democratic state pairs equal citizenship with full refugee return

**Origin and proponents.** The idea moved from a PLO slogan to an intellectual proposal and then to an organised campaign.
- **The PLO phase (1969–74).** A PLO/Fatah slogan called for a non-sectarian state to replace Israel, with citizenship for Jews who had arrived before 1947 ([Palestine-Israel Journal](https://pij.org/articles/482)).
- **Edward Said (1999)** urged "sharing the land that has thrust us together" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)).
- **Tony Judt (2003)** called Israel "an anachronism" and proposed "a single, integrated, binational state of Jews and Arabs, Israelis and Palestinians" ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2003/10/23/israel-the-alternative/)).
- **Virginia Tilley (2005) and Ali Abunimah** (*One Country*, 2006) followed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution); [Wikipedia: Abunimah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abunimah)).

**The One Democratic State Campaign (ODSC)** was launched in Haifa in 2018 as "a Palestinian-led initiative". Its ten-point programme includes ([Mondoweiss/ODSC](https://mondoweiss.net/2018/08/political-democratic-palestine)):
- one constitutional democracy between the Mediterranean and the Jordan;
- equal citizenship;
- **full return of refugees and their descendants**, with their property restored and/or compensation paid;
- constitutional protection for national, ethnic and religious associations, with "no group or collectivity" holding privileges;
- shared civil institutions and a redistributive economic policy.

The campaign frames its goal as "decolonisation". It leaves the fate of settlements to "the '67 refugees and the current inhabitants" of the West Bank. Its strategy is to "collapse Israel's apartheid regime and force Israelis to accept a transition". Jerusalem, the security forces, and any Jewish national self-determination beyond collective rights are left unspecified ([ODSC](https://onestatecampaign.org/en/qa/)).

**Legal and ethical assessment.**
- **Equality and return.** Equal citizenship fits non-discrimination norms. Full return follows the text of Resolution 194, which Israel reads as non-binding ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_194)).
- **Consent.** The central question is consent: a single state imposed on a people that does not consent would raise its own self-determination problem.
- **Demography.** If even the 2.4 million refugees registered in Jordan returned to the area, there would be about 9.8 million Palestinians against 7.4–7.8 million Jews and others, a Palestinian majority of about 55–57%. This is a calculation from PCBS, CBS and UNRWA figures ([UNRWA Jordan](https://www.unrwa.org/sites/default/files/content/resources/jordan_factsheet_2025.pdf); [Arab News/PCBS](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2643317)).
- **Physical feasibility of return.** Salman Abu Sitta argues return is physically feasible because, in his figures, 78% of Israel's Jews live on 15% of its land ([Palestine Land Society](https://www.plands.org/en/books-reports/books/palestine-right-of-return/is-the-right-of-return-feasible)).

**Critiques.** Israeli critics treat full return as ending a Jewish-majority state ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution)):
- Benny Morris calls "secular democratic Palestine" "camouflage for the goal of a Muslim Arab–dominated polity".
- Shlomo Ben-Ami called the idea "ivory tower nonsense".
- Amos Elon predicted an outcome closer to Zimbabwe than to post-apartheid South Africa.
- Hussein Ibish warned of protracted civil war.

**Public support.** In July 2024 one equal state drew 25% of Palestinians, 14% of Israeli Jews and 49% of Arab Israelis. Once supporters of two states were removed, it fell to 8% on each side. As a Palestinian first choice it fell to 12% in October 2025 and 10% in August 2026, though 33% backed dropping two states in favour of one ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf); [PCPSR Poll 96](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Poll%2096%20press%20release%20FINAL%20ENGLISH%2028%20Oct%202025.pdf); [PCPSR Poll 97](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: very low.** The campaign's own theory of change needs a regime collapse and a cross-national alliance, and neither exists.

### A Palestinian state with limited or no rights for Jews is the mirror-image pole

**Origin and proponents.** Maximalist programmes envisage an Arab or Islamic state over all of Mandate Palestine.
- **The 1968 PLO Charter** ([Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/plocov.asp)):
  - Palestine within its Mandate borders is "an indivisible territorial unit" (Art. 2);
  - only "Jews who had normally resided in Palestine until the beginning of the Zionist invasion" count as Palestinians (Art. 6);
  - "armed struggle is the only way to liberate Palestine" (Art. 9);
  - partition and Israel's establishment are "entirely illegal" (Art. 19).
- **Revision.** The Palestine National Council voted 504–54–14 in 1996 to cancel the provisions that contradicted the 1993 mutual-recognition letters, and reaffirmed this in 1998. Critics argue that the procedure, and the lack of a new text, leave the Charter's status disputed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Covenant)).
- **Hamas's 1988 charter** ([Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/hamas.asp)):
  - calls for raising "the banner of Allah over every inch of Palestine" and declares Palestine an Islamic endowment (waqf);
  - rejects negotiations: "There is no solution for the Palestinian question except through Jihad";
  - quotes a hadith on killing Jews and cites the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion*;
  - allows that other faiths may coexist "under the wing of Islam".
- **Hamas's 2017 document** keeps the river-to-sea claim and the refusal to recognise Israel, and calls refugee return non-negotiable. It also describes a fully sovereign state on the 4 June 1967 lines as "a formula of national consensus" and says its conflict is "with the Zionist project not with the Jews because of their religion" ([Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/hamas-2017-document-full)). It did not formally replace the 1988 charter ([Wilson Center](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/article/hamas-rebranding-new-manifesto)).
- **Palestinian Islamic Jihad** seeks a state under sharia across all the land and rejects any settlement ([Mapping Militants](https://mappingmilitants.org/profiles/palestinian-islamic-jihad)).
- **The 2021 "Promise of the Hereafter" conference**, sponsored by Hamas, recommended that Jewish fighters be killed, that others be allowed to leave or be prosecuted, and that educated Jews be kept for a time to prevent a "brain drain" ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/hamas-organized-confab-in-2021-to-plot-administration-of-liberated-palestine/); [MEMRI](https://www.memri.org/reports/hamas-sponsored-promise-hereafter-conference-phase-following-liberation-palestine-and)).
- **After 7 October**, Hamas official Ghazi Hamad said "We must remove that country" and that the attack would be repeated ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/r1y31101m6)).

**Legal assessment: these programmes would breach international law on several counts.**
- They deny a UN member state's right to "live in peace within secure and recognized boundaries" under Resolution 242 ([UN](https://docs.un.org/en/S/RES/242%281967%29)).
- They depend on armed force.
- Where they strip citizenship from Jewish residents who arrived after 1917 or 1948, or expel them, they would amount to deportation or forcible transfer, a crime against humanity under the Rome Statute ([ICC](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/RS-Eng.pdf)).
- They discriminate on national and religious grounds.

**Public support: significant but declining.**
- **33% of Palestinians** backed a Palestinian state without equal rights for Jews in July 2024, as did 17% of Arab Israelis ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).
- 26% of Palestinians said the PA/PLO's long-term aim is to "conquer the state of Israel and destroy much of the Jewish population". 64% of Israeli Jews believe that is the Palestinian aim ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)).
- Support for Hamas's decision to launch the 7 October attack fell from 72% in December 2023 to 53% in October 2025, and Hamas's party identification fell to 19% in October 2026 ([PCPSR Poll 96](https://pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Poll%2096%20press%20release%20FINAL%20ENGLISH%2028%20Oct%202025.pdf); [PCPSR](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)).

**Feasibility as of November 2026: negligible.** The outcome presupposes Israel's military defeat or collapse. Hamas's 2017 formula fits better in a model as a "phased" posture: an interim state on the 1967 lines that does not end the conflict.

- whether settlers are evacuated, annexed or turned into residents;

## Endgame-design inputs: eight variables, a dozen veto players and four clocks

### The board starts in a hybrid, not at any named endgame

No named blueprint describes conditions in November 2026. The actual state of the world combines four elements:
- creeping annexation in the West Bank, through land registration, police enforcement and record settlement approvals;
- PA civil rule in Areas A and B, now overlaid by Israeli enforcement;
- a Gaza split by the Yellow Line, with a thin international administration that has not yet entered;
- partition endorsed abroad but rejected by the Israeli government.

This is the "one-state reality" with a Gaza twist. The evidence favours making it the default attractor: the state the game drifts back to whenever players fail to assemble the coalition a named endgame requires. Several notes in the research recommend modelling formal and creeping annexation as separate tracks with different costs, and treating "international legitimacy" and "facts on the ground" as separate meters. The record from 2024 to 2026 shows the first meter rising (ICJ rulings, recognitions) while the second moves the other way, with weak enforcement linking them ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/); [Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php/sports/world/news.php?id=2468983)).

| Security model | Israeli overriding; Israeli plus local police; international force with a non-militarised state; joint; integrated; Palestinian | All | IDF across the West Bank and most of Gaza; international force about 500 |

Several research findings translate directly into mechanics:
- **Land swaps.** A swap slider running from about 2% (Geneva) to about 7% (Olmert) covers the whole negotiated range. The Trump plan (about 30%) and Smotrich's map (82%) sit far outside it ([Palestine-Israel Journal](https://pij.org/articles/16); [JVL](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/ehud-olmert-s-peace-offer)).
- **Holy sites.** Sovereignty over the Haram/Temple Mount works best as a binary deal-breaker card, with intermediate options: divided, deferred, no state's sovereignty, or international. It was the decisive variable at Camp David ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)).
- **Voluntariness.** In transfer scenarios, how "voluntary" departure is should depend on humanitarian pressure in Gaza, with legal and diplomatic costs rising as pressure rises. The ICRC's genuine-choice test makes coerced consent legally equal to forcible transfer ([ICRC](https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/customary-ihl/v1/rule129)).
- **Residency quotas.** The Holy Land Confederation's one-for-one quota of settlers and Palestinian residents is a tunable parameter that lets settlements and refugees trade off without annexation ([ECF](https://content.ecf.org.il/files/The%20Holy%20Land%20Confederation%20as%20a%20Facilitator%20for%20the%20Two-State%20Solution%20-%20English%20-%202nd%20edition.pdf)).
- **Veto points.** The conditional statehood "pathway" in the 2020 plan, the 2025 plan and Resolution 2803 is undated, and from 2025 it names no arbiter of readiness. That makes it a natural veto point ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan)).

The incentive and framing results deserve special weight in the game's design. Public opinion is endogenous to process rather than fixed. In 2024 a single targeted incentive raised Israeli Jewish support for the two-state package from 25% to between 51% and 60%; examples were Palestinian textbook reform (60%) and compensation for Jews from Arab countries (56%). Release of all prisoners took Palestinian support to 66% ([Pulse](https://www.pcpsr.org/sites/default/files/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2012%20Sept%202024.pdf)). Perceived feasibility works as a hidden variable that drives support. A practical design would compute each constituency's support as a base preference adjusted by a feasibility signal, a violence term and a package multiplier.

### Four clocks are already on the calendar

Several deadlines will force phase changes whatever players do.
- **The Israeli coalition.** The new Knesset was due to be sworn in on 10 November 2026. Coalition formation, or another election, follows ([Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/2026-11-05/live-updates-913880)).
- **Gaza disarmament.** A newsletter reports a 15 November 2026 deadline. It is single-source, and the Board's envoy calls progress "performance-based, not calendar-based" ([Gaza Biweekly](https://jaser2.substack.com/p/gaza-biweekly-nineteen-seats)).
- **Palestinian legislative elections** are now set for **11 September 2027** ([PCPSR](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1018)).
- **The Board of Peace's UN mandate** expires on **31 December 2027**, and Trump's term ends in **January 2029** ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/07/gaza-refugees-displacement-board-of-peace-trump-netanyahu-palestine-israel)).

Two slower clocks sit behind these. South Africa's reply in the ICJ genocide case is due on 22 November 2027 and Israel's rejoinder on 22 May 2029 ([South African Presidency](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/presidency-notes-icj-order-south-africa-and-israel-case-02-jun-2026)). Area C land registration is expected to take about 30 years and converts unregistered land to state land as it proceeds ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/)).

### What the evidence cannot yet settle

Several inputs remain uncertain and should be treated as ranges, not point values.
- **Opinion data are dated or partial.** The last joint Israeli–Palestinian poll on one state, confederation and annexation dates from July 2024. Pew and Gallup did not survey Gaza in 2025–26.
- **Israeli control of Gaza** ranges from 53% to 70% depending on source and date.
- **Casualty figures** are contested, and the Gaza Ministry of Health count sits below some independent estimates.
- **Settler counts** for 2026 come from a pro-settlement registry.
- **Olmert's offer** survives in conflicting versions: 6.3% against 5.8%, or 6.8% against 5.5%.
- **The stabilisation force's size and the disarmament deadline** come from a single newsletter.
- **Party positions.** The 2026 platforms of the leading opposition parties on Palestinian statehood were not documented in the sources reviewed.
- **The coalition outcome** was unknown as of 6 November 2026.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question a political simulation should ask. The decisive split is not between one state and two. It runs between outcomes that require overriding one people's consent and outcomes that require both peoples' consent. The first group comprises transfer, annexation without rights, enclaves imposed from above, the Egypt–Jordan handover and the Palestinian-maximalist state. The second runs from two states through confederation to one democratic state. The first group commands the largest single constituencies on each side, 42% of Israeli Jews and a third of Palestinians, and it is the group international law prohibits. The second is lawful but lacks majorities, and its support moves 20–35 points with packaging, perceived feasibility and leadership. That inversion is the core tension "The Solution" can make playable: legality and popularity point in opposite directions, with large "reject-all" blocs in between.

The second insight is about time. The present equilibrium is stable mainly because each veto player can block the others' preferred endgame more cheaply than it can achieve its own. Washington blocks annexation and transfer, Cairo and Amman block outsourcing, Israel blocks statehood and the Gaza transition, and Hamas blocks disarmament. The levers most likely to break this equilibrium within 24–30 months are political rather than legal:
- the make-up of Israel's next government;
- whether Palestinians renew their leadership in September 2027;
- the Board of Peace's sunset at the end of 2027;
- whether Washington and Riyadh turn their current vetoes and conditions into positive inducements.

A model that hands these levers to players, and lets facts on the ground accumulate whenever no one pulls them, would reproduce the conflict's real dynamics better than any model that treats the named blueprints as menu choices.
